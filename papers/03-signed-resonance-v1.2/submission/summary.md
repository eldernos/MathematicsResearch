# Mathematical summary

Fix a radix *b* ≥ 2 and a prime *p* coprime to *b*. For bounded integer phases *c*<sub>*j*</sub>, insert the jump *c*<sub>*j*</sub> − *p c*<sub>*j*−1</sub> at the position *p*<sup>*j*</sup>, with the usual Stoneham weights. The radix orbit then behaves, shell by shell, like a finite rational orbit determined by *c*<sub>*j*</sub>/*p*<sup>*j*</sup> modulo one. Shells with *c*<sub>*j*</sub> ≠ 0 are uniformly distributed, and shells with *c*<sub>*j*</sub> = 0 concentrate near zero. Consequently the extreme interval discrepancy at every prefix equals the proportion of positions in zero shells up to *o*(1) (Theorem 1). Reading the zero pattern backwards in time identifies the attainable upper discrepancies with the base-*p* values of binary words that dominate all their suffixes. Every such value is realized with phases in {0, 1} (Theorem 7). Rational translation multiplies the main term by the discrepancy of a rational cycle, and cyclic de Bruijn words give transcendental examples of arbitrarily small positive discrepancy. For finite rational combinations of dilated components with one odd prime parameter, normality holds exactly when the cyclotomic polynomial of order ord<sub>*p*</sub>(*b*) divides no explicit cell polynomial (Theorem 3). This yields nonnormal sums of normal numbers in every radix (Theorem 11). The main theorems are kernel-verified in Lean 4. The transcendence statements use Roth's and Ridout's theorems.
