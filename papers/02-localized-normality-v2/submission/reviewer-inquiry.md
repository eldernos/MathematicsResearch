## 1. Author of the Korobov-type differencing estimate (J. Vandehey)

Subject: A question about Corollary 1.2 of "Differencing methods for Korobov-type exponential sums"

Dear [Name],

I am writing about your paper *Differencing methods for Korobov-type exponential sums*, J. Anal. Math. 138 (2019), 405-439 (arXiv:1606.07911), which I use in a preprint on the normality of the series sum over m in M(S) of m^{-s} b^{-m}, where M(S) is the set of positive integers whose prime factors lie in a finite set S of primes. The preprint's Theorem A states that the base-b interval discrepancy of this series tends to 0 if some prime of S does not divide b and to 1 otherwise. Your Theorem 7.4 gives the unit-numerator axis case when S consists of one prime; for three or more primes, some of which divide b, the sparse insertion positions are not a divisibility chain, and the argument uses Corollary 1.2 (in the numbering of the author manuscript arXiv:1606.07911v1, pp. 2-3) as a black box on blocks of length about the square root of N with reduced denominators q supported on the fixed set of primes of S not dividing b, with q at most a fixed power of the block length.

Question: is Corollary 1.2, as stated for a fixed finite set P of primes, valid for all N >= m^epsilon with constants depending only on (epsilon, b, P) and for every numerator a coprime to m, so that it applies uniformly to the blocks in Lemma 2.1 of the preprint (reduced denominators m between a large threshold Q_0 and B^C, block length B >= m^{1/C})? If you know of a place where a hypothesis I have overlooked (for example a lower bound on the order of b modulo m) is needed, I would be grateful to know.

## 2. Author of the generalized Stoneham theorem (D. H. Bailey)

Subject: A question about Corollary 4.9 and Theorem 4.8 of "Random generators and normal numbers"

Dear [Name],

I am writing about *Random generators and normal numbers* (Bailey and Crandall, Experimental Mathematics 11 (2002), 527-546). A preprint of mine, "Normality of localized logarithms and polylogarithms on finite prime supports" (version 2.0), proves in its Theorem D(iii) that for a prime p not dividing b and a rational polynomial numerator P with a pole at zero, the series sum over k in K of P(p^k) p^{-kt} b^{-p^k} is normal in base b exactly when the set K of retained exponents is infinite, with no condition on the gaps in K and no bound on the degree of P. For unit numerators on an infinite K this is Vandehey's Theorem 7.4 (J. Anal. Math. 138 (2019); unit numerators only), and for the full axis it is your Corollary 4.9(i). For signed integer numerators at order one, Kano's Theorem 1 (Osaka J. Math. 30 (1993), 909-919, p. 911) appears to give the case of an infinite K after clearing the denominators of the coefficients, splitting off the integer polynomial part and normalizing the constant numerator by its power of p (with mu_j = p^{k_j} and lambda_j equal to k_j minus the fixed valuation of that numerator); I have not checked its hypotheses in detail. What I have not located is a statement for signed polynomial numerators at order s >= 2 on an arbitrary infinite K; Vandehey's proof seems to adapt, but I have not carried this out.

Question: do the growth conditions (i) and (ii) of your Theorem 4.8 for (b, c, m, n)-PRNGs, or the Section 7 questions, have a known extension to arbitrary infinite subsequences of {c^k} with varying signed numerators of this kind, beyond Kano's Theorem 1 and Vandehey's Theorem 7.4, that I should cite as the antecedent of Theorem D(iii)?

The preprint is at [link]. It was produced in an AI-assisted project and has not been reviewed by a human specialist; the disclosure is in the manuscript.

With thanks,
[Your name]
