# Mathematical summary

For a finite set S of primes let M(S) be the set of positive integers all of whose prime factors lie in S, let b >= 2 be a radix, let J be the set of primes of S not dividing b, and for a rational polynomial P let X_s(P) = sum over m in M(S) of P(m) m^{-s} b^{-m}. The paper proves, with Lean verification of the classification theorems, that for P = 1 and every s >= 1 the interval discrepancy of the radix-b shifts of X_s tends to 0 if J is nonempty and to 1 otherwise, and that for a nonzero rational P the sum X_s(P) over all of M(S) is normal exactly when P(X)/X^s has a pole at zero and J is nonempty; in the failing cases (no pole, or J empty) the sum over every restriction of M(S) is nonnormal, with a discrepancy lower bound 1/L for an integer L prime to b, and the discrepancy tends to 1 when P has integer coefficients. Public work of Captain Sude and of Mira and Mira Cult already covers order one for supports prime to the radix, binary supports containing 3 and two generators, and all orders for supports prime to the radix, and the paper records that those arguments extend to further cases, for instance to all orders when the least prime of S is off the radix; the cases that remain, and that the paper treats, are supports with at least three primes whose least prime divides b (other than radix 2 with 3 in S), the value 1 of the limit when J is empty, and the polynomial, joint and retained-subset statements on multi-prime supports. The key step is that, at an off-radix prime p, the set of positions on which the p-adic denominator of the shifted prefix cancels persistently has measure zero. This is proved by a recurrence argument for rotations on the logarithmic circle that takes multiplicative differences over the cofactor primes, followed by a p-adic separation of finitely many geometric rays; no independence of reciprocal logarithms is needed. If J is nonempty, the shifts of X_{s_1}(P_1),...,X_{s_d}(P_d) at distinct positive orders on one support, with rational numerators P_i(0) != 0, are jointly equidistributed in the unit cube (a theorem about a Lean declaration; common retained masks on a single prime-power axis are included). For a prime p in J, a nonzero polynomial with a pole at zero and a retained subset of M(S), the discrepancy is bounded by the missed density of the windows [p^k, 2p^k) of retained powers of p, and on the axis S = {p} normality holds exactly when infinitely many p^k are retained. For a prime p in J, finitely many orders and a positive tolerance, a depth at which every finite certificate lies below the tolerance is computable. The written proofs and these comparisons have been reviewed only by AI models.
