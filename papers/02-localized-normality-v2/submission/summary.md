# Mathematical summary

For a finite set S of primes let M(S) be the set of positive integers all of whose prime factors lie in S, and for an integer radix b >= 2 and a rational polynomial P let X_s(P) = sum over m in M(S) of P(m) m^{-s} b^{-m}. The paper proves, with Lean verification of the classification theorems, that for P = 1 and every s >= 1 the interval discrepancy of the radix-b shifts of X_s tends to 0 if some prime of S does not divide b and to 1 otherwise, and that for a nonzero rational P the number X_s(P) is normal exactly when P(X)/X^s has a pole at zero and some prime of S does not divide b; in the failing cases the number is nonnormal for every restriction of the support, with a discrepancy lower bound 1/L. The key step is that, at an off-radix prime p, the set of positions on which the p-adic denominator of the shifted prefix cancels persistently has measure zero. This is proved by a recurrence argument for rotations on the logarithmic circle that takes multiplicative differences over the cofactor primes, followed by a p-adic separation of finitely many geometric rays; no independence of reciprocal logarithms is needed. The shifts of X_{s_1}(P_1),...,X_{s_d}(P_d) at distinct positive orders and numerators with P_i(0) != 0 are jointly equidistributed in the unit cube on one fixed support (a theorem about a Lean declaration, including common retained masks on one prime-power axis), the discrepancy of a retained subset is bounded by the missed density of windows [p^k, 2p^k) of retained prime powers, normality on a single prime-power axis holds exactly when the retained exponents are unbounded, and, for finitely many orders and a positive tolerance, a depth at which every finite certificate lies below the tolerance is computable. The comparison with public work shows substantial overlap with Mira's notes (coprime supports, joint values at one prime) and Captain Sude's repositories (order one, two generators), which is credited; the written proofs and these comparisons have been reviewed only by AI models.
