## Exact statements versus extensions of their proofs

The paper separates what a source states from what follows from its proof by a short adaptation. The extensions below are deductions, not statements of the sources. Items 1 to 5 were identified in review-1 (finding 2); the drafter read the pinned proofs of items 1 and 2 on 25 November 2026 and agrees with them (item 2 recomputed below), and has not independently checked items 4 and 5.

1. **Least prime off the radix, any order, polynomial numerators with a pole (Sude `xi-normality` Theorem 5.1 (1); Mira Cult Theorem 2).** The pinned proofs use only that `p = min S` supplies the unique term of maximal `p`-adic valuation: for `p^k <= n < p^(k+1)` the only index divisible by `p^k` is `p^k`, since any other has a cofactor above `p`. The other primes of `S` dividing `b` are cleared from the denominators by omitting `O(log N)` positions after each of the `O((log N)^|S|)` insertions, exactly as in Sude's binary mixed case (the second case of his proof of Theorem 5.1). This yields every positive order when `min S` does not divide `b`, e.g. `b = 15`, `S = {2,3,5}`, and a polynomial numerator with a pole by the one-term valuation comparison of Paper II, Section 5.
2. **`b = 2`, `3 in S`, every order `s >= 1` (Sude Theorem 5.1 (2)).** For `n < 3^(k+1)` the maximal `3`-adic valuation is attained by `3^k` and possibly `2*3^k`. As `k -> infinity`, `2^(-3^k) -> -1` in `Z_3` (since `2 = -1 + 3`), so with `2*3^k` present the normalized top row tends to `-1 + 2^(-s)`, whose `3`-adic valuation is `0` for odd `s` and `1 + v_3(s/2) < s` for even `s` (lifting the exponent). For large `k` the residue is stable modulo `3^s`, and every lower row is divisible by `3^s`.
3. **Two generators at higher orders (Sude `xi-family` Lemma 4).** The slope `v_p(c)/log c` is replaced by `s v_p(c)/log c` and the common denominator bound is raised to the `s`-th power; the spacing of equal valuations and the clearing of radix primes are unchanged.
4. **Singleton supports with polynomial numerators (Theorem B sufficiency and Theorem C(i) for `S = {p}`; Mira Theorem 1).** Clear coefficients by a fixed integer `D` and collect the Laurent polynomial in `m`. Its nonzero pole part is a rational combination of values `sum_k p^(-ek) b^(-p^k)` with distinct `e`, which Mira's joint theorem shows is normal (and jointly uniform at distinct orders). The nonnegative powers have integer coefficients after clearing `D`; their shifted prefixes are integers and their shifted tails are uniformly small off `o(N)` positions by sparsity. Weyl's criterion and the invariance of normality under rational scaling finish.
5. **Axis, unit and signed numerators (Theorem D(iii), Theorem C(ii)).** Vandehey Theorem 7.4 states the unit-numerator case on any infinite `K` (direct inheritance). Kano Theorem 1 (p. 911), as read by the referee, gives the effective order-one signed case after clearing coefficient denominators, splitting off the integer polynomial part and normalizing the nonzero constant numerator by its `p`-power, with `mu_j = p^(k_j)` and `lambda_j = k_j` minus the fixed numerator valuation, discarding finitely many terms (an adaptation). At orders `s >= 2` with signed polynomial numerators, Theorem 7.4 is stated only for unit numerators; its proof appears to adapt once the reduced denominator growth and the polynomial tails are handled (an adaptation not carried out). For Theorem C(ii), the unsigned case for eventually periodic infinite common masks is within October S07 Theorem 9.1: after a finite rational correction one periodic indicator is used at each distinct order, the columns occupy different order blocks and each has a positive cycle sum in every phase, so the matrix has full column rank. What requires the paper's argument: arbitrary infinite masks, polynomial numerators of any degree and order, and the general hypotheses.

The general cancellation problem is the one in which the smallest support prime divides `b` and neither the binary-with-3 protection nor a one-cofactor-generator separation is available; Paper II's Theorem 4.1 addresses it.

## Literature trail (earliest relevant, in order)

- Stoneham (1973), Acta Arith. 22, 277-286: `alpha_{b,c}` normal under `c` odd prime and `b` a primitive root of `c^2` (as reported in Bailey-Crandall's introduction; the article itself was not read).
- Korobov (1972), Math. USSR-Sb. 18, 659-676 (DOI verified; contents not read) and Kano (1993): constructions with varying numerators; Kano's Theorem 1 contains Korobov's case `A_n = 1` (Kano, Remark 1).
- Dibag (1989): localization `L_S` of `log(1+x)` at a set of primes (Definitions 1.1-1.2, p. 520, read).
- Bailey-Crandall (2002), Corollary 4.9(i): generalized Stoneham numbers `sum 1/(b^{m_i} c^{n_i})` for coprime `b, c`; Section 7 asks about replacing coprimality by mutual non-divisibility (answered negatively by Sude `stoneham-prime-support`).
- Bailey-Crandall (2001), Exp. Math. 10, 175-190: Hypothesis A, polylogarithmic constants (not used beyond context).
- Vandehey (2019): Corollary 1.2 (fixed-prime-support short-orbit bound), Theorem 7.4 (unit-numerator series on an infinite subset of a divisibility chain, gap condition `exp((1+eps) log c_k / log log c_k) = o(mu_k)`), Theorem 2.5 (Bourgain's Theorem 8.28, unbounded support).
- Bourgain (2008), J. Anal. Math. 106, 1-93 (Crossref record verified; Theorem 8.28 as restated in Vandehey).
- Wang, "Sparse polynomial-weighted expansions", arXiv:2606.24972 (read the abstract only): if `sum_{n in S} p(n) b^{-n}` is rational for a nonzero rational polynomial `p`, then `S` has positive lower density in long windows. A relation by subject (polynomial weights, sparse radix sums), not a comparison of results; Theorem B/D(iii) give normality, hence irrationality, for specific sparse supports.

## Headline theorems

### Theorem A: unsigned dichotomy (LS-01)
- **Statement and hypotheses:** `S` finite set of primes, `b >= 2`, `s >= 1`. `D_N(sum_{m in M(S)} m^{-s} b^{-m}) -> 0` if `J != empty`, `-> 1` if `J = empty`.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. `unsigned_discrepancy_limit`, `unsigned_finite_support_normal`; the mathlib-only statement `PaperII.unsignedClassification` (limit and normality for `J` nonempty) is checked in `Check.lean`, and "normal iff `J` nonempty" is the case `P = 1` of `PaperII.polynomialClassification`.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Stoneham (1973) and Bailey-Crandall (2002, Cor. 4.9(i)) for `|S| = 1`, `s = 1`; Dibag (1989) for the localized logarithm itself.
- **Closest published results:** Bailey-Crandall Corollary 4.9(i) (singleton, order one, up to the term `1/b`); Vandehey Theorem 7.4 (infinite subsets of one prime-power axis with a divisibility chain); Kano (1993) Theorem 1 (varying numerators on one axis).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Mira S09 Theorem 1 / Mira Cult Theorem 2 (no radix primes, all `s`); Sude `xi-normality` Theorems 1.1, 5.1 (order one; no radix primes, or binary with 3); Sude `xi-family` Theorem 1 (two generators); Sude `stoneham-prime-support` (singleton axis, composite `c`).
- **Relation:** Inherits the statements listed above (coprime supports, binary-with-3 at order one, two generators at order one). Several further cases follow from the proofs of those sources by the adaptations in the previous section: least prime off the radix at every order; `b = 2`, `3 in S` at every order; two generators at higher orders. Apparently new in the sources examined: order one on supports with at least three primes whose least prime divides `b`, other than `b = 2` with `3 in S` (e.g. `b = 10`, `S = {2,3,5}`); orders `s >= 2` on mixed supports outside those adaptations; and the value 1 of the limit when `J` is empty for `|S| >= 2` (for `|S| = 1` the zero-digit frequency of Sude's Stoneham note already gives it). Different method for the new cases: recurrence on the logarithmic circle plus `p`-adic separation of rays (Theorem 4.1), in place of the equal-valuation separation used for two generators.
- **Search record:** "localized logarithm normality finite prime support", "localized polylogarithm normality smooth numbers", "normal numbers series restricted to integers with prime factors in a finite set Stoneham generalization", "sparse series 1/(m b^m) smooth numbers normal Li_s(1/b)", "Bailey Borwein Crandall polylogarithm constants normal"; general web search only (no direct zbMATH Open, MathSciNet or Google Scholar queries were possible); reference trails: Bailey-Crandall Sections 4 and 7, Vandehey Sections 1, 2 and 7, Kano's introduction, Sude's `PRIOR-ART-CHECK.md`. Nothing matching the statement for `|S| >= 3` with radix overlap was found.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** A specialist should check that no earlier statement for `|S| >= 3` mixed supports (for example in Korobov-Kano-type results for numerators supported on products of several generators) implies Theorem A, and the relation of the recurrence step to known multiplicative-recurrence arguments.

### Theorem B: rational polynomial weights (MW-01)
- **Statement and hypotheses:** as in the paper: (i) normal iff `r < s` and `J != empty`; (ii) if `r >= s` or `J` is empty, for every restriction `A` there is `L` coprime to `b` with `liminf D_N >= 1/L`; (iii) if `P` is integral and (`r >= s` or `J` empty), `D_N -> 1` on every restriction. Corrected after review-1: the integral clause is "(`r >= s` or `J` empty)", not two separate sufficient conditions.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified, one declaration per part: (i) `full_rational_normal_iff`, (ii) `rational_boundary`, (iii) `integer_mask_maximal`. The mathlib-only restatement `PaperII.polynomialClassification` covers (i) only. The identification `L = D_0` is a written argument; in the case `J` empty the weights `D P(m)/m^s` are not integers and the radix factors are cleared by old shifts.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Stoneham (1973); Kano (1993) for varying integer numerators on one axis.
- **Closest published results:** Kano Theorem 1 (integer numerators `A_n`, coprime `a, b`, growth and divisibility conditions on one axis).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Mira S09 Theorem 2 / T31 and Mira Cult Theorem 1 (rational combinations of `L_{p,s}`, one prime); Sude `xi-family` (periodic weights in the exponent of `c`, `a >= 2`); Mira #2 S07 (periodic arrays on one axis).
- **Relation:** For `S = {p}` the sufficiency in (i) follows from Mira's Theorem 1 by the perturbation argument of the previous section (item 4); the paper regards it as obtainable from that theorem. Apparently new in the sources examined for supports with `|S| >= 2` (beyond the cases of the previous section), and for the failing-case lower bound `1/L`, the integral clause and the two limits equal to 2/3 (Example 5.1) on every restriction.
- **Search record:** as for Theorem A plus "rational polynomial weights normal numbers Stoneham sparse series", "Kano normal numbers Korobov type varying numerators".
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Same as Theorem A; plus the Kano reduction to the axis case in the previous section (item 5).

### Theorem C: joint distribution at distinct orders (LS-09, MW-08)
- **Statement and hypotheses:** one support `S` with an off-radix prime, distinct positive orders, rational numerators `P_i(0) != 0`; box frequencies tend to the volume. (ii) single axis with common mask `K` iff `K` infinite. (iii) odd `b`, `2 in S`, late powers of 2.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified, one declaration per part: (i) `joint_full_polynomial_orders` (unsigned case `joint_full_orders`, `distinct_orders_checked`), (ii) `joint_axis_orders_iff`, (iii) `joint_late_twos_orders`; the mathlib-only statement of (i) is `PaperII.jointPolynomial` in `Check.lean`. The unsigned case was a written corollary in version 1.1.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Weyl (1916) criterion; Bailey-Crandall Theorem 2.2.
- **Closest published results:** none located for joint distribution on a fixed multi-prime support.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Mira September S09 Theorem 2 / Mira Cult Theorem 1 (one prime per coordinate, singleton supports, distinct `(p, s)`); Mira October S07 Theorems 1.2, 9.1 (periodic arrays on one axis, rank condition, mixed orders). Sude `xi-family` Theorem 2 gives joint uniformity by Weyl for distinct `c` at fixed `a, b`, a different indexing family.
- **Relation:** Inherits the one-prime, several-orders case (Mira) and, by the perturbation argument of the previous section (item 4), polynomial numerators on a singleton support; the unsigned case for eventually periodic infinite common masks on the axis is within Mira October S07 Theorem 9.1 (item 5). Different class otherwise: fixed multi-prime supports, radix primes in `S`, arbitrary infinite common masks, polynomial numerators of any degree. The proof is the standard collection of a character into one scalar series; the new input is Theorem B.
- **Search record:** "joint normality several Stoneham numbers same base uniform distribution torus", "joint normality of localized polylogarithms distinct orders same support".
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** the correspondence of `JointUniform` and `maskedValue` with the displayed statement (read, not machine-checked beyond `PaperII.jointPolynomial`).
