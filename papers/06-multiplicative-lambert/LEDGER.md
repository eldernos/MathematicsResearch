### Theorem A: moving-window word counts

- **Statement and hypotheses:** Under (H1)–(H4) (S ⊇ a reduced class mod q₀, Σ_{p≤x,p∈S} 1/p = δ log log x + O(1) with a bounded remainder, 0 < δ < 1; c ≥ 0 integer multiplicative, c(pᵉ) ≤ e+1, c = 2 on S, c = 0 on other primes ∤ q₀; (H4) c(p₀^{2u}) = 2u+1 for one p₀ ∈ S and all u ≥ 0, used to plant arbitrary words), with κ = 1−δ and fixed 7/12 < a ≤ 1: for N ≥ N₀(w,a) and N^a ≤ H ≤ N, #_w(N,H) ≍ H/(log N)^κ for every fixed word containing a 1, and H − #_{0^k}(N,H) ≍ H/(log N)^κ. The upper bounds hold for every fixed a > 0 under (H1)–(H3). Theorem 10.3 extends the lower bounds to a > 0.53.
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof (paper §§3–6). Lean: `ErdosBorweinArrivals.arrival_sum` (L41), `WeightedLambertIdentities.nonneg_one_bit_count_le` (L166), pinned at e4809cf.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** P. Erdős, *On arithmetical properties of Lambert series*, J. Indian Math. Soc. (N.S.) 12 (1948) 63–66, https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1948-04.pdf: irrationality of E = Σ 1/(2ᵐ−1) by a congruence construction forcing a long prescribed block.
- **Closest published results:** **D. H. Bailey, J. M. Borwein, R. E. Crandall and C. Pomerance, *On the binary expansions of algebraic numbers*, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 16 (2004), no. 3, 487–518, DOI 10.5802/jtnb.457, §9 (pp. 505–512).** With z = Σ_{n≥0} 2^{−n²} and z′ = 2z − 1 = θ₃(1/2), z′² = Σ_{n≥0} r₂(n)2^{−n} = 1 + 4L_{χ₄} (p. 505). Theorem 9.4 (p. 509): “Almost all bits of z² are 0's; in fact the 1's-count has asymptotic behavior #(z², N) ∼ C₀N/√(log N) for an absolute constant C₀ ≈ 0.7996…”. The next paragraph: “the asymptotic density also holds for z′² as follows from a slight modification (actually simplification) of the proof.” Lemma 9.5 (p. 509): the number of n ≤ N with r₂(z,n) > 0 and r₂(z,m) > 0 for some 0 < |n − m| < B is at most cBN/log N. The proof (pp. 509–511) keeps the n whose coefficient is isolated, so that its bits appear intact in the expansion, and gives C₀ = (16L³/π²) Σ_g b(d(g))/ψ(g); the asymptotics for T_g(N) are “achieved as corollaries of the Landau asymptotic formula” without a written proof (p. 511). Consequence for this paper: the one-bit prefix count of L_{χ₄} is ∼ C₀M/√(log M) (multiplying by 4 shifts digits by two, adding 1 changes none), and by subtraction the one-bit count in windows ηN ≤ H ≤ N is asymptotic as well. This is stronger than Corollary 1.1 for w = 1 in that example, and it already gives non-simple-normality and irrationality of θ₃(1/2)². Irrationality: S. Chowla, Proc. Nat. Inst. Sci. India Part A 13 (1947) 171–173 (full text not examined; cited in the footnote on p. 63 of Erdős 1948): Σ χ₄(n)/(tⁿ − 1) irrational for integers t ≥ 5. P. Erdős, J. Indian Math. Soc. 12 (1948) 63–66, Theorem p. 63: f(1/t) and g(1/t) are irrational for every integer |t| > 1, where g(x) = Σ xⁿ sin(nπ/2)/(1 − xⁿ), so g(1/2) = L_{χ₄}. F. Luca and Y. Tachiya, Int. J. Number Theory 10 (2014), no. 3, 623–636, DOI 10.1142/S1793042113501121 (abstract checked via Crossref): for an integer q, |q| > 1, and an eventually periodic rational sequence (aₙ) not eventually zero, the associated Lambert series is irrational; this covers L_χ for every real nonprincipal χ. Crandall, Integers 12 (2012), DOI 10.1515/integers-2012-0007: posed the 11 problem. Bailey–Crandall, Experiment. Math. 10 (2001) 175–190, and *Random generators and normal numbers*, Experiment. Math. 11 (2002), no. 4, 527–546, DOI 10.1080/10586458.2002.10504704, §5 (Conjecture 5.3 and Theorem 5.4: E is 2-normal iff Conjecture 5.3 holds; checked in the authors' posted text): normality of E open. Apart from BBCP for χ₄ and the digit one, none treats sparse multiplicative coefficients, two-sided word counts, short windows or non-normality.
- **Closest preprints:** Campbell, arXiv:2605.24160 (2026, submitted for publication; not a journal publication): the word 11 occurs infinitely often in the binary expansion of E, via an Erdős-style congruence construction and the Alford–Granville–Pomerance estimate (abstract).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Captain Sude, `erdos-borwein-disjunctivity` tag v1.0.0 at [bd98789](https://github.com/CaptainSude/erdos-borwein-disjunctivity/tree/bd98789a177470cc4b3e33e6769e859f6144c906): `lean/ErdosBorwein/Main.lean` L140 `binaryDisjunctive_lambertE (hAGP : AGP) (hSupply : PrimeIntervalSupply)`, i.e. conditional Lean disjunctivity of E. The paper gives a quantitative bound N exp(−C_w(log log N)³). Captain Sude, [`generalized-Lambert-disjunctivity-and-nonnormality` fe33be2](https://github.com/CaptainSude/generalized-Lambert-disjunctivity-and-nonnormality/blob/fe33be210446c6c0b127ca7757fc8f1182c5b541/lean/PowerLambert/Results.lean): Σ 1/(b^{m^r}−1), r ≥ 2, positive word frequencies and non-normality (different family). Mira Cult, [claim 0002 at 1498319](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math/blob/149831943806a656c9efaa14cacbd9c915b2a2ba/notes/number-theory/normal-numbers/erdos-borwein-positive-word-density/README.md): every fixed word has ≥ c_{a,w}H occurrences in every late window [N, N+H), N^a ≤ H ≤ N, 7/12 < a ≤ 1, for E. - **Relation:** For L_{χ₄} and the digit one, **specializes and is weaker than** BBCP 2004 Theorem 9.4 and the remark after it, in prefixes and macroscopic windows (order of magnitude instead of an asymptotic constant). ### Theorem B: exact discrepancy gap

- **Statement and hypotheses:** Under (H1)–(H4), 1 − D_N(L) ≍ (log log N)/(log N)^κ, where D_N is the extreme (unanchored) interval discrepancy of {2ⁿL}, 0 ≤ n < N.
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof. Lean: `Phase17DiscrepancyBounds.one_sub_discrepancy_le_one_bits` (L98), `upper_freq_le_one_bits` (L77), `one_sub_discrepancy_ge_of_separated_mass` (L123).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Discrepancy and normality: a real number is normal iff D_N → 0 (classical; Kuipers–Niederreiter). We did not locate published exact-order discrepancy gaps 1 − D_N for explicit arithmetic constants.
- **Closest published results:** None found for the exact order. For L_{χ₄}, BBCP 2004 Theorem 9.4 gives one-bit frequency → 0, hence D_N → 1 and, through the elementary Lemma 7.1, an upper bound of the same order as ours for 1 − D_N; BBCP do not discuss discrepancy, and the matching lower bound with the factor log log N is not in their paper. Combinatorially constructed disjunctive non-normal sequences are classical. See Calude–Priese–Staiger, *Disjunctive sequences: an overview*, CDMTCS Research Report 63 (1997); bibliographic record only, full text not examined (**unverified**).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Captain Sude, `generalized-Lambert-disjunctivity-and-nonnormality` at fe33be2 (now cited in the paper): Σ (b^{m^r} − 1)^{−1}, r ≥ 2, disjunctive and non-normal with positive word frequencies, the opposite regime. Repository registry CC-46/CC-48 (development notes for this paper). For E, the repository's Phase 13 note derives D⁺(E) < 1 from Mira Cult's density theorem. That result goes in the opposite direction.
- **Relation:** Apparently new in the sources examined. The lower-bound mechanism, runs of about (κ/2) log₂ log N zeros before isolated ones detected by a separated-mass inequality, appears specific to this setting.
- **Search record:** As for Theorem A, plus “discrepancy binary digits multiplicative function” and zbMATH `rich numbers & discrepancy`.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** The disjointness of the marked preceding starts, and the choice R = ⌊(κ/2) log₂ log M⌋ balancing ε against Δ.

### Corollary 1.2: translation rate

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For every N ≥ 1, sup_x |D_N(x+L) − D_N(x)| = 1 − D_N(L), attained at x = 0, and hence ≍ (log log N)/(log N)^κ under (H1)–(H4).
- **Evidence:** The identity (for every real translation z) is Kernel-verified: `SharpTranslation.sharp_translation_isGreatest` (L386). It is Theorem 3.1 (Theorem A in the introduction) of the companion manuscript C. Anderson, *The Arithmetic of Extreme Discrepancy: Translation, Addition Spectra and Rational Operations*, version 1.0, preprint, same release (manuscripts-v2.0.0; papers/10-discrepancy-arithmetic), and is not reproved here. The order is a paper deduction from Theorem B. (Before revision, version 1.0 cited the synthetic-expansions manuscript, papers/07-synthetic-expansions, for this identity; that citation is replaced.)
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Classical: normality is preserved under addition of rationals (Wall) and under certain translations (Rauzy's characterization of normality-preserving translations, via deterministic sequences). We did not consult these primary sources for this manuscript; they are not cited in the paper.
- **Closest published results:** None examined state the exact supremum identity.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** The companion manuscript H cited above. `notes/phase18/translations.md` and SY-24 (`notes/phase18/phase17-transfer.md`). At the coordinator's request we also checked `notes/phase19/geometry.md` at c2c9da6. It recasts 1 − D_N(x − y) as a pseudometric and shows that the identity fails for anchored (star) discrepancy and for two-dimensional box discrepancy. This confirms the scope of the corollary, which concerns one-dimensional unanchored discrepancy only, and changes nothing in it. Not cited in the paper.
- **Relation:** Specializes the companion manuscript's identity. The new content is the order of the right-hand side.
- **Search record:** Reference trail through the companion manuscript and repository notes only. No independent literature search on translation identities was made for this manuscript.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** None beyond Theorem B and the companion theorem.

### Theorem C: transcendence for odd characters

- **Statement and hypotheses:** χ an odd Dirichlet character of any modulus (primitive or imprimitive), q algebraic, 0 < |q| < 1 ⇒ Σ χ(m)qᵐ/(1−qᵐ) transcendental; in particular L_χ for odd real χ.
- **Evidence:** Paper proof (paper §9).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Yu. V. Nesterenko, *Modular functions and transcendence questions*, Sb. Math. 187 (1996) 1319–1348, DOI 10.1070/SM1996v187n09ABEH000158.
- **Closest published results:** A. Hamieh and M. R. Murty, *A note on q-analogues of Dirichlet L-functions*, Int. J. Number Theory 12 (2016) 765–773, DOI 10.1142/S1793042116500494. Theorem 1.1 (p. 766): “Let N and k be positive integers, and let χ be a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor N such that χ(−1) = (−1)^k. If q is an algebraic number with |q| < 1, then L_q(k, χ) is transcendental”, where L_q(s,χ) = Σ_n (Σ_{d|n} χ(n/d) d^{s−1}) qⁿ. At k = 1 this is our series for primitive odd χ. Theorem 3.1 (p. 771): “Let f be a non-zero modular form of level N and weight k with algebraic Fourier coefficients. If τ ∈ H is such that e^{2πiτ} is algebraic, then f(τ) is transcendental.” Imprimitive characters are not treated explicitly. Duverney, Ke. Nishioka, Ku. Nishioka, Shiokawa, *Transcendence of Jacobi's theta series*, Proc. Japan Acad. Ser. A 72 (1996), no. 9, 202–203, DOI 10.3792/pjaa.72.202: transcendence of theta values at algebraic q from Nesterenko's theorem. Metadata checked against Crossref; our account of the content comes from secondary descriptions (Duverney–Tachiya 2019, Remark 1.1, and BBCP 2004, p. 506), and the full text was not read, so the bibliography entry is now marked **unverified**. It is cited only as an aside for χ₄. Bell–Bruin–Coons, Trans. AMS 364 (2012) 933–959: a generating function of a multiplicative function is rational or transcendental as a function. This concerns functions, not values.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** None beyond the development note.
- **Relation:** Inherited for primitive χ (Hamieh–Murty Thm 1.1). The imprimitive extension specializes Hamieh–Murty Thm 3.1 through the oldform combination F(τ) = Σ_{d|P} μ(d)χ*(d) E_{1,χ*}(dτ), which is routine.
- **Search record:** “Hamieh Murty International Journal of Number Theory 2016 Lambert series transcendence Dirichlet character q-analogue”; “q-analogues of Dirichlet L-functions Hamieh Murty theorem primitive character …” (extended); “Duverney Nishioka Shiokawa transcendence of Jacobi's theta series”; “Bell Bruin Coons transcendence of generating functions whose coefficients are multiplicative”. The full text of Hamieh–Murty was read from the archived author PDF with journal pagination (web.archive.org copy of mast.queensu.ca/~murty/hamieh-murty.pdf).
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** That “modular form of level N” in Hamieh–Murty Thm 3.1 covers forms on Γ₁(M) with integral-power q-expansions (it does, since Γ(M) ⊂ Γ₁(M) and the proof uses Prop. 2.3(b) for level-M modular functions). That E_{1,χ*}(dτ) ∈ M₁(Γ₁(q*d)). Even characters remain open.

### Theorem 10.3: lower bounds for a > 0.53

- **Statement and hypotheses:** Under (H1)–(H4), Proposition 5.3 holds with ν_* = 0.53, so the lower bounds of Theorem A and the window statement of Corollary 1.1 hold for every fixed 0.53 < a ≤ 1.
- **Evidence:** Paper proof (paper §10.1: Theorem 10.1 quoted, Lemma 10.2, Theorem 10.3). External input: A. Kumchev, *The difference between consecutive primes in an arithmetic progression*, Q. J. Math. 53 (2002), no. 4, 479–501, DOI 10.1093/qjmath/53.4.479, main Theorem (i)–(iii) with (1.7)–(1.10), as stated in the author's posted text (pp. 2–3; a re-typeset draft dated 11 September 2015). The journal printing was not accessible to us.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Huxley 1972 (exponent 7/12 for primes in short intervals); Perelli–Pintz–Salerno 1985.
- **Closest published results:** R. C. Baker, G. Harman and J. Pintz, *The exceptional set for Goldbach's problem in short intervals*, in Sieve Methods, Exponential Sums, and their Applications in Number Theory, LMS Lecture Note Ser. 237, CUP 1997, pp. 1–54, DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511526091.004, Theorem 3(I), p. 4: for x^{0.55+ε} ≤ M ≤ x/log x and q ≤ (log x)^A, (a,q) = 1, the count π(x;q,a) − π(x−M;q,a) lies between 0.99M/ℒ and 1.01M/ℒ as printed (the factor 1/φ(q) is absent from the printed display; Kumchev's restatement (1.3) has M/(φ(q) log x)). - **Search record:** Reference trail from referee 1; Kumchev's introduction (Baker–Harman–Pintz Theorem 3, Jutila, Perelli–Pintz–Salerno, Timofeev). We did not search for fixed-progression exponents below 0.53 beyond these sources.

### Proposition 6.1: density of the support

- **Statement:** #{m ≤ x : c(m) > 0} ≍ x/(log x)^κ under (H1)–(H4).
- **Evidence:** Paper proof (upper bound from Shiu; lower bound from the planted construction).
- **Closest published results:** For χ₄ this is Landau's theorem. For S a union of classes, Wirsing, Math. Ann. 143 (1961), Satz 1, p. 76, gives an asymptotic formula. Its hypothesis Σ_{p≤x} f(p) = (τ + o(1)) x/log x is not implied by the harmonic hypothesis (1) alone.
- **Relation:** Inherited in the regular cases; the general harmonic-hypothesis case is a routine consequence of Shiu's theorem and the construction.

Further claims of the development note that were changed:

- **Withheld:** “the harmonic hypothesis also permits every real δ ∈ (0,1)” via an ordered selection of primes. Not needed and not checked in detail. The paper states only that unions of residue classes give every rational δ ∈ (0,1).
- **Restated to match Lean:** the discrepancy upper bound (note's (9), using L·B_{M+L−1}/M) is replaced by the kernel-verified form 2^{−L} + (L−1)/N + L·F_N(1/2,1).

- **Not claimed:** transcendence for even real characters and for the δ = 1/4 example. Any extension of a below 7/12 is also not claimed.

No step of the development note was found to be false. The changes above are simplifications, narrowings and citation corrections.
