# Mathematical summary

Let c be a nonnegative integer multiplicative function with c(pᵉ) ≤ e+1, c(p) = 2 on a set S of primes that contains a reduced residue class and satisfies Σ_{p≤x, p∈S} 1/p = δ log log x + O(1) with δ ∈ (0,1), c(p) = 0 at the other primes coprime to a fixed modulus, and c(p₀^{2u}) = 2u+1 for one p₀ ∈ S and all u ≥ 0. Put κ = 1−δ and L = Σ c(n)2⁻ⁿ, a Lambert series with coefficients c∗μ. Examples are L_χ = Σ χ(m)/(2ᵐ−1) for nonprincipal real χ (κ = 1/2), including 4L_{χ₄} = θ₃(1/2)² − 1, and an analogue with c(p) = 2 exactly at the primes p ≡ 1 (mod 5) (κ = 3/4). For θ₃(1/2)², Bailey, Borwein, Crandall and Pomerance (2004, Theorem 9.4 and the remark after it) proved that the number of ones among the first M binary digits is asymptotic to C₀M/√(log M), C₀ ≈ 0.7996, by keeping coefficients isolated enough that their bits survive the carries. Writing c(m) = 2^{V(m)}U(m) with U odd, the fractional parts {2ⁿL} equal fractional parts of a nonnegative displaced sum of the odd parts U. Shiu's Brun–Titchmarsh theorem for multiplicative functions bounds the sum of U over (x−y, x] by O(y/(log x)^κ) for x^σ ≤ y ≤ x, so ones occur O(H/(log N)^κ) times in [N, N+H) for any H ≥ N^σ. Conversely, for a > 7/12 and N^a ≤ H ≤ N, take b = p₀^{2u}m with m squarefree, built from small primes of S∖{p₀}, and primes p ≡ a₀ in a short interval. The coefficient c(bp)2^{−j₀} then equals the midpoint of the cylinder of a prescribed word. A Selberg sieve of dimension 1+κ over the primes outside S, Montgomery–Vaughan's Brun–Titchmarsh inequality, and the Perelli–Pintz–Salerno short-interval prime number theorem (the source of 7/12) show that a positive proportion of these primes leave all nearby coefficients zero and a negligible tail. This gives ≫ H/(log N)^κ distinct occurrences of every word. A lower-bound prime input from Kumchev (2002) extends this to a > 0.53. Every word containing a one therefore has count ≍ H/(log N)^κ, the zero word has deficit of the same order, and L is disjunctive, irrational and not simply normal. Planting a one after R ≈ (κ/2) log₂ log N zero coefficients and applying a separated-mass inequality, together with a one-bit upper bound (both Lean-verified as finite statements), gives 1 − D_N(L) ≍ (log log N)/(log N)^κ. The kernel-verified sharp translation identity sup_x |D_N(x+z) − D_N(x)| = 1 − D_N(z), Theorem 3.1 of the companion manuscript *The Arithmetic of Extreme Discrepancy*, then gives the same exact rate for translation by L. For odd χ, primitive or imprimitive, L_χ is transcendental by Hamieh–Murty (Nesterenko's theorem).
