## Inquiry 1: transcendence for imprimitive odd characters (Theorem C)

Dear [Name],

Your paper with [Name], *A note on q-analogues of Dirichlet L-functions* (Int. J. Number Theory 12 (2016) 765–773), proves in Theorem 1.1 that L_q(k, χ) is transcendental for primitive χ with χ(−1) = (−1)^k and algebraic q. Its Theorem 3.1 extends this to nonzero modular forms of level N with algebraic Fourier coefficients. In a preprint draft on the binary digits of Lambert series, we use the case k = 1 at q = 1/2. For an odd character χ induced from a primitive χ* of conductor q*, we write Σ χ(m)qᵐ/(1−qᵐ) as a constant plus half of F(τ) = Σ_{d|P} μ(d)χ*(d)E_{1,χ*}(dτ), where P is the product of primes dividing the modulus but not q*. We regard F as a nonzero weight-one form on Γ₁(q*P) and apply your Theorem 3.1.

Our question: does “modular form of level N” in your Theorem 3.1 include forms on Γ₁(N) with integral-power q-expansions, as we assume, so that the imprimitive case follows as above? Or is the imprimitive case already recorded elsewhere?
