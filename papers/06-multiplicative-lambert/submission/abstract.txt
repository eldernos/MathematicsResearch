Let c be a nonnegative integer-valued multiplicative function with c(p^e) <= e+1, equal to 2 on a set S of primes of reciprocal-sum density delta in (0,1) that contains a full reduced residue class, and equal to 0 at the other primes not dividing a fixed modulus. Examples are c = chi*1 for a nonprincipal real Dirichlet character chi (delta = 1/2; for chi_4 this is r_2(n)/4) and the analogous function supported on primes p = 1 (mod 5) (delta = 1/4). We study the binary expansion of $L=\sum_{n\ge1} c(n)2^{-n}=\sum_{m\ge1}(c*\mu)(m)/(2^m-1)$. For every fixed a > 7/12, every fixed binary word containing a one occurs between constant multiples of $H/(\log N)^{1-\delta}$ times among the shifts in [N, N+H), uniformly for $N^a \le H \le N$, and the all-zero word occurs $H-\Theta(H/(\log N)^{1-\delta})$ times. Hence L is irrational and disjunctive in base two, while the digit one has frequency zero. The extreme discrepancy satisfies $1-D_N(L) \asymp (\log\log N)/(\log N)^{1-\delta}$; consequently translation by L changes the discrepancy of any real number by at most this amount, with equality at 0. For odd characters, L is transcendental by a theorem of Hamieh and Murty, which we extend to imprimitive characters. The proofs combine Shiu's Brun-Titchmarsh theorem for multiplicative functions, a planted-prime construction with a Selberg upper-bound sieve, and the short-interval prime number theorem of Perelli, Pintz and Salerno. The finite discrepancy transfer inequalities are verified in Lean 4.
