Let c be a nonnegative integer-valued multiplicative function with c(p^e) <= e+1. Suppose that c(p) = 2 for the primes of a set S that contains a reduced residue class and satisfies $\sum_{p\le x, p\in S} 1/p = \delta\log\log x + O(1)$ with $0<\delta<1$, that c(p) = 0 at the other primes not dividing a fixed modulus, and that $c(p_0^{2u}) = 2u+1$ for one $p_0$ in S and all u >= 0. Examples are c = chi*1 for a nonprincipal real Dirichlet character chi (delta = 1/2; for chi_4 this is r_2(n)/4). We study the binary expansion of $L=\sum_{n\ge1} c(n)2^{-n}=\sum_{m\ge1}(c*\mu)(m)/(2^m-1)$. For every fixed a > 7/12, each fixed word containing a one occurs between constant multiples of $H/(\log N)^{1-\delta}$ times among the shifts in [N, N+H), uniformly for $N^a \le H \le N$, and the all-zero word occurs $H-\Theta(H/(\log N)^{1-\delta})$ times. Hence L is disjunctive in base two, but the digit one has frequency zero. For $1+4L_{\chi_4}=\theta_3(1/2)^2$, Bailey, Borwein, Crandall and Pomerance had already proved that the number of ones among the first M digits is asymptotic to $C_0 M/\sqrt{\log M}$; the new content is the short windows, all words, the discrepancy order and the general class. The extreme discrepancy satisfies $1-D_N(L) \asymp (\log\log N)/(\log N)^{1-\delta}$, which by a companion paper is the exact worst-case effect of translation by L on discrepancy. For odd characters, L is transcendental by a theorem of Hamieh and Murty, extended here to imprimitive characters. Finite discrepancy inequalities are verified in Lean 4.
