# Mathematical Summary — Paper IV (Version 2.0)

This manuscript develops a block-level arithmetic certificate framework for base-$b$ normality alongside deterministic, measure-theoretic, method-specific, computability, and proof-theoretic limitations. On the positive side, a radix orbit is normal whenever, for every positive tolerance and denominator floor, almost all blocks on a prescribed geometric schedule admit rational models with mean circle error below the tolerance, reduced denominators above the floor on a fixed prime support coprime to $b$, and denominators bounded by a fixed power of the block length, while partial coverage by disjoint low-discrepancy blocks gives a quantitative discrepancy bound $D_\infty \le 1 - g(1 - \delta)$. On the negative side, accurate fixed-support models on sufficiently long overlaps must agree modulo one after alignment (preventing denominator growth along compatible chains), polynomial-denominator rational models meeting the mean circle error threshold $1/(16b^4)$ on an independently prescribed geometric schedule with polynomially many starts per scale occur only finitely often for Lebesgue-almost every real (Theorem 4.1), explicit computable binary pairs separate odd-primary rational data and logarithmic word laws from ordinary normality, and eight concrete barriers isolate the information lost by specific normality-proof methods. In the computability section, two uniform bounded-evaluation halting reductions show that neither binary normality nor nonnormality is computably enumerable on arbitrary digit-oracle codes `Nat.Partrec.Code`, even under the promise that the named real is transcendental with limiting discrepancy $0$ or $1$. Lean 4 kernel-verifies the qualitative fixed-support estimate (Theorem 1.2), uniform overlap separation (Theorem 3.1), the certificate transfer step, radix-orbit partial-coverage bounds, the finite weighted word inequality underlying Theorem 4.1, and—under explicit endpoint hypotheses—each individual finite-edit, halting-reduction, and nonrecognizability declaration; the measure and Borel–Cantelli deduction, two-way transcendence step, bundling, endpoint existence, and partial-decider consequence are written deductions. The computability section is positioned explicitly as an elementary formalizable two-limit promise reduction, distinct from the boldface Borel $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^0_3$-completeness theorem of Ki–Linton and the effective and boldface $\Pi^0_3$-completeness theorems of Becher–Heiber–Slaman on unrestricted reals and computable-real indices. A separate written Rosser construction exhibits theory-dependent primitive recursive digit programs whose normality is independent of PA or ZFC assuming only consistency, with an explicit remark that the results establish neither normality nor independence for any named classical constant.
