# Mathematical Summary — Paper IV (Version 2.0)

This manuscript develops a block-level arithmetic certificate framework for base-$b$ normality alongside deterministic, measure-theoretic, method-specific, computability, and proof-theoretic limitations. On the positive side, a radix orbit is normal whenever almost all blocks on a prescribed geometric schedule admit small mean-error rational models whose reduced denominators grow on a fixed prime support coprime to $b$ and stay bounded by a fixed power of the block length, while partial coverage by disjoint low-discrepancy blocks gives a quantitative discrepancy bound $D_\infty \le 1 - g(1 - \delta)$. On the negative side, accurate fixed-support models on sufficiently long overlaps must agree modulo one after alignment (preventing denominator growth along compatible chains), polynomial-denominator approximations on prescribed schedules occur only finitely often for Lebesgue-almost every real, explicit computable binary pairs separate odd-primary rational data and logarithmic word laws from ordinary normality, and eight concrete barriers isolate the information lost by specific normality-proof methods. In the computability section, two uniform bounded-evaluation halting reductions (formalized in Lean 4 over arbitrary digit-oracle codes `Nat.Partrec.Code`, taking endpoint existence as an explicit hypothesis) show that neither binary normality nor nonnormality is computably enumerable even under the promise that the named real is transcendental with limiting discrepancy $0$ or $1$, positioned explicitly as an elementary formalizable two-limit promise reduction alongside the stronger Borel and effective completeness theorems of Ki–Linton and Becher–Heiber–Slaman. A separate written Rosser construction exhibits theory-dependent primitive recursive digit programs whose normality is independent of PA or ZFC assuming only consistency, with an explicit remark that nothing in the paper bears on the truth or provability of normality for any named classical constant.
