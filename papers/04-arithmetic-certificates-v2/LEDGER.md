### Theorem 1.1 (AC-01): Normality from mean rational orbit approximation on prescribed geometric schedules

- **Statement and hypotheses:** Fix coprime integers $b,c \ge 2$ and integers $L \ge 2, B \ge 1, d \ge 0$. Let $P_n \in \mathbb Q$ satisfy $b^n x - P_n \to 0$. At scale $k$, put $A=(LB)^k$, $T=B^k$, $m=L^k$. If for each $u \in \{1,\dots,LB-1\}$, each $M \ge 1$, and each $\eta > 0$, all sufficiently large $k$ admit an exceptional set $E \subseteq \{0,\dots,m-1\}$ with $|E| \le \eta m$ such that every non-exceptional block starting at $n = uA + jT$ admits a rational $y$ with reduced denominator $Q \mid c^v$, $M \le Q \le T^d$, and $T^{-1}\sum_{i<T} \|P_{n+i} - b^i y\|_{\mathbb T} \le \eta$, then $x$ is normal in base $b$.

### Theorem 1.2 (AC-04): Fixed-support short exponential-sum estimate (qualitative form of Vandehey)

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For coprime $b,c \ge 2$, integer $d \ge 0$, and $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $M(b,c,d,\epsilon)$ such that $|T^{-1}\sum_{i<T} e(b^i a/Q)| \le \epsilon$ whenever $T \ge 1$, $\gcd(a,Q)=1$, $Q \mid c^j$, and $M \le Q \le T^d$.
- **Evidence:** **Kernel-verified** (`NormalityChecker.fixed_support_orbit_estimate`, `SmoothOrbit.lean#L207`; order bound `NormalityChecker.fixed_support_order_bound`, `StonehamOrder.lean#L74`).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** N. M. Korobov (1972), Math. USSR-Sb. 18(4), 659–676, DOI `10.1070/SM1972v018n04ABEH001870` (complete and incomplete periods).
- **Closest published results:** J. Vandehey (2019), J. Anal. Math. 138(1), 405–439, DOI `10.1007/s11854-019-0038-2` / arXiv:1606.07911, Corollary 1.2 (p. 409; preprint p. 2), which proves the strictly stronger quantitative bound $O_{b,c,d}(Q^{-\sigma}(1+\log Q))$ for $T \ge Q^{1/d}$ and some $\sigma(d) > 0$.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** `CaptainSude/Stoneham-sum-normal` (`https://github.com/CaptainSude/Stoneham-sum-normal/tree/f4d3d1f01d00c57425c684409849053e58e42261`) and `CaptainSude/xi-normality` (rev. `06961817`) use Vandehey's Corollary 1.2 or prime-power differencing in Lean.
- **Relation:** **Inherited (qualitative consequence)** — strictly weaker qualitatively than Vandehey's Corollary 1.2; included solely to provide a self-contained, elementary, kernel-verified proof (including even $c$ via $\varphi(c^2)$) with no unformalized analytic black box.
- **Search record:** Directly checked against Vandehey (arXiv:1606.07911) and Crossref record for J. Anal. Math. 138(1).
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** None beyond verifying the even-support lifting step ($\varphi(c^2)$) and Lean statement match.

### Proposition 2.1 (AC-02): Partial coverage discrepancy bound

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For arbitrary $x_i \in [0,1)$, if disjoint subsets of $[0,N)$ cover $G$ positions and each nonempty subset has interval discrepancy $\le \delta \in [0,1]$, then $D_N \le 1 - (1-\delta)G/N$; asymptotic version $D_\infty \le 1 - g(1-\delta)$ and uniform-share version $D_\infty \le 1-g$; fixed-$q$ mesh uniformity gives $D_\infty \le 2/q$.
- **Evidence:** **Lean-supported paper proof** (specialized to radix orbits in `QualityCoverage.lean#L13,38` and `PartialUniformity.lean#L39,75`; general-sequence proof is elementary on paper).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** L. Kuipers and H. Niederreiter (1974), *Uniform Distribution of Sequences*, Wiley, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.6 (concatenation inequality $D_N \le \sum (N_i/N) D_{N_i}$ for disjoint blocks; also cited as Lemma 4.2 in Bailey–Crandall 2002).
- **Closest published results:** Classical concatenation discrepancy inequality with uncovered positions charged at $1$.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Standard accounting used in localized-logarithm and Stoneham papers (including `CaptainSude/xi-normality`).
- **Relation:** **Inherited (elementary)** — standard weighted triangle-inequality bound recorded here as the exact quantitative companion to Theorem 1.1; no claim of priority.
- **Search record:** Verified against Kuipers–Niederreiter (1974) and Bailey–Crandall (2002).
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** None.

### Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 (AC-03): Uniform separation and overlap rigidity for smooth rational models

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For coprime $b,c \ge 2$ and $d \ge 0$, there exists $\gamma(b,c,d) > 0$ such that for any $T \ge 1$, any rationals $y,z$ with reduced denominators supported on $c$ and $\le T^d$ satisfying $y-z \notin \mathbb Z$, and any real observations $f_0,\dots,f_{T-1}$, $T^{-1}\sum_{i<T}(\|f_i - b^i y\|_{\mathbb T} + \|f_i - b^i z\|_{\mathbb T}) \ge \gamma$. Consequently, vanishing combined mean error on overlaps forces eventual agreement modulo one, and along an overlapping chain forces eventually constant reduced denominator.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 3.1 is **Kernel-verified** (`OrbitRigidity.lean#L32`); Corollary 3.2 is **Lean-supported paper proof** (both halves individually kernel-verified in `OrbitRigidity.lean#L83,101`).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Built directly on the short exponential-sum cancellation of Korobov (1972) and Vandehey (2019, Cor. 1.2).
- **Closest published results:** Vandehey (2019), Cor. 1.2 supplies the underlying exponential-sum cancellation on fixed prime supports. We have not located an explicit published statement of this exact finite-overlap separation and denominator-constancy obstruction for aligned rational orbit models in the sources examined.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** None located in `CaptainSude` or `Mira-Cult` repositories examined at the baseline.
- **Relation:** **Apparently new formulation in the sources examined (direct consequence of AC-04)** — an elementary deduction from fixed-support short-orbit cancellation that isolates why compatible overlaps cannot supply growing denominators in Theorem 1.1.
- **Search record:** Searched Google Scholar, arXiv, and zbMATH-style keyword combinations ("overlap rigidity rational orbits", "uniform separation radix orbits smooth denominators", "mean circle distance periodic fractions fixed prime support", "compatible overlapping rational models normal numbers").
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Specialist check on whether an equivalent separation or rigidity statement for smooth rational orbits appears in the Korobov/Vandehey or symbolic-dynamics literature.

### Theorem 4.1 (AC-10): Counting obstruction for polynomially bounded rational models on prescribed schedules

- **Statement and hypotheses:** Fix $b \ge 2$, $d \ge 0$, $a = b^2$, $\eta_b = 1/(16a^2)$. For any prescribed start $n \ge 0$ and length $T \ge 2$, the set $E_{n,T}$ of $x \in [0,1)$ admitting a rational $y$ with $\operatorname{den} y \le T^d$ and $T^{-1}\sum_{i<T}\|b^{n+i}x - b^i y\|_{\mathbb T} \le \eta_b$ has Lebesgue measure $\le T^{2d}\exp(-\lfloor T/2\rfloor/(4a))$. On any schedule $T_k = B^k$ ($B \ge 2$) with $O(T_k^A)$ prescribed starts per scale, almost every $x \in [0,1)$ admits no such model at any sufficiently large scale $k$.
- **Evidence:** **Lean-supported paper proof** (`NormalityChecker.few_symbol_words`, `ModelCounting.lean#L14` verifies the finite weighted word inequality; measure bound and first Borel–Cantelli deduction are written paper proofs).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** W. Hoeffding (1963), *Probability inequalities for sums of bounded random variables*, J. Amer. Statist. Assoc. 58(301), 13–30, DOI `10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830` (exponential lower tails for independent bounded variables); É. Borel (1909), *Les probabilités dénombrables et leurs applications arithmétiques*, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 27, 247–271, DOI `10.1007/BF03019651` (almost-everywhere normality).
- **Closest published results:** Average-metric (mean-Bowen / $\bar d$) ball volume estimates in ergodic theory and rate-distortion theory (e.g., Lindenstrauss–Tsukamoto, arXiv:1702.05722, for mean-metric covering numbers). Here the union bound is specialized explicitly to rational orbits of denominator $\le T^d$ on prescribed geometric schedules.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** None located.
- **Relation:** **Specializes standard large-deviation / Borel–Cantelli counting** — elementary measure-theoretic obstruction showing that almost every normal real lies outside the scope of prescribed-schedule polynomial-denominator certificates; no claim of priority on the underlying tail bound.
- **Search record:** Searched for "rational orbit approximation mean distance Borel-Cantelli", "average Bowen balls Lebesgue measure rational periodic orbits", and "polynomial denominator shadowing almost every real".
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Specialist check that the prescribed-schedule caveat is unambiguous (does not rule out $x$-dependent block selection or specific constants).

### Theorem 8.1 (AC-11, part 3): Consistency-relative Rosser independence for theory-dependent digit programs

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For $T \in \{\mathrm{PA}, \mathrm{ZFC}\}$, there is an effectively specified primitive recursive digit stream $s_T$ such that if $T$ is consistent, then $T \nvdash \mathsf{Norm}(s_T)$ and $T \nvdash \neg\mathsf{Norm}(s_T)$. Externally under consistency, $s_T$ coincides with the binary Champernowne stream $C$.
- **Evidence:** **Paper proof** (written arithmetization and consistency-relative proof-theoretic argument; not formalized in Lean).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** J. B. Rosser (1936), *Extensions of some theorems of Gödel and Church*, J. Symbolic Logic 1(3), 87–91, DOI `10.2307/2269028`, Theorem II.
- **Closest published results:** Standard Rosser incompleteness applied via finite-edit switching of an arithmetizable property (cf. standard folklore transferring Rosser sentences to analytic/combinatorial properties of theory-dependent programs).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** None specific to the binary Champernowne/Liouville Rosser switch located, though the mechanism is completely standard in mathematical logic.
- **Relation:** **Standard proof-theoretic transfer (folklore mechanism)** — included to separate proof-theoretic independence of theory-dependent digit descriptions cleanly from both the general computability theorems of Section 7 and the open status of named classical constants ($\ln 2, \pi, e, \sqrt{2}$, Erdős–Borwein). No claim of priority on the Rosser-switch technique; Remark 8.2 explicitly records the weak-arithmetic boundary ($Q$ vs. PA, and $I\Delta_0 + \neg\mathrm{Exp}_2$) and disavows any implication for classical constants.
- **Search record:** Verified Rosser (1936) DOI and standard arithmetization references (including formal analyses of Rosser's theorem).
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Proof-theorist check that the PA-internal verification of $T \vdash \mathsf{Norm}(s_T) \leftrightarrow R$ (via integer-only Champernowne counting and the $p + 1 + \lfloor \log_2 N \rfloor$ bound for $F$) has no missing induction or coding detail.
