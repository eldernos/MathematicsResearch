# Reviewer guide for Paper IV

The report gives sufficient conditions on rational approximations to a radix orbit and two obstructions to satisfying them. The first is deterministic and compares two rational models on common observations. The second concerns Lebesgue measure on a prescribed family of search blocks. [Manuscript](paper.pdf) · [LaTeX](paper.tex) · [Declaration correspondence](CORRESPONDENCE.md)

## Claims and scope

| Result | Arithmetic content | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AC-01, Theorems 1 and 2 | Large reduced denominators on a fixed finite prime support yield cancellation on polynomial-length scales. A vanishing exceptional share and small mean circle error transfer that cancellation to all prefixes. | Kernel-checked. The geometric schedule and the real tail of the rational prefixes are part of Theorem 1. The lower denominator floor and precision come before the scale. The analytic estimate is a qualitative consequence of a stronger published theorem. [Vandehey, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07911) |
| AC-02, Propositions 3 and 4 | The saving from disjoint pieces is their covered proportion times one minus their discrepancy. Edges between partial-sum endpoints can be used in either direction. | Elementary centered-count and triangle inequalities. The Lean discrepancy estimates concern radix orbits. The scalar optimality statement uses only the edge constraints and has a direct paper proof. [Interval bounds](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/a09e011720bc74396a27f4cb3ce2d8a5b3ce5035/NormalityChecker/IntervalCoverage.lean#L15) |
| AC-02, Theorem 5 | At fixed individual precisions, any prime that passes forces a large denominator of the same shifted prefix. Only simultaneous failures are charged. | Kernel-checked for unsigned localized series of positive integer order. The nonempty prime set selected for the test is disjoint from the prime divisors of the radix. The full support may also contain primes dividing the radix. [Joint soundness](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/a09e011720bc74396a27f4cb3ce2d8a5b3ce5035/NormalityChecker/DiscrepancyChecker.lean#L113) |
| AC-03, Theorem 6 and Corollary 7 | Separated rational phases incur a uniform total mean error on a shared segment. A chain of compatible overlaps has an eventually constant reduced denominator. | Kernel-checked. Both size bounds are polynomial in the actual overlap length. Models are aligned by the shift to the start of the overlap. This consequence of short-orbit cancellation may have uses outside the examples here. [Finite separation](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/a09e011720bc74396a27f4cb3ce2d8a5b3ce5035/NormalityChecker/OrbitRigidity.lean#L32) |
| Theorem 8 | For any fixed starting position, even allowing all denominators up to a fixed power of the block length leaves an exponentially small set of well-approximated orbits. | The word inequality is kernel-checked. The measure estimate and Borel–Cantelli deduction are proved in the paper. The prescribed starts may be dependent across blocks. There is a count per scale and the schedules are fixed before sampling the real number. [Finite word inequality](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/a09e011720bc74396a27f4cb3ce2d8a5b3ce5035/NormalityChecker/ModelCounting.lean#L14) |

The example in Section 4 has three distinct objects. The upper ordinary density of first-precision joint failures rounds to 0.233241755, by a finite rational enclosure and density of a two-prime phase orbit. Its logarithmic density is about 0.0441. The discrepancy of the number is zero, already by a second-precision kernel evaluation. The first-precision inequality 0.234 is retained to illustrate how partial information combines. It is superseded for normality by the second-precision certificate, and in general by the unsigned classification in Paper II. The unrestricted logarithm falls outside this fixed-support argument. [Example evaluations](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/a09e011720bc74396a27f4cb3ce2d8a5b3ce5035/NormalityChecker/DiscrepancyChecker.lean#L141) · [Full-support classification](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/a09e011720bc74396a27f4cb3ce2d8a5b3ce5035/NormalityChecker/UnsignedNormality.lean#L187)

## Literature comparison

Searches on 24 November 2026 used the following queries and the references in the papers returned. Bibliographic details for the six journal references were checked against Crossref DOI metadata. The repository preprint has an immutable revision link.

- “Vandehey differencing methods Korobov-type exponential sums Corollary 1.2 Theorem 7.4 normal numbers fixed prime set” and “N M Korobov The distribution of digits in periodic fractions 1972”. Korobov studies the digits of rational fractions through periods and portions of periods. Vandehey's Corollary 1.2 gives a power saving up to a logarithm for lengths at least a fixed power of a modulus on a fixed prime support. His Theorem 7.4 assumes a strictly increasing divisibility chain for coefficient denominators and a quantitative insertion-gap condition. Paper IV applies the qualitative cancellation estimate to reduced block models, with exceptions and with mean error. [Korobov](https://doi.org/10.1070/SM1972v018n04ABEH001870) · [Vandehey](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11854-019-0038-2)
- “normal numbers discrepancy approximation rational orbits periodic fractions Stoneham Bailey Crandall normality”. Bailey and Crandall's Theorem 3.1 compares the perturbed radix recurrence and the shifts of the corresponding number. Theorems 4.8 and 4.11 treat synchronized lacunary series. The localized-logarithm manuscript gives exact power-of-three denominators off an exceptional set and a quantitative binary discrepancy estimate in a particular case. These are direct precedents for transferring arithmetic information from rational phases to digits. [Bailey–Crandall](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2002.10504704) · [Localized-logarithm manuscript, revision 06961817](https://github.com/CaptainSude/xi-normality/blob/06961817c7f61181ed9a5c73badb8d908d6ccdcc/paper/localized-logarithm-normality.pdf)
- “radix rational orbits mean distance uniform separation small denominators fixed prime factors periodic fractions normal numbers overlapping intervals” and “normal number construction rational approximations overlapping blocks discrepancy discrepancy of concatenated sequences”. Concatenation estimates in normal-number constructions and the triangle inequality for centered counts are standard. The question of prior formulations concerns the specific finite-overlap conclusion for aligned rational phases of polynomial height on a fixed prime support. The report makes no claim of priority for AC-03. [Short exponential sums supplying the separation bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07911)
- “Hoeffding probability inequalities for sums of bounded random variables” and “average Bowen balls expanding maps Lebesgue measure rate distortion dynamical systems”. The pair-digit calculation is an elementary lower-tail argument for independent symbols. Average orbit metrics also occur in rate-distortion theory. The explicit union over rational starting phases and over a prescribed polynomial number of search blocks is the application in this report. No result on optimal rates in the orbit metric is claimed. [Hoeffding](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830) · [Lindenstrauss–Tsukamoto](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05722)

The material most amenable to separate mathematical review is the use of prime-by-prime survival on the same shifted rational, the endpoint treatment of partially overlapping observations, and the resulting uniform separation for aligned models. Establishing historical priority for any particular formulation would require a wider literature search.

## Questions for an expert reader

1. Does the proof of the qualitative short-orbit estimate cover even denominator support throughout? The choice of φ(c²) in the order bound and the order at the divisor of the correlation modulus are the two relevant steps.
2. Do the floors, the exceptional proportions and the incomplete blocks in Theorem 1 control all prefix lengths with the asserted order of quantifiers? In Theorem 5, can any omitted insertion affect a retained block or its common denominator?
3. In the finite enclosure for the binary example, do the two families of right endpoints exhaust all possible prefix peaks, including a cut inside the newest shell? Does the common cutoff give the stated absolute error after maximizing over relative scale?
4. Are there antecedents for the joint-failure upper bound or the overlap separation statement with these exact support, size and error hypotheses? In particular, the upper ordinary density and the logarithmic density of joint failures play different roles.
5. Does the typical-point obstruction in Theorem 8 make the prescribed-start restriction sufficiently explicit? Where stronger arithmetic information on one named constant is available, the premise for the metric union bound can be irrelevant.
