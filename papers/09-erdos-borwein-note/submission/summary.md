# Summary

Let E = Σ 1/(2^n − 1) = Σ τ(m)2^(−m) and write τ(m) = 2^V(m)·U(m) with U(m) odd, so that the term m deposits the mark U(m) at binary position m − V(m); the digit after a shift n is determined by the resulting column totals C(n+1), C(n+2), …. Comparing with the model Q_∞ of independent Poisson(1) columns of i.i.d. marks (first-digit frequency p_* ∈ [0.492450290, 0.492460864], certified), and using the elementary estimate that the discarded tail has mean μ + o(1) with μ ∈ [2.0762592, 2.0763285], the note proves the following. A bounded statistic Ψ_16 of the 16 packed first columns with E_{Q_16} Ψ_16 ≤ 0.4960081 forces limsup f < 1/2 as soon as its actual mean over dyadic windows stays below 0.4962723 (Lean-supported). For every D ≤ 15 there are exact column-process laws with R_D ~ Q_D and tail mean ≤ μ whose first-digit frequency is at least 1/2. Exact joint laws of every set of at most four columns are compatible with first-digit frequency exactly 1/2. For odd r, matching r Poisson factorial moments forces E(−1)^N ≥ Σ_{k≤r}(−2)^k/k! (Lean-supported), so order ≤ 4 never decides, order ≤ 8 fails on 16 columns, and an explicit order-92 witness succeeds. Floating-point computations with explicit error radii (numerical evidence, not certificates) indicate that a degree-116 Selberg–Vaaler majorant would certify the bias from 58 odd harmonics, whereas no trigonometric majorant of degree ≤ 110 does. These results do not decide whether E is normal; they identify lattice-scale r-point correlations of e(θV)χ(U), with r between 9 and 92, as what the arithmetic would have to supply, and the note proposes three concrete collaborative tasks.
