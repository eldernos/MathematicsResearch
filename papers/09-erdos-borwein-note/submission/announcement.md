## Paragraph for a mathematics forum

I have posted a research note on the binary digits of the Erdős–Borwein constant E = Σ 1/(2^n − 1). It does not claim that E is normal or non-normal. Writing τ(m) = 2^V(m)·U(m) with U(m) odd, the digits of E are determined by V and U at consecutive integers; a natural Poisson model for these predicts a one-frequency of about 0.49245 (certified to within 10^-5). Using an elementary tail-mean estimate, the note shows that a single bounded statistic of 16 packed columns would force a digit frequency below 1/2 if its limiting law matched the model, that no information set built from at most 15 columns can do so, that exact joint laws of up to four columns cannot, and it gives parity-Bonferroni thresholds for factorial-moment information (order at least 9 and at most 92 on 16 columns). It also audits Crandall's 2012 bit counts, credits related work of Campbell, Captain Sude and Mira Cult, and proposes three concrete tasks: certified shell frequencies to s ≈ 30, an upper-bound sieve analogue for the 16-column statistic, and almost-all-scales r-point correlations beyond the two-point theorem of Tao and Teräväinen. The note was produced in an AI-assisted project; the written proofs have not been checked by an independent human expert, and I would welcome corrections.
