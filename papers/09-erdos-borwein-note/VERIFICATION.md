**Note on floating-point computations (Prop. 8.2, 8.3).** Section G of the Phase 17 script evaluates `φ(m)` in IEEE doubles (round-to-nearest, C-library `cmath.exp`, `math.tan`) with a hand-derived radius (series truncation at 64 columns, missing mark mass, uncertainty of μ, and absolute margins of 1e-10…1e-12 for rounding) plus a 1e-9 safety margin. This is not directed-rounding or interval arithmetic, so these two statements are labelled **Numerical evidence** (relabelled from Finite certificate after referee report 1, Finding 1) and are not exact certificates like Theorems 4.2, 5.1, 5.4, 6.1, 7.4 and 7.5. The margins are 4.1×10^{-5} (degree 116) and a quadrature-weight lower bound of 3.2×10^{-3}. An interval- or exact-rational recomputation is an open task that would restore the label (the Vaaler majorant property would remain an external input).

## What is completely kernel-verified

Nothing in this note is completely kernel-verified. Lean verifies the two pointwise inequalities `carry_knapsack_le` and `parity_bonferroni_dual_le` and the finite identities `source_phase`, `arrival_sum`, `one_bit_carry`. Every probabilistic step, the tail mean, all dynamic-programming and linear-programming certificates, and the Fourier computations are written proofs or computations.

## External analytic dependencies

- Selberg–Vaaler majorant inequality (Prop. 8.2): J. D. Vaaler, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 12 (1985), 183–216, Theorems 6, 18 and 19 (pp. 193, 210–212; Theorem 20, pp. 213–214, is the interval case). Verified against the AMS PDF by the referee of review 1; sanity-checked numerically on a grid in `crosscheck.py` (not a proof).
- Crandall's published counts and Table 1 (Prop. 10.1): used as data, read from the journal PDF.
- No deep analytic theorem is used for Theorems 5.1, 5.4, 6.1, 7.1, 7.4, 7.5 and Proposition 7.2. The tail mean (Prop. 3.1) uses only elementary counting of squarefull integers.
- Not dependencies: Tao–Teräväinen (2025) and the other correlation results cited in Section 9 are described, not used. The Section 9 observation that case (ii) of their Theorem 3.1 applies to `e(θV)χ(U)` at each fixed θ is not a theorem of the note; it rests on a referee's reading of the arXiv source and on the pretentious-distance computation of their Section 4.1 (not re-derived).

Human specialist review is outstanding. Cross-model review (by models from other providers) is not independent human peer review.
