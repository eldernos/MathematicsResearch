# Mathematical summary

For an integer base $b\ge2$ and the extreme discrepancy $D_N$ of the radix orbit, the paper proves the finite identity $\sup_x|D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)|=1-D_N(z)$ (kernel-verified), deduces that translation by $z$ preserves both limiting discrepancies of every real number exactly when $z=c+w$ with $(b-1)c\in\mathbb Z$ and $N^{-1}\sum_{n<N}\lVert b^nw\rVert\to0$, and shows that this class is strictly smaller than Rauzy's class of normality-preserving translations. Two applications of the identity give the finite inequalities $\max(0,\alpha+\beta-1)\le\gamma\le1-|\alpha-\beta|$ for $(D_N(x),D_N(y),D_N(x+y))$; conversely every triple in this five-vertex polytope is a triple of limits (kernel-verified in every base), realized for each odd prime $p\nmid b$ by positive $p$-power coefficients on the schedule $tk^3$ with uniform error $O(N^{-1/3})$, by computable and algebraically independent witnesses (written proofs); for $d$ summands the deficit vectors are exactly those satisfying the polygon inequalities, and a six-form inequality shows that triples do not determine joint behaviour. The deficit $d_N(x,y)=1-D_N(x-y)$ is a translation-invariant pseudometric whose quotient at finite $N$ is the circle $\mathbb R/(b-1)^{-1}\mathbb Z$; it rewrites Lévy's concentration function and lies between the Ky Fan distance to constants and three times it, so it is not a new probability metric. Its asymptotic kernel $K_b=\{z:D_N(z)\to1\}$ is preserved by a rational scalar exactly when the scalar lies in $\mathbb Z[1/b]$, individual scalings are decided by a residue-density test, and for integer dilation the attainable limits of $D_N(x)$ on the boundary $D_N(mx)\to1$ are exactly $[1/m_0,1]$, $m_0$ being the part of $m$ prime to $b$.
