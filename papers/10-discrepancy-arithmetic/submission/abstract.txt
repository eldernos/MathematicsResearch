For an integer base $b \ge 2$ let $D_N(x)$ be the extreme discrepancy of $x, bx, \ldots, b^{N-1}x$ modulo one. We prove the finite identity $\sup_x |D_N(x+z) - D_N(x)| = 1 - D_N(z)$, attained at $x = 0$, and classify the translations that preserve both limiting discrepancies of every real number: they are the numbers $c + w$ with $(b-1)c$ an integer and $N^{-1} \sum_{n<N} \|b^n w\| \to 0$. The limits of $D_N(x)$, $D_N(y)$ and $D_N(x+y)$ are exactly the triples in $[0,1]^3$ with $\max(0, \alpha+\beta-1) \le \gamma \le 1 - |\alpha-\beta|$. Every such triple is realized by positive rational expansions on a cubic schedule with simultaneous error $O(N^{-1/3})$, uniformly in the triple, by computable and algebraically independent witnesses; the analogous deficit polytope for $d$ summands is determined. The translation deficit $1 - D_N(x-y)$ is a pseudometric, an exact form of Levy's concentration function comparable to the Ky Fan distance to constants. The rational scalars preserving its asymptotic kernel are exactly Z[1/b], an individual rational scaling is decided by a residue-density test, and on the boundary $D_N(mx) \to 1$ the attainable limits of $D_N(x)$ form $[1/m_0, 1]$, where $m_0$ is the part of $m$ prime to $b$. The finite identity, the pseudometric axioms and the qualitative addition spectrum are verified by the Lean 4 kernel.
