## What is completely kernel-verified

- The first 71 cells are those evaluated at v/2 for 1 ≤ v ≤ 71, where cell v is the prime band 2N/(v+1) < p ≤ 2N/v. The exact branch-and-bound declaration gives weight at least 13/30 outside (0, 0, 0, −5) and (0, 0, 0, −2). Two separate declarations give the weights 7/18 and 73/180 at those points. All three are **Kernel-verified** for the rational parameter and the definitions in their statements. The weighted tail, trace completeness, primitive-divisor descent and identification with asymptotic reduced-denominator rates are paper arguments. [Global bound][mass] · [Minimum row][min] · [Runner-up row][runner]
- The named Gaussian arithmetic, polynomial moment bounds and fixed-support estimate are **Kernel-verified** under their declaration hypotheses. The real-only `optimal_rate` takes prime-valuation balance, finiteness of the zero set and primitive-divisor hypotheses. The Gaussian arctangent rate uses a paper proof of the imaginary-part congruence. The fixed-prime logarithm partition takes one common prime. The fresh-prime and BBP partitions and all their joint distribution conclusions are paper deductions. The declaration `fixed_support_orbit_estimate` states exactly the fixed-support block bound used in Step 5 of the ray theorem and nothing else about it. [Real-only declaration][real-only] · [Fixed-prime identity][fixedray] · [Orbit estimate][smooth]
- The golden-ratio equivalence in §7 is **Kernel-verified**. Its positive-lower-density condition is open here. [`NormalityChecker.golden_discrepancy_iff_small_index`][golden]
- The recorded replay and axiom audit cover the pinned declarations. This manuscript revision checked statement text and line numbers at that Git object. [Checkpoint verification](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/notes/phase18/verification.md)

## External analytic dependencies

- **External input.** Bang, *Taltheoretiske Undersøgelser*, 1886, pp. 70–80, and Zsigmondy, *Zur Theorie der Potenzreste*, 1892, §XII, pp. 282–283, give a primitive prime of 2^k − 1 for every k > 6. This is the specialization to base pair (2, 1). Bang's scanned article text was unavailable during this revision. The journal contents and the attribution in Grechkoseeva's corrected paper were checked. Zsigmondy's own statement and specialization were checked in the archival text. [Bang first installment](https://www.jstor.org/stable/24539988) · [Zsigmondy](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01692444)
- **External input.** Grechkoseeva, corrected version, Theorem 2, PDF p. 2, Suzuki parameters (v, n, m) = (2, 4, n), supplies divisors of order 4n in each factor for odd n ≥ 7. The preprint is the corrected statement used here. [Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04729v1)
- **External input.** Hadamard, *Sur la distribution des zéros de la fonction ζ(s) et ses conséquences arithmétiques*, §19, p. 219, gives the logarithmic prime sum in progressions. The cases of modulus 1 and 4 suffice here. Prime powers beyond the first contribute O(√x log x), giving $\log L_{\lfloor x\rfloor}=x+o(x)$. [Primary paper](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMF_1896__24__199_1/)
- **External input.** DLMF Table 18.3.1, Legendre row, and Eq. 18.10.5 supply orthogonality and Laplace's integral. The $O(\rho^{-D})$ and $O(\rho^{-2D})$ errors use polynomial truncation and the manuscript's rational moment construction. [Orthogonality](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3#T1) · [Integral](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.10.E5)
- **External input.** Marcovecchio, Theorem 1.1, p. 147, and Zeilberger–Zudilin, p. 407 and Part II, supply explicit finite irrationality-exponent upper bounds. Rukhadze, pp. 25–29, and Hata, Theorem 1.1, p. 336, supply earlier bounds. Euler's continued fraction (E71) supplies the exponent calculation for e. [Marcovecchio](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa139-2-5) · [Zeilberger–Zudilin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06345) · [Rukhadze](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/vmumm/y1987/i6/p25) · [Hata](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-63-4-335-349) · [Euler](https://scholarlycommons.pacific.edu/euler-works/71/)
- **External input.** BBP Eq. (1.2) and Bellard Eq. (1) give the original series. The cited fixed-support estimate of Vandehey, Corollary 1.2, applies with fixed off-base prime support and lengths at least a fixed power of the denominator. The manuscript uses the same qualitative input available in Lean. [BBP](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-97-00856-9) · [Bellard](https://bellard.org/pi/pi_bin/pi_bin.html) · [Vandehey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07911#page=3)

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

```powershell
wsl -d Ubuntu -- bash papers/wsl-build.sh 05-classical-denominators-v2
wsl -d Ubuntu -- python3 -B papers/check_release.py --verify-pins
python -B scripts/phase16_adaptive_log.py
python -B scripts/phase16_nonflat.py
python -B scripts/phase17_classical_approximants.py
python -B scripts/phase18_classical.py
```

The WSL builder requires TinyTeX and resolves bibliography and cross-reference passes with Latexmk. `--verify-pins` requires Poppler. `python3 -B` can replace `python -B` where available. For a full separate Lean replay use the pinned checkpoint's [verification guide](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/docs/verification.md) in its Lean environment. The release build of the PDF does not invoke Lean. In a Windows worktree with a drive-letter path in .git, set WSL's GIT_DIR to the parent clone's Git directory translated with wslpath for the read-only pin check. The full release checker also compares the aggregate checksum file, which is maintained at release assembly. The scoped PDF and source pins validate before that assembly.
