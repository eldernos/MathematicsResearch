# Mathematical summary

For a balanced trace with roots sharing modulus $2^{a/e}$ and root-of-unity phases, the prefix keeps Taylor terms with effective binary exponent at most the cutoff. Every such prefix is an integral combination of real and Gaussian logarithm prefixes. For a fixed finite rational combination targeting $\log 2$, the reduced-denominator rate of its shifted prefix is the total prime-band weight of its nonzero short evaluations. The two lowest rates are $5/12$ and $13/30$, and the real dyadic minimum is $1/2$. The mass comparison on 71 half-cells has a Lean certificate. The paper also gives adaptive-height bounds, a $D^{-2}$ Chebyshev residual floor, and Legendre–Bézout models for $\log 2$ and $\pi$ at every sufficiently large binary shift. The latter attain the nearest-grid numerator under an explicit separation condition, and the window–denominator inequality bounds precision by $(\mu-1)N+\mu\log_2q_N+O(1)$. Positive Taylor grouping delays, exact geometric rays and a functional support obstruction explain additional limits of rearrangement. Exact one-prime completion, superpolynomial periods for the ordinary Taylor moduli and horizontal row discrepancies distinguish three features of the Taylor representation. The arithmetic bounds leave the limiting digit distribution of the five classical constants open.
