# Mathematical summary

For an integer base $b\ge2$ and a prime $p\nmid b$ with $t=\operatorname{ord}_p(b)$, the paper studies sums $\sum_kc_kb^{-n_k}$ whose shifted rational prefixes are controlled exactly.

**The valuation family.** With $c_k=p^{-sv_p(k)}$ and $t\mid n_k$, the reduced denominator after the $K$th insertion is exactly $p^{s\lfloor\log_pK\rfloor}$. Hence the number is normal for $s\ge1$, and has discrepancy tending to one for $s=0$, on every schedule with $n_k\ge k^2$; this is kernel-verified. An explicit all-prefix criterion (sparse insertions, polynomially bounded fixed-support denominators, small denominators of density zero) reduces normality to denominator information. For $n_k=tk^d$ with $d\ge s+2$, complete periods give $D_N\ll N^{-s/d}+N^{-(d-s-1)/d}+N^{-(d-1)/d}\log N$; for the binary cubic example $\xi=\sum_k3^{-v_3(k)}2^{-2k^3}$, $D_N\le200N^{-1/3}$.

**Prescribing the orbit.** Choosing $c_k\in(0,1]$ to force a prescribed rational state at each insertion, and retiring a weighted proportion of the gaps, realizes every limiting discrepancy $\delta\in[0,1]$ at rate $O(N^{-1/3})$ uniformly in $\delta$, every pair of lower and upper limits, and every nonempty compact connected set of invariant measures as a limit set.

**Quadratic images.** For odd $p$, $d\ge3$ and distinct orders $a$, every nonconstant rational polynomial of total degree at most two in the numbers $X_a=\sum_ip^{-av_p(i)}b^{-ti^d}$ is normal; hence $\xi^2$ is normal, $(\xi,\xi^2)$ is jointly normal, and an algebraic $X_s$ has degree at least three. After normalization, the shifted quadratic prefixes converge $p$-adically to a step function $M(u)$ of the position of the shift in its $p$-adic shell. Primitive jumps of $M$ on the grids $p^{-H}\mathbb Z$ are isolated from competing jumps in a positive proportion (van der Corput's third-derivative estimate and Erdős–Turán); a second-moment argument puts almost every point in infinitely many isolated neighbourhoods, so every level set of $M$ is null, and the all-prefix criterion applies. This is a written proof without a rate; it supersedes an earlier certified bound $\limsup D_N(\xi^2)<2.94\cdot10^{-10}$.

**Infinite support.** A distinguished prime $3$ with doubled order keeps a growing arithmetic grid in every reduced prefix, so $\sum_{\ell\text{ odd}}\sum_j\ell^{-s_\ell j}2^{-16^{\ell^j}}$ ($s_3=2$, $s_\ell=1$ otherwise) is normal and Liouville with $D_N=O(1/\log N)$, while positive rational multiples of its jointly normal components can sum to any positive rational. The translation and addition properties of $D_N$ used for comparison are proved in the companion manuscript *The Arithmetic of Extreme Discrepancy*.
