# Mathematical summary

For an integer base $b\ge2$, a prime $p\nmid b$ and $t=\operatorname{ord}_p(b)$, the paper studies sums $\sum_kc_kb^{-n_k}$ whose shifted rational prefixes are controlled exactly. With $c_k=p^{-sv_p(k)}$ and $t\mid n_k$, the reduced denominator after the $K$th insertion is exactly $p^{s\lfloor\log_pK\rfloor}$, so the number is normal for $s\ge1$ and has discrepancy tending to one for $s=0$, on every schedule with $n_k\ge k^2$ (kernel-verified); for $n_k=tk^d$ with integers $d\ge s+2$ complete periods give $D_N\ll N^{-s/d}+N^{-(d-s-1)/d}+N^{-(d-1)/d}\log N$, and $\xi=\sum_k3^{-v_3(k)}2^{-2k^3}$ satisfies $D_N(\xi;2)\le200N^{-1/3}$. Since every radix orbit is, after a finite shift, the orbit of such a positive expansion, prescribed discrepancy limits, pairs of lower and upper limits and invariant-measure limit sets exist by classical realization (Colebrook, Sigmund); the paper's contribution there is quantitative: two explicit targets with coefficient denominators at most $pk$ on the schedule $tk^3$ give every limit $\delta\in[0,1]$ with error $O(N^{-1/3})$ uniformly in $\delta$, and the limit sets are realized with state period at most $k$ on block $k$. For odd $p$ and integers $d\ge3$, every nonconstant rational polynomial of total degree at most two in the numbers $X_a=\sum_ip^{-av_p(i)}b^{-ti^d}$ of distinct orders $a$ is normal, so $\xi^2$ is normal and $(\xi,\xi^2)$ jointly normal; the proof shows that the limiting $p$-adic mass of the normalized quadratic prefixes, a function whose reduction modulo each power of $p$ is a finite step function, has Lebesgue-null level sets, using van der Corput's third-derivative estimate, the Erdős–Turán inequality and a second-moment argument (a written proof without a rate, reviewed only by AI models). Finally a distinguished prime $3$ with doubled order keeps a growing arithmetic grid in every reduced prefix, so $\sum_{\ell\text{ odd}}\sum_j\ell^{-s_\ell j}2^{-16^{\ell^j}}$ ($s_3=2$, $s_\ell=1$ otherwise) is normal and Liouville with $D_N=O(1/\log N)$, while positive rational multiples of its jointly normal components can sum to any positive rational.
