# Changes from Paper I, version 1.1

Version 2.0, *Polynomial Images of Stoneham Numbers*, supersedes [the released version 1.1](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials/paper.pdf). The older theorem numbers below refer to that edition. Its [correspondence with Lean](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials/CORRESPONDENCE.md), [review guide](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials/REVIEW.md) and [cross-model reports](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/tree/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/reviews/01-stoneham-polynomials) remain available with the release.

Sections 3–6 introduce the odd-radix parameter-two square profile, the quadratic equivalence, higher-degree certificates and sixteen cyclic-descent families, the echelon criterion, the all-degree orbit minimum and explicit scalar discrepancy exponents. The normality result in the extended finite range combines exact partitions with a written transfer. The all-degree minimization proves nonvanishing on a specified interval. Neither result settles every dyadic degree or the fixed-radix quadratic α² − 2α.

Section 6 gives an explicit exponent for the effective power saving that S08 Theorem 1.1(b) already states with an unspecified exponent. Lemma 6.2 states Vandehey’s all-length estimate (preprint Theorem 5.1) and proves it with the auxiliary divisor formed from the radical of the current modulus, because the preprint’s equation (12) uses the whole prime set; it is labelled *Paper proof*, not *External input*. Lemmas 6.3 and 6.4 isolate the piece-length moment and the exponent margin. Section 5.1 adds Proposition 5.2: the open plateaus of the dyadic shell have full measure, the leading mass is constant on each, and nonvanishing on every plateau suffices for normality in a given degree. Remark 5.3 records that this test fails in degree 2 and that the curvature argument proving normality of the square of a synthetic cubic schedule does not apply to α² − 2α; that quadratic and whole-shell nonvanishing remain open.

The historical comparison credits Mira Cult’s S08 (7 October 2026) in the abstract’s first sentences, in the headings of Theorems A (S08 Theorem 1.1(a), case q = c) and B (S08 Corollary 1.2), at the start of §1 and at the start of §6. Theorems A and B are not presented as new; the contributions claimed for them are the complete Lean proof and the inclusion–exclusion form of the mass argument. The September archive concerns earlier single-series and prime/order results. Details and primary sources are in the [claim ledger](LEDGER.md).

## Revision after cross-model review (2.0, 25 November 2026)

The two AI model reports in `papers/reviews/01-stoneham-polynomials-v2/` (not independent human peer review) led to the following changes; the full disposition is in `resolution.md` there.

- §1.1 is a notation and terminology section defining shell, shell parameter, insertion point, shifted prefix, real tail, deficit, weak and strict cuts, anchor, mass, loss and phase, with forward references for resonant, Hilbert series, live/absorbed transitions, orbit order and plateaus. Clashing symbols were renamed (ρ = v_p(c), period π₀, vertex count g, multiplicities m_i, intervals I_i in Theorem B, numerator y and quotient q̄ in Lemma 6.2).
- The proof of Theorem A is split into Lemmas 2.2–2.5 (reflection, uniform loss, prefix denominators, block cancellation); §4.1 states the certificate algorithm with its correctness lemmas 4.5–4.6.
- Every statement in §§3–5 repeats its hypotheses; the coefficient test is Proposition 3.3; the extended-degree labels name the finite certificate; Corollary 4.4 excludes constants explicitly.
- The statement check `check.lean` now also audits the three inline kernel claims (radix 48, one-axis sum, square of α(2,3)); the paper lists the declaration names.
- Editorial: `\binom`, citation separators, Kano–Shiokawa preprint numbering, the precision threshold in Lemma 2.2, the quartic grid cells, the normalized Erdős–Turán inequality, the bibliography author of the archived edition.
