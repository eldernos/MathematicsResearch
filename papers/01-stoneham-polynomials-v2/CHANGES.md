# Changes from Paper I, version 1.1

Version 2.0, *Polynomial Images of Stoneham Numbers*, supersedes [the released version 1.1](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials/paper.pdf). The older theorem numbers below refer to that edition. Its [correspondence with Lean](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials/CORRESPONDENCE.md), [review guide](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials/REVIEW.md) and [cross-model reports](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/tree/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/papers/reviews/01-stoneham-polynomials) remain available with the release.

| Version 1.1 | Version 2.0 | Mathematical change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §1, Theorem 1 and §4, Corollary 6 | §§1–2, Theorems A and B | The single generator may have any coprime parameter c > 2, including composites with genuinely periodic local units. The rectangle theorem uses arbitrary distinct positive powers. |
| §§2–4, Lemmas 3–5 | §2, Proposition 2.1 and proof of A | Algebraic nonintegrality gives nonvanishing for periodic weights on every nonempty upper set. Compactness bounds the pole loss. The c = 35 example exhibits cancellation at a single prime. |
| §1, Theorem 2 and §5, Theorem 7 | Closing paragraph of §2 | Retains the common-prime product theorem and the four late denominator ranges of the binary square with parameter three. The original proofs, figures, small-denominator table and exceptional initial shells remain in v1.1. |
| §6, Theorem 8 and the mixed-product portion of §7, Corollary 9 and Proposition 11 | Forward reference in §3 to *Exceptional Orbits and Discrepancy of Stoneham Products* (manuscript D, directory `papers/08-stoneham-product-orbits/`) | Independent-parameter products, including the binary parameters 3 and 5, belong to D. Its scope also includes the later exceptional rational orbits and plateau-cycle discrepancy laws. Paper I uses the denominator criterion and one finite example to locate the open quadratic. |
| §7, Corollaries 9–10 | Transcendence observation in §1 and the linked archive | The ordinary generator's transcendence is credited to Coons. The separate Ridout argument for one-axis products remains in v1.1, Corollary 10. |
| §8, formal statements | Final two sections and [verification map](VERIFICATION.md) | Gives a statement-level correspondence, explicit external inputs, executable certificate parameters, the checkpoint axiom record, the named byline and the full AI-use disclosure. |

Sections 3–6 introduce the odd-radix parameter-two square profile, the quadratic equivalence, higher-degree certificates and sixteen cyclic-descent families, the echelon criterion, the all-degree orbit minimum and effective scalar discrepancy rates. The normality result in the extended finite range combines exact partitions with a written transfer. The all-degree minimization proves nonvanishing on a specified interval. Neither result settles every dyadic degree or the fixed-radix quadratic α² − 2α.

The historical comparison now credits Mira Cult’s S08, Theorem 1.1 (7 October 2026), for the broader polynomial theorem, its ordinary specialization and the related monic nonintegrality argument. The complete Lean proof here applies to the ordinary coprime series. The September archive concerns earlier single-series and prime/order results. Details and primary sources are in the [claim ledger](LEDGER.md).
