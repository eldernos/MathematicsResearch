# Specialist review packet

**Theorem A · Mira Cult, S08, Theorem 1.1(a), case q = c (not new; the contribution is the Lean proof) · Kernel-verified.** If b ≥ 2, c > 2 and gcd(b,c) = 1, let α be the sum of c^(−j)b^(−c^j) over j ≥ 1. For every nonconstant P in ℚ[X], every length-m word in the fractional base-b digits of P(α) has limiting frequency b^(−m). [Formal statement and proof](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/NormalityChecker/PeriodicPolynomialNormal.lean#L145)

**Proof sketch.** For a degree-d ordered tuple insert at the sum of its d exponents c^j. With k = floor(log_c n), reflect j to k − j and clear c^(dk). At p dividing c the local units agree to any fixed precision with a reversed periodic cycle of roots of unity on each late phase. The full one-dimensional mass S has nonzero conjugates of modulus at most 1/(c − 1), so its norm excludes integrality. Every nonempty upper set is a finite union of orthants by Dickson’s lemma. Inclusion–exclusion expresses its mass as a monic polynomial in S with integral coefficients in the cyclotomic field. Nonvanishing and compactness of upper sets containing a fixed anchor bound the lost p-powers. Lower-degree terms cannot cancel the remaining top pole. After a fixed radix shift a common coefficient denominator is coprime to b. Reduced denominators grow uniformly, are polynomially bounded and have fixed prime support. O((log N)^d) insertion positions meet o(N) positions in blocks of length floor(√N). The remaining blocks have rational radix orbits. The frozen fixed-support estimate gives cancellation at each nonzero frequency, summability of the tuple weights gives a vanishing shifted real tail, and Weyl’s criterion gives the asserted word frequencies. No unproved analytic hypothesis enters the complete declaration. **Closest prior theorem.** The October public archive (S08, Theorem 1.1) proves the polynomial theorem for x = Σ q^(−j) b^(−c^j), j ≥ 1, whenever q > 2 and some prime divides both c and q but not b. Setting q = c yields the ordinary specialization in Theorem A. Corollaries 1.2–1.3 cover the ordinary powers and the independence criterion for polynomial vectors. The contribution here is a complete kernel-verified proof for the ordinary case and separate parameter-two results. The scalar exponent in Theorem 6.1 makes the effective power saving of S08 Theorem 1.1(b) explicit.
