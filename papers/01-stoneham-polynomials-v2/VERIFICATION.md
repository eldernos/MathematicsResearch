## What is completely kernel-verified

Theorems A, B, 3.1, 3.2 and 4.1 and Proposition 2.1 have complete declarations. The [radix-48 counterexample](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/NormalityChecker/AlternateBaseStoneham.lean#L385) qualifies the radix scope in §1. In §2 the inherited [unique highest-degree monomial](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomial.lean#L107) and [binary square profile](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/NormalityChecker/StonehamSquare.lean#L251) are also kernel-verified. In §3 the [integer recurrence denominator](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/NormalityChecker/Phase16StonehamBounds.lean#L126) specializes to the numerator X² − 2X, with the Lean index one less than the paper index. Its growth at any fixed odd radix remains open.

The [checkpoint record](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/notes/phase18/verification.md) records the integrated build, independent kernel replay and axiom audit. The accompanying [statement check](check.lean) identifies the positive-index generator by definitional equality, checks the public statement, the parameter-two profile and square normality by `example`, and audits fifteen declarations: the twelve used for the labelled results and, since this revision, the three inline claims `AlternateBase.square_nonnormal_48`, `StonehamPolynomial.same_prime_sum_normal` and `StonehamSquare.exact_profile`. On 25 November 2026 it was run with `lake env lean` against a prepared build whose `NormalityChecker` sources, manifest and toolchain match checkpoint 18 except for four added Phase 19 modules that none of these declarations imports. Every audited declaration depends on at most `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` (`descent31Check_true` on `propext` alone, `descent31_all_words_nonzero` on `propext` and `Quot.sound`). No full rebuild was performed for this edition. Kernel replay and cross-model review have different scopes. Independent human specialist review is outstanding.

## External analytic dependencies

The qualitative proof uses the kernel-checked [fixed-support orbit estimate](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/NormalityChecker/SmoothOrbit.lean#L207). Theorem 6.1 uses Lemma 6.2 for the two *unnormalized* short-orbit terms at depth k = d − 1, uniformly in the reduced numerator and each positive length. The logarithmic exponent is 2⁻ᵏ. Preprint (12) uses the entire ambient prime product. Lemma 6.2 starts with preprint Lemma 2.3, proves the differencing step with the actual prime support, and gives uniform constants for the inner sums in (11) and for Fourier reduction. Erdős–Turán converts the harmonic frequency sum into interval discrepancy. The Lean transfer takes both inequalities as hypotheses. Section 6 constructs the partition up to the last incomplete shell and proves effectivity. [Vandehey, preprint Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07911v1#page=13) · [Bailey–Crandall, Theorem 2.2(9)](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bcnormal-em.pdf#page=4) · [Checkpoint discrepancy argument](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/notes/phase14/quantitative-composites.md)

## Reproduce

For the manuscript, run the first command from the publication worktree. For the mathematics, set `B` to this edition's absolute directory before changing to a checkout of `e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec`. Use Lean 4.36.0 with that checkout's manifest. A prepared checkpoint build can run the statement check directly.

```powershell
wsl -d Ubuntu -- bash papers/wsl-build.sh 01-stoneham-polynomials-v2
```

```sh
# In the publication checkout, inside WSL
B="$PWD/papers/01-stoneham-polynomials-v2"
cd "$CHECKPOINT"   # previously set to the frozen checkout
lake build NormalityChecker.PeriodicPolynomialNormal NormalityChecker.ParameterTwoSquare \
  NormalityChecker.ParameterTwoQuadraticOrbits NormalityChecker.StonehamOddDegree \
  NormalityChecker.DyadicQuartic NormalityChecker.Phase16StonehamBounds \
  NormalityChecker.Phase17DiscrepancyBounds NormalityChecker.AlternateBaseStoneham \
  NormalityChecker.StonehamPolynomial NormalityChecker.StonehamSquare
lake env lean "$B/check.lean"
for module in QuantitativeCompositeDenominators QuantitativeStonehamTail QuantitativeStonehamGaps \
              QuantitativeStonehamSupport QuantitativeStonehamExponents QuantitativeStonehamDiscrepancy
do
  lake build "NormalityChecker.$module" && lake env leanchecker "NormalityChecker.$module" || exit 1
done
python3 -B scripts/phase16_dyadic_stoneham.py --quick --max-degree 120
python3 -B scripts/phase17_dyadic_stoneham.py --degree-128
```

The [first verifier](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/scripts/phase16_dyadic_stoneham.py) checks every degree from 3 to 120 inclusive. It reported 41992 branch nodes across the range. In degree 120 its 336 leaves gave smallest loss 716 and largest loss 835, first at 2157/2048. Its seven residue pairs are (3,p) for p = 31, 127, 257, 337, 1801, 8191 and (4,8191). The quick option shortens auxiliary comparisons; the explicit degree option controls the exhaustive partition.

The [second verifier](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/blob/e4809cf561573aef588e6ffc491a35cdb79fd9ec/scripts/phase17_dyadic_stoneham.py) returned 689 nodes, 345 leaves and sharp precision 907 for degree 128. Its extremal losses were 776 and 906, with the first maximum at 138241/131072. The nine additional pairs consist of e = 3,4 at each of 65537, 131071, 524287 and 2147483647, and e = 5 at 524287. The last orbit contained 338544 vectors. The reduced (3,31) orbit contained 57 vectors of dimension five. The all-degree least-order proof has auxiliary dynamic-programming checks through degree 300. The computed table, the identification of its automata with masses of real cuts, and the normality inference are distinct steps.
