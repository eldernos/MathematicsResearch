# Claim-to-source ledger

This ledger uses the numbering of [version 2.0](paper.pdf). A kernel label concerns the exact mathematical assertion and its definitions. It carries no claim about priority. Throughout, α denotes the positive-index Stoneham series in the manuscript. For c = 2 the radix b is always an odd integer at least 3. The [verification map](VERIFICATION.md) gives the declarations and commands.

## Primary sources and search record

- R. G. Stoneham, *On absolute (j, ε)-normality in the rational fractions with applications to normal numbers*, Acta Arith. 22 (1973), 277–286, especially pp. 282–284. The construction includes the familiar binary generator with parameter 3. A. N. Korobov, *Continued fractions of certain normal numbers*, Math. Notes 47 (1990), 128–132, is the source Kano credits for an increasing-exponent criterion. Kano, *General constructions of normal numbers of Korobov type*, Osaka J. Math. 30 (1993), 909–919, pp. 909–911 and (13) on pp. 914–915, treats the single series and its discrepancy. [Stoneham][stoneham] · [A. N. Korobov][kor] · [Kano][kano]
- D. H. Bailey and R. E. Crandall, *Random generators and normal numbers*, Exp. Math. 11 (2002), 527–546. Theorem 4.8 and Corollary 4.9(i) give the ordinary coprime generator; Theorem 2.2(5),(7)–(9) collects the digit criterion, rational affine invariance, Weyl and Erdős–Turán. D. H. Bailey and J. M. Borwein, *Nonnormality of Stoneham constants*, Ramanujan J. 29 (2012), 409–422, Theorem 2 and §4, study changes of radix and pose a sum question. [Bailey–Crandall][bc] · [Bailey–Borwein][bb]
- M. Coons, *An arithmetical excursion via Stoneham numbers*, arXiv:1212.3449v2 (28 November 2013), Appendix A, Corollary A.3, p. 11; J. Aust. Math. Soc. 96 (2014), 303–315. The transcendence assertion holds for all b,c ≥ 2. The 2013 date identifies the revised preprint. [Preprint][coons] · [Journal edition](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788713000682)
- H. Kano and I. Shiokawa, *Rings of normal and nonnormal numbers*, Israel J. Math. 84 (1993), 403–416, Theorems 4–5 and conditions (15)–(16) in the institutional version. G. Wagner, *On rings of numbers which are normal to one base but non-normal to another*, J. Number Theory 54 (1995), 211–231, publisher abstract. These constructions establish normal rings for chosen generators. The ratios for the ordinary schedule are $j/c^{j-1}$ and log c, so that schedule fails the two divergence conditions of the cited Kano–Shiokawa construction. This test does not exclude other representations. [Kano–Shiokawa][ks] · [Wagner][wag]
- Y. Nakai and I. Shiokawa, *Normality of numbers generated by the values of polynomials at primes*, Acta Arith. 81 (1997), 345–356, p. 345. Their digits come from concatenating polynomial values at primes. N. M. Korobov, *On completely uniform distribution and conjunctly normal numbers*, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser. Mat. 20 (1956), 649–660, studies simultaneous normality. These are separate precedents for two uses of “polynomial” and “joint”. [Nakai–Shiokawa][ns] · [N. M. Korobov][joint]
- J. Vandehey, *Differencing methods for Korobov-type exponential sums*, J. Anal. Math. 138 (2019), 405–439. Theorem numbers here refer to arXiv:1606.07911v1 (2016). Theorem 5.1, pp. 13–14, is the all-length estimate. Corollary 1.2 is a power saving on sufficiently long short orbits. The sparse-series application, Theorem 7.4, imposes conditions on successive denominators and gaps. Y. Bugeaud, *Distribution Modulo One and Diophantine Approximation*, Cambridge University Press (2012), §5.1, discusses the scalar Stoneham discrepancy. [Vandehey][van] · [Bugeaud][bug]
- Mira Cult (Mira-acc), *Normality research*, pinned revision d699a69, 23 September 2026, S07/S09, and *Normality research #2*, pinned revision 41b5a78, 7 October 2026, S08. The Gist revision timestamps are 17:18:05 UTC and 16:31:31 UTC respectively. The October archive identifies S08 as a conventional proof awaiting external review. Its Theorem 1.1 explicitly covers q = c > 2 and states an effective power saving in synchronized star discrepancy. Its Corollaries 1.2 and 1.3 state ordinary joint powers and independence of polynomial classes modulo constants. The proof uses Vandehey’s Corollary 1.2, a nonintegral cyclotomic tail and monicity. It explicitly leaves q = 2 outside its polynomial theorem. [September archive][sep] · [October archive][oct]
- Captain Sude’s September repositories give a two-positive-summand theorem and a single-series prime-support criterion. These concern sums and linear series. Mira Cult’s November write-up also treats families indexed by prime and order. The powers in Theorem B are powers of one real value. [Two-sum paper][sude] · [Prime support][support] · [November write-up][nov]

### Theorem A: Polynomial images

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For coprime b ≥ 2, c > 2, every nonconstant P ∈ ℚ[X] gives a base-b normal P(α).
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. The complete positive-index statement is `coprime_polynomial_normal`, with the interface equality in check.lean.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Stoneham][stoneham], pp. 282–284; [Korobov’s criterion as reported by Kano][kano], pp. 909–910.
- **Closest published results:** [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Cor. 4.9(i), gives α; rational affine invariance gives degree one. [Kano–Shiokawa][ks], Thms. 4–5, and [Wagner][wag] concern other generators.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Mira, S08][oct], Thm. 1.1, states the result for $x=\sum_{j\ge1}q^{-j}b^{-c^j}$, q > 2, with a prime dividing c and q and not dividing b. Its text explicitly identifies the ordinary case. [Captain Sude][sude] treats sums of different generators.
- **Relation:** Specializes S08 by setting q = c. The contribution claimed here is the complete Lean proof for this specialization.
- **Search record:** The polynomial-value, normal-ring and concatenation queries above distinguish applying P to a real value from applying P to its insertion indices.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check the normalization in passing from the dilated zero-index axis to the positive-index series and the reach of older normal-ring criteria.

### Theorem B: Synchronized powers

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For distinct positive rᵢ, the synchronized shifts of $(\alpha^{r_1},\ldots,\alpha^{r_m})$ visit each half-open box with frequency equal to its volume under the hypotheses of A.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. `coprime_joint_powers` includes the limiting frequency, the endpoints and the common starting position.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [N. M. Korobov][joint], 1956, pp. 649–660.
- **Closest published results:** [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Thm. 2.2(8), gives the scalar character criterion. Transcendence alone, including [Coons][coons], Cor. A.3, gives no digit frequencies.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Mira, S08][oct], Thm. 1.1 and Cor. 1.2, include contiguous powers; project the vector through the largest exponent onto the selected coordinates. [S09][sep] treats prime/order pairs.
- **Relation:** Specializes the stated joint theorem. The rectangle limit is fully formalized here.
- **Search record:** Synchronized powers and joint normality queries above, followed by both of Mira’s archives and the Math-Net issue record.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check the common digit origin and the distinction between powers of the value and powers of the parameter.

### Proposition 2.1: Periodic mass

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For p dividing c > 2, nonempty upper U in ℕᵈ and periodic roots of unity in a number field embedded in ℚₚ, $\sum_{v\in U}\prod_i c^{v_i}u_{v_i}$ is nonzero.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. `periodic_upper_mass_nonzero` uses `periodicGeometricValue_not_isIntegral`.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** The rational prefix method is present in [Stoneham][stoneham], 1973, and [Kano][kano], 1993, pp. 909–911.
- **Closest published results:** [Vandehey][van], Cor. 1.2, controls an exponential sum once the denominator survives. The nonvanishing problem occurs before that estimate.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Mira, S08][oct], section on the nonintegral algebraic tail and subsequent high/low expansion, bounds every conjugate and obtains monicity outside guard intervals.
- **Relation:** Related method. Here Dickson’s lemma and inclusion–exclusion yield a monic polynomial for every nonempty upper set, including cuts at insertions.
- **Search record:** Compared the two constructions under the formulations periodic root-of-unity tail, monic Cauchy truncation and reflected upper-set mass.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check the embedding in the local field and transitivity of integrality over the ring of integers.

### Theorem 3.1: The parameter-two square

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For odd b ≥ 3, α² is normal. On 2ᵏ ≤ n < 2ᵏ⁺¹, k ≥ 2, the reduced denominator is 2^(2k−2) before 3·2^(k−1) and 2^(k−1) thereafter. Before n = 4 it is 1.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. `ParameterTwoSquare.exact_profile` and `square_normal`.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Kano][kano], pp. 909–911, and [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Cor. 4.9(i), cover the generator.
- **Closest published results:** [Bailey–Borwein][bb], Thm. 2, concerns the radix of α. [Coons][coons], Cor. A.3, supplies transcendence of α.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Mira, S08][oct] explicitly excludes q = 2. The [checkpoint square proof][square] treats the upper half after loss of the leading mass.
- **Relation:** Different parameter regime. The exact two-piece profile supplies the missing denominator growth.
- **Search record:** Compared the square, base change and dyadic generator in the sources above and the archived proof for the binary square with c = 3.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check the upper-half endpoint and the two valuations of Aₖ ± 1; b is odd throughout.

### Theorem 3.2: The quadratic obstruction

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For x = (Aα²+Bα+C)/D with A ≠ 0 and D a positive integer, x is normal exactly when the reduced denominators of the numerator prefixes at 3·2^(k−1) tend to infinity.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. `normal_iff_upper_denominator`. The recurrence for X²−2X also has a declaration, and the sufficient coefficient test is derived in §3.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Thm. 2.2(7),(10), p. 530, for rational affine operations and uniform distribution.
- **Closest published results:** [Vandehey][van], Cor. 1.2, supplies a quantitative precedent for the sufficiency direction once denominators grow. It gives no growth test for the quadratic.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Quadratic denominator calculation][quad] and [orbit equivalence][orbit]. [Mira, S08][oct] does not cover q = 2.
- **Relation:** An equivalence and a sufficient valuation test. The recurrence for X²−2X supplies an exact denominator at each k, leaving its asymptotic growth open.
- **Search record:** Checked the dyadic quadratic query against earlier generator and polynomial criteria and the arithmetic proof for both half-shells.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Can upper denominators be proved to grow for X²−2X for even one specified odd radix? Finite searches do not establish this.

### Theorem 4.1: Quartic and odd prime-power degrees

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For odd b ≥ 3, all rational polynomials of degree 4 or pʳ for p an odd prime and r ≥ 0 take base-b normal values at α.
- **Evidence:** Kernel-verified. `quartic_polynomial_normal` and `odd_prime_power_degree_polynomial_normal`.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Cor. 4.9(i), and Thm. 2.2(7), give the linear member.
- **Closest published results:** [Kano–Shiokawa][ks], Thms. 4–5, construct polynomial families under different separation hypotheses.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Earlier higher-degree calculation][higher] supplies the quartic grid. [Mira, S08][oct] covers independent parameters with q > 2.
- **Relation:** Different arithmetic proof for q = 2, using cyclic symmetry or the exact quartic grid.
- **Search record:** Followed the normal-ring hypotheses and the odd-prime-power and quartic cases in the frozen sources.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check the positive-index factor of 16 in translating the quartic grid.

### Proposition 4.2: The finite certificates

- **Statement and hypotheses:** Every cut on [1,2) has nonzero M_d for 3 ≤ d ≤ 120 and d = 128. For the sixteen pairs (e,p) in Table 1 all residues M_e(t) mod p omit zero.
- **Evidence:** Finite certificate. The two scripts, flags and outputs appear in [Reproduce](VERIFICATION.md#reproduce).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Stoneham][stoneham], 1973, concerns periodic digit counts. The binary mass and its finite state calculation are developed in the linked checkpoint notes.
- **Closest published results:** The hypothesis of [Vandehey][van], Cor. 1.2, concerns a rational denominator. It supplies no mass certificate.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [First seven pairs and degree range][first], [nine further pairs and degree 128][second]. [Mira, S08][oct] leaves this parameter outside its theorem.
- **Relation:** Exact finite exhaustion of cuts or reachable vectors. Only the reduced (3,31) automaton has the additional formal word invariant cited here.
- **Search record:** Checked the dyadic mass and quadratic queries, the verifier recurrences and the archived higher-degree proof.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check truncation after the dth one, endpoint conventions and the passage from the reduced automaton to the mass.

### Theorem 4.3: Certified degrees and infinite families

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For odd b ≥ 3 every rational polynomial of a degree in 3–120, of degree 128, or of degree epʳ for a listed pair and r ≥ 0 has normal value at α.
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof. Proposition 4.2 supplies the finite data; §4 proves cyclic descent and uses `binary_polynomial_normal_of_retention`.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Kano][kano], 1993, and [Bailey–Crandall][bc], 2002, treat the underlying single generator.
- **Closest published results:** [Kano–Shiokawa][ks], Thms. 4–5, treat chosen ring generators. Their displayed schedule conditions fail at (j,2ʲ).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [First descent calculation][first] and [extended descent families][second]. [Mira, S08][oct] assumes q > 2.
- **Relation:** Extends the finite classifications by a written cyclic group action. The sixteen pairs include overlapping degrees; the theorem does not classify all degrees.
- **Search record:** Followed the cyclic orbit argument and the first- and higher-order descent comparisons in the two certificate notes.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check evaluation at 2 only after clearing the rational-series denominator, and compactness when thresholds approach 2 from below.

### Corollary 4.4: Joint normality from pivot degrees

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For linearly independent rational polynomials, all echelon pivots must be positive and covered by 4.1 or 4.3, or equal to 2. A quadratic pivot requires no linear pivot and must pass (2), the nonresonance test in §3. Then all synchronized box frequencies are uniform.
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof. `jointUniform_of_echelon_degrees` checks the integer, distinct-degree implication. Rational changes of basis and the restricted quadratic case are proved in §4.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [N. M. Korobov][joint], 1956, pp. 649–660.
- **Closest published results:** Scalar characters and [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Thm. 2.2(8), p. 530, supply the standard Fourier argument.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Mira, S08][oct], Cor. 1.3, gives a criterion modulo constants at q > 2. [The echelon argument][second] applies the method at q = 2 with specific scalar inputs.
- **Relation:** Specializes the character method, using a sufficient list of dyadic pivot degrees. The span must omit nonzero constants.
- **Search record:** Compared the two joint criteria with synchronized-power and simultaneous-normality formulations.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check that each rational combination with top pivot two is a nonzero multiple of that basis element.

### Theorem 5.1: Least orbit order and the interval J_d

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For all d ≥ 1 the minimum of Φ(c) over multiplicity vectors of size d with Kraft sum K(c) < 2 equals Σ_{k=1}^d ⌊log₂ k⌋. For d ≥ 2 the mass attains valuation v₂(d!) + F(d) precisely on J_d. For 2ʳ−1 ≤ d ≤ 2^(r+1)−2 its left endpoint is 2−2^(1−r), included. Its right endpoint is 2 if d = 2ʳ−1 and 2−2^(−r) otherwise, excluded.
- **Evidence:** Paper proof. Section 5 counts slack at every level and classifies equality. Dynamic programming through 300 checks examples.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Legendre’s factorial valuation is the elementary counting input. The digit problem arises from the [Stoneham][stoneham] schedule.
- **Closest published results:** The normal-ring and orbit-discrepancy sources above give no equality classification for this constrained minimum.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Finite conjecture and checks][first]; [all-degree proof][second], Thm. 1 and Cor. 2. [Mira, S08][oct] uses a different obstruction for q > 2.
- **Relation:** Proves the checkpoint’s earlier finite pattern for all d. It establishes nonvanishing on J_d only.
- **Search record:** Followed the factorial, binary Kraft-budget and least-loss formulations in both notes. No exhaustive search of prefix-code optimization literature was conducted.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check completeness of the minimizing multiplicities at d = 2ʳ−1 and possible antecedents in combinatorial optimization.

### Theorem 6.1: Effective discrepancy

- **Statement and hypotheses:** For c > 2 every degree d ≥ 1 has scalar interval discrepancy ≤ C N^(−1/[2(2^(d+1)−2)]) for all N ≥ 2. For c = 2 the assertion covers degree one, odd prime-power degrees and quadratics satisfying the coefficient test. In covered degrees 1,2,3 the refined pairs of saving and log exponent are (1/2,2), (1/6,3/2), (1/14,5/4).
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof. The finite support transfer and denominator, tail, length and exponent lemmas are identified in [the map](VERIFICATION.md).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** [Stoneham][stoneham], pp. 282–284, on digits in periodic fractions; [Kano][kano], (13), pp. 914–915, on lacunary-series discrepancy.
- **Closest published results:** [Bailey–Crandall][bc], p. 535, give the linear rate for the perturbed orbit. [Bugeaud][bug], §5.1, records the real orbit rate. [Vandehey][van], Thm. 5.1, pp. 13–14, supplies the two all-length terms.
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** [Mira, S08][oct], Thm. 1.1, already states an effective vector power saving for q > 2. Its analytic input is Vandehey’s Cor. 1.2. The [checkpoint calculation][rate] specifies scalar exponents and treats stated dyadic cases.
- **Relation:** An explicit scalar application of published analytic estimates. No optimal exponent or improvement on an unspecified exponent is asserted.
- **Search record:** Compared scalar discrepancy, synchronized star discrepancy, arbitrary terminal cuts and the short-orbit estimate against both primary texts.
- **Dependencies and outstanding review:** Check the dependence on prime support after frequency reduction, counts on partial shells and effective constants. Erdős–Turán is used in the interval normalization of [Bailey–Crandall][bc], Thm. 2.2(9), p. 530.
