## Audit IV `G4(a)–(d)` resolution record

- **`G4(a)` — Montgomery (1994) primary-text check:** Web search located the exact reference in H. L. Montgomery, *Ten Lectures on the Interface Between Analytic Number Theory and Harmonic Analysis*, CBMS **84** (1994), Appendix: Some Unsolved Problems, **Problem 45, p. 204** ("Find a normal number whose continued fraction coefficients are bounded"), recording R. C. Baker's unpublished observation combining Jarník (1928), Davenport–Erdős–LeVeque (1963), and Kaufman (1980) to prove existence. Reading label: **[S]** (verified via indexed excerpts and two independent secondary sources, Queffélec 2006 pp. 232–233 and Jordan–Sahlsten 2016 p. 3; the full print monograph was not directly inspected). Paper XVI states prominently that Montgomery's existence question is classical and that nothing in Paper XVI answers or strengthens it.
- **`G4(b)` — Hochman–Shmerkin (2015) theorem numbers and hypotheses:** Verified via `arXiv:1302.5792` / *Invent. Math.* **202** (2015), 427–479 (and Phase 24 §3 verification of the author's preprint): **Theorems 1.4 and 1.5** give pointwise $\beta$-normality (for integer and Pisot bases $\beta > 1$) for quasi-product measures of regular $C^{1+\varepsilon}$ / totally nonlinear $C^\omega$ IFSs and their pushforwards under $C^1$ diffeomorphisms $g \in \mathrm{diff}^1(\mathbb{R})$ (with Theorem 1.7 for non-affine real-analytic images of linear IFSs); **Theorem 1.12** and **§1.3.3** apply this to quasi-product measures on arbitrary finite-alphabet continued-fraction Cantor sets $\mathcal{C}_\Lambda$ ($|\Lambda| \ge 2$). Reading label: **[A/S]** (verified against web/arXiv abstracts and Phase 24 §3 preprint notes; full journal proofs not re-read here). Paper XVI explicitly credits Hochman–Shmerkin for non-constructive existence of polynomial-normal numbers in $F_2$ (by restricting $P$ to compact intervals avoiding its finitely many critical points).
- **`G4(c)` — Kaufman (1980), Queffélec–Ramaré (2003), and modern nonlinear Fourier-decay papers:** Bibliographic records and abstracts checked for Kaufman (*Mathematika* **27**, 1980, 262–267), Queffélec–Ramaré (*Enseign. Math.* **49**, 2003, 335–356), Jordan–Sahlsten (*Math. Ann.* **364**, 2016, 983–1023), Sahlsten–Stevens (*Amer. J. Math.* **146**, 2024, 945–982), Baker–Banaji (*Math. Ann.* **392**, 2025, 209–261, `arXiv:2401.01241`), and Algom–Rodriguez Hertz–Wang (*Cambridge J. Math.*, 2026, `arXiv:2306.01275`) **[A/S]**. Full primary texts of Kaufman (1980) and Queffélec–Ramaré (2003) were **not** obtained in full; Paper XVI states candidly (Table 4 footnote and §6) that $C^2$ stationary-phase lemmas for Möbius branches already appear in their arguments, that non-effective polynomial Fourier decay for nonlinear pushforwards is established by Baker–Banaji (2025) and Algom–Rodriguez Hertz–Wang (2026), and that Paper XVI claims no novelty for the nonlinear Fourier-decay mechanism itself — only for the **fully explicit rational** exponents, thresholds, and majorants $(\eta, \bar{\xi}_0(P), \bar{C}(P))$ suitable for computable selection.

### Theorem A (Lemmas 2.1, 3.1, Propositions 3.2, 3.4, Theorems 4.1, 5.1): Computable badly approximable polynomial-absolutely-normal numbers ($M = 100 / 14 / 8$)

- **Evidence.**
  - $M = 100, L = 52, s = 19/20$: **Paper proof** (zero external inputs, zero computer certificates).
  - $M = 14, L = 344, s = 18/19$ (and $M = 42, L = 4$): **Paper proof + Finite certificate** (zero external inputs).
  - $M = 8, L = 226, s = 46/51$ (and $M = 14, L = 4$; $M = 30, L = 2$): **Paper proof + Finite certificate + External input** (Weil 1948, Estermann 1961).
  - No Lean 4 formalization.
- **Closest results.**
  - **Existence** of badly approximable absolutely normal numbers is classical (`Kaufman1980` + Baker in `Montgomery1994` for $M \ge 3$; `QueffelecRamare2003` for $M = 2$; `JordanSahlsten2016`), and non-constructive existence of polynomial-absolutely-normal numbers in $F_2$ follows from `HochmanShmerkin2015` (Theorems 1.4, 1.5, 1.12) and nonlinear pushforward Fourier decay (`BakerBanaji2025`, `ARHW2026`, `SahlstenStevens2024`) **[A/S]**.
  - **Computable** absolutely normal numbers (`Sierpinski1917`, `BecherFigueira2002`, `BecherFigueiraPicchi2007`, `BecherHeiberSlaman2013`, `BecherHeiberSlaman2015`, `AlvarezBecher2017`, `ABSS2017`, `Scheerer2017`, `BecherYuhjtman2019`, `LutzMayordomo2021`) all have unbounded partial quotients **[A/S]**.
  - The fourth-moment prime Kloosterman bound (`Kloosterman1926`), prime-power stationary phase (`Salie1931`, `Estermann1961`), Gallagher's Sobolev inequality (`Gallagher1970`), and the Ruelle transfer operator for Gauss-map Cantor sets (`Hensley1992`, `JenkinsonPollicott2018`) are all classical **[A/S]**; no novelty is claimed for these tools individually.
- **Relation (provisional).** Not located in the web/arXiv sources examined: a Turing-computable badly approximable number whose nonconstant rational polynomial images are all absolutely normal, with fully explicit rational Fourier-decay constants and exact transfer-operator certificates at caps $M = 100 / 14 / 8$ **[M]**. Formal MathSciNet/zbMATH search by a human specialist has not been performed.
