### Theorem A (with Propositions 4.1–4.2 and Corollary 4.3): Discrepancy threshold $4/11+2.85\cdot10^{-10}$, zero-slack $4/11$ equivalence, and certified multi-depth bounds
- **Statement and hypotheses:** For $x=\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$ with $R_n=\sum_{3^i+5^j\le n}2^{n-3^i-5^j}/(3^i5^j)$ and $C_0=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\#\{0\le n<N:R_n\in\Z\}/N$: $D^-(x)=0$, $\{2^n x\}$ is logarithmically equidistributed, $D^+(x)\le 4/11+2.85\cdot10^{-10}$, and $D^+(x)\le 4/11\iff C_0\le 4/11$ (if either fails, $D^+(x)=C_0$). More generally, for $(B,p,q)\in\{(2,3,5),(2,3,7),(2,3,13),(36,5,7)\}$, exact multi-depth non-coincidence certificates on $[1,a_{\max}]\times[1,d_{\max}]$ give $D^+(\alpha(B,p)\alpha(B,q))\le M_{p,q}+(p/(p-1)+q/(q-1))\eta+\delta$ with $M_{p,q}=(p-1)(q-1)/(2pq-p-q)$. Ordinary normality of $\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$ remains open.
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof (Theorem A, Proposition 4.1, Corollary 4.3) + Finite certificate (Proposition 4.2). Lean supports the fixed-support short-orbit step (`SmoothOrbit.fixed_support_orbit_estimate`), circle perturbation (`CircleDiscrepancy.disc_sub_tendsto`), binary endpoint congruences (`ProductThreeFiveInterpolation.{endpoint_num_shape,ternary_endpoint_num,quinary_endpoint_num}`), boundary-strip bound (`Phase16StonehamBounds.boundary_strip_piecewise_le`), binary simultaneous spacing (`Phase16StonehamBounds.simultaneous_ultrametric_spacing`), and single-shape crossing identity (`Phase16StonehamBounds.single_shape_one_one_max`, `Phase17DiscrepancyBounds.{general_single_shape_one_one_max,general_single_shape_one_one_attained}`). ### Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 2.2: Exact empirical-mixture limit law, scalar discrepancy convergence, and uniform-in-$N$ bound
- **Statement and hypotheses:** For $x=\alpha(B,p)\alpha(B,q)$ ($p<q$ odd primes coprime to $B$), along any sequence $N\to\infty$ on which plateau cycle weights satisfy $w_{\mathfrak c,N}\to w_{\mathfrak c}$ for every $\{p,q\}$-smooth $B$-cycle $\mathfrak c$, $\mu_N(x)\to\sigma(w)=(1-\sum_{\mathfrak c} w_{\mathfrak c})\lambda+\sum_{\mathfrak c} w_{\mathfrak c}\nu_{\mathfrak c}$ weakly on $\T$ and $D_N(x)\to\Delta(\sigma(w))$, giving $D^+(x)=\max_{w\in\mathcal W}\Delta(\sigma(w))$ (Theorem 2.1). Uniformly in $N$, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exist $M,N_0$ such that $D_N(x)\le\varepsilon+N^{-1}\#\{0\le n<N:\den(R_n)\le M\}$ for all $N\ge N_0$ (Lemma 2.2).
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof (`SmoothOrbit.fixed_support_orbit_estimate`, `CircleDiscrepancy.disc_sub_tendsto`); written proof in Section 2.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** H. Weyl (1916), *Über die Gleichverteilung von Zahlen mod. Eins*, Math. Ann. 77, 313–352 (DOI `10.1007/BF01475864`); N. M. Korobov (1970), *Trigonometric sums with exponential functions and the distribution of signs in repeating decimals*, Mat. Zametki 8(5), 641–652 / Math. Notes 8, 831–837 (DOI `10.1007/BF01146941`); N. M. Korobov (1972), *On the distribution of digits in periodic fractions*, Mat. Sb. 89(131):4, 654–670 / Math. USSR-Sb. 18(4), 659–676 (DOI `10.1070/SM1972v018n04ABEH001870`); J. Bourgain (2005), *Exponential sum estimates over subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_q^*$, $q$ arbitrary*, J. Anal. Math. 97, 317–355 (DOI `10.1007/BF02807410`); J. Bourgain (2008), *The sum-product theorem in $\mathbb{Z}_q$ with $q$ arbitrary*, J. Anal. Math. 106, 1–93 (DOI `10.1007/s11854-008-0044-2`); J. Vandehey (2019, Cor. 1.2); Y. Bugeaud (2012, Ch. 1 & 4).
- **Closest published results:** General-modulus short-orbit exponential-sum estimates are classical: Korobov (1970 Lemma 1; 1972 Lemma 2, recorded via Vandehey 2019 / `notes/phase19/literature.md`) gives square-root bounds requiring $N>\sqrt m\log m$, and Bourgain (2005; 2008 Thm. 8.28, via Vandehey Thm. 2.5) gives power-saving bounds for arbitrary $m$ under divisor-order lower bounds $\operatorname{ord}(b,m')>(m')^\gamma$ for large $m'\mid m$. Neither hypothesis holds for arbitrary $\{p,q\}$-smooth prefix denominators with $m\le T^r$, making Vandehey's fixed-prime-support setting (2019, Cor. 1.2; qualitatively internalized in `SmoothOrbit.lean`) the relevant input. Portmanteau/weak convergence alone does not yield convergence on arcs with an atom at an endpoint, so our proof establishes uniform arc convergence for $\widetilde\mu_N$ and transfers scalar discrepancy via circle-arc enlargement (`CircleDiscrepancy.disc_sub_tendsto`).
- **Closest unpublished or public-repository results:** Analogous single-cycle plateau laws appear in this repository's `QuadraticPlateauSpectrum.lean` (Theorem 7.1 below) and in `papers/03-signed-resonance` (for signed expansions with one cycle type). No external repository treatment of countable-cycle mixtures for Stoneham products was located.
- **Relation:** Specializes general weak-limit/discrepancy transfer techniques and fixed-support short-orbit estimates to two-dimensional Stoneham plateaus; we have not located this exact countable-cycle mixture law for $\alpha(B,p)\alpha(B,q)$ in the sources examined.

### Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.2, Proposition 3.3, and Theorem 3.4: Local $p$-adic Mahler–Newton isometry, Eisenstein non-integrality, logarithmic equidistribution, and $O_\varepsilon(\log^* T)$ scale sparsity
- **Statement and hypotheses:** For distinct odd primes $p,q\nmid B$, the $p$-layers lost in $\den(R_n)$ equal $\min(K,v_p(G_{a,d}(L)))$. Under the finite simple-zero conditions (satisfied by infinitely many radices $B$ for every pair $(p,q)$), $G_{a,d}(a+uz)/p$ is a bijective isometry of $\Z_p$ with unique root $\zeta_{p,a,d}$, yielding $v_p(G_{a,d}(a+uz))=1+v_p(z-\zeta_{p,a,d})$ and $\zeta_{p,0,0}=0$ (Proposition 3.1). For $(2,3,5)$ and $(2,5,3)$, backward analytic continuation gives $x_0=1/2$ and $x_{-h}=q^h\theta_h$ with compatible $q^h$th roots of $1/2$ (Lemma 3.2), and Eisenstein irreducibility proves $\zeta_{p,a,d}\notin\Z$ for all $a,d\ge1$ (Proposition 3.3). Under the simple-zero conditions on both axes: joint $(h,k)$-loss logarithmic density is $1/(uvp^{h-1}q^{k-1})$ (also conditional on each shape), exceptional logarithmic scales up to $T$ number $O_{B,p,q,\varepsilon}(\log^* T)$, $\sum_{D_N>\varepsilon}1/(N\log N)<\infty$, $\{B^n x\}$ is logarithmically equidistributed, and $D^-(x)=0$ (Theorem 3.4).
- **Evidence:** Lean-supported paper proof (Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.4) + Paper proof (Lemma 3.2, Proposition 3.3). Lean verifies binary endpoint congruences (`ProductThreeFiveInterpolation`), boundary-strip bound, and binary simultaneous ultrametric spacing (`Phase16StonehamBounds`).
- **Earliest relevant literature:** K. Mahler (1958), *An interpolation series for continuous functions of a $p$-adic variable*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 199, 23–34 (DOI `10.1515/crll.1958.199.23`); A. Nishioka (1996), *Mahler Functions and Transcendence*, LNM 1631, Springer (DOI `10.1007/BFb0093672`); M. Tsuji (1952), *On the uniform distribution of numbers mod. 1*, J. Math. Soc. - **Closest published results:** Mahler (1958) established $p$-adic binomial interpolation for continuous functions on $\Z_p$ (see Robert 2000 for analyticity criteria and Newton-polygon root counting; our proof proves the isometry congruence $G(a+u(z+y))-G(a+uz)\equiv y S_u\bmod p^{t+2}$ directly from $v_p(\nabla^k g(0))\ge k+1$). ### Theorems 6.1 and 6.3: Isolated-insertion $43/45$ bound, irrationality, transcendence, and $D^+<1$ for independent integer parameters

### Theorem 7.1, Propositions 7.2–7.3, and Corollary 7.4: Dyadic ($c=2$) quadratic plateau spectrum, resonant radices, exact cycle discrepancies, and unconditional sub-quarter bounds
- **Statement and hypotheses:** For odd $b\ge3$, $\alpha=\alpha(b,2)$, and $y=(A\alpha^2+E\alpha+C)/D$ ($A\ne0,D\ge1$): $D_N(y)-\max_k w_{k,N}\Delta_b(y_k)\to0$, $D^+(y)=\frac14\limsup_k\Delta_b(y_k)$, $D^-(y)=0$, $\{b^n y\}$ is logarithmically equidistributed, $y$ is normal iff $q_k\to\infty$, $q_k=2^{[k-v_2(2A)-s-v_2(k-\kappa)]_+}$ (with $\lambda_2=\lim_j \delta_j$), and algebraicity of $\kappa$ implies normality (Theorem 7.1). At fixed shell $k$ for $X^2-2X$, resonant radices form zero or two opposite classes modulo $2^t$ (Proposition 7.2). For $D=1$, exact dyadic cycle discrepancies yield $D^+(y)=2^{-2-[H-s]_+}$ ($b\equiv1\bmod4$), singleton characterization of $D^+(y)=1/4$, sub-quarter bound for $b\equiv3\bmod4$ ($H\ge2$), and explicit $Z_k$ plateau points (Proposition 7.3). For explicit families ($D=3,b\equiv2\bmod3$ odd, $C\not\equiv1\bmod3$; and $b=5,D=9,C=0$), unconditional sub-quarter bounds hold: $D^+((\alpha^2-2\alpha+C)/3)\le\frac14(1-1/(3\cdot2^{v_2(b+1)}))$ and $D^+((\alpha^2-2\alpha)/9)\le5/72$ (Corollary 7.4). Normality of $\alpha^2-2\alpha$ for any fixed odd integer $b\ge3$ remains open.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 7.1 and Proposition 7.2 are **Kernel-verified** (`QuadraticPlateauSpectrum`, `ParameterTwoQuadratic*`, `ResonantRadix*`). Proposition 7.3 and Corollary 7.4 are **Lean-supported paper proofs** (`QuadraticPlateauSpectrum.quadratic_exact_limsup`, `Phase16StonehamBounds.{quadZ_eq_cancellation,quadratic_upper_den_eq_quadZ}`) together with a finite exact cycle enumeration for $b=5,D=9$ ($0\le h\le4$) and supplementary finite checks in `scripts/phase17_dyadic_stoneham.py`.
- **Earliest relevant literature:** Bailey–Crandall (2002) for degree $1$ ($\alpha(b,2)$ is normal for odd $b$); Y. Bugeaud and M. Laurent (1996), *Minoration effective de la distance $p$-adique entre puissances de nombres algébriques*, J. Number Theory 61(2), 311–342 (DOI `10.1006/jnth.1996.0152`); K.
