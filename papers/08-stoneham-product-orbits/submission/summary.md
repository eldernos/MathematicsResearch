This manuscript analyzes the exceptional radix-shift orbits and extreme interval discrepancy of products of Stoneham numbers on independent prime axes and of resonant rational quadratics on the dyadic axis $c = 2$. For $x = \alpha(B,p)\alpha(B,q)$ with distinct odd primes $p < q$ coprime to $B$, every subsequential distribution of $\{B^n x\}$ is a convex mixture of Lebesgue measure and $\{p,q\}$-smooth rational cycles, and $D_N(x)$ converges to the mixture's discrepancy, yielding $D^+(x) = \max_{w \in \mathcal{W}} \Delta(\sigma(w))$. Under finite local simple-zero conditions satisfied by infinitely many radices per prime pair, Mahler–Newton $p$-adic interpolation establishes a bijective isometry on each nonrectangular cutoff shape, logarithmic equidistribution, $D^-(x) = 0$, and $O_\varepsilon(\log^* T)$ sparsity of exceptional logarithmic scales. For the binary product $\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$, Eisenstein irreducibility rules out integer shape roots, a finite non-coincidence certificate yields $D^+(x) \le 4/11 + 2.85 \times 10^{-10}$, and exact binary cycle bounds prove $D^+(x) \le 4/11 \iff C_0 \le 4/11$, separating the $4/11$ threshold from the still-open binary normality problem. The paper also consolidates the kernel-verified $43/45$ bound for $\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$, transcendence and $D^+ < 1$ for general multiplicatively independent integer parameters via Ridout's theorem, and the kernel-verified plateau-cycle law and resonant-radix classification for rational quadratics in $\alpha(b,2)$, together with Lean-supported unconditional sub-quarter bounds for explicit families (using a finite exact cycle enumeration for $b=5, D=9$).
