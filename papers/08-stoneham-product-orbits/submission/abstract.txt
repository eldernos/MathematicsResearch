For the product $x = \alpha(B,p)\alpha(B,q)$ of two Stoneham numbers with distinct odd prime parameters $p < q$ coprime to an integer radix $B \ge 2$, we show that every subsequential weak limit of the empirical radix-shift measures is a convex mixture of Lebesgue measure and rational periodic orbits supported on $\{p,q\}$, and that the scalar extreme interval discrepancy converges to that of the mixture. For the binary product $\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$, the upper discrepancy satisfies $D^+(x) \le 4/11 + 2.85 \times 10^{-10}$, and the exact threshold $D^+(x) \le 4/11$ is equivalent to the upper density $C_0$ of integer shifted prefixes being at most $4/11$ (with $D^+(x) = C_0$ whenever either fails). Ordinary binary normality of $\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$ remains open.

Under a finite pair of local $p$-adic simple-zero conditions—satisfied by infinitely many radices $B$ for every distinct odd prime pair $(p,q)$—Mahler interpolation yields a bijective $p$-adic isometry for each nonrectangular cutoff shape, logarithmic equidistribution, lower discrepancy $D^-(x) = 0$, and at most $O_\varepsilon(\log^* T)$ exceptional logarithmic scales up to $T$. For $(2,3,5)$ and $(2,5,3)$, Eisenstein irreducibility proves that no shape root is an ordinary integer. We also consolidate the kernel-verified $43/45$ bound for $\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$, transcendence and $D^+ < 1$ for general multiplicatively independent integer parameters via Ridout's theorem, and the kernel-verified plateau-cycle law, resonant-radix classification, and unconditional sub-quarter discrepancy bounds for rational quadratics in $\alpha(b,2)$.
