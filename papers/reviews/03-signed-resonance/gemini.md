## 2. Mathematical Verification by Topic

### 2.1 Cyclotomic `iff`, component hypotheses, and composite-parameter example (Theorem 3, Appendix A / Theorem 11)
- **Theorem 3 (`paper.tex:90–127`):** The $p$-adic Teichmüller-lift proof is mathematically valid. Every edge case holds: $M = \varnothing$ ($D = 1$, $U = 0$, $P_u = 0$, $R = [S, pS)\cap\Z$, $\rho = 1$), zero weights $v_m = 0$, signed rational weights, indices divisible by $p$, and coincident insertions (e.g., $m_2 = pm_1$, handled cleanly because $w^p = w$). The exact rational reduction modulo $Y^d - 1$ (`paper.tex:124`) is valid, including $d = 1$ (empty product).
- **Odd-prime hypothesis vs. paper proof (Minor):** Theorem 3 assumes $p$ is an odd prime, matching Lean's `cyclotomic_normal_iff` (which inherits `Odd p` from `stoneham_orbit_small`). However, Lemma 2 in the paper already covers $2 \in P$, and the proof of Theorem 3 works verbatim for $p = 2$ (odd $b$, $d = 1$, $w = 1$, $\Phi_1(Y) = Y - 1$, $b^{2^j} \equiv 1 \pmod{2^{j+1}}$). A one-sentence remark noting that $p = 2$ is also covered by the paper proof (or noting that the hypothesis matches the formalization) would prevent readers from wondering where oddness of $p$ is used.
- **Composite-parameter example (Theorem 11, `paper.tex:298–316`):** The separation from Theorem 3 is mathematically well-motivated. For composite odd $c$, $\Z/c\Z$ splits across prime powers with potentially distinct multiplicative orders of $b$, so no single $\Phi_d$ test applies. Appendix A's lifting $c^{j+1} \mid B^{c^j} + 1$ works for any odd $c \ge 3$ because $-1$ is a root of unity of order $2$ modulo every odd prime divisor of $c$. Individual normality of $x = mT_{b,c,m}$ and $y = \ell T_{b,c,\ell}$ follows cleanly from Lemma 2 (on the finite prime support of $c$) and Lemma 5; irrationality, the simultaneous lower bound $(c-\ell)/(c-1)$ for all $h \ge 1$, and the explicit family $m = 1, \ell = 2d+1, c = \text{odd part}(b^{2d}+1)$ (which satisfies $\ell < c$ for all $d \ge 1, b \ge 2$) are completely verified.

### 2.2 Zero-shell discrepancy up to $o(1)$, endpoints, and signs (Theorem 1, Lemmas 2, 4, 5, Eq. (7))
- **Lemma 2 (`paper.tex:70–85`):** The lifting-the-exponent period bound $q \mid (g-1)t$ (including $4 \mid g-1$ at $\ell = 2$), the $L^2$ orthogonality + shift-window argument for segments of length $U \le t$, and the trigonometric sandwiching / grid bracketing are valid, including for negative unit numerators $A$.
- **Lemma 4 (`paper.tex:130–140`):** The Euler-theorem induction ($b^{p^j(p-1)} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^j}$, valid at $p=2$ as well), the reduced denominator bound $p^j/C \le \den(P_n) \le p^j$ for $c_j \ne 0$ (using $|c_j|$ for negative phases), and the tail majorant $\frac{(p+1)C}{(b-1)p^{j+1}}$ are exact.
- **Lemma 5 (`paper.tex:143–148`):** The wrapping-arc formulation $\Disc(\mu) = \sup_{I \text{ half-open arc}}(\mu(I) - |I|)$ and the strict enlargement by $\epsilon > \delta$ (followed by $\epsilon \downarrow \delta$) handle half-open endpoints and atoms rigorously without losing a factor of $2$ on $K/N$. (This is slightly sharper than the $2K/N$ bound in Lean's `CircleDiscrepancy.transport`, and the paper proof is airtight.)
- **Theorem 1 (`paper.tex:47–54, 150–162`):** Valid for all $m \in \Z\setminus\{0\}$ (both signs), all $r \in \Q$ (both signs and $b$-adic rationals with $q_0 = 1$), $p = 2$ in odd bases, and arbitrary cutoffs $N$ inside shells. Position $0$ is consistently excluded from shells ($Z(1)=0$), matching `SignedPhases.regularPositions`. One minor straddling-shell omission at $n = s$ is noted in Section 3 below.
- **Equation (7) (`paper.tex:166–170`):** This paper-only deduction is sound: on resonant cells $B_n/p^t \in \Z$, while on nonresonant cells \eqref{eq:cell} identifies $\{B_n/p^t\}$ with a single rational radix orbit of denominator $q_{u,k} = p^{k+E-v_p(W_u)} \asymp p^k$. Applying the same $(|m|q_0)^{-1}$ and $q_0$ denominator bounds, Lemma 2, periodic counting, and Lemma 5 yields $D_N(mU+r) = d_b(r)Z_R(N)/N + o(1)$ at all cutoffs, and $D_\infty(6F) = 2/3$ in the $p=7$ example.

### 2.3 Exact vs. periodic values and spectrum sufficiency (Proposition 6, Theorem 7, Corollary 8)
- **Proposition 6 (`paper.tex:181–191`):** Both formulas are exact for purely periodic phases ($c_{j+L} = c_j$ for all $j \ge 0$), with the first indexing densities *before* shell $r$ and the second *after* shell $r$. However, the remark at the end of the proof claiming `"The same proof applies when only the zero pattern is eventually periodic"` is imprecise for the first formula (see Finding M2 below).
- **Theorem 7 (`paper.tex:217–232`):** Both directions are solid. Forward: compactness of the cluster set $K$ of reverse histories $h_j$, $\sigma K \subseteq K$, greedy lexicographic maximum $s \in K$, and continuity/monotonicity of $V_p$. Converse (sufficiency): the padded concatenation $0^k s_{k-1}\cdots s_0$ with $c_j = 1 - z_j \in \{0,1\}$ accurately bounds every intermediate reverse history by $V_p(s) + p^{-(k+i)}$ and matches $s$ on $k$ digits at block ends, while keeping infinitely many nonzero phases even for $s = 0^\infty$ and $s = 1^\infty$. The dyadic-endpoint remark at $p = 2$ is accurate.
- **Corollary 8 (`paper.tex:234–241`):** Compactness, minimum positive value $1 - 1/p$, period-$2k$ approximation within $p^{-k}$, and non-isolation of every positive point (splitting into infinite-support truncations from below and finite-support periodic extensions from above, strictly separated term-by-term even at $p = 2$) are all sound.
- **Carminati–Tiozzo reflection (`paper.tex:250`):** Checked at all boundary cases ($t = 0, 1, 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/3$). Because any suffix-maximal word eventually $1^\infty$ must be $1^\infty$, complementing gives a nonterminating binary expansion for every $t \in (0,1]$ on which lexicographic and numerical orders strictly agree. The identification $1 - \mathcal{S}_2 = \{t \in [0,1] : \text{doubling orbit of } t \text{ avoids } (0,t)\}$ is exact.

### 2.4 Rational translation, de Bruijn words, and external Roth/Ridout (Proposition 9, §6, Theorem 10)
- **Proposition 9 (`paper.tex:256–272`):** The Euler-circuit existence (including $h = 1$), the oscillation formula $\frac{1}{L} + \max_j(j/L - y_j) - \min_j(j/L - y_j)$, and the rotation-permutation identity $j/L - y_j = -v_j/L$ yielding $d_b(r) = (1 + y_{L-1} - y_0)/L$ are completely valid. (Note that $1/L < d_b(r) < 2/L$ even holds strictly since $L = b^h \ge 2$ forces $0 < y_0 < y_{L-1} < 1$.)
- **Inverse-power family and division-by-9 example (`paper.tex:276–282`):** Both are verified. Altering $c_0$ by $1$ adds $1/2 - 1/8 = 3/8$, so $X' = 9Y + 3/8$ has the same zero pattern as $Y$ at every shell (not merely eventually) and $D_\infty(X') = 3/4$, while $X'/9 = Y + 1/24$ has $D_\infty(X'/9) = d_2(1/3)\cdot (3/4) = 1/2$.
- **Theorem 10 (`paper.tex:284–295`):** The rationality criterion holds for arbitrary integers $b, p \ge 2$ without primality or coprimality. For transcendence, the tail bound gives infinitely many distinct reduced approximants $R_j = u_j/q_j$ with $0 < |X - R_j| < Q_j^{-\mu} \le q_j^{-\mu}$ for any $1 < \mu < p$. Roth's theorem ($p > 2$, $\mu > 2$) and Ridout's theorem ($p \ge 2$, $\mu > 1$, finite prime support dividing $pb$) apply validly, with a minor notational bridge needed for $\max(|u_j|, q_j)$ in Ridout (Finding M5).
