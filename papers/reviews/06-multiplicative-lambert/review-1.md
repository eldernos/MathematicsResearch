1. **Major — the one-bit asymptotic for the flagship constant is already published.** At lines 57–66, 134–139, 194–206, 235–263 and 1031–1033, and in the Theorem A ledger, the closest comparison is missing. Bailey, Borwein, Crandall and Pomerance, *On the binary expansions of algebraic numbers*, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux **16** (2004), 487–518, §9, Theorem 9.4, p. 509, prove a prefix one-bit **asymptotic** for the square of their one-sided theta sum. Immediately after the theorem they explicitly extend the same asymptotic to the square of their two-sided sum $z' = \theta _3(1/2)$. Here $(z')^2=1+4L_{\chi_4}$. Adding the integer has no effect on fractional digits, and multiplication by four shifts them by two places. Thus the prefix count for the *same* $L_{\chi_4}$ is asymptotic to $C_0 M/\sqrt{\log M}$, with $C_0\approx 0.7996$. Subtracting prefixes even gives the one-bit asymptotic uniformly for $\eta N\le H\le N$, for each fixed $\eta >0$. Their Lemma 9.5 bounds nearby pairs of sums of two squares, and their proof uses isolated coefficients whose bits survive the carries. Credit both the special result and the methodological overlap. Identify the additional content of A and B as the general coefficient class, polynomially short windows, arbitrary words and the discrepancy gap. Qualify Question 1 for the one-bit, macroscopic-window special case. [Published paper, §9](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/JTNB/2004-3/pages487-518.pdf)

   The same ledger should also credit prior irrationality results. Chowla treated the alternating odd-denominator series in integer bases at least five in 1947. The second Lambert series in the theorem on p. 63 of the **already cited** Erdős 1948 paper gives the $\chi_4$ case at base two, since its sine weight is $\chi_4$. Luca and Tachiya subsequently proved irrationality for eventually periodic rational numerator sequences that are not eventually zero, at every integer base of absolute value greater than one. Taking the numerator sequence to be any real Dirichlet character covers the irrationality assertion in Example 1.3, including even and imprimitive characters. These precedents leave the arbitrary-word and discrepancy conclusions to be assessed separately.
