# Paper V review disposition

Both reviewers examined [author revision 28186f0](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/tree/28186f0e08ea889827b489f03f1c213456581c91/papers/05-classical-denominators). They checked the denominator and error estimates, the equality condition for the half-rate identity, and the finite word counts. Each independently recovered the 31,232 admissible words in the overlapping test. [Gemini report](gemini.md) · [Sonnet report](sonnet.md)

| Finding | Change in the release manuscript |
| --- | --- |
| Principal results and motivation | The introduction explains why different binary expansions for the same number give different denominator problems. The abstract foregrounds the rate and optimality theorems. The prime-invariance hypothesis now makes its quantifiers explicit. Section 2 explains how to clear fixed rational coefficients. |
| Corrected denominator budgets | Section 3 gives a baseline with the same approximation horizon. For horizons one half, one and two, truncating later and shifting back gives common-denominator rates of about 1.097, 1.693 and 2.886. The corrected models give about 0.746, 1.096 and 1.963. |
| What the finite sieve proves | Table 2 describes the words excluded using selected residues. The overlapping test forces at least 18 transitions among the 64 bits after position 513. Direct computation gives 38 for the actual word. The distinction explains the purpose of testing partial information. |
| Earlier results and secondary material | Section 7 now attributes periodicity to Sondow and Schalm and the stronger exception estimate to Berndt, Kim and Zaharescu before the proposition. The prime-by-prime proof gives an explicit paired-zero bound. Section 6 explains the use of a residue modulo the square of a prime. The historical discussion adds Wu–Chen, Alladi–Robinson, Zsigmondy, Borwein–Borwein–Galway and Bugeaud. Takahashi's equations (11)–(19) were checked in the source. His attribution of (13) differs from the historical suggestion in one report, so the paper cites the formula without assigning first discovery. |
| Discrepancy criterion and literature scope | Section 8 proves the transition criterion directly, then gives the finite-prefix estimate for any irrational of finite irrationality exponent and specializes it to π. An auxiliary result specific to log 2 is linked in the correspondence table. The unexplained decimal target was removed from the paper. The mathematical status of the stronger asymptotic question remains explicit. |
| Presentation | The correspondence table omits internal identifiers, documents the valuation hypotheses, and has more space below its caption. The calculations, figure, sources and author-revision review reports accompany the PDF. |

The denominator-rate and optimality results in Sections 1–3 are the main candidates for specialist appraisal. Historical priority for the precise rate and residue statements remains provisional.
