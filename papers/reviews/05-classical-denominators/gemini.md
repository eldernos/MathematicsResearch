### 3.2 Half-rate and prime-band cancellation (Sections 1–2) — **Verified**
- **Theorem 1.1 (`th:half`):** The identity $(1+2^{-3})/(1-2^{-2})^2 = 2$ gives grouped weights $w(j) = 4\ind_{2\mid j} + 3\ind_{3\mid j} - 6\ind_{6\mid j}$, repeating $(0, 4, 3, 4, 0, 1)$ from $j=1$. Nonnegativity and $w(j) \le 4$ yield $0 \le 2^N\log 2 - Y_N \le 4/(N+1)$ for all $N \ge 0$. Every term in the ungrouped sums has denominator dividing $L_{\lfloor N/2 \rfloor}$. Only $v=1$ has $P_v(w) = 0$ (since $P_2(w) = 1/2$ and $w(j) \ge 0$), so Theorem 2.3 yields rate $1/2$.
- **Proposition 2.1 (`prop:standard`) and Corollary 2.2 (`cor:poly`):** All inequalities in the $116 N^{2/3}$ proof check out: for $N \ge 8$, $K = \lceil N^{1/3} \rceil \ge 2$, $N \le K^3 \le K^4 < p^{2(l+1)}$ gives $v_p(L_N) \le 2l+1 \le 3l$, while $p > K^2$ forces $p^2 > N^{4/3} > N$ and $1 \le \lfloor N/p \rfloor < K$. With $\log L_k \le 7k$ and $7 + \log 2 < 8$, $3\log L_{K^2} + \sum_{k=1}^K \log(2^k L_k) \le 29 K^2 \le 116 N^{2/3}$. In Corollary 2.2, $\sum_{i=1}^k b^{-i}/i^s < 1$ holds for all $b \ge 2, s \ge 1$, and $K^2 > b$ absorbs all primes dividing $b$ into $L_{K^2}^{3s}$.
- **Theorem 2.3 (`th:bands`):** The Fermat congruence $p S_N(W) \equiv 2^N P_{\lfloor N/p \rfloor}(W) \pmod p$ for $p^2 > N$ and the tail estimates $\vartheta(N/(V+1))$ and $\sqrt{N}\log N$ (for higher prime powers, since $\pi(\sqrt{N}) \le \sqrt{N}$) are completely rigorous, including for infinite zero-prefix sets. (Minor quantifier-order ambiguity in line 171 noted in Section 6 below.)

### 3.3 Finite dyadic class and external Bang hypothesis (Theorem 2.4) — **Verified**
- Rewriting $\log(1+2^{-k}) = \log(1-2^{-2k}) - \log(1-2^{-k})$ preserves every binary-exponent coefficient and therefore every truncation at binary exponent $N$.
- Exponentiation yields $\prod_k (2^k - 1)^{b_k} 2^{-\sum_k k b_k} = 2^m$. Bang's theorem (1886) supplies a primitive odd prime divisor of $2^k - 1$ for every $k > 6$, forcing $b_k = 0$ for $k > 6$. Valuations at $31, 5, 7, 3, 2$ uniquely force $b_5 = b_4 = 0$, $b_3 = -b_6$, $b_2 = -2b_6$, $m = b_6 - b_1$, and $W(j) = -b_1 + b_6 w(j)$.
- When $b_1 = 0$, $W = m w$ ($m \ne 0$) gives rate $1/2$. When $b_1 \ne 0$, $P_1(W) = -b_1/2 \ne 0$ already contributes $1/2$, and $P_v(W) \to m\log 2 \ne 0$ forces at least one additional $v \ge 2$ with $P_v(W) \ne 0$, making the rate strictly greater than $1/2$.
- **Lean bridge accuracy:** `DyadicRelations.optimal_rate` takes odd-prime valuation balance, primitive divisors for $k > 6$, and finiteness of the zero set `Z` as explicit hypotheses. The paper states the Bang and finite-zero-set bridges clearly in Section 9 (lines 851–855), though Table 3 omits noting that valuation balance and plus-to-minus rewriting are also paper bridges (documented in `CORRESPONDENCE.md` row 2.4).
- **Remark on generality:** The proof extends verbatim to finite $\Q$-linear combinations of $\log(1\pm 2^{-k})$ by clearing denominators (scaling $S_N(W)$ by a fixed integer changes $\log \den$ by $O(1)$). Mentioning this would strengthen Theorem 2.4 at no cost.
