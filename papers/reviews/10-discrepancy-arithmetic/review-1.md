### 1. Sharp translation identity, transfer, and universal preservers (`paper.tex` ll. 49–65, 161–206, 260–404)

- **Preliminaries and atoms (`Lemma 2.1`, ll. 167–179; `Lemma 2.2`, ll. 181–191; `Lemma 2.3`, ll. 197–206).** Identifying $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ with $[0,1)$, a wrapping half-open arc $[\theta,\theta+l)\bmod 1$ ($\theta\in[0,1)$, $\theta+l>1$) is $[0,\theta+l-1)\cup[\theta,1)=[0,1)\setminus[\theta+l-1,\theta)$, the exact set-theoretic complement of the half-open interval $[\theta+l-1,\theta)$. Because both the arc and its complementary interval are closed on the left and open on the right, point masses at the endpoints are partitioned without overlap or omission, and $\mu(I)-|I|=-(\mu(I^c)-|I^c|)$. Consequently $D(\mu)=\sup_I(\mu(I)-|I|)$ over half-open arcs $I$, and $D(\mu)$ is rotation-invariant. (See Finding 3 below for a minor boundary-case detail at $l=1-2\epsilon$ in the written proof of `Lemma 2.2`.)
- **Sharp translation identity and attainment (`Theorem 3.1`, ll. 262–282; paired form l. 285).** Given a half-open arc $J=[\gamma,\gamma+l)$ of centre $c=\gamma+l/2$ containing a fraction $1-e=\mu_N^z(J)$ of the points $\{b^nz\}$ ($0\le n<N$), every $n$ with $\{b^nz\}\in J$ satisfies $\lVert b^n(x+z)-(b^nx+c)\rVert=\lVert b^nz-c\rVert\le l/2$. By rotation invariance (`Lemma 2.1`) the rotated sample $(\{b^nx+c\})_{n<N}$ has discrepancy $D_N(x)$, so `Lemma 2.2` with $\epsilon=l/2$ yields $|D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)|\le e+l=1-(\mu_N^z(J)-|J|)$. Taking the infimum over $J$ gives $|D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)|\le 1-D_N(z)$. Since $\{b^n\cdot 0\}=0$ for all $n$, testing $[0,h)$ as $h\downarrow 0$ gives $D_N(0)=1$, so equality holds at $x=0$.

### 4. Novelty, literature attribution and web/arXiv/zbMATH search (`paper.tex` ll. 60–64, 112–150, 347–360, 443–483, 557–563, 591–593, 846–851; `LEDGER.md`)

- **Bergelson–Downarowicz (arXiv:2506.12929) credit and entropy vs. discrepancy.** The introduction (`paper.tex` ll. 131–149) and Section 5 (ll. 591–593) explicitly credit Bergelson and Downarowicz \cite[Proposition 4.9 and Corollary 4.10(1)]{BD25}, Kamae's subadditivity bound, and \cite[Section 4.3]{BD25} for the point-entropy addition inequalities $|h(x)-h(y)|\le\underline h(x+y)\le\overline h(x+y)\le\min(\log b,h(x)+h(y))$, and state directly that in normalized deficit coordinates $1-D$ the polytope of `Theorem B` is the same five-vertex triangle-inequality polytope as that of normalized point entropy $h/\log b$. The manuscript also clearly explains why the two invariants are distinct (ll. 139–143): they agree on the mixtures $\theta\delta_0+(1-\theta)\lambda$ (where $h/\log b=1-\theta$ and $D=\theta$), but every periodic orbit of period at least $2$ has entropy $0$ and discrepancy strictly less than $1$ (e.g., $x=1/3$ in base $2$ has $h(1/3)=0$ and $D_N(1/3)\to 2/3$), so neither inequality family implies the other. Qualitative sufficiency via Sigmund's generic-point theorem \cite{Sigmund74} is likewise credited in the introduction (ll. 143–146), `Proposition 4.25` of \cite{BD25} is credited for rational dilation invariance of point entropy and normality (ll. 112–114, 557–559), and Section 8 of \cite{BD25} is credited as a stronger signed binary precedent for the sparse-preserver product (ll. 848–851).
- **Classical probability-metric ingredients.** `paper.tex` ll. 117–129 and Section 4.1 (ll. 443–483) explain that extreme discrepancy on $[0,1)$ equals Kuiper's rotation-invariant circular statistic $V_N=D_N^++D_N^-$ \cite{Kuiper60}, that $1-D(\mu)=\inf_l(1-Q_\mu(l)+l)$ is an exact rewriting of Lévy's circular concentration function $Q_\mu(l)$ \cite{Levy39}, and that $1-D(\mu)$ is bi-Lipschitz equivalent ($k(\mu)\le 1-D(\mu)\le 3k(\mu)$, `Proposition 4.2`) to the Ky Fan \cite{Fan43} and Lévy–Prokhorov \cite{Prokhorov56,Dudley02} distance to the nearest point mass $\delta_c$. Line 126 states explicitly: *"The pseudometric $d_N$ is therefore not a new probability metric."*
- **Is the sharp translation identity folklore?** I searched the web, arXiv and zbMATH Open for the finite radix-orbit identity $\sup_x|D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)|=1-D_N(z)$, the paired-sample inequality $D(q)\le D(p)+1-D(r)$ for $q_n\equiv p_n\pm r_n\pmod 1$, and combinations of Kuiper's statistic $V_N$, circular extreme discrepancy, translation invariance and Lévy's concentration function. No source containing the verbatim paired-sample inequality or the supremum identity for radix orbits was found. Mathematically, once $D(\mu)$ is written in Kuiper–Lévy concentration form $\sup_l(Q_\mu(l)-l)$, the upper bound $|D(u+w)-D(u)|\le 1-D(w)$ is a one-paragraph combination of rotation invariance and the standard $\epsilon$-perturbation bound $|D(u)-D(v)|\le E/N+2\epsilon$ around the optimal arc centre of $w$, and attainment at $u=0$ uses only $D(0)=1$. A specialist in uniform distribution may therefore view the paired-sample bound as an elementary or folklore property of Kuiper discrepancy. The paper's framing (ll. 116–129, 479–482) and Inquiry 2 of `submission/reviewer-inquiry.md` handle this appropriately.

### Minor

**1. Evidence-label inconsistency for the universal rational-scalar theorem (`Theorem 4.5`) across the manuscript and verification map** (`paper.tex` ll. 521, 533–534, 898; `VERIFICATION.md` l. 14 vs. l. 34; `submission/alphaxiv.md` l. 46; `submission/reviewer-packet.md` l. 45)

*Problem.* In `paper.tex` l. 521 (`Theorem 4.5`, `VERIFICATION.md` row `4.5` at l. 34, and `submission/reviewer-packet.md` l. 45), the classification of universal rational scalars $(a/d)K_b\subseteq K_b\iff d\mid b^j$ carries the label `\evidence{Lean-supported paper proof}`, justified at l. 533 by transitive dependence on `Theorem 3.1` through the written classification `Theorem 3.3` (and on the subgroup property of $K_b$ in `Proposition 4.3`). However, the proof of `Theorem 4.5` (ll. 527–535) invokes no Lean declaration directly; it relies on `Proposition 4.3`, `Lemma 4.4` (a paper proof) and `Theorem 3.3(vi)` (the written step of `Theorem 3.3`). Moreover, `VERIFICATION.md` row `C` (l. 14) states *"Kernel-verified pseudometric (= Prop 4.1); the rest are paper proofs (= Prop 4.3, Thm 4.5, Prop 4.6)"*, `paper.tex` l. 898 (`Section 8`) groups *"the preserver classification, the scalar results"* under *"have written proofs"*, and `docs/registry/OP.toml` (`OP-06`, l. 61) classifies the rational-scalar result as `status = "paper"`.

*Fix.* Either relabel `Theorem 4.5` (`paper.tex` l. 521, `VERIFICATION.md` row `4.5`, `submission/reviewer-packet.md` l. 45) as `\evidence{Paper proof}` (consistent with `Lemma 4.4`, `Proposition 4.6`, `paper.tex` l. 898 and `OP-06`), or update `VERIFICATION.md` row `C` (l. 14) so that its summary of `Proposition 4.3` and `Theorem 4.5` agrees with rows `4.3` and `4.5`.

**2. Unproved $\mathrm{lcm}(\den u_k,\den u_{k-1})$ denominator bound used in the proof of `Theorem 6.2(b)`** (`paper.tex` ll. 253, 814)

*Problem.* After `Lemma 2.5` (`paper.tex` l. 253), the text states without proof: *"More generally, $c_k$ has denominator dividing $\mathrm{lcm}(\den u_k,\den u_{k-1})$."* The kernel-verified declaration in `Phase18Synthetic.lean` (`programCoeffs_den_dvd`, l. 416) proves divisibility into the product $\den(u_k)\den(u_{k-1})$, which suffices for prime-power denominators (`programCoeffs_prime_power`, l. 426), whereas the proof of `Theorem 6.2(b)` (l. 814) uses the $\mathrm{lcm}$ bound: all states $u_k$ lie in the grid $G=\{j/m_0:0\le j<m_0\}$, so $\den(u_k)\mid m_0$ and therefore $\den(c_k)\mid\mathrm{lcm}(\den u_k,\den u_{k-1})\mid m_0$.

*Fix.* Add the half-line justification at `paper.tex` l. 253: *"More generally, since $\fr{R_{k-1}}=u_{k-1}$ makes $c_k-(u_k-b^{n_k-n_{k-1}}u_{k-1})$ an integer, $c_k$ has denominator dividing $\mathrm{lcm}(\den u_k,\den u_{k-1})$."*

**3. Boundary case $l=1-2\epsilon$ in the written proof of `Lemma 2.2`** (`paper.tex` ll. 187–190)

*Problem.* The proof of `Lemma 2.2` begins: *"Let $I=[\theta,\theta+l)$. If $l>1-2\epsilon$ then $\mu_u(I)-l<2\epsilon$. Otherwise every $n$ with $u_n\in I$ and $\dist{u_n-v_n}\le\epsilon$ has $v_n$ in the half-open arc $[\theta-\epsilon,\theta+l+\epsilon+\eta)$ for every $\eta>0$, so $\mu_u(I)\le l+2\epsilon+\eta+D(v)+E/N$ by \Cref{lem:arcs}."* When $l=1-2\epsilon$ (for example $\epsilon=0,l=1$ or $\epsilon=1/4,l=1/2$), the "Otherwise" branch has $l+2\epsilon+\eta=1+\eta>1$ for every $\eta>0$, exceeding the arc length restriction $0\le l\le 1$ of `Lemma 2.1`. (In fact, for a half-open arc $I=[\theta,\theta+l)$ with $l<1-2\epsilon$, $\dist{u_n-v_n}\le\epsilon$ already places $v_n$ in $[\theta-\epsilon,\theta+l+\epsilon)$ with $\eta=0$, as in `SharpTranslation.lean` ll. 312–320.)

*Fix.* Change $l>1-2\epsilon$ to $l\ge 1-2\epsilon$ (giving $\mu_u(I)-l\le 1-l\le 2\epsilon$) and require $0<\eta\le 1-l-2\epsilon$ (or take $\eta=0$) in the "Otherwise" branch.

**4. Unused prime parameter $p$ and order $t=\ord_p(b)$ in `Theorem 6.2(b)`** (`paper.tex` ll. 795–798, 808–821)

*Problem.* `Theorem 6.2(b)` introduces *"Let $p\nmid b$ be an odd prime, $t=\ord_p(b)$"* and states the bound $|D_N(x)-\delta|+|1-D_N(mx)|\le C_{b,p,m}N^{-1/3}$ on the schedule $n_k=tk^3$. Unlike `Theorem 5.3`, however, the states programmed in `Theorem 6.2(b)` come from the $b$-cycles of the fixed grid $G=\{j/m_0:0\le j<m_0\}$ and have denominators dividing $m_0$; the prime $p$ never appears in the coefficients or in the period estimate, and the proof on ll. 808–821 works for any integer $t\ge 1$ (in particular $t=1$, $n_k=k^3$).

*Fix.* Either replace $t=\ord_p(b)$ in `Theorem 6.2(b)` by any fixed integer $t\ge 1$ (or $t=1$, writing $C_{b,m}$), or add a brief parenthetical remark noting that $t=\ord_p(b)$ is kept only to place $x$ on the same schedule $n_k=tk^3$ as `Theorem 5.3`.
