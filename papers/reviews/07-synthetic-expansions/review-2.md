## Verdict

**Accept with minor revisions.**

The manuscript *Synthetic Arithmetic Expansions and Prescribed Discrepancy* (v1.0, `papers/07-synthetic-expansions/`) is a well-structured, rigorous, and clearly written research paper establishing:

1. **The normality switch and exact shifted-prefix denominators (`Theorem A` / `Theorems 4.2, 4.5`, `paper.tex:60-73, 410-425, 484-507`):** For $b \ge 2$, a prime $p \nmid b$, $t = \operatorname{ord}_p(b)$, and any strictly increasing sequence $(n_k)_{k\ge 1}$ of positive multiples of $t$ with $n_k \ge k^2$, the valuation-weighted series $X_s = \sum_{k\ge 1} p^{-s v_p(k)} b^{-n_k}$ has shifted-prefix reduced denominator $\operatorname{den}(R_n) = p^{s\lfloor \log_p K(n)\rfloor}$ and satisfies $D_N(X_0) \to 1$ and $D_N(X_s) \to 0$ for every integer $s \ge 1$ (both completely kernel-verified in Lean 4, together with the dense geometric-retirement equivalence of `Proposition 4.1`, `paper.tex:382-406`).
2. **Complete-period discrepancy rates (`Theorem B` / `Theorems 3.1, 4.7`, `paper.tex:74-85, 315-359, 527-551`):** For odd $p \nmid b$, $s \ge 1$, $d \ge s+2$, and $n_k = t k^d$, complete periods yield $D_N(X_s) \ll_{b,p,s,d} N^{-s/d} + N^{-(d-s-1)/d} + N^{-(d-1)/d}\log N$, and a two-page self-contained proof gives $D_N(\xi; 2) \le 200 N^{-1/3}$ for all $N \ge 1$ where $\xi = \sum_{k\ge 1} 3^{-v_3(k)} 2^{-2k^3}$.
3. **Prescribed discrepancy limits, $(\liminf, \limsup)$ pairs, and invariant-measure limit sets (`Theorem C` / `Lemma 5.1, Theorems 5.3, 6.1, 6.4`, `paper.tex:90-108, 565-648, 662-774`):** Positive rational state corrections $c_k \in (0,1]$ with $p$-power denominators on the cubic schedule $n_k = t k^3$ realize every limiting discrepancy $\delta \in [0,1]$ with $|D_N(x_\delta) - \delta| \le C_{b,p} N^{-1/3}$ uniformly in $\delta$, every pair $0 \le \alpha \le \beta \le 1$ of lower and upper discrepancy limits, and (with general rational coefficients on $n_k = k^3$) every nonempty compact connected subset of $\mathcal{M}_T(\mathbb{T})$ as an empirical orbit-measure limit set.
4. **Quadratic polynomial images (`Theorem D` / `Theorem 7.1, Corollary 7.2`, `paper.tex:115-132, 776-1112`):** For odd $p \nmid b$, $d \ge 3$, and $X_a = \sum_{i\ge 1} p^{-a v_p(i)} b^{-t i^d}$ ($a \ge 1$), every nonconstant rational polynomial of total degree at most two in finitely many $X_a$ is normal to base $b$; in particular $\xi^2$ is normal to base $2$, $(\xi, \xi^2)$ is jointly normal, and an algebraic $X_s$ must have degree at least $3$.
5. **Infinite sums across all odd denominator primes (`Theorem E` / `Theorems 8.2–8.4, Proposition 8.5`, `paper.tex:135-145, 1114-1254`):** The binary series $Y = \sum_{\ell\text{ odd prime}} Y_\ell$ with $Y_\ell = \sum_{j\ge 1} \ell^{-s_\ell j} 2^{-16^{\ell^j}}$ ($s_3 = 2$, $s_\ell = 1$ for $\ell \ge 5$) is normal to base $2$ and a Liouville number with $D_N(Y; 2) = O(1/\log N)$, whereas every finite subfamily of $(Y_\ell)$ is jointly normal and positive rational multiples $\sum_\ell c_\ell Y_\ell$ can sum to any positive real $R$ (including rational $R = 1$).

## Task 2: Novelty, Antecedents, and Ledger Audit

1. **Is the normality switch (`Theorem A` / `Theorem 4.5`) already implied by Kano's Theorem 1 or by Bailey–Crandall's Theorem 4.8 after regrouping?**
   - **No.** In `Theorem 1` of Kano (1993, p. 910), the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n / (a^{\mu_n} b^{\lambda_n})$ requires strictly increasing denominator exponents $(\mu_n)$ and $(\lambda_n)$ with $\gcd(A_n, a) = 1$, $|A_n| = O(a^{\mu_n})$, and growth bounds relating $\Delta\lambda_n$ to $\Delta\mu_n$. In `Theorem 4.8` of Bailey and Crandall (2002, pp. 535–536), the series $\sum_{k=1}^\infty c^{-m_k} b^{-n_k}$ has unit numerators and strictly increasing sequences $(m_k)$ and $(n_k)$ ($\mu_k = m_k - m_{k-1} \ge 1$). In `Theorem 7.4` of Vandehey (`arXiv:1606.07911v1`), $\sum_{k=1}^\infty 1/(c_k b^{m_k})$ requires a divisibility chain $c_k \mid c_{k+1}$.
   - In `Theorem A` (`paper.tex:60-67`), $X_s = \sum_{k\ge 1} p^{-s v_p(k)} b^{-n_k}$ has non-monotone denominator exponents $s v_p(k)$ that vanish whenever $p \nmid k$ and fail $c_k \mid c_{k+1}$.
   - Attempting to regroup $X_s$ across record valuation blocks $k \in [p^J, p^{J+1})$ into a single term $A_J / (p^{sJ} b^{n_{p^{J+1}-1}})$ produces numerators $A_J \asymp b^{n_{p^{J+1}-1} - n_{p^J}}$ that grow exponentially with the schedule gaps (violating Kano's $|A_J| = O(p^{sJ})$ and Bailey–Crandall's unit numerator) and hides the $(p-1)p^J - 1$ intermediate insertion positions $n_k$ inside the block, whereas rearranging by valuation layers $v_p(k) = j$ destroys the chronological radix-shift order and the tail bound $|b^n X_s - R_n| \ll b^{-(n_{K(n)+1}-n)}$.
   - Instead, the mechanism of `Theorems 4.2 and 4.5` (`paper.tex:410-425, 484-507`) tracks the chronological shifted prefix $R_n = b^n \sum_{k\le K(n)} p^{-s v_p(k)} b^{-n_k}$ at *every* insertion $K$ and uses the alignment $t = \operatorname{ord}_p(b) \mid n_k$ to show that the $\lfloor K/p^J \rfloor \in \{1, \dots, p-1\}$ terms of maximal valuation $v_p(k) = J = \lfloor \log_p K \rfloor$ all carry the same nonzero residue $b^n \pmod p$ and therefore never cancel modulo $p$. Furthermore, `Theorem A` holds for *every* schedule of multiples of $t$ satisfying $n_k \ge k^2$, with no upper growth condition or regularity on $n_{k+1} - n_k$. The manuscript (`paper.tex:161-164`, `LEDGER.md:53, 58`) accurately characterizes the $s \ge 1$ half as combining this exact prefix-denominator identity with the fixed-support short-orbit method of Korobov and Vandehey.

## Task 3: Independent Mathematical Audit of Section 7 (Paper Proofs)

Every step of Section 7 (`paper.tex:776-1112`) was re-derived by hand and cross-checked with the exact rational scripts `scripts/phase19_nonlinear.py` and `notes/phase19/reviews/nonlinear-check.py` (both rerun in this worktree with exit code 0):

1. **Normalization and freezing (`Lemma 7.4`, `paper.tex:820-856`):**
   - For $n \ge t$, $K(n) = \lfloor (n/t)^{1/d} \rfloor \ge 1$, $J = J(n) = \lfloor \log_p K(n) \rfloor$, and $u = u(n) = n/(t p^{dJ}) \in [1, p^d)$.
   - Any pair $(i,j)$ with $t(i^d+j^d) \le n$ satisfies $1 \le i,j \le K(n) < p^{J+1}$, so $\lambda_i = J - v_p(i) \ge 0$, $p^{(a+c)J} w_a(i) w_c(j) = p^{a\lambda_i + c\lambda_j} \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $b^{n - t(i^d+j^d)} \in \mathbb{Z}$. Hence $R_n(X_a X_c)$ is integral outside $p$ and has $v_p(R_n(X_a X_c)) \ge -(a+c)J$.
   - Writing $z = i/p^J, y = j/p^J \in \Gamma \cap (0,p)$ gives $t(i^d+j^d) \le n \iff z^d + y^d \le u$. Terms nonzero modulo $p^L$ satisfy $a\lambda_i + c\lambda_j < L$, forcing $\lambda_i, \lambda_j \le L-1 < L \le J$ when $J \ge L + L/d$.

3. **Van der Corput third-derivative estimate (`Lemma 7.6`, `paper.tex:903-919`):**

4. **Localized endpoint counting (`Lemma 7.7`, `paper.tex:921-1004`):**
   - In any interval $I \subseteq \mathcal{K} \subset (0, X_{\max})$, there are at least $(1 - 1/p)|I|p^H - 2$ primitive centres of level $H$. Since $\delta_H = \epsilon/(Hp^H) < p^{-H}/2$, each competing endpoint spoils at most one centre.

   - For type-($\beta$) endpoints ($z \ne 1 \ne y$, $v = \lambda(z) \ge k = \lambda(y)$, $v+k \le H$, both orderings counted), let $f_y(z) = (z^d + B)^{1/d}$ with $B = y^d - 1 \ne 0$:
     - *Removals:* Rows with $v < 2H/5$ have $v+k < 4H/5$ and contain $O(H^2 p^{4H/5})$ points. 5. **Second-moment argument and null level sets (`Proposition 7.8`, `paper.tex:1008-1052`):**
