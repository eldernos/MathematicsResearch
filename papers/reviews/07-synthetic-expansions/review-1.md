## Mathematical checks

**Switch and rates, lines 315–359, 410–425 and 448–550.** At depth $J=\lfloor\log_p K\rfloor$ the surviving count is $\lfloor K/p^J\rfloor\in[1,p-1]$. At every completed insertion all its terms have the same nonzero residue after scaling. Between insertions multiplication by *b* preserves that denominator. This includes *p* = 2, the case *K* = 0 by convention, and *s* = 0 by integrality. For a fixed tail buffer, at most $O(W\sqrt{N+W})$ shifts approach an insertion. At order zero this gives concentration at zero. For positive order, a clean block of length $\lfloor N^{\eta/2}\rfloor$ has constant denominator, and multiplication by a fixed nonzero Fourier index loses at most that index's absolute value. The discarded blocks and the unfinished block cost $o(N)$. This proves the stated switch through all integers, including schedules with long gaps or local clusters. With *d* ≥ *s*+2 the sum of count errors is $O(K^{d-s}+K^{s+1})$, plus $O(K\log N)$ for tail buffers, where $N\asymp K^d$. The three powers at line 531 follow. In the cubic example $9h_k/k+k=55k+54+18/k\le127k$. Thus the displayed bound is at most $67.5/K<67.5\sqrt[3]{16}\,N^{-1/3}<171N^{-1/3}$ for *N* ≥ 2. The one-point case also passes.

**Programming, limits and Liouville approximation, lines 509–522, 565–678 and 681–774.** Taking the positive representative of a residue gives coefficient one in the zero case. Consecutive target denominators are powers of the same prime, so the correction has denominator dividing their least common multiple. On the cubic blocks the active error sums to $O(K^2)$ uniformly in the mask, including an unfinished block. The partial sums of the floor-rounding errors are bounded by one. Partial summation against increasing gap lengths costs at most twice the last full gap, and a partial gap costs at most one more. This proves uniformity in δ, including both endpoints. The active empirical measures and the zero atom give the asserted weak limit. For δ = 1 the unbounded digit gaps ensure irrationality. Alternating dominating stages yield both prescribed endpoint limits without intermediate overshoot. In Theorem 6.4 the reset cost for every initial segment is at most two periods per block. The successive target measures approach each other, so taking convex combinations during an unfinished stage introduces no new limit points even for a nonconvex connected target. The rotated finite grid avoiding atoms proves continuity of extreme discrepancy at atomic limits. The fixed-schedule existence comparison is in finding 2.

For the factorial normal Liouville examples, the truncation denominator is at most $b^{n_k}k^s$ and $n_{k+1}/n_k=k+2$. These two estimates and the positive tail prove the approximation claim for every exponent. The separate construction in `notes/phase18/liouville-realization.md` gives a Liouville representative for each δ on a different schedule: the periodic words have length $P_k=k^2b^k$, the truncation denominator is at most $b^{n_k}(b^{P_k}-1)$, and $n_{k+1}/(n_k+P_k)\to\infty$. Discarding the last few digits of each word controls block frequencies, and the incomplete-period cost is $o(n_k)$. That deduction does not determine the arithmetic nature of the canonical cubic witnesses at lines 654–657. The manuscript states the factorial and all-odd-prime Liouville results, without claiming the separate prescribed-δ Liouville construction.

### The quadratic theorem, lines 776–1111

1. **Freezing and tied weights, lines 819–899.** The shell normalization identifies $i/p^J$ with loss $h=J-v_p(i)$. Modulo $p^L$, a surviving pair has both losses below *L*. Thus $v_p(b^{ti^d}-1)=v_0+d(J-h)\ge L$ at the stated threshold. The indexing works in both directions, including a pair on the cutoff. At weight *D*, let $a_*$ be the smallest first order with nonzero coefficient and $g=v_p(A_{a_*})$. Other nonzero terms have total loss strictly below *H* when $H>g$. At a primitive centre the two ordered contributions of the selected term have valuation exactly $a_*H+g$ in sum. In the diagonal case the factor two is a unit. 2. **Localized counting, lines 921–959 and 989–1004.** Summing over $k\le v$, $k+v\le H$ gives $\sum p^{v+k}=O(Hp^H)$, $\sum p^v=O(p^H)$ and $\sum p^k=O(H^2p^{H/2})$. Removing the two small subsets of a retained row costs $O(1+\eta p^v)$ by monotonicity of $z^d$. Each removal is charged as a collision. The localized sublevel regions have area proportional to $(1+x^d)^{2/d}$. On a compact interior interval their difference is $O(|I|+\delta_H)$. Column rounding plus monotone area comparison costs $O(p^v+p^k)$ per loss pair, including boundary points. Multiplying by $2\epsilon/H$ and allowing for both orientations gives the required $C\epsilon|I|p^H+o(p^H)$ with an error uniform in *I*. Only the threshold for a positive proportion depends on the fixed test interval.

   The leading coefficient is $(d+1)(d+2)$, also for *d* = 3. The retained bounds on *z*, *B*, the last factor of the third derivative, and *f* give $|f'''|\gg\eta^{2d-2}$. 4. **Second moment and level sets, lines 1012–1051.** In the middle half of each fixed *I*, the isolated intervals are disjoint at one level and eventually lie in *I*. ## Prior art and bibliographic checks

**Regrouping test for the switch.** In Kano's Theorem 1 (printed p. 911), a term has the form $A_j/(a^{\lambda_j}b^{\mu_j})$. Both exponent sequences increase strictly, $a^{\lambda_j}\ll\mu_j$, $|A_j|\ll a^{\lambda_j}$, and $a^{\lambda_j-\lambda_{j-1}}$ does not divide $A_j$ eventually. The conclusion includes normality to *b* and nonnormality to *ab*. To test whether this yields the whole switch, it suffices to test the allowed cubic schedule. For a contiguous positive group from indices *i* through *k*, clearing the radix powers at $n_k$ leaves a unit at every prime of *b*: the newest term is a unit there and all earlier terms are divisible by those primes. Since $(a,b)=1$, any representation of the group as a Kano term therefore needs $\mu_j\ge n_k$. If the group has at least two terms, its size times $b^{\mu_j}$ is at least $b^{n_k-n_{k-1}}/(k-1)^s$, which tends to infinity. This contradicts Kano's bounded normalized numerator. Consequently all sufficiently late groups would be singletons. For singletons the same numerator bound gives $\mu_j\le n_k+O(\log k)$, while strict increase gives $\lambda_j\ge j-O(1)$ and eventually $k=j+O(1)$. Then $a^{\lambda_j}\ll\mu_j$ fails. This rules out even natural regroupings with padded denominator exponents on that test schedule. [Kano, institutional record and primary text](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/10356/)

Bailey–Crandall Theorem 4.8 has numerator **one** in every term $1/(b^{m_j}c^{n_j})$, nondecreasing increments of the denominator exponent, and eventually nondecreasing $(m_j-m_{j-1})/c^{\gamma n_j}$ for some $\gamma>1/2$. The same two-term lower bound rules out indefinitely grouping consecutive cubic insertions into coefficients at most one at the last radix position. Singleton insertion exponents $s v_p(k)$ repeatedly reset. Vandehey's Theorem 7.4 likewise asks for unit numerators and a **strictly increasing** divisibility chain. Thus the general switch is no direct instance of these series theorems after contiguous regrouping. The positive side does follow by combining Vandehey's short-sum Corollary 1.2 with the exact denominator calculation and block transfer. The order-zero side is elementary. This comparison does not preclude an unrelated representation or identify an earliest proof. [Bailey–Crandall, journal](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10586458.2002.10504704) · [Vandehey, primary text §7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1606.07911v1#S7.SS2)

**Quadratic images.** Kano–Shiokawa's Theorems 4–5 construct rings using separated exponents of Korobov type. Their Theorem 5 gives an uncountable ring whose nonzero elements are normal to one base and nonnormal to the product base. Wagner's published abstract explicitly gives a generator all of whose nonconstant integral polynomial images are normal to 3 and nonnormal to 15. These are direct precedents for simultaneous normality of a generator and polynomial images via integer combinations. The new candidate in Section 7 is the identification of these particular $t i^d$ generators, including several valuation orders on the same schedule. Their consecutive radix positions have ratio tending to one, and their coefficient exponents return to zero infinitely often. The ring constructions' generator hypotheses are not verified for these numbers. Nakai–Shiokawa's *Discrepancy estimates for a class of normal numbers* studies the digit string obtained by **concatenating integer parts of polynomial values**. Its block counts concern a different operation from squaring an already specified real. I found no prior statement settling the paper's specific cubic square. [Kano–Shiokawa, Keio primary preprint](https://www.math.keio.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/91006.pdf) · [Wagner, publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X85711146) · [Nakai–Shiokawa, journal](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-62-3-271-284)
