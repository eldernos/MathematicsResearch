## 1. Mathematical Audit

### 1.1 Fixed-support short-orbit estimate (Section 2, Theorem 2)

**Quantifiers and edge cases.** Theorem 2 (`paper.tex:L66-71`) asserts: for coprime $b, c \ge 2$, every integer $d \ge 0$, and every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists $M = M(b, c, d, \epsilon)$ such that for all $T \ge 1$ and all reduced $y = a/Q$ with $Q \mid c^j$ (some $j \ge 0$) and $M \le Q \le T^d$,
$$
\left|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1} e(b^i y)\right| \le \epsilon.
$$
This matches `NormalityChecker.SmoothOrbitEstimate` (`NormalityChecker/SmoothOrbit.lean:L16-18`) and `fixed_support_orbit_estimate` (`SmoothOrbit.lean:L207`). The case $d = 0$ is correctly handled as vacuous with $M = 2$ (p. 3, line 102).

**Even denominator support ($2 \mid c$).** Answering Question 1 of `REVIEW.md`: **yes, even support is covered throughout without gap.**
- Taking $r = \varphi(c^2)$ and $C = b^r - 1$ guarantees $c^2 \mid C$ by Euler's theorem (since $\gcd(b, c^2) = 1$), so $p \mid C$ for every prime $p \mid c$ and in particular $4 \mid C$ whenever $2 \mid c$.
- The lifting-the-exponent identity $v_p(b^{rm} - 1) = v_p(C) + v_p(m)$ holds at $p = 2$ because $4 \mid C$ places us in the $v_2(z) \ge 2$ regime where $v_2((1+z)^2 - 1) = v_2(2z + z^2) = v_2(z) + 1$, and raising $1+z$ to an odd exponent preserves $v_2$.
- For any $Q > 1$ supported on $c$, $Q \mid b^{r v_Q} - 1$ implies $v_p(Q) \le v_p(C) + v_p(v_Q)$ for all $p \mid Q$, so $Q \mid C v_Q$ and $Q/C \le v_Q \le Q$. (At the correlation divisor $g = \gcd(Q, b^{(l-j)v} - 1)$ on p. 3, $g \mid Q$ inherits support on $c$ and $g \ge D \ge K > 1$, so $g \le C v_g \le C(l-j)v \le C H c K$ applies directly, including when $2 \mid g$.)

**Complete-period bound and induction step.**
- Character orthogonality gives $\sum_{a \bmod Q} |S(a)|^2 = Q v_Q$, and for $\gcd(a, Q) = 1$, $S$ is constant on the $v_Q$-element coset $aG$, so $|S(a)| \le \sqrt{Q}$. Splitting $[0, T)$ into $\lfloor T/v_Q \rfloor$ complete periods and a remainder $< v_Q$ yields
  $$
  \frac{1}{T}\left|\sum_{i<T} e(b^i y)\right| \le \frac{\sqrt{Q}}{v_Q} + \frac{v_Q}{T} \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{Q}} + \frac{Q}{T},
  $$
  confirming eq. (2). (This paper argument is slightly cleaner than Lean's `long_orbit` in `SmoothOrbit.lean:L85-125`, which relies on `unit_orbit_bound`.)
- In the induction on $s \ge 2$ (uniform convergence to $0$ as $K, Q \to \infty$ with $T \ge K^3$, $Q \le K^s$): the base case $s = 2$ follows immediately from (2) since $Q/T \le 1/K$. For $s \to s+1$, $Q \le K^s$ is already covered by the induction hypothesis (worth stating explicitly in one clause), while for $K^s < Q \le K^{s+1}$ multiplying prime factors of $Q$ until first reaching $K$ produces $D \mid Q$ with $K \le D \le cK$ (every prime factor of $Q$ is $\le c$).
- With $v = v_D \le D \le cK$ and $0 \le j < l < H$, $(b^{lv} - b^{jv})y = b^{jv}(b^{(l-j)v} - 1)y$ has reduced denominator $Q/g$ where $g = \gcd(Q, b^{(l-j)v} - 1)$ (using $\gcd(b, Q) = 1$). Since $D \mid g \le CHcK$, we get $Q/g \le K^s$ and $Q/g > K^{s-1}/(CHc) \to \infty$ as $K \to \infty$ for fixed $H$ (because $s \ge 2$). The shift error is at most $2Hv/T \le 2Hc/K^2 \to 0$, and Cauchy--Schwarz gives the displayed bound $H^{-1} + 2H^{-2}\sum_{0\le j < l < H} |T^{-1}\sum_{i<T} z_{i+lv}\overline{z_{i+jv}}|$. Taking $\limsup$ as $K, Q \to \infty$ and then $H \to \infty$ closes the induction.
- Finally, for $d \ge 1$ and $K = \lfloor T^{1/3} \rfloor$, $Q \le T^d$ and $Q \to \infty$ force $T \to \infty$ and $K \to \infty$, with $T \ge K^3$ and $T < (K+1)^3 \le 8K^3 \le K^4$ for $K \ge 8$, so $T^d \le K^{4d}$. Everything is airtight.

### 1.2 Mean rational approximation and exceptional blocks (Section 1, Theorem 1; proof in Section 2)

**Quantifiers and vacuity.** Theorem 1 (`paper.tex:L46-57`) fixes $b, c \ge 2$ coprime and $L \ge 2, B \ge 1, d \ge 0$ before $u \in \{1, \dots, LB-1\}$, $M \ge 1$, and $\eta > 0$, then requires the block condition for all sufficiently large $k$. This matches `normal_of_approximate_arithmetic_certificate` (`ArithmeticCertificate.lean:L62-69`) composed with `fixed_support_orbit_estimate` (verified in `Check.lean:L11-21`). When $B = 1$ or $d = 0$, $T^d = 1$ makes $M \le Q \le T^d$ unsatisfiable for $M = 2$ and $\eta = 1/2$ (since at least one start is non-exceptional), so Theorem 1 is vacuously true, as correctly noted on p. 1 (line 59).

**Transfer to all prefixes.** Answering the first half of Question 2 in `REVIEW.md`: **yes, all prefix lengths are controlled under the stated quantifier order.**
- For fixed $h \ne 0$, $\operatorname{den}(hy) = Q/\gcd(Q, |h|) \in [Q/|h|, Q]$ stays supported on $c$ and $\le T^d$. Choosing the denominator lower bound at least $|h| M(b, c, d, \eta)$ gives $|\sum_{i<T} e(h b^i y)| \le \eta T$.
- The circle Lipschitz inequality $|e(hs) - e(ht)| = 2|\sin(\pi h \|s-t\|_{\mathbb T})| \le 2\pi |h| \|s-t\|_{\mathbb T}$ yields eq. (3): at any cut $0 \le t \le mT$, summing over $\lfloor t/T \rfloor \le m$ full blocks (at most $|E|$ exceptional, each $\le T$) plus one remainder block of length $< T$ gives
  $$
  \left|\sum_{i<t} e(h P_{uA+i})\right| \le (\epsilon + 2\pi|h|\delta)mT + (|E|+1)T.
  $$
- Taking $\epsilon = \delta = \eta$ and $|E| \le \eta m$, dividing by $A = mT$ gives $(2 + 2\pi|h|)\eta + L^{-k}$. Since $L \ge 2$, $L^{-k} \to 0$, so every partial cell sum in $[uA, (u+1)A)$ is bounded by $\rho A$ uniformly over the finitely many $u \in \{1,\dots,LB-1\}$ for all $k \ge k_0(\rho, h)$.
- For any prefix $[0, N)$ ending in $[A, (LB)A)$ with $A = (LB)^k$, each shell $j \in [k_0, k]$ contributes at most $LB - 1$ cells (completed or partial), totaling at most
  $$
  \rho(LB-1)\sum_{j=0}^{k} (LB)^j = \rho\big((LB)^{k+1} - 1\big) \le \rho LB\, A.
  $$
  Adding the fixed constant from $[0, (LB)^{k_0})$, dividing by $N \ge A$, letting $N \to \infty$ and then $\rho \to 0$ drives the character means of $P_n$ to $0$. The real tail $b^n x - P_n \to 0$ transfers this to $\{b^n x\}$ via Cesàro means and the Lipschitz bound, and Weyl's criterion concludes normality. Complete and rigorous.

### 1.4 Joint prime tests, two-prime logarithmic density, and the 5/13 example (Section 4, Theorem 5)

**Theorem 5 (`paper.tex:L168-183`).** Answering the second half of Question 2 in `REVIEW.md`: **no omitted insertion can affect a retained block or its common denominator.**
- **Residue test (eq. (5)):** For $p \in J$ ($p \nmid b$), $L = L_p$, $k = k_p(n) = \lfloor \log_p n \rfloor$, writing $m = p^{k-i} r$ ($p \nmid r$) yields $p^{ks} b^{-n} R_n = \sum_{0\le i \le k} p^{is} \sum_{r \le p^i t_p(n)} r^{-s} b^{-p^{k-i} r}$. Since $w_p = (b^{-1})^{p^{L-1}} \bmod p^L$ satisfies $w_p^p \equiv w_p \pmod{p^L}$ by Euler's theorem, whenever $k \ge 2(L-1)$ every row with $is < L$ has $k - i \ge L - 1$ (since $s \ge 1 \implies i \le L-1$), so $b^{-p^{k-i} r} \equiv w_p^r \pmod{p^L}$, while rows with $is \ge L$ vanish mod $p^L$. Nonvanishing of $U_p(t_p(n)) \bmod p^L$ implies $v_p(p^{ks} b^{-n} R_n) \le L - 1$, hence $v_p(\operatorname{den} R_n) \ge ks - L + 1$ for $ks \ge L - 1$.
- **Block partitioning and denominator bound:** For $T = \lfloor \sqrt{N} \rfloor$, discarding the remainder, $[0, T)$, and every block $[a, a+T)$ for which $[a-T, a+T]$ meets $\mathcal M(S)$ discards at most $3$ full blocks per $m \in \mathcal M(S)$ (since $m \in [(j-1)T, (j+1)T]$ hits at most $3$ integers $j$, attained when $T \mid m$). Up to $N + T$, $|\mathcal M(S) \cap [1, N+T]| \le (1 + \log_2(N+T))^{|S|}$, so discarded positions total $O(\sqrt{N} (\log N)^{|S|}) = o(N)$. On any retained block $[a, a+T)$, $(a, a+T]$ avoids $\mathcal M(S)$, giving $R_{a+i} = b^i R_a$ for $i < T$; and every $m \in \mathcal M(S)$ with $m \le a$ satisfies $m < a - T$, i.e., $a - m > T$. With $c = \prod_{p \in S, p \nmid b} p \ge 2$ (since $J$ is nonempty) and $H = \lceil \log_2(N+T) \rceil$, every $m \le a$ in $\mathcal M(S)$ has exponent vector bounded by $H$, hence $m \mid (bc)^H$ (because distinct primes of $S$ dividing $b$ multiply to a divisor of $b$) and $m^s \mid b^{sH} c^{sH}$. Once $T \ge sH$, the shift factor $b^{a-m}$ clears $b^{sH}$, so $\operatorname{den} R_a \mid c^{sH}$. Since $H \sim 2\log_2 T$, $c^{sH} \le T^d$ holds for any fixed integer $d > 2 s \log_2 c$ and all large $N$.
- **Uniform discrepancy on passing blocks:** If $[a, a+T)$ contains a passing position $a+i$, then $\operatorname{den} R_{a+i} \mid \operatorname{den} R_a$ forces $\operatorname{den} R_a \to \infty$ uniformly in $N$. The contradiction argument via Theorem 2, trigonometric polynomial approximation on $\mathbb T$, and arbitrarily fine mesh bracketing correctly yields uniform convergence of block discrepancies to $0$, and Proposition 3 finishes (6).

**Two primes on logarithmic scales (`paper.tex:L185-193`).**
- Writing $y = e^z$ maps $\frac{1}{\log N}\int_1^N \mathbf 1_{\widetilde B_p \cap \widetilde B_q}(y)\,dy/y$ to a continuous-time average on $\mathbb T^2$ along $(z/\log p, z/\log q) \bmod 1$, which is equidistributed since $\log p / \log q \notin \mathbb Q$, giving limit $a_p a_q$.
- Comparing the integral to $\sum_{1\le n < N, n \in B_p \cap B_q} 1/n$: unit intervals $[n, n+1)$ free of band endpoints incur $O(1/n^2)$ (summable), while intervals containing an endpoint incur $O(1/n)$ along finitely many geometric progressions (also summable), so the difference is $O(1)$ and vanishes after dividing by $\log N$.
- Partial summation gives $a_p a_q \le \overline d(B_p \cap B_q)$, proving that if both primes retain positive limiting logarithmic mass as precisions increase, the right-hand side of (6) stays strictly positive. Conversely, $|Z_p| \le p(\log p) a_p$ and summing $p$-shells gives $\overline d(B_p) \le p|Z_p|/(p-1)$, so $a_p \to 0$ makes the single-prime test suffice.

**Worked binary example ($b = 2, S = \{2, 5, 13\}, s = 1$, `paper.tex:L194-236`).**
- **Precision-1 residues and single-prime bounds:** Independently verified: $Z_5 = [2, 4)$ (partial sums $3, 0, 4 \bmod 5$) with $\overline d(B_5) = 5/8$; $Z_{13} = [10, 13)$ (partial sums $7, 12, 11, 8, 10, 0 \bmod 13$) with $\overline d(B_{13}) = 1/4$.
- **Peak exhaustion and cutoff error (Question 3 of `REVIEW.md`):** **Yes, mathematically the two families of right endpoints exhaust all possible prefix peaks, including cuts inside the newest shell, and the cutoff $\tau$ yields the stated absolute error $m_\tau \le D_{\rm fail} \le m_\tau + \tau$.**

  - On any interval free of endpoints of $\mathcal F = \mathcal F_5 \cap \mathcal F_{13}$, the accumulated fraction $f(x) = |\mathcal F \cap (0, x)|/x$ is nonincreasing outside $\mathcal F$ and nondecreasing inside $\mathcal F$. Hence any cut inside a shell can be slid to a right endpoint of a component of $\mathcal F$ without decreasing $f(x)$, and every right endpoint of $\mathcal F$ is a right endpoint of either $\mathcal F_5$ or $\mathcal F_{13}$.
  - **Wording defect in `paper.tex:L197` (PDF p. 6):** The manuscript writes *"The accumulated fraction can attain a local maximum at a right endpoint of either family."* This omits the crucial word **"only"** (as written, it states possibility rather than exhaustion).
  - After rescaling a right endpoint $E = e p^{j_0}$ to $1$, no $p$-copy with $j < 0$ enters $(0, 1)$ (since $pa/e \ge 1$), and writing $u = q^{\lfloor \log_q E \rfloor}/E \in (1/q, 1]$, no $q$-copy $u q^{-m}[a', e')$ with $m < 0$ enters $(0, 1)$ (since $u q^{-m} a' \ge a' \ge 1$). Continuity of the clipped piecewise-affine intersection length on $[1/q, 1]$ and density of $\{u(e p^j)\}$ (by irrationality of $\log p / \log q$) give $m_\tau \le D_{\rm fail} \le m_\tau + \tau$ rigorously.

  - Anchor 13 maximum: $m_\tau = 24154414499397933 / 103559564189453125 \approx 0.2332417550079$ (at $u = 5/13$).

### 1.5 Separation and overlap rigidity (Section 5, Theorem 6 and Corollary 7)

- **Theorem 6 (`paper.tex:L238-251`).** Completely rigorous. With $w = y - z$, $Q = \operatorname{den} w > 1$ divides $\operatorname{den}(y)\operatorname{den}(z) \le T^{2d}$ and stays supported on $c$. If $Q \ge M(b, c, 2d, 1/2)$, then $T/2 \le |T - \sum_{i<T} e(b^i w)| \le 2\pi \sum_{i<T} \|b^i w\|_{\mathbb T}$ gives mean distance $\ge 1/(4\pi)$; if $1 < Q < M$, $\gcd(b, Q) = 1$ keeps $\operatorname{den}(b^i w) = Q$, giving pointwise distance $\ge 1/Q > 1/M$. Circle triangle inequality through $f_i$ yields $\gamma = \min(1/M, 1/(4\pi))$. Matches `smooth_overlap_separation` (`OrbitRigidity.lean:L32`, where Lean uses $1/(M+1)$ since $M$ is not forced $\ge 2$).
- **Corollary 7 (`paper.tex:L253-258`).** Mathematically sound and matches `smooth_overlap_rigidity` (`OrbitRigidity.lean:L83`) and `overlap_chain_denominator` (`OrbitRigidity.lean:L101`). Note that the statement on p. 8 mixes a proof directive (*"apply this assertion to the aligned pair $b^{r_j} y_j, y_{j+1}$"*) into the italicized corollary text (editorial fix in Section 4).
- **Three necessity examples (`paper.tex:L262`, PDF p. 8):**
  1. Radix factor: $0$ and $1/b$ give mean distance $1/(bT)$ (since $\|1/b\|_{\mathbb T} = 1/b$ for $b \ge 2$). Exact.
  2. No polynomial size bound: $0$ and $c^{-T^2}$ have mean distance $\le b^T c^{-T^2} \to 0$. Exact.
  3. Changing prime support with $d = 1$: $Q_m = b^m - 1$, models $0$ and $1/Q_m$, $T = m Q_m$. Each period has sum $\le \sum_{j<m} b^j / Q_m = 1/(b-1)$, so the mean is $\le 1/(m(b-1)) \to 0$ and the character mean $T^{-1}\sum_{i<T} e(b^i/Q_m) \to 1$. **Minor edge-case slip:** when $b = 2$ and $m = 1$, $Q_1 = 1$, so $1/Q_1 \in \mathbb Z$; specifying $m \ge 2$ makes $Q_m > 1$ for all $b \ge 2$.

### 1.6 Counting obstruction and metric passage (Section 6, Theorem 8)

**Finite word count and measure bound (eq. (8), `paper.tex:L267-282`).**
- For fixed $y \in \mathbb Q$, $z = \{b^n x - y\}$ is Lebesgue-uniform on $[0, 1)$ because $x \mapsto \{b^n x\}$ and mod-1 translation both preserve $\lambda$.
- Taking $a = b^2 \ge 4$ and $J = \lfloor T/2 \rfloor$, the first $J$ base-$a$ digits of $z$ are i.i.d. uniform on $\{0,\dots,a-1\}$. If digit $j+1$ ($0 \le j < J$) equals $1$, then $\{b^{2j} z\} \in [1/a, 2/a)$ (using the terminating expansion convention from Section 1, or outside the null set of $a$-adic rationals), so $\|b^{2j} z\|_{\mathbb T} \ge 1/a$ (since $2/a \le 1/2$). Since $2(J-1) \le T-2 < T$, the condition $T^{-1}\sum_{i<T} \|b^i z\|_{\mathbb T} \le \eta_b = 1/(16 a^2)$ permits at most $K = \lfloor a \eta_b T \rfloor = \lfloor T/(16 a) \rfloor$ occurrences of digit $1$. (Note $K \le T/64 < T/3 \le J$ for $T \ge 2$, so $J - K \ge 0$ always.)
- Weighting digit $1$ by $1$ and all other $a-1$ digits by $2$ gives total weight $(2a-1)^J$ and weight $\ge 2^{J-K}$ per qualifying word (verified in Lean as `few_symbol_words`, `ModelCounting.lean:L14`), yielding probability $\le 2^K (1 - 1/(2a))^J \le \exp(K \log 2 - J/(2a))$. Since $T \le 3J$ for all $T \ge 2$, $K \log 2 \le 3J \log 2 / (16 a) < J/(4a)$, giving $\exp(-J/(4a))$.
- The number of rationals modulo $1$ with $\operatorname{den} y \le T^d$ is $\sum_{q \le T^d} \varphi(q) \le \sum_{q \le T^d} q = T^d(T^d+1)/2 \le T^{2d}$ (true for all $T \ge 1, d \ge 0$, including $d = 0$). Union bound gives eq. (8).

**Borel--Cantelli and passage to Theorem 1 (`paper.tex:L272, 281-283`).**
- For $T_k = B^k$ ($B \ge 2$) and $O(T_k^A)$ prescribed starts per scale, $\sum_{k \ge 1} T_k^{A+2d} \exp(-\lfloor B^k/2 \rfloor / (4a)) < \infty$, so the first Borel--Cantelli lemma (needing no independence) implies that almost every $x \in [0,1)$ escapes all prescribed starts for all $k \ge k_0(x)$.
- **Subtlety in the remark connecting Theorem 8 to Theorem 1 (line 283):** The deduction that numbers satisfying Theorem 1 form a Lebesgue-null set is **true**, but two small bridging facts are left implicit and should be stated in one sentence:
  1. Theorem 1 permits $B = 1$ (vacuous, hence empty/null), whereas for $B \ge 2$ the $(LB-1)L^k$ starts satisfy $(LB-1)L^k = O(T_k^A)$ with $A = \lceil \log_B L \rceil$.
  2.
