## Independent proof audit

- **Theorems 1.1 and 1.2** (`paper.tex` lines 28–99). For $p\mid c$, the first binomial term shows $v_p((1+z)^p-1)=v_p(z)+1$ when $p\mid z$ and, at $p=2$, $4\mid z$. Euler's theorem ensures the latter with $r=\varphi(c^2)$. Thus $Q\mid C v_Q$. Additive Parseval gives $\sum_a|S(a)|^2=Qv_Q$. Invariance on the unit coset $aG$ divides out $v_Q$ and yields $|S(a)|\le\sqrt Q$. Complete periods and the remainder cost at most $C/\sqrt Q+Q/T$. For the induction, choose the divisor $D\in[K,cK]$. The correlation denominator lies in $(K^{s-1}/(CHc),K^s]$, since $D\mid g$ and $g\le C(l-j)v_D$. For fixed $H$, uniform induction applies to each correlation once the lower endpoint exceeds its floor, and the translation cost is at most $2Hc/K^2$. This gives uniform cancellation in the stated range. For the main criterion the character $h\ne0$ divides the denominator by at most $|h|$. The good-block cost is $(1+2\pi|h|)\eta T$ and the omitted cost is at most $(\eta m+1)T$. Summing the resulting $o(A)$ cell bounds over geometric shells remains $o(N)$ at arbitrary cutoffs. The tail errors vanish in Cesàro mean and Weyl's criterion applies.

- **Proposition 2.1 and the mesh bound** (lines 102–123). The covered and uncovered costs are $\delta G$ and $N-G$, respectively, before dividing by $N$. For the last clause they are $o(N)$ and $N-|F_N|$. A finite list of grid unions provides cutoff-uniform frequency control, and an inner or outer grid approximation costs at most $2/q$ in length. The paper covers arbitrary observations. The pinned specialization concerns actual radix shifts.

- **Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2** (lines 125–154). Subtracting the models gives denominator $Q>1$ dividing the product of their denominators. For $Q\ge M$, reverse triangle and $|1-e(t)|\le2\pi\|t\|_{\mathbb T}$ imply mean distance at least $1/(4\pi)$. For $Q<M$, every orbit point retains denominator $Q$ and is at least $1/Q$ from an integer. These estimates are uniform in the observations and apply after alignment because powers of $b$ preserve a coprime denominator. The overlap length and mean error hypotheses suffice for eventual equality modulo one. The three examples isolate coprimality, the polynomial cap, and the fixed prime support.

- **Theorem 4.1** (lines 156–176). The transformation $x\mapsto\{b^nx-y\}$ preserves uniform measure. Among the first $J=\lfloor T/2\rfloor$ base-$b^2$ digits, every selected occurrence costs at least $1/b^2$, because $b^2\ge4$. Thus $K\le3J/(16b^2)$, and $K\log2-J/(2b^2)\le-J/(4b^2)$. There are at most $T^{2d}$ possible values of $y\bmod1$. The polynomial factor in the number of prescribed starts is summable against the exponential on $T=B^k$. Together with the tail hypothesis, a countable union over the integer schedules indeed gives the asserted null set of certificates. The counting argument does not require coprimality.

- **Propositions 5.1 and 5.2** (lines 178–212). In each group of $r$-bit integers the offset periods give total error at most $2^{r+1}$, and the boundary starts contribute at most $(k-1)m$. The floor adjustment at line 195 changes only the dyadic component and places both numbers compared for approximation in one half-open mesh cell. The logarithmic construction changes at most $O_k(J\log J)$ harmonic mass through any cutoff whose latest edited interval is $J$, against order $2^J$ total mass. At the ends of the zeroed intervals the ordinary $0^k$ proportion approaches one. The given conclusion for that construction is a limsup.
