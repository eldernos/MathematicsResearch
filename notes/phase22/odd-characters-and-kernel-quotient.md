# Odd-order characters and the radix-power kernel quotient

Phase 22, Track B, sub-tracks B3 and B4. Fix an integer radix $b\ge2$. Notation follows Phase 21 ([character-q-digamma.md](../phase21/character-q-digamma.md), [classical-barriers.md](../phase21/classical-barriers.md)). $D_N(x;b)$ is the extreme discrepancy of $\{b^nx\}$, $0\le n<N$, over intervals $[\alpha,\beta)\subseteq[0,1)$. $K_b=\{z:D_N(z;b)\to1\}$, $F_b(a)=\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)b^{-n}$, $\|t\|$ is the distance to the nearest integer and $\tau(n)$ the number of divisors. The base-$b$ digits of $x$ are $d_n(x)=\lfloor b\{b^{n-1}x\}\rfloor$, so the block $d_{N+1}\cdots d_{N+W}$ is the $W$-digit expansion of $\lfloor b^W\{b^Nx\}\rfloor$. A real number is **disjunctive in base $b$** if every finite word over $\{0,\dots,b-1\}$ occurs in its digit sequence.

# Part I. B3 — the cubic character modulo 7

## 1. Local structure

Let $\chi$ be the character modulo 7 with $\chi(3)=\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. The powers of the primitive root 3 modulo 7 are $1,3,2,6,4,5$, so

```math
\chi=1\ \text{on}\ \{1,6\},\qquad \chi=\omega\ \text{on}\ \{3,4\},\qquad \chi=\omega^2\ \text{on}\ \{2,5\}.
```

Every cubic character is even: $\chi(-1)^2=1$ and $\chi(-1)^3=1$ force $\chi(-1)=1$; here $\chi(6)=\omega^3=1$. The two real integer weights spanned by $\chi,\bar\chi$ are

```math
w_+=\chi+\bar\chi:\ \ 2\ \text{on}\ \{1,6\},\ -1\ \text{on}\ \{2,3,4,5\};\qquad
w_-=\frac{\chi-\bar\chi}{i\sqrt3}:\ \ 0\ \text{on}\ \{1,6\},\ +1\ \text{on}\ \{3,4\},\ -1\ \text{on}\ \{2,5\},
```

both $0$ on $7\mathbb Z$, both even, both with zero sum over a period. Put $c=1*\chi$, with values in $\mathbb Z[\omega]$, and write $c(n)=x(n)+y(n)\omega$ with integers $x,y$. Since $\operatorname{Re}(x+y\omega)=x-y/2$ and $\operatorname{Im}(x+y\omega)=\frac{\sqrt3}2y$,

```math
c_+:=1*w_+=2\operatorname{Re}c=2x-y,\qquad c_-:=1*w_-=\tfrac2{\sqrt3}\operatorname{Im}c=y,
```

and $L_\pm(b)=\sum_{m\ge1}w_\pm(m)/(b^m-1)=F_b(c_\pm)$. Equivalently $L_\chi(b)=F_b(c)=\frac12L_+(b)+\frac{\sqrt3}2iL_-(b)$, and $c_+=3\tau_{\pm1}-\tau_{7\nmid}$, where $\tau_{\pm1}(n)$ counts divisors $\equiv\pm1\pmod7$ and $\tau_{7\nmid}(n)$ divisors prime to 7.

The primes with $\chi(p)=1$ are those $\equiv\pm1\pmod7$, which split completely in the cyclic cubic field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+=\mathbb Q(\cos\frac{2\pi}7)$; primes $\equiv2,3,4,5$ are inert and 7 ramifies. The Dirichlet series of $c$ is $\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)$, and $\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)L(s,\bar\chi)$ is the Dedekind zeta function of that field. Neither fact is used below.

**Notation.** $\mathcal P'$ is the set of primes $\equiv2,3,4,5\pmod7$, split as $\mathcal P'_+$ ($\equiv3,4$, where $\chi=\omega$) and $\mathcal P'_-$ ($\equiv2,5$, where $\chi=\omega^2$). For $n\ge1$ put

```math
\mathcal S=\{n:\ v_p(n)\not\equiv2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)\ \text{for every}\ p\in\mathcal P'\},\qquad
\tau_1(n)=\prod_{p\equiv\pm1\,(7)}\bigl(v_p(n)+1\bigr),
```

```math
t(n)=\#\{p\in\mathcal P'_+:\ v_p(n)\equiv1\ (3)\}-\#\{p\in\mathcal P'_-:\ v_p(n)\equiv1\ (3)\}\in\mathbb Z/6 .
```

**Lemma 1.1 (local values).** $c(7^e)=1$, and $c(p^e)=e+1$ for $p\equiv\pm1\pmod 7$. For $p\in\mathcal P'_+$, $c(p^e)$ equals $1$, $1+\omega=e^{i\pi/3}$, $0$ according as $e\equiv0,1,2\pmod3$. For $p\in\mathcal P'_-$ it equals $1$, $1+\omega^2=e^{-i\pi/3}$, $0$.

*Proof.* $c(p^e)=\sum_{j=0}^e\chi(p)^j$ (Phase 21 Lemma 1.1), with $1+\omega=-\omega^2=e^{i\pi/3}$ and $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$. ∎

**Lemma 1.2 (closed form).** For every $n\ge1$, $c(n)=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S}(n)\,\tau_1(n)\,e^{i\pi t(n)/3}$. Hence

```math
c_\pm(n)=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S}(n)\,\tau_1(n)\,\varepsilon_\pm(t(n)),
```

| $t \bmod 6$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\varepsilon_+(t)=2\cos\frac{\pi t}3$ | 2 | 1 | −1 | −2 | −1 | 1 |
| $\varepsilon_-(t)=\frac2{\sqrt3}\sin\frac{\pi t}3$ | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | −1 | −1 |

*Proof.* $c$ is multiplicative; multiply the local values of Lemma 1.1. ∎

Consequences used below:

1. $c_+(n)\ne0\iff c(n)\ne0\iff n\in\mathcal S$;
2. $c_-(n)\ne0\iff n\in\mathcal S$ and $t(n)\not\equiv0\pmod3$;
3. on $\mathcal S$, $|c(n)|=\tau_1(n)$, $|c(n)|\le|c_+(n)|\le2|c(n)|$ and $|c_-(n)|\le|c(n)|$ (since $|\varepsilon_-|\le1$);
4. $|c_\pm(n)|\le2\tau(n)$.

Item 1 contains the Phase 21 observation that $c_+$ has no zero on squarefree $n$ prime to 7. The script checks Lemma 1.2 against the divisor sums of $w_\pm$ for every $n\le5\cdot10^4$, and the local values of Lemma 1.1 exactly in $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ at 169 prime powers.

## 2. Value sets

**Proposition 2.1.** $c_+$ and $c_-$ both map $\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$ onto $\mathbb Z$. Explicitly, for every $V\ge1$ and every prime $u\in\mathcal P'_+$ (so $u\ne13$):

```math
c_+(13^{V-1}u)=c_-(13^{V-1}u)=V,\qquad c_+(3\cdot11\cdot13^{V-1})=-V,\qquad c_-(2\cdot13^{V-1})=-V,
```

and $c_+(4)=c_-(1)=0$.

*Proof.* $13\equiv6\pmod7$, so $c(13^{V-1})=V$. Then $c(13^{V-1}u)=V(1+\omega)$, that is $(x,y)=(V,V)$, with $2x-y=y=V$. Next $c(3\cdot11\cdot13^{V-1})=V(1+\omega)^2=V\omega$, so $c_+=-V$. Also $c(2\cdot13^{V-1})=V(1+\omega^2)=-V\omega$, so $c_-=-V$. Finally $c(4)=0$ and $c(1)=1$. ∎

The first family, with $u$ running over primes, is the input to Theorem 5.3.

**Lemma 2.2 (least witnesses).** For $T\ge1$ and $j\in\mathbb Z/6$ the least $n\in\mathcal S$ with $\tau_1(n)=T$ and $t(n)\equiv j$ is $K(T)S(j)$. Here $K(T)$ is the minimum, over factorizations $T=f_1\cdots f_r$ with $f_1\ge\dots\ge f_r\ge2$, of $\prod_i\ell_i^{f_i-1}$, where $\ell_1<\ell_2<\cdots$ are the primes $\equiv\pm1\pmod7$ ($13,29,41,43,\dots$). And $S(j)$ is the least product of the $a$ smallest primes of $\mathcal P'_+$ ($3,11,17,31,\dots$) and the $a'$ smallest of $\mathcal P'_-$ ($2,5,19,23,\dots$) over $a-a'\equiv j\pmod6$.

*Proof.* Let $n\in\mathcal S$. Deleting $7^{v_7(n)}$, deleting $p^e$ for $p\in\mathcal P'$ with $e\equiv0\pmod3$, and replacing $p^e$ by $p$ when $e\equiv1\pmod3$ changes neither $\tau_1$ nor $t$ and does not increase $n$. What remains is $n'=K'S'$ with $K'$ supported on primes $\equiv\pm1$ and $S'$ squarefree on $\mathcal P'$. For a fixed multiset of exponents, $\prod\ell_i^{e_i}$ is least when larger exponents go to smaller primes. For fixed counts $(a,a')$ the least $S'$ uses the smallest primes in each class. ∎

Since $c_\pm(n)=\tau_1(n)\varepsilon_\pm(t(n))$, the least $n$ with $c_\pm(n)=M\ne0$ is the minimum of $K(T)S(j)$ over $T\varepsilon_\pm(j)=M$. The script evaluates this for $|M|\le20$, checks it by exhaustion over $n\le10^6$, and confirms each value by a direct divisor sum. For $|M|\le10$:

## 3. Support, phase classes, means and masses

**Theorem 3.1.**

1. $\mathcal S$ has natural density $\delta=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P'}\bigl(1-\frac1{p^2+p+1}\bigr)\in[0.7503279,\,0.7503287]$. Hence $\#\{n\le X:c_+(n)\ne0\}\sim\delta X$.
2. For each $j\in\mathbb Z/6$, $\{n\in\mathcal S:t(n)\equiv j\}$ has density $\delta/6$. Consequently $\operatorname{supp}c_-$ has density $\frac23\delta=0.50021\ldots$; $c_+>0$ and $c_+<0$ each hold on a set of density $\delta/2$; $c_->0$ and $c_-<0$ each on density $\delta/3$.

*Proof.* (1) For $p\in\mathcal P'$ the set $\{n:v_p(n)\equiv2\ (3)\}$ has density $\sum_{j\ge0}(1-\frac1p)p^{-3j-2}=\frac{p-1}{p^3-1}=\frac1{p^2+p+1}$. Let $\mathcal S_y$ impose the condition only for $p\le y$. Up to a set of upper density at most $\sum_{p\le y}p^{-E}$, $\mathcal S_y$ is a union of residue classes modulo $\prod_{p\le y}p^E$, and the conditions at distinct primes are independent by the Chinese remainder theorem. Letting $E\to\infty$ gives $d(\mathcal S_y)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P',\,p\le y}(1-\frac1{p^2+p+1})$. Finally $\mathcal S_y\setminus\mathcal S\subseteq\bigcup_{p>y}\{n:p^2\mid n\}$ has upper density at most $\sum_{p>y}p^{-2}\le1/y$. The numerical enclosure uses all primes up to $10^6$ and the tail factor $\in[1-10^{-6},1]$.

(2) For $k\in\mathbb Z/6$ put $h_k(n)=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal S}(n)e^{i\pi kt(n)/3}$. It is multiplicative, because $t$ is additive over prime powers, and $|h_k|\le1$. Orthogonality on $\mathbb Z/6$ gives

```math
\#\{n\le X:\ n\in\mathcal S,\ t(n)\equiv j\}=\frac16\sum_{k=0}^5e^{-i\pi kj/3}\sum_{n\le X}h_k(n).
```

The term $k=0$ is $\delta X+o(X)$ by (1). For $k\ne0$ we show $\sum_{n\le X}h_k(n)=o(X)$ by Halász's theorem: a multiplicative $f$ with $|f|\le1$ and $\sum_p(1-\operatorname{Re}f(p)p^{-i\theta})/p=\infty$ for every real $\theta$ has mean value 0 (G. Halász, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. **19** (1968), 365–403; Tenenbaum, *Introduction to Analytic and Probabilistic Number Theory*, Ch. III.4). For $p\ne7$, $h_k(p)=e^{i\pi ks(p)/3}$ with $s(p)=+1,-1,0$ on $\mathcal P'_+,\mathcal P'_-$ and the primes $\equiv\pm1$. This depends only on $p\bmod7$, so it is a linear combination of the characters $\psi$ modulo 7.

- At $\theta=0$, Mertens' theorem in progressions modulo 7 gives $\sum_{p\le x}\operatorname{Re}h_k(p)/p=\beta_k\log\log x+O(1)$ with $\beta_k=\frac13\bigl(1+2\cos\frac{\pi k}3\bigr)<1$, so the series diverges.
- At $\theta\ne0$, $\sum_{p\le x}\psi(p)p^{-1-i\theta}$ converges for every $\psi$ modulo 7 (partial summation from the prime number theorem in progressions with error $O(x/(\log x)^2)$). Hence $\sum_{p\le x}(1-\operatorname{Re}h_k(p)p^{-i\theta})/p=\log\log x+O_\theta(1)$.

The sign statements follow from Lemma 1.2 by summing the classes. ∎

*Remark 3.2 (rates).* Write $S_\pm(X)=\#\{n\le X:c_\pm(n)\ne0\}$.

- *$k=2,4$: rate $X^{-1/2}$ (proved).* For $p\ne7$, $h_2(p)=\omega^{s(p)}=\chi(p)$. Comparing local factors gives $h_2=\chi*g_2$, with $g_2$ multiplicative and:
  - $g_2(7^e)=1$;
  - $g_2(p^e)=\mathbf 1_{e=0}$ for $p\equiv\pm1\pmod 7$;
  - for $p\in\mathcal P'_+$, $\sum_eg_2(p^e)x^e=(1-\omega x)\cdot\frac{1+\omega x}{1-x^3}=\frac{1-\omega^2x^2}{1-x^3}$, so $g_2(p^e)=1,0,-\omega^2$ for $e\equiv0,1,2\pmod3$;
  - the complex conjugate on $\mathcal P'_-$.

  Thus $|g_2|\le1$, and $g_2$ is supported on the integers $7^am$ with $m$ squarefull. The partial sums of $\chi$ have modulus at most 1, so

  ```math
  \Bigl|\sum_{n\le X}h_2(n)\Bigr|\le\sum_{d\le X}|g_2(d)|\le\#\{7^am\le X:\ m\ \text{squarefull}\}=O(\sqrt X).
  ```

  Also $h_4=\overline{h_2}$. By Lemma 1.2, $S_-(X)=S_+(X)-\#\{n\le X:n\in\mathcal S,\ 3\mid t(n)\}=\frac23S_+(X)-\frac23\operatorname{Re}\sum_{n\le X}h_2(n)$. Hence

  ```math
  \Bigl|\frac{S_-(X)}{S_+(X)}-\frac23\Bigr|\le\frac{2\,|\sum_{n\le X}h_2(n)|}{3\,S_+(X)}=O(X^{-1/2}).
  ```

  This agrees with Selberg–Delange: at $\beta_2=0$ the leading coefficient carries the factor $1/\Gamma(0)=0$.
- *$k$ odd: rate $(\log X)^{-1/3}$ (heuristic, not used below).* Here $\beta_1=\beta_5=\frac23$ and $\beta_3=-\frac13$. The Selberg–Delange method (Tenenbaum, Ch. II.5) predicts $\sum_{n\le X}h_k(n)\asymp X(\log X)^{\beta_k-1}$. So the six classes, and the sign of $c_+$, should equidistribute only at rate $(\log X)^{-1/3}$.

**Proposition 3.3 (means).** Let $\mu_\pm=\sum_{m\ge1}w_\pm(m)/m$. For every $X\ge1$,

```math
\Bigl|\sum_{n\le X}c_+(n)-\mu_+X\Bigr|\le10\sqrt X,\qquad \Bigl|\sum_{n\le X}c_-(n)-\mu_-X\Bigr|\le5\sqrt X .
```

Here $\mu_+=2\operatorname{Re}L(1,\chi)=1.0754947610\ldots$ and $\mu_-=\frac2{\sqrt3}\operatorname{Im}L(1,\chi)=-0.1215871326\ldots$, so $L(1,\chi)=0.5377473805\ldots-0.1052975456\ldots\,i$. For an even 7-periodic $w$ with zero period sum,

```math
\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{w(m)}m=-\sum_{k=1}^{6}W(k)\log\Bigl(2\sin\frac{\pi k}7\Bigr),\qquad W(k)=\frac17\sum_{r=0}^{6}w(r)\cos\frac{2\pi kr}7 .
```

*Proof.* Let $B=\max_y|\sum_{m\le y}w(m)|$ ($B=2$ for $w_+$, $B=1$ for $w_-$) and $\|w\|_\infty$ ($2$, resp. $1$). With $y=\sqrt X$, $\sum_{n\le X}(1*w)(n)=\sum_{m\le X}w(m)\lfloor X/m\rfloor$. The part $m>y$ equals $\sum_{d<X/y}\sum_{y<m\le X/d}w(m)$, of size at most $2BX/y$. The part $m\le y$ equals $X\sum_{m\le y}w(m)/m$ up to $y\|w\|_\infty$, and $|\sum_{m>y}w(m)/m|\le2B/y$ by Abel summation. In total the error is at most $(4B+\|w\|_\infty)\sqrt X$. For the formula, expand $w(m)=\sum_kW(k)e^{-2\pi ikm/7}$ (with $W(0)=0$) and use $\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-i\phi m}/m=-\log(1-e^{-i\phi})$, whose real part is $-\log(2\sin\frac\phi2)$. The imaginary parts cancel because $W(k)=W(7-k)$ is real. ∎

**Proposition 3.4 (masses).** $\sum_{n\le X}|c_\pm(n)|\asymp X(\log X)^{1/3}$ and $\sum_{n\le X}c_\pm(n)^2\asymp X\log X$.

*Proof.* Phase 21 Proposition 1.4 ($\kappa_3=\frac43$) gives $\sum_{n\le X}|c(n)|\sim C\,X(\log X)^{1/3}$. Wirsing's theorem applies to $|c|^2$: $|c(p)|^2=4$ on primes $\equiv\pm1$ (density $\frac13$) and $1$ on $\mathcal P'$ (density $\frac23$), with mean $2$, and $|c(p^\nu)|^2\le(\nu+1)^2\le8(3/2)^\nu$. So $\sum_{n\le X}|c(n)|^2\sim C'X\log X$. For $c_+$ use item 3 after Lemma 1.2.

For $c_-$ the upper bounds follow from $|c_-|\le|c|$. For the lower bounds define, on $n\in\mathcal S$ with $3\nmid n$, $\iota(n)=n$ if $t(n)\notin\{0,3\}$ and $\iota(n)=3n$ otherwise. Since $3\in\mathcal P'_+$, $3n\in\mathcal S$, $t(3n)=t(n)+1\in\{1,4\}$ and $\tau_1(3n)=\tau_1(n)$. So $|c_-(\iota(n))|=|c(n)|$, and $\iota$ is injective because $3\mid\iota(n)$ exactly in the second case. Hence $\sum_{m\le X}|c_-(m)|^j\ge\sum_{n\le X/3,\,3\nmid n}|c(n)|^j$ for $j=1,2$, and Wirsing applied to $|c|^j\mathbf 1_{3\nmid n}$ gives the matching orders. ∎

## 4. The question, and the shape of the answer

Phase 21 showed that every even-order sector gives sparse support and hence membership in $K_b$, while the odd-order sector has support of positive density (Phase 21 Theorem 3.4). Theorem 3.1 makes the density exact for the cubic character modulo 7. Positive density closes the sparse route, but the zeros are still plentiful and **arithmetically placeable**: by Lemma 1.1 a single prime $p\in\mathcal P'$ with $v_p(n)=2$ kills $c(n)$, whatever the other prime factors are. Combining this with one prime value position and an averaged tail bound gives disjunctivity, but no distributional statement. The method works for every nonprincipal character, so it is stated in that generality.

## 5. Disjunctivity

### 5.1 The digit window

For an integer sequence $a$ with $|a(n)|\le C\tau(n)$ and $x=F_b(a)$ put $T_P=\sum_{k\ge1}a(P+k)b^{-k}$. Then $b^Px\equiv T_P\pmod1$, because the head $\sum_{n\le P}a(n)b^{P-n}$ is an integer.

**Lemma 5.1 (digit window).** Let $N\ge0$, $W\ge1$, $f\ge0$ and $P=N+W+f+1$. Suppose

1. $a(N+i)=0$ for $1\le i\le W+f$;
2. $a(P)=b^fV_1$ with $V_1\in\mathbb Z$, $b\nmid V_1$ and $-b^{W+1}\le V_1<b^{W+1}$;
3. $|T_P|<\frac12$.

Then $d_{N+1}(x)\cdots d_{N+W}(x)$ is the $W$-digit base-$b$ expansion of $\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor\bmod b^W$.

*Proof.* By (1) and (2), $T_N=b^{-(W+f+1)}(b^fV_1+T_P)$, so $b^WT_N=(V_1+\vartheta)/b$ with $\vartheta=b^{-f}T_P$ and $|\vartheta|<\frac12$. Write $V_1=b\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor+\rho$ with $1\le\rho\le b-1$. Then $V_1+\vartheta\in(b\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor,\,b\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor+b)$, so $\lfloor b^WT_N\rfloor=M_1:=\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor\in[-b^W,b^W)$. If $M_1\ge0$ then $T_N\in[0,1)$ and $\{b^Nx\}=T_N$. If $M_1<0$ then $T_N\in[-1,0)$ and $\{b^Nx\}=T_N+1$. In either case $\lfloor b^W\{b^Nx\}\rfloor\equiv M_1\pmod{b^W}$ and lies in $[0,b^W)$. ∎

The script checks the case $f=0$ at every admissible position $N$ of the certified expansions of $L_\pm(2)$ and $L_\pm(3)$, for $W\le3$: 1196, 8318, 1202 and 4368 positions, with no exception.

### 5.2 Two inputs

**Lemma 5.2 (divisor sums in a progression).** For integers $D\ge1$ and $c$, a real number $X\ge1$, and $g=\gcd(c,D)$,

```math
\sum_{\substack{1\le m\le X\\ m\equiv c\ (D)}}\tau(m)\le2\sqrt X+\frac{2X}D\,\tau(g)\,(1+\log X).
```

*Proof.* $\tau(m)\le2\#\{d\mid m:d\le\sqrt X\}$ for $m\le X$. For fixed $d$, the conditions $m\equiv c\ (D)$ and $d\mid m$ are compatible only if $\gcd(d,D)\mid g$, and then they define one class modulo $dD/\gcd(d,D)$, which contains at most $X\gcd(d,D)/(dD)+1$ integers $\le X$. Hence the sum is at most

```math
2\sqrt X+\frac{2X}D\sum_{e\mid g}\ \sum_{d\le\sqrt X,\ e\mid d}\frac ed\le2\sqrt X+\frac{2X}D\,\tau(g)\Bigl(1+\tfrac12\log X\Bigr).
```

∎

**External input (E1): primes in progressions beyond Siegel–Walfisz.** There are absolute, effectively computable constants $c_0,c_1,C>0$ with the following property. For every $x\ge3$ there is at most one primitive real character $\chi_1$, of conductor $q_1\le T:=\exp(c_0\sqrt{\log x})$, whose $L$-function has a real zero in $[1-c_0/\log T,1)$ (Landau–Page). For every modulus $q'\le T$ that $q_1$ does not divide (or every $q'\le T$, if there is no such $\chi_1$) and every $a$ prime to $q'$,

```math
\Bigl|\psi(x;q',a)-\frac x{\varphi(q')}\Bigr|\le C\,x\exp\bigl(-c_1\sqrt{\log x}\bigr).
```

Sources: Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory* (3rd ed.), Ch. 14 (Page's theorem) and Ch. 20 (the formula for $\psi(x;q,a)$ uniformly in $q\le\exp(C\sqrt{\log x})$, where the exceptional term appears only for moduli divisible by $q_1$); Montgomery–Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory I*, §11.3. A fixed primitive real character $\chi^*$ is eventually not exceptional, since $L(\sigma,\chi^*)\ne0$ on some interval $[1-\delta_{\chi^*},1]$. From (E1), with $\theta(x;q',a)\ge\psi(x;q',a)-2\sqrt x\log^2x$:

```math
\pi(Y;q',a)\ge\frac{Y}{2\varphi(q')\log Y}\qquad\text{whenever }q'\le\min\bigl(Y^{1/3},\exp(\tfrac{c_1}2\sqrt{\log Y}),T\bigr),\ q_1\nmid q',\ Y\ge Y_0 .
```

### 5.3 The theorem

**Theorem 5.3.** Let $\chi$ be a nonprincipal Dirichlet character modulo $q$ of order $h\ge2$, let $\Phi:\mathbb Z[\zeta_h]\to\mathbb Z$ be a nonzero additive map, and put $a(n)=\Phi\bigl((1*\chi)(n)\bigr)$. Then for every base $b\ge2$ the number $x=F_b(a)$ is disjunctive in base $b$. More precisely, for every word $w$ of length $W$ there are $C_w$ and $X_w$ such that, for $X\ge X_w$,

```math
\#\{N\le X:\ d_{N+1}(x)\cdots d_{N+W}(x)=w\}\ \ge\ X\exp\bigl(-C_w(\log\log X)(\log\log\log X)\bigr).
```

**Corollary 5.4.** For every $b\ge2$, $L_+(b)$, $L_-(b)$ and every combination $\alpha L_+(b)+\beta L_-(b)$ with $(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}$ are disjunctive in base $b$, hence irrational. Here $\Phi(x+y\omega)=\alpha(2x-y)+\beta y$. The same holds for $2\operatorname{Re}L_\psi(b)$ and $\frac2{\sqrt3}\operatorname{Im}L_\psi(b)$ for every cubic character $\psi$. It also holds for the single-character instances of Phase 21 Theorem 1.5: for $\chi$ of even order, $\Phi\circ c_\chi$ is an integer-valued linear combination, with coefficients in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_h)$, of the Galois conjugates $c_{\chi^\sigma}$, all of the same even order $h$. Write $\Phi(\alpha)=\sum_\sigma\sigma(\gamma)\sigma(\alpha)$, with $\gamma$ in the inverse different, by nondegeneracy of the trace form. The coefficients need not be integers: for $h=4$ and $\Phi(x+yi)=x$ one gets $\frac12c_\chi+\frac12c_{\bar\chi}$. Phase 21 Theorem 1.5 allows arbitrary complex coefficients when the resulting sequence is integer-valued, so $F_b(\Phi\circ c_\chi)\in K_b$. An example is $\sum_m\chi_{-4}(m)/(b^m-1)=\sum_n\frac{r_2(n)}4b^{-n}$, which lies in $K_b$ and is disjunctive.

*Proof of Theorem 5.3.* Write $c_\chi=1*\chi$ and $C_\Phi=\max_j|\Phi(\zeta_h^j)|$. Then $a(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,(d,q)=1}\Phi(\chi(d))$, so $|a(n)|\le C_\Phi\tau(n)$.

*Step 0 (fixed choices).* Choose a class $g_0$ with $\chi(g_0)$ of exact order $d\ge2$; for every prime $p\equiv g_0\pmod q$, $c_\chi(p^{d-1})=\sum_{i<d}\chi(g_0)^i=0$. Choose a prime $\ell\equiv1\pmod q$, so that $c_\chi(\ell^{e})=e+1$. Next choose $r_0\in(\mathbb Z/q)^\times$ and $A_0$ prime to $q\ell$ with

```math
\kappa:=\Phi\bigl(c_\chi(A_0)\,(1+\chi(r_0))\bigr)\ne0 .
```

This is possible. Take $r_0=1$ and let $A_0$ be a product of $j$ distinct primes $p\equiv g_1\pmod q$, $p\ne\ell$, where $\chi(g_1)=\zeta$ generates the image $\mu_h$; then $c_\chi(A_0)(1+\chi(1))=2(1+\zeta)^j$. If $h\ge3$, $\mathbb Q(1+\zeta)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_h)$, so $\{(1+\zeta)^j:0\le j<\varphi(h)\}$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis. $\Phi$ extends to a nonzero $\mathbb Q$-linear form and is nonzero on some basis vector. If $h=2$, take $j=0$: $\kappa=2\Phi(1)\ne0$. For $L_\pm$ one may take directly $q=7$, $g_0=3$, $d=3$, $\ell=13$, $r_0=3$, $A_0=1$, which gives $\kappa=\Phi_\pm(1+\omega)=1$. Write $\kappa=b^f\kappa_1$ with $b\nmid\kappa_1$.

*Step 1 (the value).* Let $w$ correspond to $0\le M<b^W$. Choose $e\ge0$ with $b^e\ge|\kappa_1|$ and put $W'=W+e$. Among the integers $V_1=\tau\kappa_1$ with $\tau\ge1$ and $\tau\equiv1\pmod b$, which form a progression with difference $b|\kappa_1|\le b^{e+1}$, pick one in the half-open interval

```math
I=\begin{cases}[\,Mb^{e+1},\,(M+1)b^{e+1}) & \kappa_1>0,\\ [\,(Mb^e-b^{W'})b,\,((M+1)b^e-b^{W'})b) & \kappa_1<0,\end{cases}
```

of length $b^{e+1}$. Such a $V_1$ exists: the progression starts at $\kappa_1$, with $|\kappa_1|\le b^e$, either inside $I$ or on the side of $I$ nearer to 0, and moves monotonically away from 0 with step at most $|I|$. Then $b\nmid V_1$, $-b^{W'+1}\le V_1<b^{W'+1}$, and $\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor\bmod b^{W'}\in[Mb^e,(M+1)b^e)$: the $W'$-digit expansion begins with $w$. Put $A=\ell^{\tau-1}A_0$. For every prime $u\equiv r_0\pmod q$ with $u>A$, multiplicativity gives

```math
a(Au)=\Phi\bigl(\tau\,c_\chi(A_0)(1+\chi(r_0))\bigr)=\tau\kappa=b^fV_1 .
```

For $L_\pm$: $e=f=0$, $V_1=\tau=bM+1$ and $A=13^{bM}$.

*Step 2 (parameters).* Let $Y$ be large and let $L=L(Y)$ be the least integer with

```math
b^L\ge5280\,C_\Phi\,\tau(A)\,\tau(q)\,(1+\log3AY)^2,
```

so $L=2\log_b\log Y+O_{w}(1)$. Let $J=\{-(W'+f),\dots,-1\}\cup\{1,\dots,L\}$, $s=|J|=W'+f+L$, and

```math
P_0=L+W'+f+\lceil2s\log_2(d+1)\rceil+A_0+\ell+q .
```

Take the $2s$ smallest primes $\equiv g_0\pmod q$ that exceed $P_0$ and split them into two families $\mathcal P_1,\mathcal P_2$ of $s$ primes each. In each family assign the primes bijectively to the offsets, $j\mapsto p_j$, and put $Q_\nu=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_\nu}p^d$. By the prime number theorem modulo $q$ these primes are $\ll_q(P_0+s)\log(P_0+s)$, so

```math
\log(qQ_\nu)\ll_{q,h}s\log s\ll(\log\log Y)(\log\log\log Y).
```

In particular $qQ_\nu\le\min(Y^{1/3},\exp(\frac{c_1}2\sqrt{\log Y}),T)$ for $Y$ large.

*Step 3 (avoiding the exceptional modulus).* Let $q_1=q_1(Y)$ be the conductor in (E1) for $x=Y$, if it exists. It cannot divide both $qQ_1$ and $qQ_2$: otherwise it divides $q\gcd(Q_1,Q_2)=q$, and the finitely many primitive real characters of conductor dividing $q$ are not exceptional for $Y$ large. Fix $\nu$ with $q_1\nmid q':=qQ_\nu$.

*Step 4 (the residue class).* By the Chinese remainder theorem let $a_0\bmod q'$ satisfy $a_0\equiv r_0\pmod q$ and $Aa_0\equiv p_j^{d-1}-j\pmod{p_j^d}$ for $j\in J$. This is solvable because $p_j\nmid A$ (as $p_j>P_0>A_0,\ell$). The residue $a_0$ is prime to $q'$, since $p_j\nmid j$ ($p_j>P_0>|j|$). For every $u\equiv a_0\pmod{q'}$ and $m_0=Au$,

```math
v_{p_j}(m_0+j)=d-1\ \ (j\in J),\qquad\text{so}\qquad a(m_0+j)=0\ \ (j\in J),
```

because the local factor $c_\chi(p_j^{d-1})$ vanishes.

*Step 5 (the averaged tail).* Put $R(u)=\sum_{k\ge1}\tau(Au+L+k)b^{-k}$. Then $|T_{m_0+L}|\le C_\Phi R(u)$, and the zeros at $m_0+1,\dots,m_0+L$ give $T_{m_0}=b^{-L}T_{m_0+L}$. Fix $k\ge1$ and $H=L+k$. As $u\le Y$ runs over the class $a_0\bmod q'$, $Au+H$ runs injectively over integers $\le AY+H$ in the class $Aa_0+H\bmod Aq'$. Since $\gcd(A,q')=1$, $g_H:=\gcd(Aa_0+H,Aq')$ divides $\gcd(H,A)\gcd(Aa_0+H,q)\gcd(Aa_0+H,Q_\nu)$. Now $Aa_0+H\equiv p_j^{d-1}+(H-j)\pmod{p_j^d}$ and $1\le k\le H-j\le L+k+W'+f$. So $p_j\mid Aa_0+H$ only if $p_j\le L+k+W'+f$, which needs $k\ge K_0:=P_0-L-W'-f\ge2s\log_2(d+1)$. Hence

```math
\tau(g_H)\le\tau(A)\tau(q)\,(d+1)^{E(H)},\qquad E(H)=0\ \text{for}\ k<K_0,\qquad E(H)\le s\ \text{always}.
```

Assume $Y\ge L$. Then $AY+H\le3kAY$ and $1+\log(AY+H)\le(1+\log3AY)\,k$. Lemma 5.2 gives

```math
\sum_{\substack{u\le Y\\ u\equiv a_0\,(q')}}R(u)\le\sum_{k\ge1}b^{-k}\Bigl[2\sqrt3\,k\sqrt{AY}+6k^2\,\frac Y{q'}\,(1+\log3AY)\,\tau(A)\tau(q)(d+1)^{E(L+k)}\Bigr].
```

For $k\ge K_0$, $(d+1)^s\le2^{k/2}\le b^{k/2}$. Using $\sum_k2\sqrt3k2^{-k}=4\sqrt3<7$, $\sum_kk^22^{-k}=6$ and $\sum_kk^22^{-k/2}<48.05$:

```math
\sum_{\substack{u\le Y\\ u\equiv a_0\,(q')}}R(u)\le7\sqrt{AY}+330\,\tau(A)\tau(q)\,\frac Y{q'}\,(1+\log3AY).
```

*Step 6 (counting).* By Markov's inequality, the number of $u\le Y$ in the class with $C_\Phi R(u)\ge b^L/2$ is at most $(2C_\Phi/b^L)$ times the last display. By the choice of $L$ and $q'\le Y^{1/3}$, this is at most $Y/(4q'\log Y)$ for $Y$ large, which is half the lower bound $\pi(Y;q',a_0)\ge Y/(2q'\log Y)$ from (E1). So at least $Y/(4q'\log Y)-A-W'-f-2$ primes $u\in(\max(A,W'+f+1),Y]$, $u\equiv a_0\pmod{q'}$, satisfy $|T_{m_0+L}|<b^L/2$, that is $|T_{m_0}|<\frac12$.

*Step 7 (conclusion).* For each such $u$ put $N=Au-W'-f-1$; then $N\ge u-W'-f-1\ge1$, so every position used is a positive integer. Steps 1 and 4 give the hypotheses of Lemma 5.1 with $W'$ in place of $W$. The digits $N+1,\dots,N+W'$ spell $\lfloor V_1/b\rfloor\bmod b^{W'}$, whose first $W$ digits are $w$. Distinct $u$ give distinct $N\le AY$. With $Y=X/A$ (Lemma 5.2 and (E1) hold for real $Y$) the count is at least $X/(4Aq'\log X)-A-W'-f-2\ge X\exp(-C_w(\log\log X)(\log\log\log X))$. ∎

*Proof of Corollary 5.4.* $\alpha c_++\beta c_-=\Phi\circ c$ with $\Phi(x+y\omega)=\alpha(2x-y)+\beta y$, which is nonzero when $(\alpha,\beta)\ne0$. A disjunctive expansion is not eventually periodic. ∎

### 5.4 Remarks on the proof

1. **Why Siegel–Walfisz is not enough.** The modulus has $\log q'\asymp(\log\log Y)(\log\log\log Y)$, which exceeds $A\log\log Y$ for every fixed $A$. Shrinking $L$ to fit the Siegel–Walfisz range makes $b^L=(\log Y)^{o(1)}$. Heuristically this is below the typical tail size $(\log Y)^{(\log2)/3}$ coming from $\tau_1$, so most candidates $u$ would fail the tail condition.
2. **Why a Siegel-zero loss is not enough.** Keep a possible exceptional zero $\beta_1$ and bound it by the effective estimate $1-\beta_1\gg q_1^{-1/2}(\log q_1)^{-2}$. This yields lower bounds of the type $\pi(x;q',a)\gg x/(\varphi(q')\sqrt{q'}\log x)$ in the relevant range, which lose $\sqrt{q'}=e^{\Theta(L\log L)}$, more than the gain $b^L$ from the zero block. Even Siegel's ineffective bound $1-\beta_1\gg_\varepsilon q_1^{-\varepsilon}$ loses a factor $(q')^{\varepsilon}=e^{\Theta(\varepsilon L\log L)}$, which still exceeds $b^L$. Avoiding the one possible exceptional modulus by two disjoint prime families removes the loss, and no ineffective constant enters.
3. **Only one prime position.** Asking for two prime positions, for example a prescribed value at $m_0$ and the value $1$ at $m_0+1$, would be a twin-prime problem. Lemma 5.1 is arranged so that a single value position with $b\nmid V_1$ suffices, and the sign of the tail is irrelevant.
4. **Both cubic weights at once.** For $L_\pm$ the value position is $13^{bM}u$ with $u\in\mathcal P'_+$ prime, and $c_+=c_-=bM+1$ there (Proposition 2.1). The same occurrence positions serve $L_+$ and $L_-$, whenever both tails are small.
5. **Excluded case.** For the principal character $c_{\chi_0}=\tau$ never vanishes, and the method says nothing about the Erdős–Borwein constants.

## 6. What disjunctivity does not decide

- **Distribution.** Theorem 5.3 counts occurrences in a residue class of density $1/q'=e^{-\Theta((\log\log X)(\log\log\log X))}$. This lower bound is $o(X(\log X)^{-A})$ for every $A$; nothing is claimed about the true number of occurrences. It gives no positive lower word density, so neither $D^+(L_\pm(b);b)<1$ nor $D^-(L_\pm(b);b)>0$, and no simple normality or non-normality statement.
- **Kernel.** Disjunctivity is compatible with membership in $K_b$: Corollary 5.4 applies to the single-character even-order Lambert series of Phase 21 Theorem 1.5, which lie in $K_b$. Whether $L_\pm(b)\in K_b$ remains open; the support has positive density (Theorem 3.1), so the sparse transfer does not apply.
- **Why a fixed zero block does not give density (heuristic).** For fixed $L$, the integers $n$ with $c(n+1)=\dots=c(n+L)=0$ have positive density; they contain a residue class modulo $\prod p_j^3$. A coefficient zero block becomes a digit statement only if the tail $T_{n+L}$ is below $b^L/2$. On a density-one subset of its support $|c(m)|=\tau_1(m)$ is at least $(\log m)^{(\log2)/3-\varepsilon}$ (Hardy–Ramanujan for the split primes). So the tail is small only when roughly the next $\frac{\log2}{3}\log_b\log n$ coefficients also vanish, an event of density $(\log n)^{-c}$. This is why $L$ must grow like $\log_b\log Y$, and why the modulus leaves the range of positive density. We do not claim a proof that no other arrangement gives positive density.

**Finite data** (certified expansions):

- **Words.** All 126 binary words of length $\le6$ occur in the first 49,981 digits of $L_+(2)$ (length-6 counts between 634 and 870) and in the first 49,976 digits of $L_-(2)$ (counts between 456 and 1529). All 120 ternary words of length $\le4$ occur in the first 11,989 digits of $L_+(3)$ and in the first 11,988 digits of $L_-(3)$.
- **Discrepancies.** $D_N(L_+(2);2)=0.0314,0.0297,0.0200,0.0183$, $D_N(L_-(2);2)=0.0943,0.0748,0.0727,0.0832$ and $D_N(L_+(3);3)=0.0843,0.0776,0.0703,0.0632$ at $N=1000,2000,4000,8000$.

These numbers prove nothing asymptotic.

# Part II. B4 — the radix-power tower

Recall (Phase 19, [geometry.md](../phase19/geometry.md#asymptotic-kernel-and-arithmetic-operations)) that $K_b=F_b+M_b$, where now $F_b=\frac1{b-1}\mathbb Z$ and

```math
M_b=\{w\in\mathbb R:\ M_N(w;b)\to0\},\qquad M_N(w;b)=\frac1N\sum_{n<N}\|b^nw\| .
```

$M_b$ is an additive group containing $\mathbb Z$, and $M_b\subseteq K_b$ directly: for $0<\varepsilon<\frac14$, at most $NM_N(w;b)/\varepsilon$ of the points $\{b^nw\}$ lie in $[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)$, so $D_N(w;b)\ge1-2\varepsilon-M_N(w;b)/\varepsilon$. Phase 19 states $M_b=M_{b^h}$ in two sentences. Classical-barriers Proposition 2.2 proves $K_b\subsetneq K_{b^k}$ through $1-D_N(x;b^k)\le k(1-D_{kN}(x;b))$, kernel-checked as `ClassicalBarriers.one_sub_radixDiscrepancy_pow_le`. This part supplies the explicit constants, the quotient, and the base-$b$ statistics of every element of $K_{b^k}$.

## 7. The zero-orbit group does not see the radix power

**Theorem 7.1.** For all $b\ge2$, $k\ge1$, $N\ge1$, $0\le r<k$ and $w\in\mathbb R$:

```math
\text{(i)}\ \ M_N(w;b^k)\le k\,M_{kN}(w;b),\qquad
\text{(ii)}\ \ M_{kN}(w;b)\le\frac{b^k-1}{k(b-1)}\,M_N(w;b^k),
```

```math
\text{(iii)}\ \ M_{kN+r}(w;b)\le\frac{b^k-1}{b-1}\cdot\frac{N+1}{kN+r}\,M_{N+1}(w;b^k).
```

Hence $M_b=M_{b^k}$.

*Proof.* (i) The terms $\|b^{kn}w\|$, $n<N$, are among the terms $\|b^mw\|$, $m<kN$. For (ii) and (iii), $\|my\|\le m\|y\|$ for $m\in\mathbb N$ by subadditivity of $\|\cdot\|$. So $\|b^{kn+j}w\|\le b^j\|b^{kn}w\|$ for $0\le j<k$, and summing over $j$ gives $\sum_{j<k}\|b^{kn+j}w\|\le\frac{b^k-1}{b-1}\|b^{kn}w\|$. Sum over $n<N$ for (ii), and over $n\le N$ for (iii), using $kN+r\le k(N+1)$. If $M_N(w;b^k)\to0$, then (iii) and $\frac{N+1}{kN+r}\le\frac2k$ give $M_{N'}(w;b)\to0$ along all $N'$. The converse is (i). ∎

The constant in (ii) is sharp. For $b\ge3$ and $w=\frac1{b^k-1}$ we have $\|b^{kn+j}w\|=\frac{b^j}{b^k-1}=b^j\|b^{kn}w\|$ for all $n$ and $j<k$, because $b^{k-1}\le\frac12(b^k-1)$; so (ii) is an equality for every $N$. For every $b\ge2$, including $b=2$, the choice $w=b^{-k}\in M_b$ also gives equality for every $N$: $\|b^jw\|=b^{j-k}=b^j\|w\|$ for $j<k$, and every later term vanishes. The script checks (i)–(iii) exactly on rationals for $b\le6$, $k\le5$, $N\le24$ (3840 cases), and both equality cases for $b\le6$, $k\le5$, $N\le10$.

## 8. The quotient

**Theorem 8.1.** Let $b\ge2$, $k\ge1$ and $R=\frac{b^k-1}{b-1}$.

1. $K_b\subseteq K_{b^k}$ and $F_{b^k}\cap M_b=\mathbb Z$.
2. The map $m\mapsto\frac m{b^k-1}+K_b$ induces a group isomorphism $\mathbb Z/R\mathbb Z\to K_{b^k}/K_b$.
3. Every $x\in K_{b^k}$ has a unique representation $x=\frac r{b^k-1}+w$ with $r\in\{0,1,\dots,b^k-2\}$ and $w\in M_b$. Moreover $x\in K_b$ iff $R\mid r$.

*Proof.* (1) $F_b\subseteq F_{b^k}$ because $(b-1)\mid(b^k-1)$; with $M_b=M_{b^k}$ (Theorem 7.1) and Phase 19 in radix $b^k$, $K_b=F_b+M_b\subseteq F_{b^k}+M_{b^k}=K_{b^k}$. If $y\in F_{b^k}$ then $b^{kn}y\equiv y\pmod1$, so $M_N(y;b^k)=\|y\|$; thus $y\in M_{b^k}=M_b$ forces $y\in\mathbb Z$.

(2) Surjectivity: $z\in K_{b^k}$ is $\frac m{b^k-1}+w$ with $w\in M_{b^k}=M_b\subseteq K_b$. Kernel: if $\frac m{b^k-1}=f+w$ with $f\in F_b$ and $w\in M_b$, then $w\in F_{b^k}\cap M_b=\mathbb Z$. So $\frac m{b^k-1}\in F_b$, that is $R\mid m$. Conversely $R\mid m$ gives $\frac m{b^k-1}\in F_b\subseteq K_b$.

(3) Existence by (2), after reducing $m$ modulo $b^k-1$ and moving the integer into $w$. If $\frac r{b^k-1}+w=\frac{r'}{b^k-1}+w'$, then $\frac{r-r'}{b^k-1}\in F_{b^k}\cap M_b=\mathbb Z$, so $r=r'$ and then $w=w'$. ∎

The class of $x$ in $K_{b^k}/K_b$ is $r\bmod R$. Multiplication by $b$ preserves both kernels and acts on the quotient as multiplication by $b$, an automorphism of order exactly $k$: $b^k\equiv1\pmod R$, while $1\le b^j-1<R$ for $1\le j<k$. The script checks, for $b\le6$ and $k\le5$, that $\frac m{b^k-1}\in K_b$ iff $R\mid m$ iff the base-$b$ period of $\frac m{b^k-1}$ is 1 (membership of a rational decided by the Phase 19 denominator test), that there are exactly $R$ cosets, and that $b$ has order exactly $k$ modulo $R$.

## 9. Base-$b$ statistics of every element of $K_{b^k}$

For $c=\frac r{b^k-1}$ with $1\le r\le b^k-2$, the base-$b$ orbit $\{b^jc\}$ is purely periodic with least period $d\mid k$; $d$ is the least period of the $k$-digit base-$b$ block of $r$. Its points $\frac{r'}{b^k-1}$ with $1\le r'\le b^k-2$ are distinct and lie in $(0,1)$. Put $\mu_c=\frac1d\sum_{j<d}\delta_{\{b^jc\}}$ and $D(\mu)=\sup_{0\le\alpha<\beta\le1}|\mu[\alpha,\beta)-(\beta-\alpha)|$.

**Lemma 9.1 (exact discrepancy of $d$ equal atoms).** Let $0\le y_1<\dots<y_d<1$, $\mu=\frac1d\sum_i\delta_{y_i}$ and $z_i=y_i-\frac id$. Then

```math
D(\mu)=\frac1d+\max_{1\le i\le d}z_i-\min_{1\le i\le d}z_i .
```

If $d\ge2$ then $D(\mu)\le\max\bigl(1-(y_d-y_1),\,1-\frac1d,\,G\bigr)<1$, with $G=\max(y_1,1-y_d,\max_i(y_{i+1}-y_i))$.

*Proof.* An interval containing exactly $y_i,\dots,y_j$ ($i\le j$) has length at least $y_j-y_i$, and lengths arbitrarily close to it occur. So the supremum of the excess $\mu[\alpha,\beta)-(\beta-\alpha)$ is $\max_{i\le j}\bigl(\frac{j-i+1}d-(y_j-y_i)\bigr)=\frac1d+\max_{i\le j}(z_i-z_j)$. Put $y_0=0$, $y_{d+1}=1$, $z_0=0$, $z_{d+1}=-\frac1d$. An interval containing exactly the points $\{y_m:i<m<j\}$ ($0\le i<j\le d+1$) has length at most $y_j-y_i$, approached arbitrarily closely. So the supremum of the deficit is $\max_{i<j}\bigl(y_j-y_i-\frac{j-i-1}d\bigr)=\frac1d+\max_{0\le i<j\le d+1}(z_j-z_i)$.

The two maxima together cover all differences $z_\alpha-z_\beta$ with $1\le\alpha,\beta\le d$, giving $\max z-\min z$. The boundary terms are dominated: $z_j-z_0=z_j\le\max z-\min z$ since $\min z\le z_d=y_d-1<0$; and $z_{d+1}-z_i=-\frac1d-z_i\le\max z-\min z$ since $\max z\ge z_1\ge-\frac1d$. For the bound when $d\ge2$: the excess is at most $1-(y_d-y_1)$ if the interval contains all points and at most $1-\frac1d$ otherwise; the deficit is at most $G$ if the interval contains no point and at most $1-\frac1d$ otherwise. ∎

**Theorem 9.2.** Let $x=c+w\in K_{b^k}$ with $c=\frac r{b^k-1}$, $0\le r\le b^k-2$ and $w\in M_b$. Let $d$ be the base-$b$ period of $c$ ($d=1$ when $r=0$).

1. $|D_N(x;b)-D_N(c;b)|\le1-D_N(w;b)\to0$.
2. For every continuous $f:\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C$, $\frac1N\sum_{n<N}\bigl(f(b^nx)-f(b^nc)\bigr)\to0$; so the base-$b$ empirical measures of $x$ converge weakly to $\mu_c$.
3. $|D_N(x;b)-D(\mu_c)|\le1-D_N(w;b)+\frac dN$; hence $D_N(x;b)\to D(\mu_c)$, given by Lemma 9.1, and $D(\mu_c)<1$ iff $d\ge2$ iff $x\notin K_b$.
4. If $r\ne0$, then for every word $u$ of length $\lambda$ the frequency of $u$ among the base-$b$ digits of $x$ exists and equals its frequency in the periodic expansion of $c$, namely $\frac1d\#\{j<d:\{b^jc\}\in I_u\}$ with $I_u$ the cylinder of $u$. The number of positions $n<N$ at which the length-$\lambda$ blocks of $x$ and $c$ differ is at most $NM_N(w;b)/\eta_\lambda$, where $\eta_\lambda=\min_j\operatorname{dist}(\{b^jc\},b^{-\lambda}\mathbb Z)>0$.

*Proof.* (1) is the sharp translation theorem (`SharpTranslation`), and $D_N(w;b)\to1$ was shown at the start of Part II. (2) Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $\eta$ with $|f(s+t)-f(s)|\le\varepsilon$ whenever $\|t\|<\eta$. The average is at most $\varepsilon+2\|f\|_\infty M_N(w;b)/\eta$. (3) For $N=Qd+\rho$ with $0\le\rho<d$, the count of $\{b^nc\}$ in any interval is $Qd\,\mu_c(I)$ plus at most $\rho$, so $|D_N(c;b)-D(\mu_c)|\le d/N$; combine with (1). $D(\mu_c)=1$ for $d=1$ and $D(\mu_c)<1$ for $d\ge2$ by Lemma 9.1, and $d=1$ iff $c\in F_b$ iff $R\mid r$ iff $x\in K_b$ (Theorem 8.1). (4) No orbit point is in $b^{-\lambda}\mathbb Z$: $\frac{r'}{b^k-1}=\frac U{b^\lambda}$ would give $(b^k-1)\mid r'b^\lambda$, hence $(b^k-1)\mid r'$, impossible for $1\le r'\le b^k-2$. So $\eta_\lambda>0$. If $\|b^nw\|<\eta_\lambda$, then $\{b^nx\}=\{b^nc\}+\vartheta$ with $|\vartheta|<\eta_\lambda$, which stays inside the cylinder of $\{b^nc\}$. The exceptional $n$ number at most $NM_N(w;b)/\eta_\lambda$ by Markov's inequality. ∎

This holds for every $x\in K_{b^k}$ with $r\ne0$, including $x\in K_b\setminus M_b$, where the digits of $c=\frac j{b-1}$ are constant. The case $r=0$ is genuinely different. Then $x=w\in M_b$, and on a density-one set of positions the digit blocks are $0\cdots0$ or $(b-1)\cdots(b-1)$, in proportions that depend on the signs of $b^nw-\mathrm{round}(b^nw)$ and need not converge.

**Proposition 9.3 (a concrete $w$).** Euler's function $E_b=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-b^{-n})$ lies in $M_b$, with $M_N(E_b;b)\le(2\sqrt{2N/3}+2)/N$.

*Proof.* By the pentagonal theorem $E_b=1+\sum_{n\ge1}a(n)b^{-n}$, with $a(n)\in\{0,\pm1\}$ supported on generalized pentagonal numbers, of which at most $2\sqrt{2N/3}+1$ are $\le N$. Then $\|b^nE_b\|\le|T_n|\le\sum_{k\ge1}|a(n+k)|b^{-k}$. Summing over $n<N$, each $m\le N$ contributes at most $\sum_{n<m}b^{n-m}\le1$, and all $m>N$ together contribute at most $\sum_{m>N}b^{N-m}/(b-1)\le1$. ∎

**Examples** (exact $D(\mu_c)$; finite values at $N=3000$ satisfy the inequality of Theorem 9.2(3)):

The limit measure determines the orbit of $r$ under multiplication by $b$ modulo $b^k-1$; the coset is $r\bmod R$.

## 10. Simple normality in the tower

**Corollary 10.1.**

1. No element of $K_{b^k}$ is normal in base $b$.
2. For $b\ge3$, no element of $K_b$ is simply normal in base $b$.
3. $K_2$ contains numbers that are simply normal in base 2.
4. For all $b\ge2$, $k\ge1$: $K_{b^k}\setminus K_b$ contains a number simply normal in base $b$ iff $b\mid k$. When $b\mid k$, $\frac r{b^k-1}+w$ is one for every $w\in M_b$, where $r$ has $k$-digit block $(0\,1\,\cdots\,b-1)$ repeated $k/b$ times. Examples: $\frac13+E_2\in K_4\setminus K_2$ and $\frac5{26}+E_3\in K_{27}\setminus K_3$.

*Proof.* (1) If $r\ne0$, Theorem 9.2(4) gives the word frequencies of a sequence of period $d$; a word of length $\lambda$ with $b^\lambda>d$ has frequency 0. If $r=0$, then $x\in M_b\subseteq K_b$ and $D_N\to1$.

(2) For $x=\frac j{b-1}+w$ with $1\le j\le b-2$, the digit $j$ has frequency 1 by Theorem 9.2(4). For $x=w\in M_b$, for density-one $n$ the point $\{b^nw\}$ lies in $[0,\frac1b)\cup[1-\frac1b,1)$, so the digit $1\notin\{0,b-1\}$ has frequency 0.

(3) Let $x$ have binary digits in runs of lengths $1,2,3,\dots$ of alternating symbols. Positions at distance $\ge\rho$ from the end of their run have $\|2^nx\|\le2^{-\rho}$. There are $O(\rho\sqrt N)$ other positions below $N$, so $M_N(x;2)\le2^{-\rho}+O(\rho/\sqrt N)$ and $x\in M_2=K_2$. The frequency of 1 is $\frac12$.

(4) If $x\in K_{b^k}\setminus K_b$ then $r\ne0$, $R\nmid r$ and $d\ge2$. By Theorem 9.2(4) digit $j$ has frequency $n_j/k$, where $n_j$ counts $j$ in the $k$-block. Simple normality forces $k=bn_j$. Conversely, the stated block gives $n_j=k/b$ and has period $b\ge2$, so $R\nmid r$. ∎

The script confirms (4) for $b\le6$, $k\le6$. It also checks Theorem 9.2(4) for $\frac13+E_2$ and $\frac5{26}+E_3$ over $2\cdot10^4$ digits, for single digits ($\lambda=1$). In each case 458 positions have $\|b^nE_b\|\ge\eta_1$ (the Markov-exceptional positions, within the bound of Theorem 9.2(4)). The digits of $x$ and $c$ actually differ at 343 positions for $\frac13+E_2$ and at 306 positions for $\frac5{26}+E_3$, all of them Markov-exceptional; digit frequencies are $0.49995/0.50005$ and $0.33335/0.33330/0.33335$. It also checks the run-length example: frequency of 1 is $0.50000$ and $M_{20000}=0.0099$.

## 11. Recommended Lean 4 finite cores

Schematic statements; names should be fixed against the library (`NormalityChecker.radixDiscrepancy`, `SharpTranslation`, `DiscrepancyGeometry.discDist_triangle`, `ClassicalBarriers.one_sub_radixDiscrepancy_pow_le`).

```lean
namespace NormalityChecker.Phase22Quotient
open Finset Filter
open scoped Topology

/-- Distance to the nearest integer. -/
noncomputable def nint (t : ℝ) : ℝ := |t - round t|

/-- Mean circle displacement `M_N(w; b)`. -/
noncomputable def meanDisp (b : ℕ) (w : ℝ) (N : ℕ) : ℝ :=
  (∑ n ∈ range N, nint ((b : ℝ) ^ n * w)) / N

theorem nint_natMul_le (m : ℕ) (t : ℝ) : nint (m * t) ≤ m * nint t

/-- Theorem 7.1 (i). -/
theorem meanDisp_pow_le (b k N : ℕ) (hk : 0 < k) (hN : 0 < N) (w : ℝ) :
    meanDisp (b ^ k) w N ≤ k * meanDisp b w (k * N)

/-- Theorem 7.1 (ii). -/
theorem meanDisp_le_pow (b k N : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (hk : 0 < k) (hN : 0 < N) (w : ℝ) :
    meanDisp b w (k * N) ≤ ((b : ℝ) ^ k - 1) / (k * ((b : ℝ) - 1)) * meanDisp (b ^ k) w N

/-- `M_b = M_{b^k}`. -/
theorem tendsto_meanDisp_pow_iff (b k : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (hk : 0 < k) (w : ℝ) :
    Tendsto (meanDisp (b ^ k) w) atTop (𝓝 0) ↔ Tendsto (meanDisp b w) atTop (𝓝 0)

/-- The numerical kernel as an additive subgroup. -/
noncomputable def kernel (b : ℕ) : AddSubgroup ℝ :=
  AddSubgroup.closure {z | Tendsto (fun N => radixDiscrepancy b z N) atTop (𝓝 1)}

/-- For `b ≥ 2` the closure adds nothing (sharp translation and `discDist_triangle`). -/
theorem mem_kernel_iff {b : ℕ} (hb : 2 ≤ b) (z : ℝ) :
    z ∈ kernel b ↔ Tendsto (fun N => radixDiscrepancy b z N) atTop (𝓝 1)

/-- Strict inclusion `K_b ⊊ K_{b^k}` for `k ≥ 2`. -/
theorem kernel_lt_pow (b k : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (hk : 2 ≤ k) : kernel b < kernel (b ^ k)

/-- Theorem 8.1 (2), membership form. -/
theorem div_pow_sub_one_mem_kernel_iff (b k : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (hk : 0 < k) (m : ℤ) :
    (m : ℝ) / ((b : ℝ) ^ k - 1) ∈ kernel b ↔ (((b ^ k - 1) / (b - 1) : ℕ) : ℤ) ∣ m

/-- Theorem 8.1 (2), group form. -/
noncomputable def kernelQuotientEquiv (b k : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (hk : 0 < k) :
    (kernel (b ^ k) ⧸ (kernel b).addSubgroupOf (kernel (b ^ k))) ≃+
      ZMod ((b ^ k - 1) / (b - 1))

/-- Lemma 9.1, finite core: exact discrepancy of `d` sorted distinct points;
`pointSetDisc y` is the supremum over `[α, β) ⊆ [0, 1)` of
`|#{i | y i ∈ [α, β)} / d - (β - α)|`. -/
theorem sampleDisc_sorted_eq {d : ℕ} (hd : 0 < d) (y : Fin d → ℝ) (hy : StrictMono y)
    (h0 : 0 ≤ y ⟨0, hd⟩) (h1 : y ⟨d - 1, by omega⟩ < 1) :
    pointSetDisc y = 1 / (d : ℝ) + (⨆ i : Fin d, (y i - (((i : ℕ) + 1 : ℕ) : ℝ) / (d : ℝ)))
      - (⨅ i : Fin d, (y i - (((i : ℕ) + 1 : ℕ) : ℝ) / (d : ℝ)))

/-- Theorem 9.2 (3). -/
theorem tendsto_radixDiscrepancy_periodic_add (b k r : ℕ) (hb : 2 ≤ b) (hk : 0 < k)
    (hr : r < b ^ k - 1) {w : ℝ} (hw : Tendsto (meanDisp b w) atTop (𝓝 0)) :
    Tendsto (radixDiscrepancy b ((r : ℝ) / ((b : ℝ) ^ k - 1) + w)) atTop
      (𝓝 (orbitDisc b k r))

/-- `F_b(a) = ∑_{n ≥ 1} a(n) b^{-n}` and the tail `T_P = ∑_{k ≥ 1} a(P + k) b^{-k}`
(`tsum`, so they are meaningful only under the summability hypothesis below; compare
`CoefficientTransfer.coeffSeries`). -/
noncomputable def lambertValue (b : ℕ) (a : ℕ → ℤ) : ℝ :=
  ∑' n : ℕ, (a (n + 1) : ℝ) / (b : ℝ) ^ (n + 1)
noncomputable def tail (b : ℕ) (a : ℕ → ℤ) (P : ℕ) : ℝ :=
  ∑' k : ℕ, (a (P + k + 1) : ℝ) / (b : ℝ) ^ (k + 1)

/-- Lemma 5.1 (digit window), the finite core of Theorem 5.3.
`/` and `%` on ℤ are Euclidean, so the right side is `⌊V₁ / b⌋ mod b ^ W`. -/
theorem digit_window {b : ℕ} (hb : 2 ≤ b) (a : ℕ → ℤ) (N W f : ℕ) (V₁ : ℤ)
    (ha : ∃ C : ℝ, ∀ n, |(a n : ℝ)| ≤ C * (n + 1))
    (hzero : ∀ i ∈ Icc 1 (W + f), a (N + i) = 0)
    (hval : a (N + W + f + 1) = (b : ℤ) ^ f * V₁) (hV : ¬ (b : ℤ) ∣ V₁)
    (hVr : -(b : ℤ) ^ (W + 1) ≤ V₁ ∧ V₁ < (b : ℤ) ^ (W + 1))
    (htail : |tail b a (N + W + f + 1)| < 1 / 2) :
    ⌊(b : ℝ) ^ W * shift b (lambertValue b a) N⌋ = (V₁ / b) % (b : ℤ) ^ W

end NormalityChecker.Phase22Quotient
```

`orbitDisc b k r` is the right side of Lemma 9.1 for the base-$b$ orbit of $r/(b^k-1)$. `tail` and `lambertValue` are the $T_P$ and $F_b(a)$ of §5.1. The growth hypothesis `ha` makes both series summable; without it `tsum` returns 0 and the statement would be false. Theorem 7.1 needs only `nint_natMul_le` and finite sums. Theorem 8.1 needs in addition the Phase 19 decomposition $K_b=F_b+M_b$, which is not yet in Lean. It is the only nonelementary input of Part II.

## 13. Scope, external inputs and open questions

**Evidence.** Every theorem here is a paper proof. The script supplies exact checks of the algebra and of every finite inequality used, plus finite data for the asymptotic statements. Lean formalizations of the finite cores in §11 remain future work.

**External inputs.**

- B3:
  - the PNT and Mertens' theorem in progressions for fixed moduli;
  - Wirsing (1961), for Proposition 3.4 (via Phase 21 Proposition 1.4);
  - Halász (1968), for Theorem 3.1(2) only;
  - (E1): Landau–Page and the PNT in progressions for moduli up to $\exp(c\sqrt{\log x})$ away from the exceptional conductor (Davenport Ch. 14 and 20; Montgomery–Vaughan §11.3), for Theorem 5.3 only.

  Shiu (1980) enters only through the citation of Phase 21 Theorem 1.5 in Corollary 5.4, for the membership of the even-order examples in $K_b$. The odd-$k$ part of Remark 3.2 and Remark 3.5 use Selberg–Delange heuristically and are not used.
- B4: the Phase 19 decomposition $K_b=F_b+M_b$ and the sharp translation theorem (Lean).

**Structural remarks.**

1. $c_-$ vanishes on exactly one third of the support of $c_+$ (the classes $t\equiv0\pmod3$), so $\operatorname{supp}c_-$ has density $\frac23\delta$, and the ratio converges at rate $O(X^{-1/2})$ (Remark 3.2).
2. The proof of Theorem 5.3 needs $b^L\gg(\log Y)^2$. One factor $\log Y$ comes from the divisor-sum bound (Lemma 5.2), the other from passing from all integers of the class to the primes in it. Hence $L\approx2\log_b\log Y$. A sharper mean-value bound for $|c|$ in progressions (average $(\log X)^{1/3}$) would lower the constant 2, but not the order $(\log\log Y)(\log\log\log Y)$ of $\log q'$.
3. Neither the Siegel–Walfisz range nor a lower bound that retains a possible Siegel zero suffices (§5.4); the proof uses Landau–Page with two disjoint prime families.
4. The discrepancy of $d$ equal atoms is $\frac1d+\max z-\min z$ (Lemma 9.1). It exceeds the largest gap in general, because intervals containing points also contribute.
5. Base-$b$ simple normality inside $K_{b^k}\setminus K_b$ is a genuine radix-power phenomenon only for $b\ge3$: then $K_b$ contains no base-$b$ simply normal number, whereas $K_2$ contains base-2 simply normal numbers (Corollary 10.1).

**Open questions.**

1. Is $L_+(b)$ or $L_-(b)$ in $K_b$? Is either normal, or simply normal, in some base?
2. A positive lower density for some word in $L_\pm(b)$, which would give $D^+<1$. Theorem 5.3 gives only $X^{1-o(1)}$ occurrences.
3. A displaced version of Theorem 5.3 for the Erdős–Borwein constants, where no coefficient vanishes.
4. A Lean formalization of $K_b=F_b+M_b$, which would make Theorem 8.1 kernel-checked from the cores of §11.
