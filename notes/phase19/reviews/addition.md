# Independent audit: the addition spectrum of extreme discrepancy

Target: [notes/phase19/addition.md](../addition.md) and `scripts/phase19_addition.py` at commit `ca020db`. I read the note and its Phase 18 inputs: [translations.md](../../phase18/translations.md) (sharp translation) and [programming.md](../../phase18/programming.md) (coefficient recurrence, the prime-power period lemma, the deficit scheduler and summation by parts). I did not edit the author's files. My independent script is [addition_check.py](addition_check.py), which uses only the standard library and does not import the author's code.

**Verdict.** I found no mathematical error. Every theorem in the note is proved as stated, within the scope the note claims. The objections below are about how the results are presented and their scope (O1, O2), an arithmetic choice that makes the constants needlessly non-uniform (O3), gaps in what the tests cover (O4), and unchecked citation details (O5). Section S lists strengthenings that follow cheaply from what is already proved.

## What I re-derived

* **Necessity.** Sharp translation gives $|c-a|\le1-b'$ and $|c-b'|\le1-a$. Their four half-inequalities are exactly $\gamma\ge\alpha+\beta-1$ and $\gamma\le1-|\alpha-\beta|$. In deficit coordinates $e=1-\delta$ these are the three triangle inequalities, and nonnegativity follows from them. The $d$-summand derivation adds summands to $x_i$ one at a time to reach $S$, so it never needs reflection invariance $D_N(-y)=D_N(y)$. That invariance does hold in any case, because left-open and right-open arcs have the same supremum. Both derivations hold at every finite $N$.
* **Five weights.** If $s=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma-1)/2$, the conditions $\beta-s\ge0$, $\alpha-s\ge0$ and $\gamma-s\ge0$ are the three facet inequalities, and the last weight is $0$. If $s=0$ the last weight is $1-\alpha-\beta-\gamma\ge0$. The marginals are correct. Since $p$ is odd, the projected targets $u,-u,2u$ all have exact denominator $q_k$.
* **Scheduler.** The deficits sum to $1$, so the largest is at least $1/5$. With estimates within $1/(20k)$, the chosen *true* deficit is at least $1/10$. In particular a type of true weight $0$ is never chosen, which is what makes the vertices and faces exact. Each overcount lies in $[-4,1]$. The aggregate over the two types retired by a projection actually lies in $[-3,2]$, so the note's constant $8$ is safe but not tight.
* **All-prefix bound.** I checked $h_k\le7tk^2$, $k<q_k\le pk$, the period bound $tp^{\max(e-v,0)}\le p^e$, $H=(7tp^v+p)K(K+1)/2$, $E=(K+1)W$ with $W=O(\log K)$, and the summation by parts with $C=8$. I also checked that the reference law $(Z/N)\delta_0+(1-Z/N)\lambda$ has discrepancy exactly $Z/N$. Together with $N\ge tK^3$ these give $O_{b,p}(N^{-1/3})$, uniformly on the closed region, at every prefix including unfinished blocks.
* **Polynomial avoidance.** The candidates are distinct because two tails in $(0,Bb^{-h_k}]$ differ by less than $1/(2q_k)$. The Cartesian-grid root count is correct. Interval certificates survive every continuation, because every continuation, including later overrides, has coefficients in $(0,1]$. Overrides cost at most $(28t/3)K^2$. The search terminates because the provisionally nonzero candidate's boxes eventually exclude zero.
* **$\theta=\sum_j p^{j!}$ is transcendental in $\mathbb Z_p$.** The truncation argument is correct: $F(a_s)\ne0$ eventually, $p^{(s+1)!}\mid F(a_s)$, and $|F(a_s)|\ll p^{s!\deg F}$.
* **Haar mixtures.** The projection of $\lambda_H$ is $\delta_0$ or $\lambda$. The Fourier coefficient at $m$ of the joint empirical law is the first Fourier coefficient of the projected law. That gives joint weak convergence from all the projected limits. The flat description is correct, including zero and duplicate rows. The rational-row reduction $x=Qu$ is correct.
* **$d$ summands.** I independently confirmed the hull description by brute-force vertex enumeration for $d=1,\ldots,4$. The facet decomposition and the line directions are correct. In the effective version the cumulative-weight scheduler has deficits summing to $1$, and the requested activity drifts by at most $\sum2^{-j}\le1$. The note correctly says that only the projected limits are prescribed there, since the joint law may oscillate.
* **Six-form inequality.** I checked $D(u+v)\ge D(u)+D(v)-1$, $2x=(u+v)-w$, and $D(2x)\le2D(x)$, which holds because the doubling preimage of a half-open arc is two half-open arcs of half length. The argument uses only paired samples, not the orbit structure. My script confirms it on 400 random paired samples that are *not* orbits.
* **Full spectrum $S$ and the approximation $S_L$.** $D$ is weakly continuous at every law. A grid avoiding the atoms of the limit gives $D^+\in[g,g+2\eta]$, and $D=D^+$ by complementation. Invariant limits project correctly. The cycle decomposition of a balanced flow on the word graph uses simple cycles, so each periodic law has exactly the cycle's $L$-words. The grid formula with inclusive cyclic ranges is right, including $a=c$. The coupling uses the symbolic law directly, not the terminating lift, so the all-$(b-1)$ expansion of zero is handled. Both Hausdorff directions give $2b^{-L}\max\|m\|_1$. The test only certifies infeasibility. The note claims nothing more, which is correct, since membership in $S$ is only co-semidecidable by this method.
* **Numerics.** `python -B scripts/phase19_addition.py` passes in about 3 s. My independent recomputation reproduces the binary $N=100$ enclosures $10^{-6}[469999,470000]$, $[189722,189723]$ and $[318888,318889]$, with scheduled types $0,1,3,2,1,3,1$ for blocks $0$ through $6$.

## Objections

**O1. The qualitative theorem is a short corollary of classical results; say so in the theorem itself, not only in §Scope.** The "if and only if" is a direct consequence of three results: Sigmund's generic-point theorem, weak continuity of $D$, and the five Haar laws (or even the five periodic or Haar vertex laws). The note's own §"full finite-form spectrum" proves this. The new content is the uniform all-prefix rate $O(N^{-1/3})$, the one-prime arithmetic witnesses, uniform computability, and effective algebraic independence. §Scope says this. But the opening theorem block presents the characterization and the rate as one theorem, and a reader could credit the characterization as new. I recommend stating the qualitative spectrum as a proposition with a one-line proof via Sigmund, and keeping the theorem for the quantitative and arithmetic realization.

**O2. The scope of Sigmund and Colebrook.**
* Sigmund 1974 (Trans. AMS 190, 285–299) proves, according to its abstract, that under specification *every invariant measure, and every closed connected set of invariant measures*, has generic points. The full shift on $b^d$ symbols satisfies both the ordinary and the periodic form of specification. So does $u\mapsto bu$ on $\mathbb T^d$ directly, as a factor of the full shift. The lift through the terminating digit map is therefore valid but unnecessary, and one can cite Sigmund on $\mathbb T^d$ directly. The note uses only the single-measure half. The connected-set half gives the cluster-set strengthening S1 below for free.
* Colebrook (Michigan Math. J. 17, 1970) is not cited in the note. If a later draft cites it for existence, note that, as I understand it, it is a one-dimensional Hausdorff-dimension result for digit-frequency sets in the Besicovitch–Eggleston line. I did not verify this against the original. Dimension $h(\mu)/\log b$ gives nonemptiness only when $h(\mu)>0$. Here the joint law has positive entropy except at the vertex $(1,1,1)$, where it is $\delta_0$. Joint laws on $\mathbb T^2$ would also need the digit-pair alphabet, for instance interleaving in base $b^2$. Sigmund, or the explicit Madritsch–Mance construction, is the correct citation for existence. Colebrook is not.

**O3. The Liouville direction $\theta$ makes the constants super-polynomial in $m$.** Liouville-type approximations are a real phenomenon here, not just a weak bound. For $H=\mathbb T^2$, $B=I$ and $m=(a_s,-1)$ with $a_s=\sum_{j\le s}p^{j!}$, we have $\|m\|\approx p^{s!}$ but $s(m)=v_p(a_s-\theta)=(s+1)!$, so $p^{s(m)}\approx\|m\|^{s+1}$. Every block with $q_k\le p^{s(m)}$ has $m\cdot(\text{target})\equiv0$, so $m\cdot u$ really looks retired until $N\approx\|m\|^{3(s+1)}$. Each fixed $m$ still has $O(N^{-1/3})$, which is all the note claims. But no quantitative joint-discrepancy rate (Erdős–Turán–Koksma over $\|m\|\le T$) can follow from this construction. **Fix:** transcendence is not needed. Only $F(\theta)\ne0$ for nonzero $F$ of degree $<r_i\le d$ is used. Take $\theta\in\mathbb Z_p$ algebraic of degree $\ge d$. The $p$-adic Liouville inequality then gives $p^{s(m)}\ll\|mB\|^{O(d)}$, polynomial in $m$. This also makes the valuations $s_i(m)$ explicitly bounded instead of found by unbounded search. Rank-one directions, which suffice for the $d$-summand theorem, already have $s=v_p(m\cdot a)\le\log_p\|m\cdot a\|$, so the $d$-summand constant is genuinely $O_{b,p,d}$ as claimed.

**O4. Test coverage is narrower than the prose suggests.** Each of these is a scope note, not an error.
* The "stationary word flows" check only compares the truncation error of five known periodic laws. It does not build the polytope cells, check the cycle-decomposition direction, or compute any part of $S_L$. The finite approximation theorem therefore has no executable instance. One small instance would close the gap: rows $(1),(3)$ in base $2$ at $L\le3$, checking that $(2/3,1)$ is within $2\cdot3\cdot2^{-L}$ of $S_L$ and that a point such as $(0,1)$ is excluded at some $L$.
* Sparse avoidance is tested only at the nominal vertex $(1,1,1)$, with five polynomials.
* The joint Haar-mixture scheduler with $h>5$ types and rank-$\ge2$ subtori is not simulated. Only single-depth $p$-adic projections are checked.
* The six-form check uses rational orbits only. My script adds random non-orbit samples, plus finite equality at $x=y=z$ on $\{0,1/2\}$.

**O5. Citation details I could not verify.** These are the Bergelson–Downarowicz Proposition 4.9, §4.3 and Theorem 4.29, and the Bochi–Zhang Propositions 3.2 and 3.5 and the identifier arXiv:1501.00961. A quick search did not resolve the arXiv identifier. These should be checked before publication. Sigmund's single-measure and connected-set generic-point statements are confirmed by its abstract.

**Minor points.**
1. The $N^{-1/3}$ exponent is an artifact of the method. With one type per block of length $h\approx N/K$ and period denominators $q$, the cost is about $1/K+Kq/N+1/q$, which is optimized at $K\approx N^{1/3}$. Beating it needs mixing within blocks. Whether any witness achieves $o(N^{-1/3})$ for $\delta\in(0,1)$ is open. A sentence saying the rate is not claimed to be optimal would help.
2. The constant $C_{b,p}$ is never written down. It follows from the displayed bound, so state it, for example in terms of $t$, $p^v$ and $B$.
3. The six-form inequality is attained in the limit in base $3$: take $x=y=z$ generic for $(\delta_0+\delta_{1/2})/2$, which is invariant because both points are fixed by multiplication by $3$. That gives $1+1+1=2+2\cdot\frac12$. So it is a sharp new facet in base $3$ at least. The note does not claim the six-form spectrum is complete, and it probably depends on $b$. $S_L$ is the right tool for exploring it.
4. The flat hull is described as "within these mixtures". Haar measures on *disconnected* closed $b$-invariant subgroups give arithmetic points outside it while staying within an explicit family. For example, Haar on $\{0,1/3,2/3\}$ in base $2$ gives $(1/3,1)$ for the rows $(1),(3)$. This could be mentioned next to the $1/3$ example.

## Strengthenings available now

**S1. Cluster-set spectrum.** By Sigmund's connected-set theorem, Phase 18's observation that the cluster set of empirical laws is closed and connected, and continuity of $\Phi(\mu)=(D(\chi_{m*}\mu))_m$, the achievable cluster sets of the discrepancy vector are exactly the sets $\Phi(C)$ for closed connected $C\subseteq\mathcal M_b$. For the addition triple, the weight map $e\mapsto(w_i(e))$ is continuous (piecewise linear). So every compact connected subset of the triangle polytope is the cluster set of $(D_N(x),D_N(y),D_N(x+y))$ for some $(x,y)$. In particular the $\liminf$/$\limsup$ spectra follow. A quantitative version should follow from the cumulative-weight scheduler of the effective $d$-summand section with slowly varying weights.

**S2. Polynomial constants.** As in O3, an algebraic $\theta$ of degree $\ge d$ gives constants polynomial in $\|m\|$, and that in turn should give an explicit joint discrepancy rate for the Haar mixture.
