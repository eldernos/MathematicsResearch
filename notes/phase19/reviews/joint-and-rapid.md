# Independent review: the joint box rate and rapidly varying schedules

Targets, at parent commit `d539cb6`:

* [joint-rate.md](../joint-rate.md), the effective algebraic $p$-adic direction and the all-prefix multivariate ETK bound.
* Sections 2 and 3 of [nonlinear.md](../nonlinear.md): rapid schedules with $n_{i+1}/n_i\to1$, and several weighted generators with algebraic independence and all-polynomial normality/Liouville.

I also read the Phase 18 inputs they invoke: [programming.md §B1](../../phase18/programming.md#b1-the-exact-coefficient), [principal.md, all-prefix criterion and complete periods](../../phase18/principal.md#a-fixed-support-criterion-for-all-prefixes), and [algebra.md, E1 and fast-schedule polynomial normality](../../phase18/algebra.md#universal-polynomial-normality-for-fast-schedules). I edited none of the author's files. My checks are in [joint-and-rapid-check.py](joint-and-rapid-check.py). It uses only the standard library and does not import the author's code. I used no subagents and ran no degree sweeps.

**Verdict.** I found no mathematical error in any of the three results. Each is proved as stated, within the scope claimed. The objections concern attribution (O1), a missing one-line classification of what kind of compatibility each result gives (O2), hypotheses left implicit (O3), executable coverage (O4), and citation hygiene and a non-optimized exponent (O5). I list small proof tightenings last. Nothing here changes a conclusion.

## What I re-derived

### joint-rate.md

* **The direction.** $F=X^d+\ell X+\ell p$ with $\ell=2$ and $p$ odd is Eisenstein at $2$, since $2\nmid p$ gives $2\,\|\,\ell p$. Hence it is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. $F(0)=\ell p\equiv0$ and $F'(0)=\ell$ is a unit mod $p$, so Hensel gives $\theta\in\mathbb Z_p$ with $v_p(\theta)=1$. Because $F$ is the minimal polynomial of $\theta$, $P(\theta)\ne0$ for every nonzero integer $P$ with $\deg P<d$. Hence nonzero $h$ gives $P(\theta)\ne0$.
* **Polynomial valuation bound.** I rederived that $\mathrm{Res}(F,P)=\pm\prod_{F(\alpha)=0}P(\alpha)$ for monic $F$. Cauchy's root bound $|\alpha|\le1+2p$ gives $|P(\alpha)|\le dH(1+2p)^{d-1}$, so $0<|\mathrm{Res}|\le C_{p,d}H^d$. In a splitting field over $\mathbb Q_p$ all roots are integral, so every factor has $v_p\ge0$. One factor is $P(\theta)$. Hence $p^{v_p(P(\theta))}\mid\mathrm{Res}$, and so $p^{v_p(P(\theta))}\le C_{p,d}H^d$. Check script part 1 verifies the divisibility $p^{v_p(P(\theta))}\mid\mathrm{Res}(F,P)$ exactly, by Sylvester determinants and a Hensel root mod $p^{60}$, for every nonzero $h$ with $\|h\|_\infty\le4$. The cases are $(d,p)=(2,3),(3,3),(3,5)$. The ratio $p^{v}/|\mathrm{Res}|$ reaches $1$.
* **Scalar model.** $\sum_jh_jv_{j,k}\equiv P(\theta)\pmod{q_k}$. So $h\cdot u_{k}=r/q_k$ with $v_p(r)=v_p(P(\theta))$ whenever that is below $\log_pq_k$. For $k>p^aC_{p,d}H^d$ we have $q_k>k>p^{a+v_p(P(\theta))}$, so the reduced denominator is $p^e$ with $e>a$.
* **Complete periods.** For $e>a=v_p(b^t-1)$ the order of $b$ modulo $p^e$ is $tp^{e-a}$. The orbit of a unit numerator contains the full coset of $1+p^a\mathbb Z/p^e$, so it is a union of translates of the $p^{e-a}$-point equally spaced grid, and a full period sums to $0$. The period is $tp^{e-a}\le q_k$, because $t<p\le p^a$. I confirmed that $q_k\le pk$ and $k<q_k$.
* **Real tail.** Block $k$ has the exact model $\{b^mu_{k-1}\}$ plus the real perturbation $\sum_{i\ge k}c_ib^{m-n_i}$. Its sum over $m<h_k$ is at most $\sum_{i\ge k}b^{n_k-n_i}/(b-1)=O_b(1)$ per coordinate. Exponentials are $2\pi|h_j|$-Lipschitz and 1-periodic, so the transfer costs $O(dH(K+1))$ uniformly in $h$.
* **Assembly.** The cost is $O(H^{3d}+K^2+HK+1)$ with $K\ll N^{1/3}$. Early blocks $k\le k_0\asymp H^d$ cost at most $\sum_{k\le k_0}h_k=tk_0^3$. Later blocks cost at most $q_k\le pk$ each, and the unfinished block at $N$ costs the same. The weights satisfy $\sum_{0<\|h\|_\infty\le H}1/r(h)\le(3+2\log H)^d$. With $H=N^{1/(3d+1)}$ the three terms $H^{-1}$, $H^{3d}/N$ and $\log^d$ times the rest are all at most $N^{-1/(3d+1)}\log^dN$. The corner argument gives the factor $2^d$ for general boxes. The exponent bookkeeping is correct: $N^{-1/3}\le N^{-1/(3d+1)}$ and $HN^{-2/3}\le N^{-1/(3d+1)}$ since $2/(3d+1)\le2/3$.

### nonlinear.md §2 (consecutive ratios tending to one)

* **The example.** $\log n_i=\log t+\log i+(\log(i+1))^2+o(1)$ and $\log n_{\lfloor ci\rfloor}-\log n_i=2\log c\log i+O_c(1)\to-\infty$. Consecutive differences tend to $0$. Strict monotonicity holds. $n_i=t\,i\,M_i$ gives $v_p(b^{n_i}-1)=a+v_p(iM_i)\ge a+v_p(i)$ by lifting the exponent for odd $p$.
* **$n_i/i^a\to\infty$** follows from iterating $n_i/n_{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}\to\infty$. Hence $K(N)=N^{o(1)}$, the number of tuple insertions is $N^{o(1)}$, and the coefficients are polynomially bounded, since the weights are at most $1$.
* **The limit $B(a)$.** I rederived $p^{sv}U_{m-1}\to B(a)$ layer by layer. The layers are $i=ap^v\cdot(\text{index}<a)$ and, for $h\ge1$, $a(p-1)p^{h-1}$ indices of valuation $v-h$. I also rederived $B(a)+1=\sum_h\delta_h\{a/p^h\}$ with $\delta_h=p^{-s(h-1)}-p^{-sh}$ and $\sum\delta_h=1$. It lies strictly in $(0,1)$ because $p\nmid a$. I did this independently of the Phase 18 text. $S_l(B)=\sum_{j\le l}\binom rjB^{r-j}$ is monic with integer coefficients, so $B(a)\in(-1,0)$ is not a root.
* **Inequality (3).** Both subsequence alternatives hold up. For $J_m-v\to\infty$ the count of indices with $v_p(i)=J_m$ below $m$ lies in $[1,p-1]$ because $m$ is not a multiple of $p^{J_m}$, so the valuation of $U_{m-1}$ is exactly $-sJ_m$. Hypothesis 2 is used only to make $b^{-n_i}\equiv1\pmod p$ there. Check script part 2 reproduces $B(a)$ as a $p$-adic limit of the exact sums for $(p,s,b)=(3,1,2),(3,2,2),(5,1,2)$. It also checks $S_l(B(a))\ne0$ for $r\le7$ and $a<40$. Part 4 evaluates $v_p(F_l(U_{m-1},V_m))+rsJ_m$ exactly for $P=z^r$ on the actual rapid schedule. The maximum is $2$ to $4$, attained at the smallest $m$ tested ($m=p^3$), so (3) is bounded there.
* **Sparse reduction.** Any tuple of $j\le r$ indices containing an index of valuation below $J-H$ has weight valuation at least $-sjJ+s(H+1)\ge-rsJ+L$. So it vanishes mod $p^L$ after multiplying by $p^{rsJ}$, for every subset of tuples. Pure-grid tuples of degree below $r$ vanish for large $J$. Hence only the grid-indexed part matters, and the grid has at most $p^{H+1}$ elements.
* **Last grid index.** I checked: $m$ lies in $S_H$ (with $J_m\in\{J-1,J\}$), earlier grid indices are at most $\lfloor(1-p^{-H-1})m\rfloor$ by the gap $p^{J-H}>m/p^{H+1}$, and $w_m=o(n_m)$ by hypothesis 1. Outside the intervals $[jn_m,jn_m+rw_m]$ the surviving tuples are exactly those with $j\le\min(r,\lfloor n/n_m\rfloor)$ copies of $m$, and they match $F_l(U_{m-1},V_m)$ modulo $p^L$. The first case ($n_i\le n/r$ for all grid indices) gives $p^{rsJ}P(U_K)$ of valuation exactly $v_p(A)$.
* **Discard cost.** Consecutive members of $S_H$ have ratio at least $1+p^{-H-1}$, so $n_{m'}/n_m\to\infty$ along them. Their exponents are eventually at least geometric, and $\sum_{n_m\le N}\varepsilon_mn_m=o(N)$ with $\varepsilon_m\to0$. This gives density zero. The denominators satisfy $q_n\mid p^{rsJ}\le(n+1)^{rs}$ and $q_n\ge p^{rsJ-L+1}\to\infty$ off the discard set, which verifies the three hypotheses of the all-prefix criterion.
* **Consequences.** A nonconstant $P$ with $P(X)=0$ is impossible for a normal value. Hence $X$ is transcendental. Joint uniformity of $(X,\dots,X^r)$ follows because every nonzero integer combination is a nonconstant polynomial in $X$.

### nonlinear.md §3 (several generators)

* **Pole structure.** Because $f_k-f_{k-1}\sim\sqrt{\log k}\to\infty$, $e_{a,k}$ is eventually strictly increasing in $k$. So $U_a$ has exact valuation $-e_{a,k-1}$. Each term $c\,\prod U_a^{\nu_a-j_a}V_a^{j_a}$ has valuation exactly $v_p(\text{fixed integer})-\mathrm{depth}$, because valuation is multiplicative. Summing terms with a unique deepest member and a margin tending to infinity gives that member's valuation.
* **Uniqueness.** Distinct monomials $\nu\ne\nu'$ have $\gamma\cdot\nu\ne\gamma\cdot\nu'$ and distinct $j\ne j'$ have $\gamma\cdot j\ne\gamma\cdot j'$, because $\gamma$ is $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent. Both uniqueness claims use this and nothing else. The gap is then at least a fixed positive constant times $f_{k-1}$ for the monomial comparison, and a fixed positive constant times $(f_k-f_{k-1})$ for the $j$ comparison. The other monomials' extra $(f_k-f_{k-1})\gamma\cdot j'$ is $o(f_{k-1})$. The claim holds.
* **Tuple counts.** The tuple count is $O((1+\log N)^r)$, since $K(N)\sim\log N/\sqrt{\log\log N}$. The denominators divide $p^{r\max e_{a,k}}$, which is polynomial in $n_k$. The discard intervals have total length $o(N)$ because $n_{k-1}/n_k\to0$. All hypotheses of the all-prefix criterion hold. No divisibility $t\mid n_k$ is needed, because the pole depth is $e_{a,k}$ explicitly and not tied to $v_p(k)$.
* **Liouville.** The truncated rational values have denominators dividing $Db^{rn_k}p^{r\max e}$. The error is $O_P(b^{-n_{k+1}})$ on the compact box $[0,1]^m$. Since $n_{k+1}/n_k\approx e^{\sqrt{\log k}}\to\infty$ and $\log_b$ of the denominator is $rn_k(1+o(1))$, the errors beat every power of the denominator. $P(X)$ is irrational by normality, so infinitely many distinct approximants occur.
* **Algebraic independence and joint uniformity.** A relation $P=0$ contradicts normality. Distinct nonconstant monomials give nonzero nonconstant polynomials under nonzero integer combinations. Multidimensional Weyl applies.
* **Tied exponents.** If the $\gamma$'s were $\mathbb Q$-dependent, e.g. $\gamma=(1,2)$, then $z_1^2$ and $z_2$ tie in $\gamma\cdot\nu$ (check script part 3). That shows the hypothesis is *used* by this method. It does not show the theorem fails.

## Objections

**O1. Attribution: the repository's own source list contains a direct precedent for §3, and the new section does not cite it.** [phase18/sources.md](../../phase18/sources.md) records Kano and Shiokawa (1993), *Rings of normal and nonnormal numbers*, Israel J. Math. 84, as a "direct precedent for polynomial closure (Track E2) and normal Liouville rings" built from rapidly growing generator schedules. The same file records Bugeaud (2002), Becher–Heiber–Slaman (2015) and Korobov/Kano. Sections 2 and 3 cite none of them, and no priority sentence is given. I could not read Kano–Shiokawa and rely on the repository's summary. If that summary is right, the unconditional claim "every nonconstant rational polynomial in all the generators is normal and Liouville" is not first-in-field. What is plausibly new is the shared prime $p$, the separation of the generators by $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent pole growth rates $\gamma_a$, and the weighted construction that also covers a single rapid-index schedule in §2. **Fix:** add a priority paragraph in the style of the Phase 19 notes' "historical scope" sections, and mark the comparison as unassessed unless the original is read.

**O2. State explicitly which kind of compatibility each result gives.** The three results are not of the same strength, and a reader can conflate them.

| Result | What is proved | What is not |
|---|---|---|
| joint-rate.md | The shifts of $(x_1,\dots,x_d)$ are jointly equidistributed, with a rate. Equivalently every nonzero integer combination $\sum h_jx_j$ has the rate. This is *special compatibility*: a chosen $p$-adic direction makes all integer linear forms behave. | Nothing about products $x_ix_j$ or any polynomial of degree $\ge2$ in the $x_j$. Nothing about algebraic independence of the $x_j$. |
| nonlinear §2 | *Ring closure for one generator*: $\mathbb Q[X]\setminus\mathbb Q$ consists of normal numbers. | Nothing for several generators. |
| nonlinear §3 | *Ring closure for several generators*: $\mathbb Q[X_1,\dots,X_m]\setminus\mathbb Q$ consists of normal numbers, so the $X_a$ are algebraically independent. | The generators share the prime $p$ and the schedule $n_k$ by design. Nothing about a *different* family on the same schedule. |

joint-rate.md already says it is a full-torus statement and disclaims atomic-subtorus mixtures, which is accurate. It does not say that no polynomial closure is claimed. It should: its $x_j$ share a prime and a schedule, but their products enter none of the proof. A one-sentence "scope: linear forms only; polynomial closure for a shared schedule is the subject of nonlinear.md, with different hypotheses" would close this. The §1 square theorem and the opening of nonlinear.md are partial closure (degree at most two), which is a third level. A short classification line at the top would help.

**O3. Hypotheses left implicit in §2.** (a) §2 begins "put $X=\sum p^{-sv_p(i)}b^{-n_i}$" without saying $s\ge1$ is an integer. The proof needs it: $s=0$ has no poles, and a non-integer $s$ has no $p$-adic valuation $-sv$. (b) "$ti\mid n_i$ ensures the second hypothesis by lifting the exponent" should name odd $p$ and the identity $v_p(b^{n}-1)=a+v_p(n/t)$. (c) The theorem uses strict monotonicity of $n_i$ and the multiple-of-$t$ condition only through the inputs, so the statement is fine. But the proof of $n_i/i^a\to\infty$ says "using monotonicity" while the iteration of $n_{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}$ alone suffices.

**O4. Executable coverage.** `scripts/phase19_nonlinear.py` is described under "Exact checks" as covering the quadratic-shell material of §1. Sections 2 and 3 have no executable check, and joint-rate.md has none at all. My script covers only (i) the resultant divisibility behind the polynomial bound, (ii) $B(a)$, the nonvanishing of $S_l$ and inequality (3) on the actual rapid schedule up to $m\le3^7$ or $5^4$, and (iii) the necessity of linear independence for uniqueness. It does **not** test the grid reduction, the discard-set density, the shifted-prefix valuations of $P(X)$ itself at a fixed precision, the real-tail transfer, or any discrepancy value. A check of $v_p$ of the normalized prefix $p^{rsJ}R_n\bmod p^L$ for $P=z^2$ at a toy rapid schedule, counting the $n$ with valuation at least $L$, would close the gap.

**O5. Citation details and an exponent that is not optimized.**
* The ETK reference is "Kuipers and Niederreiter, Chapter 2, §2", linked to a third-party PDF host. A secondary citation I found places the inequality at p. 116 of the 1974 edition, in Chapter 2. I did not open the book or the linked PDF, so the section and theorem numbering (I recall Theorem 2.5) is unverified. Cite the printed edition (Wiley 1974, Dover 2006) and give the theorem number only after checking. The version used needs the sup over boxes anchored at the origin, which is the star form with the $2^d$ corner reduction the note applies.
* "The classical generic-point theorem gives qualitative realizations" has no citation. For the *full torus* with Lebesgue as the target, qualitative existence is just Borel's theorem (almost every point), and the generic-point theorem (Sigmund 1974) matters only for non-Lebesgue targets, as in addition.md. Cite or drop.
* The "Grid proof" link points to grids.md. The complete-period lemma actually used (the orbit contains a full inverse image of the residues mod $p^a$, so a full period sums to $0$ when $e>a$) is in [phase18/principal.md, "Complete periods"](../../phase18/principal.md#complete-periods-and-the-unfinished-gap). Link both.
* The exponent $1/(3d+1)$ is an artifact of charging all blocks with $k\le k_0\asymp H^d$ their full length, which gives the $H^{3d}$ term. It is a valid upper bound and is not claimed optimal, but one sentence saying so would match the Phase 19 style (see the minor point in the addition.md review).
* The constant depends on the choice of $\ell$. The statement says "$\ell$ a prime different from $p$" and later "choose $\ell=2$"; fix $\ell=2$ in the statement.

## Minor tightenings

1. §2: "$m>K/p$" is derived from $n_{\lfloor K/p\rfloor}=o(n_K)$. It follows directly: $m$ is the largest multiple of $p^{J-H}$ at most $K$, so $m>K-p^{J-H}\ge K(1-p^{-H})$.
2. §2: the number of members of $S_H$ through $K$ is $O(p^{H+1}\log K)$. State it where the "$+1$" in $\sum(w_m+1)$ is absorbed.
3. §3 uses $m$ for the number of generators, while §2 uses $m$ for an index. Rename one.
4. §3 says $X_a$ are "Liouville" in the first paragraph only for $P(X)$. The conclusion holds for each $X_a$ as the case $P=z_a$, which is worth one clause.
5. The $x_j$ in joint-rate.md have initial states $1/q_k,\ \theta/q_k,\ \theta^2/q_k,\dots$ with $v_p(\theta^{j-1})=j-1$. Coordinate $j$ therefore has denominator $q_k/p^{\min(j-1,\log_pq_k)}$. The proof is unaffected because only $v_p(P(\theta))$ enters, but this explains why $h_j\neq0$ for $j\ge2$ already costs $p^{j-1}$ in the resultant bound.
