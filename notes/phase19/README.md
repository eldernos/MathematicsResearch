# Phase 19. Arithmetic of discrepancy and unbounded-support normality

In any integer radix the ordinary discrepancy limits of $x,y,x+y$ are exactly the triples in the unit cube satisfying
$$
 \max(0,\alpha+\beta-1)\le\gamma\le1-|\alpha-\beta|.
$$
The five extremal triples have explicit rational-orbit models. Balancing their time proportions on one cubic schedule gives every admissible triple simultaneously to $O_{b,p}(N^{-1/3})$ for all prefixes, using positive coefficients. Inputs may be computable and algebraically independent. Lean verifies unconditional necessity and sufficiency in every radix. The uniform rate and independence proofs are on paper. The [addition proof](addition.md), [Lean module](../../NormalityChecker/AdditionSpectrum.lean) and [verification record](verification.md) distinguish the qualitative theorem, its formal components and the quantitative arithmetic witnesses.

## Operations

- For $d$ summands and their total, the $d+1$ discrepancy deficits are exactly the points of the unit cube for which each coordinate is at most the sum of the others. Finite collections of rational linear forms also admit convergent polyhedral approximations to their entire spectra. The six-form obstruction in the proof shows why separate addition-triple inequalities can miss a dependency. Full-dimensional Haar targets have an explicit joint **box** rate via an algebraic $p$-adic direction and a multidimensional exponential-sum estimate. [Finite-form spectra](addition.md) · [Joint box rate](joint-rate.md)
- At $N\ge2$, $1-D_N(x-y)$ metrizes the circle modulo radix fixed points. Its limsup vanishes exactly on differences which preserve every input's numerical discrepancy. The rational scalars preserving the entire kernel are exactly $\mathbb Z[1/b]$; individual rational scalings have a residue-density test. When $D_N(mx)\to1$, possible limiting discrepancies of $x$ fill $[1/m_0,1]$, where $m_0$ is the off-radix part of $|m|$. The note gives separate finite bounds for forward and reverse dilation and counterexamples for anchored or multidimensional versions of the sharp translation formula. [Metric and proofs](geometry.md) · [Lean declarations](../../NormalityChecker/DiscrepancyGeometry.lean)
- Sparse positive digit expansions supported on a missing-digit set of index dimension $\sigma$ have worst numerical translation error of exact order $(\log N/N)^{1-\sigma}$ under the hypotheses in the note. Two such transcendental preservers can multiply to an explicit non-preserving rational. The binary literature also has two mean-zero preservers with a normal product when signed sparse representations are allowed. [Constructions and precedents](products-of-remainders.md)

## All-prime support and nonlinear images

- In the binary series with positions $16^{p^j}$ for **every odd prime** $p$, use weight $p^{-j}$ except at 3, where it is $3^{-2j}$. This number is normal and Liouville, with $D_N=O(1/\log N)$ at all cutoffs. The exact grid comes from the order of the stride that fixes the complementary denominator. A grid comparable to $L=\lfloor(\log_2 n)/4\rfloor$ always fits, while the **maximal** grid has unbounded ratio to $L$. Other schedules allow superpolynomial reduced denominators or selectable prime depths. Return times, exhaustion and the full-period interval count with a complementary modulus have Lean proofs; quantitative normality and the Liouville property have paper proofs. [Exact grid and bounds](grids.md) · [CRT formalization](../../NormalityChecker/SurvivingGrid.lean) · [Interval counts](../../NormalityChecker/PrimePowerGrid.lean)
- The flagship number $C=\sum_{k\ge1}3^{-v_3(k)}2^{-2k^3}$ and its **square are jointly binary normal**. A localized counting estimate isolates primitive grid jumps of the limiting $3$-adic mass. A second-moment argument and the density theorem make every level set null; the all-prefix denominator criterion then applies. The proof covers all total-degree-two polynomials in finitely many distinct valuation orders on the same power schedule of degree at least three. It supplies neither a rate nor a countability assertion about the zero set. [Analytic proof](nonlinear.md) · [Independent nonlinear audit](reviews/nonlinear.md)
- Every nonconstant rational polynomial is normal for a single valuation-weighted generator on certain schedules with $\log n_k\sim(\log k)^2$ and even $n_{k+1}/n_k\to1$. There are also polynomial-normal Liouville algebras on distinct prime axes: each coordinate uses just its own coefficient prime, and its numerators are chosen by congruences at the other primes. These are constructed compatible families. [Rapidly varying schedules](nonlinear.md) · [Independent-prime construction](independent-axes.md)

## Classical arithmetic and remaining problems

The horizontal Taylor rows $L_d=Z_d/d$ of $\log2$ are deterministic. Finite rational combinations of the $Z_d$ and a constant preserve numerical discrepancy exactly when all the coefficients are dyadic. A finite cycle algorithm gives $D_N(L_3)\to2/3$ and $(D^-(L_5),D^+(L_5))=(1/5,8/15)$. The conclusion concerns actual named summands; summing all of them still requires a global argument. For the actual shifted Taylor prefixes the order of the radix modulo the surviving large primes grows faster than every power of the cutoff, even after one pole is removed. This rules out the full-period and fixed-complement grid sampling strategies on polynomial-length windows. An infinite signed transform of the logarithm gives a sharp moving-tail threshold and inherits the same obstruction for its chronological prefixes. [Exact row spectra](logarithm-rays.md) · [Period lower bound](taylor-periods.md) · [Group cancellation and other classical tests](classical.md)

For each prime $p\ge5$, the earliest positive dyadic group containing the Taylor term of index $p$ finishes at exactly $p k(p)$, where $k(p)$ is the first positive subset cancellation at $p$ including multiplier one. The odd denominators left by that local cancellation can be repaired at the same cutoff. The unconditional upper bound is $k(p)=O(\sqrt p)$; a matching logarithmic upper bound would need new control of the subset residues. A separate argument bounds the mean log-denominator loss through $X$ by $O(X^{3/7})$. [Proofs and assumptions](classical.md) · [Independent derivation](reviews/classical.md)

For the established dyadic Stoneham leading mass, the complementary open plateaus carry the entire Lebesgue measure, reducing a sufficient test to nonvanishing on these plateaus. The curvature argument that resolves the flagship square does not apply to the degree-two resonant combination of the classical parameter-two Stoneham number or the simultaneous exceptional shell indices of the unmodified mixed-prime product. The Phase 17 Erdős–Borwein finite-statistic thresholds still require actual divisor correlations to establish a bias. We obtain no strict upper discrepancy bound for the whole $\log2$ orbit. [Dyadic and mixed-prime scope](nonlinear.md) · [Earlier correlation obstruction](../phase17/erdos-borwein-bias.md)
