# Partial coverage for ln 2, π and the golden ratio

Write $D_N^{(b)}(x)$ for the extreme interval discrepancy of $\{b^j x\}$ at $0\le j<N$, and $D_\infty^{(b)}$ for its limit superior. Intervals are half-open. In base two let $t(x)$ be the lower density of starts of adjacent unequal digits, using the terminating expansion for dyadic rationals. A transition occurs exactly when $\{2^j x\}\in[1/4,3/4)$.

## Fixed quality and unfinished blocks

**Theorem (Lean).** In a prefix of length $N>0$, take disjoint pieces containing $G$ observations altogether. If the interval discrepancy on each nonempty piece is at most $d\le1$, then
$D_N^{(b)}(x)\le1-(1-d)G/N$. For a new choice of pieces in each prefix, $\liminf G/N\ge g$ gives $D_\infty^{(b)}(x)\le1-g(1-d)$. For one interval of length $\ell$, the centered count on a piece of size $s$ has absolute value at most $ds$ and each uncovered observation costs at most one. Sum these bounds before taking the supremum. The pieces may come from different series, rational models or starting indices. A cut through the last piece requires either a certificate for the shortened piece or charging its positions as uncovered. [Kernel proof](../NormalityChecker/QualityCoverage.lean) · [Restriction and endpoint bounds](../NormalityChecker/IntervalCoverage.lean)

In particular $d=1/3$ and $g=3/20000$ suffice for $D_\infty^{(2)}(\ln2)\le9999/10000$. Every qualifying twelve-position piece must contain at least two transitions, by testing $[1/4,3/4)$. The previously tested small-denominator blocks impose considerably more structure. The new half-length prefix approximates any fixed twelve shifts with error at most $2^{11}\,4/(N+1)$; the model at $N+r$ divided by $2^r$ also gives a certified model at $N$ with $2^{-r}$ times its error bound. Both observations concern accuracy. The proportion of starts at which the model's orbit has the required interval distribution remains open. [Half-length denominator theorem](strided-logarithm.md) · [Small-denominator search](complementary-models.md#how-many-additional-positions-are-certified)

**Finite exact computation.** Direct enclosures of the first 1,024 shifts also allow testing all twelve-position blocks without imposing a small-denominator constraint. Enclose $\ln2$ in a single certified binary cylinder, multiply its endpoints by $m2^n$, evaluate the discrepancy of the orbit of the lower endpoint, and add twice the maximum shift error. For $m=1,2,3,5$ respectively, 650, 650, 641 and 626 starts pass at quality $1/3$. Earliest-finish packing covers 888, 888, 888 and 864 distinct positions respectively. The older small-denominator test packed 48 positions. The direct computation checks local distribution of the constant itself. None of these finite shares bounds its limiting lower coverage. [Exact test](../scripts/strided_logarithm.py)

## Intermittent dyadic shells

Put $f(n)=1-\cos(2\pi 2^n x)$ and suppose that in each $h$ consecutive sufficiently late shells $[2^j,2^{j+1})$ at least one has mean at least $e\ge0$. If $2^k\le N<2^{k+1}$, the most recent $h$ completed shells alone give $\sum_{n<N} f(n)\ge e\,2^{k-h}$. Thus the lower prefix mean is at least $e/2^{h+1}$. Summing the disjoint groups of $h$ earlier completed shells improves this to $e/[2(2^h-1)]$: the lower bound on the normalized sum is $(e/2)\sum_{a=1}^{\lfloor(k-K)/h\rfloor}2^{-ah}$ once $K$ is beyond the initial exceptions. This argument permits an arbitrary unfinished shell and uses only nonnegativity there. Certificates from rational models may differ on every shell. An average circle error $\eta$ in the model reduces its certified deficit by at most $2\pi\eta$ by the cosine Lipschitz bound. Vanishing errors leave the limiting bound intact.

The first-frequency theorem gives $t(x)\ge\frac14\liminf N^{-1}\sum_{n<N} f(n)$ and $D_\infty^{(2)}(x)\le\max(2/3,1-t(x))$. With $h=4$, $e=0.0128$, even the single-group bound gives $t(x)\ge0.0001$. The kernel checks the resulting $0.9999$ implication for arbitrary real $x$. The sum over all groups shows on paper that $e=0.012$ would suffice. Existence of those good shells for $\ln2$ is an arithmetic hypothesis. [Kernel shell theorem](../NormalityChecker/QualityCoverage.lean) · [Fourier-to-transition estimate](../NormalityChecker/FirstFrequency.lean)

## Multiplying a constant and returning to the binary orbit

For a positive integer $m$ put $E(u)=1-\cos(2\pi u)$. The addition formula for sine gives $|\sin(mv)|\le m|\sin v|$. Squaring at $v=\pi u$ gives $E(mu)\le m^2E(u)$. A lower bound $a$ on the limiting first-frequency deficit for $mx$ therefore gives $t(x)\ge a/(4m^2)$. This extends both coverage tests to alternative expansions of fixed integer multiples, with an explicit cost. [Kernel inequality](../NormalityChecker/StridedLogarithm.lean)

There is a stronger transition transfer for large odd multipliers. Remove the power of two from $m$, since this shifts the digit sequence only finitely. For its odd part $u>1$ put $R=1+\lceil\log_2 u\rceil$. If $x$ has no transition at $n,\ldots,n+R-1$, the $R+1$ corresponding bits are identical. Consequently $\|2^n x\|\le2^{-(R+1)}$, whence $\|2^n ux\|<1/4$. Every transition of $ux$ charges to at least one of the next $R$ starts of $x$. Each start can receive at most $R$ charges. Thus $t(mx)\le R t(x)$, including endpoint loss $O(R)$ before normalization. For odd part 1 there is equality. In particular, a lower limiting Fourier deficit $a$ for $mx$ yields $t(x)\ge a/(4R)$ as well. The discrepancy upper bound follows by the binary transition theorem. This proof uses the observable that is transferred; it makes no invariance assumption about numerical interval discrepancy. [Binary transition theorem](../NormalityChecker/SharpDiscrepancy.lean)

## Separating long runs from positive density

Marcovecchio's irrationality measure $\mu(\ln2)<3.57455391$ rules out a constant binary run of length exceeding $(3.575-1)n$ starting immediately after digit $n$, for all sufficiently large $n$. Such a run of length $L$ would put the logarithm within $2^{-(n+L)}$ of an integer divided by $2^n$. Reduction can only decrease the denominator, and bounded reduced denominators are excluded by irrationality. Iterating over successive run endpoints gives at least $\log N/\log(3.575)-O(1)$ transitions. This paper consequence of a published theorem improves the geometric constant 34 in the repository's self-contained Lean estimate. It permits logarithmically many runs in a prefix. [Marcovecchio](https://ricerca.unich.it/handle/11564/648605) · [Self-contained Lean count](../NormalityChecker/LogTwoTransitions.lean)

The gap, cylinder and truncated-moment targets all require frequency information. In the spike formulation $v_n=-\log|\cos(\pi2^n\ln2)|$, a transition followed by a run of length $r$ can cost up to $(r+1)\log2$. Bounding every individual run by a multiple of its starting index gives no uniform bound on the mean squared cost or on the mean excess above a fixed cutoff. The diagonal residue theorem excludes many repeated residues of the actual numerators across indices, and the two-stride test fixes numerous residues at one index. For a length-$T$ low-transition cylinder the numerator test must use the real fractional part of the same rational, equivalently its least residue modulo the **full** denominator. Neither modular estimate controls the joint placement of those residues on a positive proportion of observations. The established run, moment, four-position miss and middle-third criteria therefore remain open at the requested thresholds. [Modular restrictions](strided-logarithm.md) · [Moment and gap criteria](classical-discrepancy.md)

## Hexadecimal observations for π

For every real $x$ one has
$$
D_\infty^{(2)}(x)\le\frac14\sum_{r=0}^3\min(1,2^r D_\infty^{(16)}(x)).
$$
Split binary positions into their four classes $4j+r$. The preimage of a test interval under multiplication by $2^r$ modulo one consists of $2^r$ half-open intervals of total length equal to the original length. Sum the interval errors and also use the trivial error of one. Each class is a hexadecimal prefix whose length differs from the others by at most one. A strict hexadecimal bound thus gives a strict binary bound. Alternatively, a lower mean $a$ of $E(16^j x)$ on all long prefixes implies a binary lower mean of at least $a/4$ by retaining the positions divisible by four. Hence $D_\infty^{(2)}(x)\le\max(2/3,1-a/16)$.

For $\pi$ the BBP moment in the repository uses $t^8$ and $P(t)=4-2t^3-t^4-t^5$ on [0,1]. Here $0\le P(t)\le4$. Using $F_d$ normalized at 16 bounds the unwrapped error at hexadecimal position $n$ by $4/[15(8n+9)T_d(31)]$, by integrating $t^{8(n+1)}$. Multiplication by $16^i$ gives the error at an additional shift $i$. As an independent representation, Machin's identity gives $\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$. For each arctangent two consecutive partial sums of $\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k q^{-(2k+1)}/(2k+1)$ enclose the value. At the 128 indices $4j$ for $128\le j<256$, exact rational enclosures certify 66 binary transitions, giving hexadecimal first-frequency mean at least $66/128$ **on this block**. A positive asymptotic mean or fixed-quality coverage share for $\pi$ remains open. [BBP integral](../NormalityChecker/BBPShadow.lean) · [Exact Machin computation](../scripts/strided_logarithm.py) · [Original BBP paper](https://www.cecm.sfu.ca/~pborwein/PAPERS/P123.pdf)

## Growing Fibonacci moduli for the golden ratio

Let $k(M)$ denote the least occupied Zeckendorf index of $M$, with $F_2=1$. On a binary transition of $\varphi$ one has $\|2^n\varphi\|\ge1/4$. The upper bound $\|M\varphi\|\le\varphi^{1-k(M)}$ and $\varphi^{-3}<1/4$ imply $k(2^n)\le3$. Applied to $\varphi-1$ (which has the same fractional bits), Bugeaud and Evertse's Theorem 3.1 therefore gives the unconditional lower bound
$$
\#\{n<N:k(2^n)\le3\}\ \gg\ \frac{(\log N)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\log\log N}}
$$
for all sufficiently large $N$. The literature theorem supplies the digit-change count. The Zeckendorf deduction is on paper using the existing kernel-verified distance estimate. [Bugeaud–Evertse](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.1560) · [Zeckendorf distance theorem](../NormalityChecker/GoldenOrbit.lean)

There is also a finite arithmetic test with a growing modulus. Choose $q=F_j$ with $q^2\ge 2^{N+4}$. The identity $|F_{j+1}-\varphi F_j|=\varphi^{-j}$ gives $|\varphi-F_{j+1}/q|<1/q^2$. For $n<N$, the residue of $2^n F_{j+1}\bmod q$ in $[q/3,2q/3]$ therefore forces a transition of $\varphi$ and a least index at most three. Choosing the first admissible $j$ gives $(N,j,\mathrm{hits})=(128,97,41),(256,189,82),(512,374,157),(1024,743,327)$. These exact counts yield a criterion along increasingly large Fibonacci moduli. The sampled orbit length is only logarithmic in the modulus, and a positive lower share of interior visits remains unproved. This is consistent with the obstruction for any fixed modulus. [Finite recurrence](../scripts/strided_logarithm.py) · [Fixed-modulus obstruction](../NormalityChecker/GoldenCongruence.lean)
