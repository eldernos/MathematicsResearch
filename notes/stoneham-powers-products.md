# Powers, polynomials and products of Stoneham numbers

For integers $`b,c\ge2`$ write $`\alpha_{b,c}=\sum_{j\ge1}c^{-j}b^{-c^j}`$. The starting index is one. In particular the square discussed here is literally the square of $`\alpha_{2,3}`$. Adding an initial term to a generator creates cross terms after squaring.

**Polynomial theorem.** Let $`p\ge3`$ be prime, $`p\nmid b`$. For every nonconstant $`P\in\mathbb Q[X]`$, $`P(\alpha_{b,p})`$ is normal in radix $`b`$. In particular $`\alpha_{2,3}^3,\alpha_{2,3}^4,\alpha_{2,3}^5`$ are binary normal. Lean verifies the theorem in every positive degree, including nonzero rational leading coefficients. The proof below explains why enumeration of insertion regions is unnecessary. [General Lean theorem](../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomial.lean) · [Upper-orthant argument](../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomialOrthant.lean) The square also has a separate kernel proof of binary normality and its complete exact denominator profile, including the small-scale exception. Each nonconstant rational polynomial value is transcendental by the established transcendence of $`\alpha_{b,p}`$ and transitivity of algebraicity. [Square theorem and exact profile](stoneham-square.md) · [Coons, transcendence appendix](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788713000682)

## The surviving numerator in every degree

Set $`d\ge1`$ and $`p^k\le N\lt p^{k+1}`$. Expand the $`d`$th power as an absolutely convergent sum over ordered $`d`$-tuples. In the shifted rational prefix $`R_N`$, retain precisely the insertions with $`\sum_a p^{j_a}\le N`$. All $`j_a\le k`$, and $`\mathrm{den}(R_N)\mid p^{dk}`$. Put $`t=N/p^k`$ and $`i_a=k-j_a`$. Consider the upward closed subset of $`\mathbb N^d`$

```math
U_t=\{i:\ \textstyle\sum_a p^{-i_a}\le t\}.
```

Over $`\mathbb Q_p`$ the following sum converges unconditionally, since only finitely many tuples have a given total degree:

```math
F(t)=\sum_{i\in U_t}p^{\sum_a i_a}.
```

Let $`w`$ be the Teichmüller unit lifting $`b^{-1}\bmod p`$. For any fixed precision $`L`$, uniformly over $`N`$ in the shell, $`p^{dk}b^{-N}R_N\equiv w^d F(t)\pmod {p^L}`$ once $`k\ge2L`$. Terms with $`\sum i_a\ge L`$ disappear. For each remaining tuple $`j_a=k-i_a\ge L`$, so $`(b^{-1})^{p^{j_a}}\equiv w\bmod p^L`$ and all the original indices are positive. This uses just the principal-unit congruence, or Euler's theorem applied at modulus $`p^L`$.

By Dickson's lemma an upward closed subset $`U`$ of $`\mathbb N^d`$ has finitely many minimal elements. For a nonempty $`U`$, let $`v_1,\dots,v_m`$ be these elements. The union of their upper orthants is $`U`$ and the intersection of any collection of the orthants has vertex equal to the coordinatewise maximum. Hence

```math
\sum_{i\in U}z^{\sum i_a}=\frac{K_U(z)}{(1-z)^d},
\qquad
K_U(z)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}}
(-1)^{|J|+1}z^{|\bigvee_{j\in J}v_j|_1}.
```

This is a formal power series identity and can also be evaluated at $`z=p`$ in the $`p`$-adic field. Inclusion-exclusion gives $`K_U(1)=1`$. As an **ordinary integer**, $`K_U(p)\equiv1\bmod(p-1)`$, so it cannot be zero for $`p\ge3`$. The argument permits cancellation to positive but finite $`p`$-adic order. It applies to $`U_t`$ with either $`\le t`$ or $`\lt t`$, since both contain all vectors with coordinates sufficiently large for every $`t\gt 0`$.

**Uniformity across shell boundaries.** Suppose no finite precision works simultaneously on $`[1,p)`$. Choose failing $`t_L`$ along precisions tending to infinity and pass to a monotone convergent subsequence in $`[1,p]`$. Along an increasing sequence strictly below its limit $`t`$, each fixed tuple eventually has the indicator of the strict cut at $`t`$. Along a decreasing sequence or a constant subsequence the weak cut applies. At any fixed precision only finitely many tuples contribute, so the corresponding limiting $`p`$-adic sum vanishes at all precisions. This contradicts the nonvanishing just proved, also for a one-sided endpoint. It follows that there is a constant $`C=C(d,p)`$ such that

```math
 p^{dk-C}\le \mathrm{den}(R_N)\le p^{dk}
```

on every sufficiently late shell. The proof gives a terminating finite search for a precision. For $`p=3`$ the first passing precisions in degrees 1 through 9 are **1, 4, 5, 9, 11, 14, 14, 18, 20**. These values are exact computations by summing the ordered-tuple multiplicities at the rational thresholds. [Finite checker](../scripts/stoneham_power_cells.py)

To pass to a polynomial, clear the coefficient denominators and handle its constant term by rational translation. At degree $`d`$, a fixed nonzero integer leading coefficient costs only its own $`p`$-valuation. Every lower-degree term has denominator dividing $`p^{(d-1)k}`$. Thus the leading pole survives for all sufficiently large $`k`$. Through position $`N`$ at most $`O_d((1+\log N)^d)`$ tuples can insert a term. On blocks of length $`\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor`$, discard the initial and incomplete blocks and those meeting an insertion; the discarded share tends to zero. On the other blocks the rational prefix is an exact radix orbit. The shifted tail tends uniformly to zero at late positions by dominated convergence over tuples, since $`\prod p^{-j_a}`$ is summable and every individual insertion is eventually past. Reduced denominators grow uniformly on the retained blocks, have fixed prime support and are polynomial in block length. The fixed-support short-orbit theorem and Weyl's criterion give normality. Nonzero rational scaling and rational translation preserve normality. [Kernel orbit estimate](../NormalityChecker/SmoothOrbit.lean) · [Arithmetic transfer](../NormalityChecker/ArithmeticCertificate.lean) · [Affine normality equivalences](../NormalityChecker/FourierTest.lean)

## Related parameters and the limit of the argument

Lean also verifies normality for a product of generators $`\alpha_{b^{m_i},p}`$, for arbitrary fixed positive integer radix dilations $`m_i`$, and finite rational combinations with a unique leading monomial. The threshold uses $`\sum m_i p^{-i_i}`$ and the frozen unit becomes $`w^{\sum m_i}`$. [Kernel product theorem](../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomial.lean)

**Products on the same prime axis (Lean).** For fixed positive integers $`e_1,\ldots,e_d`$, the product $`\prod_a\alpha_{b,p^{e_a}}`$ is normal in base $`b`$ under the same assumptions on $`b,p`$. In the proof restrict $`i_a`$ to $`h_a+e_a z_a`$, where $`h_a`$ is the residue of $`k`$ modulo $`e_a`$ and $`z_a\ge0`$. On each of finitely many choices of $`k\bmod\mathrm{lcm}(e_1,\ldots,e_d)`$ the allowed $`z`$ form a nonempty upper set. The geometric denominator is now $`\prod_a(1-p^{e_a})`$. The numerator is $`p^{\sum h_a}`$ times a signed inclusion-exclusion sum of powers of $`p`$, still congruent to 1 modulo $`p-1`$. Strict cuts and the compactness argument apply verbatim. [Kernel theorem for independent positive parameter exponents and radix dilations](../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomial.lean)

Thus, for example, $`\alpha_{2,3}\alpha_{2,9}`$ is binary normal. A multivariate polynomial with a unique top-degree monomial in such generators also satisfies this argument. Different monomials of the same maximal degree can have canceling leading residues.

There is a composite-parameter version. Suppose $`c\gt 2`$, $`\gcd(b,c)=1`$, and some prime divisor $`p`$ of $`c`$ has $`b^{-c^j}`$ converging in $`\mathbb Z_p`$ to a unit. Equivalently its residue modulo $`p`$ is eventually constant, since $`p\mid c`$ kills the principal-unit part; for $`p=2`$ the residue condition is automatic. Normalize by $`c^{dk}`$ and use the same numerator at $`z=c`$. Nonvanishing follows from $`K_U(c)\equiv1\bmod(c-1)`$ and yields a growing $`p`$-power in the reduced denominator, which is supported on the primes of $`c`$. This includes all prime powers $`c\gt 2`$ and all even $`c\gt 2`$ coprime to $`b`$. For $`c=2`$, the leading sum for the square vanishes on $`3/2\le t\lt 2`$: its upward set contains all pairs except $`(0,0)`$, so the sum is $`(1-2)^{-2}-1=0`$. A smaller denominator can still grow there. A universal polynomial theorem for every Stoneham parameter remains open in this work.

## A product on different prime axes

**Theorem.** Lean proves that the product is irrational and that the binary discrepancy of $`\alpha_{2,3}\alpha_{2,5}`$ satisfies $`D_\infty\le43/45`$. A paper corollary proves transcendence. [Kernel discrepancy and irrationality theorems](../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFive.lean)

The estimate uses an isolated insertion and gives a positive uniformly distributed share in every long prefix. Full binary normality remains open here.

Put $`P=3^k`$ and choose $`Q=5^\ell`$ with $`P/3\le Q\lt 5P/3`$, possible on every sufficiently late scale because the ratio of these endpoints is five. At $`s=P+Q`$ the ordered pair of exponents $`(k,\ell)`$ is the only insertion. Indeed smaller pairs with both components at most $`P,Q`$ give sums at most $`\max(P+Q/5,P/3+Q)`$. A pair exceeding either component gives a sum at least $`\min(3P,5Q)`$. The gaps on the two sides of $`s`$ are each at least $`4P/15`$, since $`Q/P\in[1/3,5/3)`$. Both adjacent windows of this length are contained in $`[P,3P)`$. Round down their lengths and take them half-open, with the insertion in the right window.

Compare the left rational prefix transported to $`s`$ with the actual prefix at $`s`$. Their difference is $`1/(PQ)`$, of 3-adic valuation $`-k`$. At least one has valuation at most $`-k`$. Throughout its clean window the reduced denominator is supported on $`\{3,5\}`$, is at least $`P`$, and is $`O(P^2)`$. The true shifted tail tends to zero as above. The short-orbit bound applies uniformly in the starting numerator and over intervals of the specified length, so one such window on every late ternary shell is uniformly distributed up to $`o(P)`$ count error. For $`3^k\le N\lt 3^{k+1}`$ even the *completed* shells supply a lower share of at least $`(4/15)/(2\cdot3)=2/45`$. Uniform pieces and the trivial cost on the complement give the stated bound. [Uniform-share theorem](../NormalityChecker/PartialUniformity.lean)

These uniform pieces also imply irrationality: a rational orbit has finite eventual support, so some interval of positive length misses it. For transcendence, truncate at the isolated insertion $`s`$. The reduced denominator is at most $`2^s O(P^2)`$ and has prime factors only in $`\{2,3,5\}`$. The next insertion is at least $`s+4P/15`$, and the sum of the unshifted coefficient weights is finite. The positive error is at most a constant times $`2^{-s-4P/15}`$. Since $`s\le8P/3`$, these infinitely many distinct rational approximants have exponent greater than any fixed $`\mu\lt 1.1`$ for all large $`k`$ (in particular choose $`1\lt \mu\lt 1.1`$). Ridout's restricted-denominator theorem gives transcendence. This use of Ridout is a paper deduction. [Ridout](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300001339)

**General two-parameter consequence (paper).** Let $`c,d\ge2`$ be multiplicatively independent integers, both coprime to the construction radix $`b`$. The product $`\alpha_{b,c}\alpha_{b,d}`$ is transcendental and has radix-$`b`$ discrepancy strictly below one. Choose a compact interval of ratios with nonempty interior inside $`(1/(d-1),c-1)`$; this interval exists since the parameters cannot both equal 2. Narrow it to length less than one period in log base $`d`$. For a set of $`k`$ with bounded gaps there is an $`l`$ with $`d^l/c^k`$ in this interval: irrational rotation by $`\log_d c`$ visits every nonempty open arc syndetically. Finitely many inverse translates of the arc cover the circle by compactness, which gives the required bounded-gap statement at every sufficiently late start.

Write $`P=c^k`$, $`Q=d^l`$. Each chosen ratio is bounded away from the ends of the admissible interval, so the bounds $`\max(P+Q/d,P/c+Q)`$ and $`\min(cP,dQ)`$ leave gaps of length at least $`\delta P`$ on both sides of $`P+Q`$ for some fixed $`\delta\gt 0`$. Shrink $`\delta`$ so that both windows lie in $`[P,cP)`$. At least one side retains the full size of the insertion pole at any selected prime dividing $`c`$, since the radix is a unit there. All model denominators divide a product of one power of $`c`$ and one of $`d`$, of size $`O(P^2)`$, and hence have fixed prime support. Bounded gaps between good shells give a positive lower share of late uniform windows at every cutoff. A rational orbit is impossible as above. The infinitely many right-side insertion gaps give a fixed approximation exponent greater than one on denominators supported on the primes of $`bcd`$, so Ridout gives transcendence. This conclusion does not require algebraic independence of the two factors. The argument establishes strict discrepancy, while full normality for arbitrary independent parameters remains open here.

## Prior normal rings and a change of radix

Wagner constructed rings all of whose nonzero elements are normal to one base and nonnormal to another. Kano and Shiokawa likewise established polynomial families under strong growth and separation hypotheses on their generators. For the standard Stoneham sequence the relevant exponents are $`\lambda_j=j`$ and $`\mu_j=p^j`$. Here $`\lambda_j/\mu_{j-1}\to0`$ and $`(\log\mu_j)/\lambda_j=\log p`$, whereas the cited normal-ring constructions use divergence hypotheses. Their existence theorems alone do not identify this particular generator as a member. Coons proves transcendence of the standard constants. Bailey and Crandall prove normality of the individual coprime-parameter constants. We make no claim of historical priority for the specific square or polynomial theorem. [Wagner](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X85711146) · [Kano–Shiokawa, especially Theorems 4–5](https://www.math.keio.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/91006.pdf) · [Kano's comparison with Wagner's hypotheses](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/10356/ojm30_04_15.pdf) · [Bailey–Crandall](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2002.10504704)

There is a further distinction for the specific binary generator. Kano–Shiokawa's Remark 2 observes that every irrational in the rings considered there is Liouville. The standard $`\alpha_{2,3}`$ has irrationality exponent 3. For a direct proof let $`x_k`$ be the prefix through $`j=k`$, $`q_k`$ its reduced denominator, and $`e_k=\alpha_{2,3}-x_k`$. The last term forces both the full binary and the full ternary part of $`q_k=3^k2^{3^k}`$ (each prime has a unique largest pole). The next term bounds the positive tail from above and below up to an absolute factor, so $`e_k=q_k^{-3+o(1)}`$, and $`q_{k+1}=q_k^{3+o(1)}`$. For a large denominator $`q`$ choose the first $`k`$ such that $`2q q_k e_k\lt 1`$. Failure at $`k-1`$ gives $`q_k\le q^{3/2+o(1)}`$. Every reduced $`a/q\ne x_k`$ then has error at least $`1/(2q q_k)\ge q^{-5/2-o(1)}`$ by separation of rational numbers. A prefix itself has exponent $`3+o(1)`$. This proves both bounds on the irrationality exponent and rules out membership of this generator in those particular Liouville rings. It makes no assertion about other possible prior proofs of the normality theorem.

For base three, multiplying a standard binary prefix by $`3^N`$ clears its ternary factors once $`N\ge j`$, but leaves the dyadic denominator. In fact the unshifted prefix through $`j=k`$ has **exactly** $`2^{3^k}`$ in its reduced denominator: the last insertion alone has maximal 2-adic pole. A ternary shift has no effect on that exponent. The unshifted tail is comparable to $`3^{-k-1}2^{-3^{k+1}}`$, so a prefix accurate near ternary position $`N`$ must have $`3^k`$ of order at least $`N`$ (up to logarithmic terms). The dyadic part of that model's denominator is then exponential in the position. This explains why the polynomial-size fixed-support argument for the binary square does not transfer to the base-three question. Bailey and Borwein explicitly discuss that open question in §3 and establish nonnormality in base six. [Published problem](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/nonnormality.pdf)

In the alternate radix, the Subspace Theorem gives an unconditional separation estimate for distinct ternary shifts of this binary constant. Its ordinary ternary factor complexity grows faster than linearly. The proof does not decide ternary normality. [Separation and word complexity](ternary-stoneham-complexity.md)
