### 1.1 CM coefficients, index mapping, and ring class field (lines 7–22, 60)
- **Index mapping.** Since $\eta(\tau) = q^{1/24}\prod_{j \ge 1}(1 - q^j)$, we have $\eta(12\tau)^2 = q \prod_{j \ge 1}(1 - q^{12j})^2 = \sum_{m \ge 0} a(m) q^{12m + 1}$. Thus $a(m) = c(12m+1)$ and $c(l) = 0$ for $l \not\equiv 1 \pmod{12}$. Writing $X = 12N + 1$ and $S_0(Y) = \#(\mathcal{N} \cap [1, Y])$ where $\mathcal{N} = \{l \ge 1 : c(l) \ne 0\}$, we have $1 \in \mathcal{N}$ (corresponding to $m_0 = 0$) and $S = \#\{0 < m_i \le N\} = S_0(X) - 1$. **Verified.**
- **Support and multiplicative sign.** By Serre's weight-one CM eigenform identification (Glasgow Math. J. 27 (1985), 203–221) and the explicit $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ Hecke character (reconstructed in `GF-29` §1 and `Phase 22` §4), $c(p^e) = (e+1)\epsilon_p^e$ for $p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$, $c(p^{2e}) = (-\chi_{-4}(p))^e \in \{\pm 1\}$ and $c(p^{2e+1}) = 0$ for $p \in \mathcal{P} = \{p \text{ prime} : p \equiv 5, 7, 11 \pmod{12}\}$, and $c(2^e) = c(3^e) = 0$ ($e \ge 1$). Every $v^2 u$ with $p \mid v \Rightarrow p \in \mathcal{P}$ and $p \mid u \Rightarrow p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$ satisfies $v^2 u \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$ automatically (since $p^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$ for all $\gcd(p, 6) = 1$), so $\mathcal{N} = \{v^2 u\}$ with unique decomposition. Crucially, since $e + 1 > 0$, $\operatorname{sgn}(c(u)) = \prod_{p \equiv 1(12)} \epsilon_p^{v_p(u)}$ is **completely multiplicative** on the semigroup $\mathcal{U} = \{u \ge 1 : p \mid u \Rightarrow p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}\}$, including when $p \mid m$ in a factorization $u = pm$. **Verified.**
- **Ring class field and Frobenius classes.** For the order $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[6i]$ of discriminant $-144 = -2^4 \cdot 3^2$ in $E = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, the class group $\operatorname{Cl}(-144) \cong C_4$ consists of $[x^2+36y^2]$ (order $1$), $[4x^2+9y^2]$ (order $2$), and $[5x^2 \pm 4xy + 8y^2]$ (order $4$). The ring class field $K$ has $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong C_4 \rtimes C_2 = D_4$, with index-$2$ cyclic subgroup $H = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}(i)) \cong C_4$ and central singleton conjugacy classes $C_+ = \{1\}$ ($p = x^2 + 36y^2$) and $C_- = \{r^2\}$ ($p = 4x^2 + 9y^2$), each of Chebotarev density $1/8$. Indeed, $K = \mathbb{Q}(i, \sqrt[4]{-3})$ (splitting field of $X^4 + 3$, ramified only at $2, 3$); our independent Python check verified $p = x^2 + 36y^2 \iff (-3)^{(p-1)/4} \equiv 1 \pmod p$ on all $4{,}472$ primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$ up to $200{,}000$ with $0$ mismatches. Because $K$ ramifies only at $2, 3$ while every $\mathcal{P}$-smooth modulus $q$ satisfies $\gcd(q, 6) = 1$, $K \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)$ is unramified at every finite prime and therefore equals $\mathbb{Q}$ by Minkowski's theorem. **Verified.**

### 1.2 Endpoint-selection measure and lost mass (lines 46–48)
- Fix $V \ge 1$ and $0 < e < b < 1/4$, and put $I = (X^e, X^b]$. For $u \in \mathcal{U}$ with distinct prime factors $p_1, \dots, p_k \in I$ of multiplicities $e_1, \dots, e_k \ge 1$, the pairs $(p, m)$ with $pm = u$ and $p \in I$ correspond bijectively to the distinct primes $p_j$, with $m = u/p_j$.
  - If $e_j = 1$, then $\omega_I(u/p_j) = k - 1$, giving weight $1/k$.
  - If $e_j \ge 2$, then $\omega_I(u/p_j) = k$, giving weight $1/(k+1) < 1/k$.
  - Summing over $j = 1, \dots, k$ gives $w(u) = \sum_{j=1}^k (1 + \omega_I(u/p_j))^{-1} \in [0, 1]$, equal to $1$ whenever $k \ge 1$ and every prime factor in $I$ is simple.
  - Because $(1 + \omega_I(m))^{-1}$ depends solely on $m$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(c(v^2 pm)) = \operatorname{sgn}(c(v^2)) \epsilon_p \operatorname{sgn}(c(m))$ holds even when $p \mid m$, for fixed $v$ and sign $\sigma$ the selected measure in $u$ is exactly the sum of two nonnegative convolutions. **Verified.**
- **Discarded mass.**
  - Repeated prime in $I$: at most $\sum_{p > X^e} X/p^2 = O(X^{1-e}) = o(S)$.
  - Cut $v > V$: $\sum_{V < v \le X^{1/4}} S_0(X/v^2) + \sum_{X^{1/4} < v \le X^{1/2}} X/v^2 \ll S/V + O(X^{3/4})$.
  - Cut $l \le X^{1-b}$: at most $X^{1-b} = o(S)$.
  - Cut $\omega_I(u) = 0$: since $p \in I$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$) allows arbitrary multiplicity in $\mathcal{N}$, $\#\{l \in \mathcal{N} \cap [1, X] : p \mid l\} = S_0(X/p)$ and $\#\{l \in \mathcal{N} \cap [1, X] : p_1 p_2 \mid l\} = S_0(X/(p_1 p_2))$ exactly for $p_1 \ne p_2$. Because $2b < 1/2$, $X/d \ge X^{1-2b} \ge X^{1/2} \to \infty$ uniformly for $d \le X^{2b}$, so $S_0(X/d)/S_0(X) = (1 + o(1))(\log X / \log(X/d))^\delta / d$ uniformly on $1 \le d \le X^{2b}$. With $J = \sum_{p \in I,\, p \equiv 1(12)} 1/p \to \frac{1}{4}\log(b/e)$, we obtain $(1-o(1))J \le \mathbb{E}[\omega_I] \le ((1-b)^{-\delta}+o(1))J$ and $\mathbb{E}[\omega_I(\omega_I-1)] \le ((1-2b)^{-\delta}+o(1))J^2$. Expanding $\Pr(\omega_I = 0) \le \mathbb{E}[(\omega_I - J)^2/J^2] = \mathbb{E}[\omega_I(\omega_I-1)]/J^2 + \mathbb{E}[\omega_I]/J^2 - 2\mathbb{E}[\omega_I]/J + 1$ reproduces the exact upper limit $(1-b)^{-\delta}/(\frac{1}{4}\log(b/e)) + (1-2b)^{-\delta} - 1$. Choosing $b$ small, then $e$ small, then $V$ large bounds the total *unsigned* discarded proportion by $\eta + o(1)$ for any $\eta > 0$, leaving at least $(1/2 - \eta - o(1)) N\rho$ mass for each sign $\sigma$. **Verified.**
  - Moreover, as noted in lines 14 and 128, the proof of $S_+ - S_- = o(S)$ via Corollary 1.1 ($q = 1, \Phi \equiv 1$) uses only this unsigned discarded-mass bound (nonnegative Wirsing) and Chebotarev at modulus $1$, so there is **no circularity** with Halász–Wirsing / Hall–Tenenbaum. **Verified.**

### 1.3 Box enlargement, small conductors, and large-sieve convolution (lines 50–70)
- **Box decomposition.** Partitioning $I = (X^e, X^b]$ (clipped at its endpoints) and $(X^{1-2b}/v^2, X^{1-e}/v^2]$ into intervals of multiplicative ratio $\Delta = 1 + (\log X)^{-D}$ ($D > 4$ fixed) yields $O((\log X)^{2D+2})$ disjoint boxes. Taking all boxes intersecting $R_v = \{p \in I, X^{1-b}/v^2 < pm \le X/v^2\}$ produces a measure $B_v \ge w_v$ pointwise; because the boxes are disjoint and every $p$-interval lies inside $I$, $B_v(u) \le 1$ still holds for every $u$, and $B_v(u) - w_v(u)$ is supported on the boundary shells $(X^{1-b}/(v^2\Delta^2), X^{1-b}/v^2] \cup (X/v^2, \Delta^2 X/v^2]$, adding at most $O_V(1 + X/(\log X)^D) = o(N\rho)$ mass. Every box has $P \gg X^e$, $M \gg_V X^{1-2b}$, and $PM \ll_V X$. **Verified.**
- **Orthogonality and large conductors.** Every $p \in I$ ($p \equiv 1 \pmod{12}$) and $m \in \mathcal{U}$ is automatically coprime to every $\mathcal{P}$-smooth modulus $q$, so every induced character $\chi \bmod q$ agrees identically with its primitive inducer $\chi^* \bmod r$ on every operand (zero prime-dividing-modulus correction). Using $\varphi(rs) \ge \varphi(r)\varphi(s)$ gives $\sum_{s \le Q/r} 1/\varphi(rs) \ll (\log X)/\varphi(r) \ll (\log X)^2/\varphi(r)$. On dyadic conductor ranges $R < r \le 2R$ with $(\log X)^F < R \le Q \le X^{1/2-\kappa}$, writing $1/\varphi(r) \le (2/R)(r/\varphi(r))$, applying Cauchy–Schwarz and the multiplicative large sieve $\sum_{r \le 2R} (r/\varphi(r)) \sum_{\chi}^* |\sum a_n \chi(n)|^2 \le (Y + O(R^2))\sum |a_n|^2$ gives
  $$\sum_{r \asymp R} \frac{1}{\varphi(r)} \sum_{\chi \bmod r}^* |A_\chi B_\chi| \ll \frac{\sqrt{PM}}{R} \sqrt{(P + R^2)(M + R^2)} \ll \frac{PM}{R} + \frac{PM}{\sqrt{P}} + \frac{PM}{\sqrt{M}} + R\sqrt{PM}.$$
  Since $P \gg X^e$, $M \gg_V X^{1-2b} \gg_V X^{1/2}$, and $R \le X^{1/2-\kappa}$, the last three terms enjoy power savings ($X^{1-e/2}$, $X^{1/2+b}$, $X^{1-\kappa}$), while the first term summed over $O(\log X)$ dyadic $R$, $(\log X)^2$, and $O((\log X)^{2D+2})$ boxes is $\ll_V X/(\log X)^{F - 2D - 5}$, which is $\ll X/(\log X)^A$ for $F = A + 2D + 5$. **Verified.**
- **Small conductors ($1 < r \le (\log X)^F$) and Murty–Petersen Theorem 0.2 check.**
  We checked Murty–Petersen (*Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* **365** (2013), no. 9, **4987–5032**, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-2012-05805-3`; preprint `arXiv:1201.5312` / FSU `paper443.pdf`) directly against its primary statement:
  - *Primary statement of Theorem 0.2:* For a Galois extension $K/M$ of number fields with group $G$, conjugacy class $C \subset G$, largest abelian subgroup $H \le G$ with $H \cap C \ne \emptyset$, fixed field $E = K^H$, $\eta = \max\{[E:\mathbb{Q}] - 2, 2\}$, and $Q = x^{1/\eta - \epsilon}$, for any $A > 0$,
    $$\sum_{\substack{q \le Q \\ K \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q) = \mathbb{Q}}} \max_{(a, q)=1} \max_{y \le x} \left| \pi(y, C, q, a) - d(C, q, a)\pi(y) \right| \ll_A \frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.$$
  - *Verification of hypotheses here:* $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois with $G = D_4$, $C \in \{\{1\}, \{r^2\}\}$, $H = C_4$ is a maximal abelian subgroup of $D_4$ (order $4$, largest possible in $D_4$) meeting $C$, $E = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ has $[E:\mathbb{Q}] = 2$, so $\eta = \max\{2-2, 2\} = 2$, and $K \cap \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q) = \mathbb{Q}$ holds for all $\mathcal{P}$-smooth $q$. Thus every hypothesis of Murty–Petersen Theorem 0.2 is satisfied unconditionally (no Artin conjecture is needed because $H$ is abelian). Summing $\chi(a)[\pi(P_2, C, r, a) - \pi(P_1, C, r, a)]$ over $(a, r) = 1$ cancels the main term for nonprincipal $\chi \bmod r$ and gives $\ll_{F, A, e} P/(\log X)^A$ for any subinterval $(P_1, P_2] \subseteq [P, 2P]$ with $P \gg X^e$.
  - *Independent cross-check without Bombieri–Vinogradov:* In fact, because only $r \le (\log X)^F$ is used, Murty–Petersen's power-level theorem is stronger than needed. Over $E = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, the extension $K(\zeta_r)/E$ is **abelian** with group $C_4 \times (\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and conductor norm $\le 36 r^2 \le 36 (\log X)^{2F}$; expanding $\mathbf{1}_{C_\pm}$ in the four characters $\psi_j$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(K/E) \cong C_4$ reduces $\sum_{p \in (P_1, P_2],\, \mathrm{Frob}_p \in C_\pm} \chi(p)$ (up to $O(\sqrt{P})$ from inert primes of $\mathbb{Q}(i)$) to a linear combination of $\sum_{P_1 < N\mathfrak{p} \le P_2} \psi_j(\mathfrak{p})\chi(N\mathfrak{p})$ for four **nontrivial** ray class characters of $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, where the bound $\ll_{F, A, e} P/(\log X)^A$ is the classical Siegel–Walfisz theorem for Hecke $L$-functions over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ (Landau 1918 / Fogels 1962 / Goldstein 1970 / Lagarias–Odlyzko 1977).
  - *Minor findings M1 and M2* record the wrong end page `5007` (should be `5032`), the notation $\eta$ vs $\theta$, and the redundant log-weight/prime-power remark when citing Theorem 0.2 (which is already stated for $\pi$, not $\psi$).

### 1.4 Predecessor Selberg sieve, gamma normalization, and the exact constant `1/(3\pi)` (lines 72–118)
- **Small-prime filter modulo $M_0 = \prod_{\ell \in \mathcal{P},\, \ell \le T} \ell^{2J_0}$ ($T \ge V$).** Every genuine predecessor $l' = v^2 u - 12h \in \mathcal{N}$ has even valuation at each $\ell \in \mathcal{P}$, hence lies in $\{0, 2, \dots, 2J_0 - 2\}$ or $\ge 2J_0$. Because $\gcd(12, M_0) = 1$, for *every* unit $u \bmod M_0$ (regardless of whether $\ell \mid v$), $v^2 u - 12h$ sweeps all of $\mathbb{Z}/M_0\mathbb{Z}$ uniformly as $h$ runs over $\mathbb{Z}/M_0\mathbb{Z}$. Summing $\sum_{j=0}^{J_0 - 1} (\ell - 1)\ell^{-(2j+1)} + \ell^{-2J_0} = \frac{\ell}{\ell+1} + \frac{1}{(\ell+1)\ell^{2J_0}}$ gives the exact $v$-independent mean $b_* = \prod_{\ell \le T,\, \ell \in \mathcal{P}}(\frac{\ell}{\ell+1} + \frac{1}{(\ell+1)\ell^{2J_0}})$ (independently verified as exact `Fraction` for $v \in \{1, 5, 7, 11, 25, 35\}$). **Verified.**
- **Large-prime Selberg sieve ($T < \ell < \xi = N^\beta$, $\beta < 1/4$).** Every genuine predecessor with square part $\le V \le T$ has no prime factor in $\mathcal{P}$ above $T$. For $\ell \in \mathcal{P}$, $T < \ell < \xi$: since $\ell > T \ge V \ge v$, we have $\ell \nmid 12v$.
  - If $\ell \mid h$, then $v^2 u - 12h \equiv v^2 u \not\equiv 0 \pmod{\ell}$ for all units $u$, so no class is sifted.
  - If $\ell \nmid h$, then $v^2 u - 12h \equiv 0 \pmod{\ell}$ cuts out the unique **unit** class $u \equiv 12 h v^{-2} \pmod{\ell}$.
  - On each admitted unit class $u \equiv a_0 \pmod{M_0}$, CRT modulo $q = M_0 d$ ($d \mid P_h := \prod_{T < \ell < \xi,\, \ell \in \mathcal{P},\, \ell \nmid h} \ell$) produces a unique **unit** class modulo $M_0 d$, with main-term density $f(\ell) = \frac{1}{\ell - 1}$ relative to $M_v/\varphi(M_0)$.
  - Selberg diagonalization yields $g(\ell) = \frac{f(\ell)}{1 - f(\ell)} = \frac{1}{\ell - 2}$ (valid since $\ell \ge 5$), optimal weights $|\lambda_d| \le 1$, and main term $G_{T, h}(\xi)^{-1}$. Removing a prime $\ell \mid h$ from $G_T(\xi)$ satisfies $G_{\text{with }\ell}(\xi) = G_{\text{without }\ell}(\xi) + (\ell - 2)^{-1} G_{\text{without }\ell}(\xi/\ell) \le (1 + (\ell - 2)^{-1}) G_{\text{without }\ell}(\xi)$ (all terms nonnegative, $\xi/\ell < \xi$), so $G_{T, h}(\xi)^{-1} \le G_T(\xi)^{-1} R_T(h)$ with $R_T(h) = \prod_{\ell \in \mathcal{P},\, \ell > T,\, \ell \mid h}(1 + 1/(\ell - 2))$.
  - Summing over the $\varphi(M_0) b_v(h)$ admitted classes modulo $M_0$ cancels $\varphi(M_0)$ in the main term to give $M_v b_v(h) G_T(\xi)^{-1} R_T(h)$.
  - **Remainder uniformity in $h$:** Each squarefree lcm $d = [d_1, d_2] < \xi^2$ arises from at most $3^{\omega(d)} \le 3^{\omega(M_0 d)}$ pairs $(d_1, d_2)$, and for fixed $T, J_0$ and $\beta < 1/4$, $M_0 \xi^2 = M_0 N^{2\beta} < X^{1/2-\kappa}$ for large $N$. Because $E(q)$ takes the maximum over all $(a, q) = 1$ and $|\lambda_d| \le 1$, the remainder $\le \varphi(M_0) \sum_{q \le M_0 \xi^2} 3^{\omega(q)} E(q) \ll_{A, T, J_0, V} X/(\log X)^A$ is **completely uniform in $h$**. **Verified.**
- **Triangular mean over $1 \le h < H = \lfloor t/\rho \rfloor$.** Expanding $R_T(h) = \sum_{d \mid h} \mu(d)^2 r(d)$ ($p \mid d \Rightarrow p \in \mathcal{P},\ p > T$, $r(d) = \prod_{\ell \mid d}(\ell - 2)^{-1}$), every $d$ is coprime to $M_0$, so $m \mapsto b_v(dm)$ has period $M_0$ and mean $b_*$, giving $\frac{2}{H^2}\sum_{1 \le m < H/d}(H - dm) b_v(dm) \to b_*/d$. Meanwhile $0 \le b_v \le 1$ and $\sum_{1 \le m < M}(M - m) \le M^2/2$ give the uniform dominating bound $1/d$, with $\sum \mu(d)^2 r(d)/d = \prod_{\ell \in \mathcal{P},\, \ell > T}(1 + \frac{1}{\ell(\ell-2)}) < \infty$. Dominated convergence gives the exact limit $b_* \prod_{\ell \in \mathcal{P},\, \ell > T}(1 + \frac{1}{\ell(\ell-2)})$. **Verified.**
- **Independent re-derivation of every factor in $K = 3\pi/4$:**

     $$G_T(\xi)^{-1} \sim e^{\gamma_E \delta} \Gamma(1 + \delta) \prod_{T < \ell < \xi,\, \ell \in \mathcal{P}}\left(1 - \frac{1}{\ell - 1}\right).$$
  2. *Wirsing for support density $\rho \sim S/N$:* For $f_B = \mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}$ (prime mean $1 - \delta = 1/4$), Wirsing gives

  3. *Exact local cancellation:*
     - At $T < \ell < N^\beta$: $(1 - \frac{1}{\ell-1})(1 + \frac{1}{\ell(\ell-2)}) = \frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}\cdot\frac{(\ell-1)^2}{\ell(\ell-2)} = 1 - \frac{1}{\ell}$ (exact).

     - $e^{\gamma_E \delta}$ cancels identically between $G_T(\xi)^{-1}$ and $\rho$.
