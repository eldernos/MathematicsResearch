## GF-38. Derivation of the period

**The reduced denominator and local orders (lines 310–313, 320).** Put $D=\prod_{j\le k}(b^j-1)$ and $T=\mathrm{ord}_D(b)$ with $j$ starting at 1. Every factor is coprime to the numerator $b^{k(k+1)/2}$, so $T$ is the actual radix period. For an odd $p\nmid b$ put $s=\mathrm{ord}_p(b)$, $a=v_p(b^s-1)$ and $n=\lfloor k/s\rfloor$. Only $j=su$ contribute and LTE gives $v_p(b^{su}-1)=a+v_p(u)$. Hence $E=v_p(D)=na+v_p(n!)$. For $E>0$, $\mathrm{ord}_{p^E}(b)=s\,p^{E-a}$. This also handles $p=2,\ b\equiv1\bmod4$ with $s=1$.

For $b\equiv3\bmod4$, write $a=v_2(b^2-1)$ and $h=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$. Each odd index contributes one and index $2u$ contributes $a+v_2(u)$. Thus $E=\lceil k/2\rceil+ha+v_2(h!)$. The order at $2^E$ equals 1 at $E=1$, equals 2 for $2\le E\le a$, and equals $2^{1+E-a}$ for $E\ge a$. These agree at the common endpoint. A different prime $\ell\mid D$ can supply at most $\lfloor\log_p k\rfloor$ to $v_p(T)$, since the order modulo $\ell$ divides some $j\le k$ and lifting adds only powers of $\ell$. At the stated threshold $n\ge\lfloor\log_p k\rfloor+2$, $E-a\ge n-1$ in the ordinary case. In the exceptional case the threshold implies $k\ge3$, and $1+E-a\ge k$. The local power therefore gives the exact valuation of the global order in both cases.

**Cyclotomic factors (lines 314, 322–324).** The real part of the series for $\log(1-\zeta/b)$ converges absolutely for each primitive $d$th root $\zeta$. The sum over roots is finite. For the Ramanujan sum take $g=\gcd(d,r)$ and use $c_d(r)=\mu(d/g)\varphi(d)/\varphi(d/g)$, so $|c_d(r)|\le g\le r$. The resulting error in $\log\Phi_d(b)-\varphi(d)\log b$ is at most $\sum_{r\ge1} b^{-r}=1/(b-1)$, uniformly in $d$.

If $s=\mathrm{ord}_p(b)$ and $d=s$, $v_p(\Phi_s(b))=v_p(b^s-1)$ because all proper divisors miss $p$. At $d=sm>s$ in the ordinary LTE case the constant cancels in Möbius inversion. Expanding $v_p(u)=\sum_{i\ge1}\mathbf1_{p^i\mid u}$ gives $\sum_{u\mid m}\mu(m/u)v_p(u)=1$ precisely when $m$ is a positive power of $p$, and zero otherwise. For odd $b$, $\Phi_2(b)=b+1$ and $\Phi_{2^i}(b)=b^{2^{i-1}}+1$ has two-adic valuation one for $i\ge2$. Odd $d>1$ and $d$ with a nontrivial odd factor and even part have no two-adic contribution, by the same divisor inversion. Thus each imprimitive prime occurs at most once and divides $d$, with the stated $d=2$ exception. For example, $\Phi_6(2)=3$ and $\mathrm{ord}_3(2)=2$, so $R(6)=1$. The argument requires no primitive-prime existence theorem such as Bang–Zsigmondy.

**The least common multiple (lines 326–333).** Let $U$ be the product of the pure $p$-powers in the local orders. These powers have distinct prime bases, hence $U\mid T$. Each residue-field order divides $L=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)$, so $T\mid UL$. This bounds the entire overlap by $\log L\le\log(k!)=O(k\log k)$. For an ordinary prime of order $d$, its contribution to $\log U$ is

$$
\bigl((\lfloor k/d\rfloor-1)v_p(\Phi_d(b))
       +v_p(\lfloor k/d\rfloor!)\bigr)\log p .
$$

For $k/2<d\le k$ the primitive power occurs just once in $D$ and gives zero in the first term. This checks the subtraction even at the last index. Summing the factorial terms costs at most $\log(k!)$. The exceptional two-adic exponent, as well as its counterpart in the grouped expression, is $O_b(k)$ by the formula for $E$. The fixed indices $d=1,2$ cost $O_b(k)$ if handled separately. For all other indices $\log R(d)=\varphi(d)\log b+O_b(1+\log d)$. Its weighted error is bounded by a constant times $k\sum_{d\le k}(1+\log d)/d=O(k\log^2 k)$. All constants here allow fixed $b$ and are uniform in $k$.

**Evaluation of the main term (lines 315–317, 334).** Counting integers by gcd gives $\sum_{d\mid j}\varphi(d)=j$. Therefore the weighted sum of $\lfloor k/d\rfloor\varphi(d)$ is exactly $k(k+1)/2$. For the subtracted weight use $\varphi(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)n/d$. With $h_d=\lfloor k/d\rfloor$,

$$
\sum_{n\le k}\varphi(n)
=\frac12\sum_{d\le k}\mu(d)h_d(h_d+1)
=\frac{k^2}{2\zeta(2)}+O(k\log k).
$$

The floor error is $O(\sum_{d\le k} k/d)$, and extending the absolutely convergent series for $1/\zeta(2)$ costs $O(k^2\sum_{d>k}d^{-2})=O(k)$. Since $\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$, the primitive-power loss is $(3/\pi^2)k^2\log b$. Subtracting from the triangular main term gives exactly $(1/2-3/\pi^2)k^2\log b+O_b(k\log^2 k)$. Also $\sum_{j\le k}\log(1-b^{-j})=O_b(1)$, so the ratio to $\log D$ tends to $1-6/\pi^2$. For the integer shadow index at lines 39–41, $\Delta_N=o(N)$ gives $k_b(N,\Delta_N)=N+o(N)$ and $\log N=o(N^2)$. This proves the stated exponent of $N/T$ without interchanging any limits.

**Independent check on which coefficient belongs to the period.** Let $H=\mathrm{lcm}_{j\le k}(b^j-1)$ and $Q=D/H$. For each ordinary prime, the valuation in $H$ is $a+\lfloor\log_p(k/s)\rfloor$ when $s\le k$. This is also its valuation in $\prod_{d\le k}\Phi_d(b)$, from the first occurrence and the subsequent single lifts. At $p=2,\ b\equiv3\bmod4$, the contributions at $1,2,4,\ldots$ give the same maximum, including $k=1$. Thus $H=\prod_{d\le k}\Phi_d(b)$. The local order formulas give $Q\mid T$. Also $H\mid b^L-1$ and, for each $p\mid H$, raising $b^L$ to the power $Q$ increases the valuation of $b^L-1$ by at least $v_p(Q)$. Hence $T\mid QL$. In particular $\log T=\log D-\log H+O(k\log k)$. Summing the uniform cyclotomic error over $d$ costs $O_b(k)$ in $\log H$. This independently recovers the sign and even suffices for an $O_b(k\log k)$ remainder.

## GF-38. Universal barrier C2

1. **Shared factors (lines 160–172).** If $p$ occurs on both sides of the new gcd with valuations $e,f$, the new denominator has exponent $e+f$. Ordinary LTE gives local order exponent $e+f-a\ge\min(e,f)$ since each side already contains at least $a$. At $p=2,\ b\equiv3\bmod4$, a shared exponent of one forces order divisible by 2 modulo 4. For $\min(e,f)\ge2$ the new index is even, so $f\ge v_2(b^2-1)$ and the lift again suffices. Multiplication over primes proves $G_k\mid T_k$.
2. **Cost of reusing exponents (lines 174–187).** Write $g_k=\gcd(L_{k-1},m_k)$, now with $L_k=\mathrm{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_k)$. Every exact prime power $u=p^r\parallel g_k$ divides some earlier index and the new one, so $b^u-1\mid G_k$. For $u\ge7$, removing the possible single factor $p$ from $\Phi_u(b)$ leaves primes of exact order $u$. Their product exceeds $2^{u/2}/u>e^{u/15}$, since $(\log 2/2-1/15)t-\log t$ is positive at 7 and increasing afterwards. Distinct $u$ give coprime products. Consequently $\sum_{u\ge7}u\le15\log T_k$. The smaller exact powers have product dividing 60. The number of larger powers is at most $\sqrt{30\log T_k}$ by summing distinct integers. This proves the displayed upper bound on $\log g_k$ and in particular $\log g_k=o(\log T_k)$ when $T_k\to\infty$.
3. **Iteration and sharpness (lines 193–211).** Each $m_j\mid T_k$ for $j\le k$. Infinitely many nonunit factors force $T_k\to\infty$, since $D_k\mid b^{T_k}-1$ and $D_k\to\infty$. Put $A_k=\log L_k$, $B_k=\log T_k$. Then $0\le A_k\le B_k$. Eventually $A_k+\epsilon B_k\ge A_{k-1}+\theta B_{k-1}$, by $L_k=L_{k-1}m_k/g_k$. For $0<\epsilon<\theta$, the right side dominates $(1+\theta)(A_{k-1}+\epsilon B_{k-1})/(1+\epsilon)$. Iterate from a positive starting value, take the lower limit for each fixed $\epsilon$, then send $\epsilon$ to zero. The index shift in $\log m_k\ge\theta B_{k-1}$ preserves the lower limit after taking one more logarithm and dividing by $k$. This gives both bounds. The triple $(2,4,15)$ has orders $(2,12,60)$ in base 2. For sharpness at $\theta=1$, GF-34 supplies $m_k=\omega_{k-1}T_{k-1}+s$ and $\log T_k\sim c_*2^k$. Its schedule meets the required hypothesis. For the three examples on line 211 the lower bounds on $\log T_{k-1}$ are respectively of order $k^2$, at least a constant times $m^{k-2}$, and at least $\log(k!)$. Each dominates the corresponding $\log m_k$. [GF-34, lines 495–499](../../phase23/convergent-reciprocals-and-schedules.md)

## GF-39. Effective selection and discrepancy

**Cylinder measure (lines 181–194).** For current and previous denominators $q,q'$ and $y=q'/q$, telescoping the lengths of the first $A$ children gives $A/(A+1+y)$ times the parent's length. The reciprocal inflation is at most $1+2/A$, since $0\le y\le1$. The normalized child masses sum to the parent's rational mass, so these finite computations consistently define $\nu$. With $L_m=\prod_{i\le m}(1+2/A_i)$, the depth-$m$ cylinder masses are at most $L_m$ times their lengths. The recurrence $q_{i+2}\ge2q_i$ gives diameter at most $2^{1-m}$. Cylinders meeting a union of $K$ intervals enlarge its total length by at most $2K\,2^{1-m}$. Monotonicity and unboundedness of the computable caps give $\log L_m\le2j+2m/A_j$ for $j\le m$. Searching for the first $j$ with $A_j$ above a specified integer terminates. This gives an effective rate of decay for the maximum cylinder mass. Since a point belongs to at most two depth-$m$ cylinders, every singleton has measure zero.

**One test and its explicit tail (lines 196–206).** Fix a base $B$, a word of length $k$, a rational tolerance $\epsilon>0$ and a polynomial $P$ of degree $r\ge1$. A length-$N$ prefix has $N-k+1$ starts. Splitting the starts modulo $k$ gives independent Bernoulli trials *within* each class under Lebesgue measure. A weighted average outside the tolerance forces one class outside it. Each class contains at least $\lfloor(N-k+1)/k\rfloor$ starts. The centered Bernoulli log moment has second derivative at most $1/4$, so the two tails contribute at most $2k\exp(-2\epsilon^2\lfloor(N-k+1)/k\rfloor)$. No independence between classes is used. Padding by $B^{-2N}$ on both sides adds at most $2B^{-N}$ in total length and covers ambiguous radix endpoints.

Here is one explicit choice of the exponential parameters. For $N\ge4k$ put $\lambda=\min(\epsilon^2/k,1/2)$ and $\gamma=\lambda/r$. The padded target on the circle has length at most $(2k+2)e^{-\lambda N}$. Choose an integer $H=2\lceil\sum_i|[X^i]P|\rceil+5$ bounding the number of period copies in range, or $H=5$ for $X-S$. The translated target has total length $u\le H(2k+2)e^{-\lambda N}$, and its preimage has at most $2rHB^N$ interval components.

For $r=1$ the input length is at most $u/|P'|$. For $r>1$, on $|P'|\ge\eta$ change variables on each of at most $r$ monotone branches. This costs at most $ru/\eta$. Where $|P'|<\eta$, a factor of $P'$ gives distance at most $(\eta/|r\,\mathrm{lc}(P)|)^{1/(r-1)}$ from a complex root. The real slice of each such disk has length at most twice its radius. Taking $\eta=u^{(r-1)/r}$ gives input length at most $C_Pu^{1/r}$ with $C_P=r+2(r-1)|r\,\mathrm{lc}(P)|^{-1/(r-1)}$. The empty case is immediate. Small derivatives, multiple critical points and wraparound are all covered.

Take $h=\lceil\log_2 B\rceil$ and $m=2h(N+2)$. The endpoint term $2K\,2^{1-m}$ is at most $8rH B^{-N}$. Thus the parentheses in (8) are at most $C e^{-\gamma N}$, where one can choose any rational $C>1+C_P[H(2k+2)]^{1/r}+8rH$, taking $C_P=1/|P'|$ in degree one. Find $j$ with $A_j\ge32h/\gamma$. For $N\ge2$, $m\le4hN$. Choose $N_0\ge4k$ large enough that $m\ge j$, $u\le1$ by its explicit upper bound, and $\log C+2j\le\gamma N/4$ thereafter. Rational upper enclosures suffice for these comparisons. Then $\nu(E_N)\le e^{-\gamma N/2}\le2^{-\delta N}$ with rational $\delta=\gamma/2$. Every choice is effective in the test and the cap program, with no prescribed speed of cap growth.

**Computing surviving mass and choosing a digit (lines 208–210).** Number tests from $e=1$. Increase $N_e\ge N_0(e)$ until the geometric tail $2^{-\delta_e N_e}/(1-2^{-\delta_e})<2^{-e-2}$ is certified. The sum of these budgets is $1/4$. For any cylinder $I$, compute $\nu(F\cap I)$ with any prescribed absolute error by the following finite operations.

1. Omit a tail of the test indices and then a tail of prefix lengths for each retained test. Their absolute mass errors, also after intersecting with $I$, are bounded by the two explicit geometric sums.
2. For the remaining finite union, the bad words are decidable by integer counting and rational comparison. Rational-polynomial preimages use real algebraic root isolation, including repeated roots. Translation by $S$ uses computable endpoints. The tail of $S$ after $n$ terms is at most $b^{-n}/(b-1)$. A computable bounded range of integer translates suffices, even if a range endpoint is an integer.
3. Include the two boundaries of $I$ among the finitely many event boundaries. At depth $m$, every endpoint affects at most two closed cylinders, each of mass at most $L_m2^{1-m}$. Increase $m$ until twice this bound per endpoint fits the remaining error. Enumerate the rational depth-$m$ boundaries and enclose each event endpoint in a rational interval narrower than their minimum distinct separation. Sum known inside/outside masses and charge all ambiguous cylinders to those enclosures. This works with coincident endpoints and avoids deciding equality of unrelated computable reals.

For an explicitly decidable stage rule, carry a rational lower bound $h_0>0$ on the surviving parent mass, initially $3/4$. With $M=A_{i+1}$ children, compute each surviving mass to absolute error $h_0/(8M)$. At least one true mass is $\ge h_0/M$ by additivity. Choose the first rational estimate $\ge h_0/(2M)$ in digit order, then carry $3h_0/(8M)$ as the next lower bound. These are finite rational comparisons at every stage. The source's parallel positivity search gives the same existence and computability conclusion.

Depth-$m$ midpoints approximate the selected $x$ to at most $2^{1-m}$. If $x$ lay in any discarded open event, a neighborhood of it would contain an entire sufficiently deep selected cylinder, contradicting positive surviving mass. Thus $x\in F$. This argument constructs a computable Cauchy name and shows directly why positive measure of a closed set alone would have been insufficient.

**All polynomials, bases and prefix lengths (lines 196, 206, 208–210).** Rational polynomials of positive degree, bases, words and tolerances $1/j$ have an effective enumeration. Include $X-S$ for the specified radix. The selected point satisfies each test at *every* prefix length beyond its own $N_e$. To obtain an interval-discrepancy modulus for a fixed $P,B$, fix a grid of depth $k$ and take the maximum of the cutoffs for all $B^k$ words at tolerance $\epsilon$. For $N$ orbit points use the prefix of length $N+k-1$, giving exactly $N$ starts. Inner and outer grid approximations to any interval give

$$
D_N(P(x);B)\le B^k\epsilon+2B^{-k}
$$

once that prefix length exceeds the maximum. Taking first a fine grid and then a small rational tolerance gives an effective modulus for every finite list of pairs $(P,B)$. The quantifiers matter. For example, at a given $N$ in base 2, $P_N(X)=2^{-2N}X$ places all $N$ orbit points for $x\in[0,1]$ in $[0,2^{-N})$. A decay rate uniform over all unrestricted polynomials at once is impossible even in degree one. GF-39 claims simultaneous pointwise convergence.

## GF-39. Diophantine bound, prime support and external theorem

**All denominators and the exponent (lines 146–152, 172–179, 212).** The convergent determinant gives $q_i|q_ix-p_i|=1/(\alpha_{i+1}+q_{i-1}/q_i)>1/(a_{i+1}+2)$. For $q_i\le q<q_{i+1}$ use the last applicable $i$ if $q_0=q_1=1$. In the unimodular basis of two consecutive convergents, a competing integer pair either is a multiple of the first or has two nonzero coefficients of opposite signs. The two convergent errors have opposite signs, so their contributions in the latter case add in magnitude. Consequently $q\|qx\|\ge q_i|q_ix-p_i|$, including $q=1$. From $q_i\ge2^{(i-1)/2}$ one gets $i+1\le\lfloor2\log_2 q\rfloor+2$. Monotonicity justifies the slightly looser index $+3$ in (7).

For $A_i=\max(2,\lceil\log_2(i+1)\rceil)$ this gives $q\|qx\|\gg(1+\log\log(3q))^{-1}$. It excludes infinitely many approximations of exponent $2+\eta$ for each $\eta>0$, and Dirichlet gives irrationality exponent exactly 2. With a user-prescribed unbounded computable cap one may take its pointwise minimum with this logarithmic cap to obtain both conclusions for the same point. For the fixed digit set $\{1,2\}$ the strict bound is $q\|qx\|>1/4$ on every sample.

**A new persistent prime at every site (lines 154–166, 172, 210–212).** Enumerate all primes $\ell_n\nmid b$. The computable real $x-S$ is base-$b$ normal and therefore irrational, so finite interval refinement computes each digit $d_n$ without an endpoint decision. The coefficient $d_n+1/\ell_n$ is strictly between 0 and $b$ and has reduced denominator $\ell_n$. For the shifted $N$-term prefix multiply by $H_N=\prod_{n\le N}\ell_n$. Modulo $\ell_j$, only the $j$th fractional summand survives, with residue $b^{N-j}H_N/\ell_j\ne0\bmod\ell_j$. Thus the reduced denominator is exactly $H_N$ at every $N$, with no later cancellation of an earlier prime. The full series equals $Z=x-\lfloor x-S\rfloor$. Integer translation preserves $q\|qx\|$ and maps the family of rational polynomials to itself by substitution. The specified radix is used to encode the coefficients. Absolute normality of the polynomial images holds across all bases.

**Hochman–Shmerkin on $\{1,2\}$ (lines 138–145, 164).** I checked Theorem 1.4 and §1.3.3 in the authors' text, and the publication metadata. The theorem covers regular orientation-preserving $C^{1+\varepsilon}$ interval systems, a Pisot $\beta>1$ (including integer bases), and a branch whose fixed-point derivative is multiplicatively independent of $\beta$. Its conclusion includes the pushforward of any quasi-product measure under each fixed global $C^1$ diffeomorphism. The authors also describe the digit-pair system immediately before Theorem 1.12. [Authors’ text](https://math.huji.ac.il/~mhochman/preprints/normality.pdf) · [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-014-0573-5)

For $i,j\in\{1,2\}$, the maps $(j+t)/(i(j+t)+1)$ have positive derivative $(i(j+t)+1)^{-2}\le1/4$ on $[0,1]$ and disjoint cylinder interiors. All four paired symbols have independent weight $1/4$, giving a product measure. For the branch $f_1^2$ the derivative at $\rho=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ is $\rho^4$. This is a unit of norm 1 in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$. If $(\rho^4)^m=b^n$ with integers $m\ne0,n$, taking norms gives $b^{2n}=1$. Then $n=0$, contradicting $0<\rho^4<1$. For each polynomial, cover its noncritical locus by countably many compact intervals where $P'$ has constant nonzero sign. Extend $P$ or $-P$ from each interval by its endpoint tangents to obtain a global increasing $C^1$ diffeomorphism. Negation preserves equidistribution. Critical points have zero measure by the cylinder weights. Countable intersections over intervals, polynomials and integer bases give the metric result, and the fixed translation used for the coefficients is also a diffeomorphism. All effective cutoffs for the growing-cap construction come from the elementary estimates above.
