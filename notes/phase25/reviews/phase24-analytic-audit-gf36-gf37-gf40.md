### Mean theorem and infinite tails

Write $g(p^e)=s_2(e+1)$, $g(1)=1$, and $h=g*\mu$. For $e\ge1$, $h(p^e)=1-v_2(e+1)$, so $h(p)=0$. The floor-sum expression for the average of $g$ is dominated termwise by $|h(d)|/d$ and tends to $\sum h(d)/d=C$. The local factor is $(1-1/p)\sum_{e\ge0}s_2(e+1)p^{-e}=\sum_{j\ge0}p/(p^{2^j}+1)$, by summing the periodic indicators of the individual bits. [Analytic note lines 164–206](../../phase24/dense-digit-mass-and-lambert.md#L164)

For the true mean, take $u(e)=\operatorname{odd}(e+1)$ and independent geometric valuations with $\Pr(e_p=e)=(1-1/p)p^{-e}$. Removing powers of two preserves popcount. For integers, a large prime of exact valuation $e$ costs at most $g(p^e)HX/p^e$ in weighted mass up to $X$, by writing $n=p^e m$, $(m,p)=1$, then using $\sum_{m\le Y}g(m)\le HY$. The partial products of the majorant increase, so monotone convergence gives its full expectation exactly as the convergent Euler product $C$. The bad-event indicators tend to zero almost surely. This supplies uniform integrability where mere convergence of finite-prime distributions would be insufficient.
