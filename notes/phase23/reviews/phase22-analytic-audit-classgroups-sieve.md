# Mathematical referee report on GF-21 and GF-27 Theorem S

**GF-21 — ACCEPT AFTER REVISION.** The even-radix threshold and both class-group discrepancy constants survive the audit. The main proof needs a finite-modulus argument in two places. Uniformity of the residues of the prime counts does not imply uniformity after conditioning on the **exact** other counts. Below I give the missing argument, a counterexample to the genus lemma as quantified, and a family disproving a subsidiary claim about bounded excess.

**GF-27 Theorem S — SOUND / ACCEPT.** The Selberg weights, the sum of the remainders over both divisors and square factors, and the average over every $1\le H\le N$ give the stated exponent $3/2$. The application excludes every reduced denominator with an odd prime factor coprime to the radix. In particular, for an odd prime denominator $d$, the exclusion requires $d\nmid b$. The case $d\mid b$ gives membership.

The line numbers refer to the four files at Git revision **0342f1fdc573e38804e4f321a68776a6d91350da**. “Radix” and “Sieve” below refer to the [GF-21 note](../../phase22/radix-thresholds-and-class-groups.md) and the [GF-27 note](../../phase22/partitions-carry-and-lemma-s.md). Both results have **Paper proof** status with the external inputs identified below. Cross-model review is not independent human peer review. Finite computations do not prove asymptotic theorems.

## GF-21 findings requiring revision

1. **Major — Conditioning on exact multiplicities, Radix 370 and 418–424.** Lemma 2.7 gives joint equidistribution modulo each *fixed* integer. Its conclusion at line 424 about $j\bmod M$ given the other *exact* counts does not follow. For example, let $Y$ be uniform up to $X$ and $J=\lfloor\sqrt Y\rfloor$. For each fixed $M$, $(Y,J)\bmod M$ tends to the uniform law. On each interval with $J=k$ the $Y$ residues have discrepancy $O(1)$ in counts, and the weights $2k+1$ equidistribute over $k\bmod M$. Given $Y$, however, $J$ is deterministic. A uniform bound on the number of bad residues for every coefficient vector alone cannot prevent an exact count from selecting a bad residue.

   **Repair for both primes.** Fix the local pattern and the requested bad proportion. Use Lemma 2.8 first to choose the valuation threshold and the period of the varying coordinate. In a single finite local field containing all the conjugates involved, let $\mathfrak m^t$ represent that threshold. Choose a common modulus $M$ divisible by the period, by 2, and by the orders modulo $\mathfrak m^t$ of **all unit bases in all coordinates**, including those entering the coefficients. At 2, remove $2^{m_K^>}$ first as at line 365. The factors left in the varying exponents are units. At odd $q$ even the base 2 is a unit. The fixed coefficients are integral and may be nonunits. The bad-event indicator now factors through the whole finite vector modulo $M$. Increasing the period preserves the exceptional fraction. In each admissible fiber of **residues**, count bad $j\bmod M$ by Lemma 2.8 and sum over the fibers using Lemma 2.7. The free coordinates have trivial genus. In §2.5 the integral change of variables with first entry $w_1=1$ is invertible modulo this same $M$, including for negative entries of $\mathbf w$. Take $N\to\infty$ only after fixing $M$. This proves the two probability bounds without a conditional local limit theorem.

2. **Major — Lemma 2.7 needs to fix the ramified primes, Radix 322–324, 348.** For the advertised arbitrary cutoff $P_0$, the genus need not be a function of the stated pattern and split counts modulo 2. For $\Delta=-15,\ P_0=2$, the integers 1 and 3 both have $v_2=0$ and zero large split counts. Their genera differ. The ramified ideal of norm 3 is nonprincipal (the principal form $x^2+xy+4y^2$ misses 3) in a class group of order two. The two parity choices for this ramified factor have positive limiting weights, so conditioning on the genus can give a mixture of cosets. Require $P_0\ge\max_{p\mid\Delta}p$, or record all ramified exponents separately. Step 0 explicitly intends this choice. The resulting genus is the product of the fixed local genus and the parity contributions of the remaining split pairs.

3. **Minor — False topological equivalence, Radix 326–334.** If $\ell\mid T$, $j\mapsto(j\bmod T,j)$ is not dense in $\mathbb Z/T\times\mathbb Z_\ell$. The entries must agree modulo $\ell^{v_\ell(T)}$. For example, at $\ell=2$ an even $T$ omits the open set with first entry 0 and second entry odd. Such a factor in $T$ may be needed to reach the logarithm domain. Delete the purported equivalence or use the actual closure. The proof at line 334 already uses the correct measure, the equally weighted $T$ charts $j=j_0+Tk$ with $k\in\mathbb Z_\ell$.

The unique ideal contributes 1 and valuation zero. Including the ramified prime in the printed sum gives $v_2(4)=2$. Inert primes of even exponent also contribute the identity. The implementation already sums over the split list.

5. **Minor — A claimed uniform excess bound has an unbounded counterfamily, Radix 378–384, especially the final sentence of 384.** In $C_5$, take one split prime $p$ in a generator class and $n=p^e$, $e=5(2^j-1)$. Thus even the excess over the minimum individual valuation can grow arbitrarily large. The powers $p^e$ are compatible with probabilistic tightness. State tightness in the general case and keep the deterministic Lucas bound under its stated four-exact-divisors hypothesis, relative to the common factors already removed. The script at 483–494 checks a restricted squarefree experiment and prints a bound without the restriction. Also insert “even $r\ge2$” in the $C_8$ principal-coefficient example at Radix 287. 6. **Minor — Quantifiers and normalizations.** At Radix 69, 81 and 155 qualify the isolation and far-band claims by the exceptional set. **Selberg quadratic form, Sieve 63–89.** The interval discrepancy per root class is at most one, including for moduli larger than the interval.
