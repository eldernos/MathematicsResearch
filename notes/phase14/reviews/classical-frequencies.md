# Review of binary transitions and Erdős–Borwein word frequencies

**Logarithm.** If, on every sufficiently large power-of-two shell $[N,2N)$, at least $\eta N$ of the actual $T$-bit words of $\log 2$ have at least $\rho T$ internal transitions, then its lower transition density is at least $\eta\rho/6$. Its upper limiting interval discrepancy is at most $\max(2/3,1-\eta\rho/6)<1$. Here $T=\lfloor N^\delta\rfloor$, $0<\delta\le1$, and $\eta,\rho>0$ are fixed. The required counts are verified on seven individual shells for explicit thresholds. Establishing it on all late shells remains open. Reviewed at `50ed44a3661df8e34926b28a8f1bc3542f50ad58`. [Track F proof](../transition-windows.md) · [Lean criterion](../../../NormalityChecker/TransitionWindows.lean#L197)

**Erdős–Borwein.** Write $E=\sum_{m\ge1}\tau(m)2^{-m}$, where $\tau$ counts divisors. If its prefix frequency of ones tends to $1/2$, the actual law of the packed first 32 displaced divisor coefficients for a uniform integer shift in $[N,2N)$ has limiting inferior total variation distance greater than $0.006$ from the specified independent compound-Poisson law. The range $[0.492230,0.492685]$ encloses the infinite comparison model's one-bit probability. The actual limiting frequencies remain undetermined. Reviewed at `096b6e4056a4a1b17bdf1cf03b24fad619a76fe2`. [Track G theorem](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#binary-word-frequencies-and-the-odd-part-of-the-divisor-function) · [Carry argument](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#a-test-for-independence-and-a-conditional-word-law)

The halving argument for arbitrary prefixes uses the comparison hypothesis on $[N,2N)$ for **all integer starts** tending to infinity. Its $O(\log N/N)$ accounts for rounding the endpoints. The convergence hypothesis supplies no rate for the averages on the resulting intervals. ## F. From actual words to all cutoffs

Let $b_n$ be the first fractional bit of $2^n x$, with terminating dyadic expansions. The condition $\{2^t x\}\in[1/4,3/4)$ is exactly $b_t\ne b_{t+1}$. In a length-$T$ word starting at $n$ the eligible gaps are $t=n,\ldots,n+T-2$. Summing over the qualifying starts gives at least $\eta\rho NT$ incidences. Every gap occurs at most $T-1$ times, by injectivity at each fixed offset. For $T\ge2$ the last possible gap begins at $2N+T-3$, even for $T=N$. The case $T=1$ contributes zero. [Digit equivalence](../../../NormalityChecker/BinaryDiscrepancy.lean#L76) · [Overlap bound](../../../NormalityChecker/TransitionWindows.lean#L93)

For an arbitrary large integer $M$, take the largest power of two $N\le\lfloor M/3\rfloor$. Then $3N\le M<6N$ and all charged gaps lie before $M$. Dividing by the weaker multiplicity bound $T$ gives at least $\eta\rho N$ distinct hits before $M$. Thus the bound $\eta\rho/6$ already holds at **each** sufficiently late cutoff, before taking a lower limit. The sharp discrepancy inequality supplies the asserted upper bound. At a single good shell the counting gives frequency at least $\eta\rho/3$ at $3N$. To see why good shells of unbounded size alone suffice only for an upper frequency bound, alternate on $[A_j,3A_j)$ for $A_1=4$, $A_{j+1}=A_j^3$, and put zeros elsewhere. Every shell word there has $T-1$ changes. By $A_{j+1}$ the entire prefix contains only $O(A_j)$ changes. [All-cutoff Lean theorem](../../../NormalityChecker/TransitionWindows.lean#L120) · [Discrepancy inequality](../../../NormalityChecker/SharpDiscrepancy.lean#L137)

The unconditional Lean input for the **actual logarithm** locates a transition $2B\le t<68B$ for every sufficiently large $B$. For $2\le T\le B$, exactly the consecutive starts $t+2-T,\ldots,t$ contain that gap. At dyadic $B$ this block meets at most two shells with left endpoints between $B$ and $64B$. One of those shells therefore has at least $\lceil(T-1)/2\rceil$ such starts. Taking $T=\lfloor B^\delta\rfloor$ gives order $N^\delta$ starts with **one** change on some shell among every seven. For length $N$, the paper deduction giving $cN$ one-change starts on *every* large shell for each $c<1/3$ uses Liu–Jiang–Zhang, Theorem 1 at $r=2$. Take $c<c'<1/3$ and $2<b<(3-c')/(1+c')$. A run with no transition on $[m,bm)$ would give a dyadic approximation of error $2^{-bm+O(1)}$, contradicting the preprint with any exponent strictly between 2 and $b$. Taking $m=\lceil(1+c')N\rceil$ leaves at least $c'N-O(1)$ eligible starts. The preprint supplies a separate arithmetic input for this deduction. [Lean block theorem](../../../NormalityChecker/TransitionWindows.lean#L21) · [Liu–Jiang–Zhang v3, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10192v3#page=2)

### The actual CRT numerator and the seven shells

Let $y_N$ be the diagonal beta-corrected rational approximating $2^N\log 2$, $L=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,2N)$, $A=\prod_{N<p\le2N}p$ and $B=L/A$. Expanding the beta integral shows that $L y_N$ is integral. The calculation starts with its actual least residue $v$. It computes $a=vB^{-1}\bmod A$ and $b=vA^{-1}\bmod B$, so $aB+bA\equiv v\pmod L$. Both factors matter. If the four-bit cell indices of $\{2^ja/A\}$ and $\{2^jb/B\}$ sum modulo 16 to an integer from 5 through 9, then $\{2^j y_N\}\in[5/16,11/16)$. The unwrapped error is at most $1/\sqrt{N+1}<1/16$ for $N\ge256$, $0\le j\le2N$. Each mark therefore forces an actual change at $N+j$. Lean proves the implication from the fractional-part identity and the cell inequalities. The rational construction and enumeration supply its arithmetic premises. ### What the counterexamples preserve

Let $M$ be the odd part of $\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)$. Changing the real target by $s/(2^NM)$ and each $y_u$ by $2^{u-N}s/M$ for $u\ge N$ preserves the exact displacements, the unwrapped errors, integrality at the natural denominators, and **every** value $p y_u\bmod p$ for $u<p\le2u$. Indeed $M$ is a unit at those primes. Take $s=-\lfloor M\{2^N\log 2\}\rfloor$. The first $N$ bits persist and the translated phase at $N$ belongs to $[0,1/M)$. By the prime number theorem $\log M=N+o(N)$, which covers all $N+T-1$ bits touched by the shell if $T=\lfloor N^\delta\rfloor$, $0<\delta<1$. For $N=64$, $T=8$, the exact calculation gives $M=18472920064106597929865025$ and $s=-14568311876442763788337183$. It guarantees at least 83 zero bits and agrees in all 1,139 prime observations and all 63 adjacent displacements tested. This construction varies with the shell and changes the Taylor initial condition. The linear-length variants relax either exact values or exact displacements. [Counterexample and variants](../transition-windows.md#what-simultaneous-upper-band-incidences-leave-undetermined) · [Exact replay](../../../scripts/phase14_transition_windows.py#L67)

There are $B$ fractional completions at the anchor with the specified data, since preserving the prime observations requires $A\mid Lq$ for the translation $q$. They split into translates of the $M$-point grid. In a uniform *Lebesgue* complementary phase, offsets in any one class modulo four read disjoint four-bit blocks. Each has mark probability $5/16$ for its prescribed upper cell. Markov's inequality with weight $(3/4)^X$ and a union over the four classes give $4((59/64)(4/3)^{1/4})^{N/4}$. Restoring the grid costs at most $2^{N+3}/M$, one point per cylinder. Both terms decay. Thus the fraction of compatible completions with at least $N/4$ marks tends to one exponentially fast, even in the presence of the all-zero example. The Taylor lift $s=0$ still needs a bound of its own. [Completion count](../transition-windows.md#what-simultaneous-upper-band-incidences-leave-undetermined)

## G. One source and the Fourier band

Write $\tau(m)=2^{V(m)}U(m)$ with $U(m)$ odd, $t(m)=m-V(m)$, and $C(t)=\sum_{t(m)=t}U(m)$. A nonintegral contribution at raw offset $j=m-n$ has remaining denominator $2^d$ for $d=j-V(m)>0$. Summing a test $F(U(m))$ at level $d$ counts each source once at $n=t(m)-d$. For $1\le d\le N$, moving this sum to the ordinary source interval $[N+d,N+H+d)$ costs two strips of width $O(\log N)$, since $V(m)\le\log_2 m$ and the relevant $m$ are $O(N)$. [Finite source identities](../../../NormalityChecker/ErdosBorweinArrivals.lean#L14)

The one-source law $\nu$ is the law of $U_*=\prod_p\mathrm{odd}(B_p+1)$ with independent geometric exponents $\Pr(B_p=a)=(1-1/p)p^{-a}$. Only exponents at least two can affect this product. Möbius inversion of $g(m)=F(U(m))$ is supported on squarefull numbers, even for nonmultiplicative $F$. At a prime of exponent exactly one the paired terms cancel since $U(pn)=U(n)$ for $p\nmid n$. For $|F(u)|\le u^b$, the coefficient bound $2^{\omega(q)}\tau(q)^b$ on squarefull $q$ is summable against $q^{-1/2-\epsilon}$. Counting multiples and summing the tail gives mean $\nu(F)+O_{b,\epsilon}(N^{1/2+\epsilon}/H)$ uniformly in $d\le N$. In particular $\mu=\mathbb E U_*\le\prod_p(1+2/(p^2-1))=5/2$. On $N^{1/2+\eta}\le H\le N$ this proves that the actual discarded mass beyond depth $D$, scaled by $2^D$, has mean $\mu+o(1)$ for each fixed $D$. The $d>N$ tail is exponentially small since $C(t)\ll(t+1)^2$. [Marked mean and tail](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#counting-each-source-at-its-remaining-denominator)

**Pointwise local law.** Set $T=\log\log N$ and take the ordinary integer measure on $[N,2N)$. For a fixed test $F$ on the odd residues modulo $2^r$, a fixed $r\ge1$, and $p_T(k)=e^{-T}T^k/k!$ with $p_T(-1)=0$, the claimed expansion is
```math
\mathbb E[F(U)1_{V=k}]
=\nu(F)p_T(k)+\gamma_F\bigl(p_T(k-1)-p_T(k)\bigr)+O_{r,F}(T^{-3/2}).
```
For each character $\chi$, the local series for $\chi(U)z^V$ begins $1+zw$. Multiplication by $(1-w)^z$ cancels its linear term. On $|z|\le2$ the remaining coefficients have polynomial growth in the local degree, so the Dirichlet coefficients of the resulting product $G_\chi$ are absolutely summable against $n^{-3/4}$, uniformly. This implies Selberg's precise weighted hypothesis $\sum |b_z(n)|(\log 2n)^{B+3}/n\ll1$ with $B=2$. Subtract the two endpoint asymptotics and divide by $N$. On $|z|=1$ the result is $A_\chi(z)e^{T(z-1)}+O(1/\log N)$ with $A_\chi(z)=G_\chi(1,z)/\Gamma(z)$ and $A_\chi(1)=\nu(\chi)$. In Fourier inversion the constant and linear terms of $A_\chi$ give exactly the two coefficients displayed. The remainder is bounded by $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\theta^2 e^{-T(1-\cos\theta)}d\theta\ll T^{-3/2}$ uniformly in $k$. The same character expansion of $A'_\chi(1)$ gives $\gamma_F$. In the central band $|k-T|\le A\sqrt T$, division by the result for $F=1$ gives the conditional mark law with $O_{A,r}(T^{-1/2})$ error for residue indicators. This pointwise correction is for dyadic-length windows. [Track G derivation](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#a-pointwise-signed-local-limit-on-dyadic-windows) · [Selberg, Theorem 2, pp. 84–85](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/alladik/files/selberg-note-on-a-paper-by-sathe-1953.pdf#page=2)

The integrated Gaussian statement on long windows uses a different argument. Conditional on a fixed residue class, truncating $\omega(m)$ at primes $p\le N^{1/T^2}$ gives Bernoulli moments by counting fixed divisibility patterns. The omitted primes have mean $O(\log T)$, and $V-\omega$ has bounded first absolute moment by the repeated-prime-power sum. Both are negligible on the $\sqrt T$ scale. Fixing finitely many prime exponents first recovers the odd mark independently of the Gaussian in this **one-source** limit. The moment bound above permits polynomial-growth tests. Consequently the signed sum of individual changes has limit $\kappa(h)=\sum_{d\ge1}(\mathbb E e(hU_*/2^d)-1)$, where $e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$. A full orbit phase multiplies the terms sharing a reading position. [Gaussian and signed assertions](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#where-the-signed-contribution-comes-from)

**Removal of the logarithmic loss.** On $N^\delta\le H\le N$ for any fixed $0<\delta\le1$, let $S_B(n)$ be the sum of $\tau(n+j)2^{-j}$ over positive raw offsets $|j-T|\le B$. Reindex the absolute omitted mass by $d$ first. For $0\le B\le T/4$, put $r=B/T$. On the enlarged interval of sources at fixed $d\le N$, the functions $Ue^{\pm rV}$ are nonnegative and multiplicative, take prime values $e^{\pm r}$, and have the common majorant $\tau^2$. Shiu's bound, in the unrestricted form recorded by Henriot, thus gives means $\ll_\delta\exp((e^{\pm r}-1)T)$ uniformly in $r,d$. Choose a length exponent strictly below $\min(\delta,1/2)$ to absorb shifts of the interval. For the late and early events $V+d>T+B$ and $V+d<T-B$, the respective weights left to sum over $d$ are $2^{-d}e^{rd}$ and $2^{-d}e^{-rd}$. Both geometric sums are bounded. The remaining exponent is at most $-rB+e^{1/4}r^2T/2\le-B^2/(3T)$. This gives mean absolute phase error $\ll_{\delta,h}e^{-B^2/(3T)}$ for fixed $h$. Any $B=A(N)\sqrt T$ with $A(N)\to\infty$ suffices, using monotonicity of the *omitted positive mass* above $T/4$. A fixed number of standard deviations gives a fixed tail allowance. Cancellation in the retained Fourier average remains open. [Band proof](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#a-narrower-raw-offset-band-on-every-power-window) · [Shiu, Theorem 1, p. 163](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN243919689_0313?tify=%7B%22pages%22%3A%5B167%5D%7D) · [Henriot, p. 1, uniform dependence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.1643v1#page=1)

## G. Certified comparison probability and total variation

Let $J$ be the sum of a Poisson(1) number of independent marks with law $\nu$. Let $Q_D$ be the law of $\sum_{d=1}^D2^{D-d}J_d\bmod 2^D$ for independent copies $J_d$. Write $R_D(n)$ for the same packed residue of the **actual** columns $C(n+d)$. Convergence of the laws of $R_D(n)$ to $Q_D$ for *every fixed* $D$ is an extra hypothesis. Together with the actual mean-tail bound, it gives the limiting orbit law of $\{\sum_{d\ge1}2^{-d}J_d\}$. Its Fourier coefficients are $\exp(\kappa(h))$. At fixed separation $b$, counting two distinct actual sources $m,m+k$ requires the arithmetic condition $V(m+k)-V(m)=k-b$. The one-source estimates leave that joint count open. [Comparison hypothesis](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#a-test-for-independence-and-a-conditional-word-law) · [Two-source condition](../erdos-borwein-frequencies.md#the-missing-arithmetic-and-a-finite-check)

Every mark is odd, so $J$ has the parity of its Poisson count. Its largest parity probability is $p_0=(1+e^{-2})/2<3/5$. Reveal columns from the least significant end. At each stage a prescribed packed residue forces a parity after the incoming carry, so its probability is at most $p_0^D$. Conditional on the independent model tail $Y$ in units of $2^{-D}$, at most $2\lceil Y\rceil$ grid starting points can cross the two endpoints of one word interval. As $\mathbb E Y=\mu\le5/2$, the cylinder error is at most $7(3/5)^D$. Conditioning on the same tail bounds the probability of an individual circle point by $p_0^D$ for every $D$, which justifies passing from weak convergence to digit intervals.

The pinned script uses positive lower masses on the $2^{-80}$ grid. Exponents zero and one give mark one. After convolution at the primes through $P=65536$, omitted primes leave the mark unchanged with probability at least $1-1/P$. Cutting off marks above 255 and columns above 383 only removes mass. The alternating series through term 31 bounds $e^{-1}$ below, and $jb_j=\sum_{u=1}^j u\nu(u)b_{j-u}$ for $b_j=\Pr(J=j)$ propagates lower bounds. Carrying from depth 24 gives even and odd lower masses $0.507348999918408596\ldots$ and $0.492264097091285301\ldots$. Subtract $7(3/5)^{24}$ from the lower odd mass, and add it to one minus the lower even mass. The resulting endpoints are $0.492230928421917050\ldots$ and $0.492684168750959654\ldots$, hence the published outward rounding. [Integer certificate](../../../scripts/erdos_borwein_poisson.py)

An independent exact check conditioned explicitly on each Poisson count $0\le k\le18$. It formed the $k$-fold additive convolution of the retained mark masses and weighted it by a lower bound for $e^{-1}/k!$ using terms 0 through 23 of the exponential series. It rounded down on a $2^{-90}$ grid and kept the same prime, mark, column and depth cutoffs. For each prime it dropped individual geometric exponent masses below one grid unit and pooled equal odd products at each multiplicative convolution. Every omitted prime is odd, so $\sum_{p>P}p^{-2}\le1/(2(P-1))$ by integrating $x^{-2}$ on $[n-2,n]$ for odd $n>P$. For each bit separately, initialize $f_1(c)=\sum_{j:\,(c+j)\bmod2=\mathrm{bit}} b_j$. Compute $f_{d+1}(c)=\sum_j b_j f_d(\lfloor(c+j)/2\rfloor)$ with downward rounding for $0\le c\le383$ and read $f_{24}(0)$. This propagates the desired event from the first column toward the truncation boundary. Adding the same infinite-tail allowance yields the independent outward enclosure **[0.49232145, 0.49259165]**, inside the published interval.

For the actual orbit, set $\Delta_D=d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(R_D),Q_D)$. The discarded tail can depend on the head. Markov costs at most $(\mu+o(1))/s$ for a carry of size at least $s$. For the upper one-frequency bound, take the upper half of the grid together with the last $s$ points of the lower half as **one event**. Its actual probability costs one $\Delta_D$ above its model probability. The reverse bound uses the lower half and the last $s$ upper-half points. Thus each sign costs a single TV distance, $s p_0^D$ for the extra cells, and the model's cylinder error. With $p_*$ the infinite model probability and $f_I$ the actual frequency, this gives
```math
|f_I-p_*|\le\Delta_D+(s+7)(3/5)^D+\frac{5}{2s}+o(1).
```
For $D=32$, $s=4096$, the explicit error is $0.0009368954278411008\ldots$. If prefix one-frequency tends to $1/2$, subtraction of prefix counts gives $f_{[N,2N)}\to1/2$. Even using the published upper endpoint, the limiting inferior of $\Delta_{32}$ is at least $0.5-0.492685-(4096+7)(3/5)^{32}-5/8192>0.00637810$. This necessary condition under simple normality holds without assuming the Poisson hypothesis. The distance compares the laws of a single 32-level packed integer.
