# Review of denominator loss in the mixed Stoneham product

Let $x=\alpha(2,3)\alpha(2,5)$, where $\alpha(2,q)=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-q^j}/q^j$. At position $n\ge5$, put $K=\lfloor\log_3 n\rfloor$, $L=\lfloor\log_5 n\rfloor$, $P=3^K$ and $Q=5^L$. The shifted rational prefix $R_n$ sums $2^{n-3^i-5^j}/(3^i5^j)$ over $i,j\ge1$ with $3^i+5^j\le n$. A lost layer is a factor of three or five removed when reducing $A_n/(PQ)$, where $A_n=PQR_n$.

**Verdict.** Track C's fixed-depth law, the analytic isometry on each shape's failure sheet, the ordinary upper failure density bound $4/11$, and logarithmic equidistribution of the actual binary orbit all follow. The bounded-denominator criterion also holds along any prescribed sequence of ordinary cutoffs. Pointwise sublinear loss on all nonrectangular prefixes and ordinary binary normality remain open. The full-precision endpoint congruences and the first lift are kernel proofs. The all-depth lift, interpolation, and density conclusions are paper deductions. The reviewed source and proof can be read with `git show d323f59:NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveInterpolation.lean` and `git show d323f59:notes/phase14/mixed-interpolation.md`. ## The cutoff and the affine lift

Use $(p,q,u)=(3,5,2)$ or $(5,3,4)$, and let $H,m$ be the corresponding shell exponents. Let $a$ count missing entries of the top $p$ row and $b$ missing entries of the top $q$ column. The orientation reverses at the other prime. Every pair strictly below both tops fits, since $P/3+Q/5\le 8n/15$. In a nonrectangle the lengths of the $p$ fibres are therefore $H$ up to $j=m-a$, then $H-1$ up to $j=m-1$, and $H-b$ at $j=m$. This includes a completely empty top row or column.

Write $x_j=q^{-j}2^{-q^j}$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ and $S_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j}x_i$, with $S_0=0$. Multiplication by $2^{p^H}$ removes the dependence of the binary unit on the row index $i$ modulo $p^i$, since $\varphi(p^i)$ divides $p^H-p^i$. The row coefficient $p^{H-i}$ restores full precision. Summing the geometric progression in each fibre gives the following residue representative, up to a unit modulo $p^H$.

```math
G=S_{m-a}+p(S_{m-1}-S_{m-a})+p^b x_m .
```

For a rectangle use $G=S_m$. The actual loss is $\min(H,v_p(G))$. This clipping matters when comparing a high-precision analytic zero with the denominator of a finite prefix.

On a fixed nonrectangular shape, reduction modulo $p$ selects $m=a+uz$. For the lift put $s=up^t$ and $d=q^s-1$. The factor $(p-1)p^{t+1}$ divides $d$, so $d^2$ is a multiple of $(p-1)p^{t+2}$, including at $t=0$. Thus $D_i=2^{-q^i d}\equiv1\pmod{p^{t+2}}$, and modulo $p^{t+3}$ the correction to $x_{i+rs}-q^{-rs}x_i$ is $r x_i(D_i-1)$. Summing over $0\le r<p$ kills that correction. Also $\sum_{r<p}q^{-rs}\equiv p\pmod{p^2}$. Starting from $S_u\equiv6\pmod9$ or $15\pmod{25}$ gives $S_s\equiv p^t S_u\pmod{p^{t+2}}$.

At an arbitrary start $J$, telescoping introduces the extra term $(q^{-s}-1)S_J$ modulo $p^{t+2}$. It vanishes when $u\mid J$, as required at $J=m-a$. All other partial-sum increments and the single-term increment are divisible by $p^{t+1}$ before multiplying by $p$ or $p^b$. Consequently, for $f(z)=G(a+uz)/p$ and any nonnegative integer $e$,

```math
f(z+ep^t)-f(z)\equiv e p^t(S_u/p)\pmod{p^{t+1}} .
```

The slope in this congruence is a unit. For two distinct nonnegative indices, take $t$ to be the valuation of their difference. Their images have exactly the same distance. On each finite quotient the induced injection is a permutation, so the extension to $\mathbb Z_p$ is a surjective isometry, with a unique zero $\zeta$. The valuation is $1+v_p(z-\zeta)$ before clipping. Affinity describes **each successive digit lift**. For example on the ternary one-corner shape, $f(0),f(1),f(2)$ are $4,3,5\bmod9$. Their consecutive differences certify that the interpolating map itself need not be affine.

Analyticity requires a separate argument. On $j=i+uz$, $q^{uz}$ interpolates as $\exp(z\log(q^u))$. The Teichmüller factor in $2^{-q^j}$ stays constant because $q^u\equiv1\pmod{p-1}$. Both principal-unit logarithms have norm at most $p^{-1}$, strictly inside the exponential radius for these odd primes. This gives an analytic block increment $g(z)=S_{u(z+1)}-S_{uz}$ on a closed disc of radius $R>1$. For its discrete primitive, the Stirling-number identity gives $|\Delta^j g(0)/j!|_p\le C R^{-j}$. The Newton term $\Delta^jg(0)\binom{z}{j+1}$ has norm at most $C R^{-j} r^{j+1}|1/(j+1)|_p$ on any $1<r<R$. This tends to zero, and differencing the primitive recovers $g$. Finite sums and integral translates then give the interpolant for each fixed pair of deficits and each residue sheet. On the failure sheet its zero has Haar measure zero and its depth-$h$ ball has measure $p^{1-h}$.

## Joint logarithmic density and ordinary cutoffs

At fixed $h,k\ge1$, write $A=4\cdot5^{k-1}$ and $B=2\cdot3^{h-1}$. In continuous time $t=\log n$, use coordinates $t/\log3\bmod A$ and $t/\log5\bmod B$. Every nonconstant character has nonzero frequency by irrationality of $\log3/\log5$. Hence the orbit averages to uniform area. At fractional coordinates $v,w$, outside the axes, the top-row deficit is $-\lfloor w+\log_5(1-3^{-v})\rfloor$ and the top-column deficit has the transposed formula. Their boundaries are locally finite curves away from the axes, so their union with the axes has area zero. Clipping a negative endpoint count to zero affects only the unit intervals starting at powers, of finite total logarithmic length. Elsewhere each physical unit interval has constant membership, with the new pair admitted at equality. Replacing $\log(1+1/n)$ by $1/n$ costs a bounded total error.

Above each fractional pair, the ternary condition selects one value of **$L\bmod B$** and the quinary condition one value of **$K\bmod A$**. Exactly one of the $AB$ sheets qualifies. If a fixed shape occupies fractional area $c>0$, joint failure on that shape has logarithmic density $c/(AB)$. Its conditional density is therefore the same as the unconditional density,

```math
d_{h,k}=\frac{1}{8\,3^{h-1}5^{k-1}} .
```

A crossed-residue example detects an erroneous single-index Chinese remainder argument. On the ternary shape $(a,b)=(2,1)$, at $(h,k)=(3,2)$ the required classes are $L\equiv14\pmod{18}$ and $K\equiv1\pmod{20}$. Their parities differ. The actual pair $(K,L)=(21,14)$ realizes both. The shape occupies $10{,}704{,}493{,}828\le n<11{,}681{,}056{,}328$. Summing the defining numerator modulo $PQ$ gives $\gcd(A_n,PQ)=2025=3^4 5^2$ at both ends inside the interval. The values immediately before and after it are $25$ and $5$. Doubling preserves the reduced denominator throughout its interior.

The ordinary construction uses the shape missing only the top corner. Its half-open interval has endpoints $C=\max(P+Q/5,P/3+Q)$ and $N=P+Q$ when $Q/P$ is close to $5/6$. Both next powers exceed $N$. Its length is $\min(4Q/5,2P/3)$, giving $(N-C)/N\to4/11$. Within the two prescribed shell congruences, the rotation step $A\log3/(B\log5)$ remains irrational, so arbitrarily late pairs approach the required ratio. Counting $n<N$ includes the entire interval and excludes the inserted corner at $N$. This proves the lower bound at **each fixed depth pair**, even if the losses are prescribed exactly using passing children. An ordinary density, if it existed, would equal the logarithmic density, which is at most $1/8$. The lower bound concerns failure counts. A plateau losing fixed depths can still have denominators tending to infinity.

The related first-precision analysis at `4a5dd43` uses the ordinary prefix mass, which weights earlier logarithmic times $s$ by $e^{-s}$. I reproduced its rational enclosure $0.575523806608492<\limsup F_{1,1}(N)/N<0.575523806608494$ and independently checked the lower witness. At phase $1250/81$ and cutoff $493/81$, partitioning at each retained power and sum and testing both offsets at the left edge of each of 2,207 intervals gives the certificate's exact lower fraction at truncation depth 16. The two-axis outer envelopes cover unfinished prefixes by maximizing at the start of the cutoff range and at clipped right endpoints. Density of actual phases and continuity of the integrated mass justify using the rational witness as a limit of integer cutoffs. The separate Lean bound $250000F_{1,1}(N)\le143881N$ for $N\ge81^5$ also re-elaborated. These results concern first precision. The nested failure sets inherit its upper bound at higher precision. The numerical enclosure comes from the paper argument and rational computation in `notes/phase14/mixed-flow.md` and `scripts/phase14_mixed_flow.py` on the ordinary-prefix track.

## The actual orbit and near-full loss

Let $F_{h,k}(N)$ count joint failures with $n<N$ and let $D_N$ be extreme interval discrepancy for $\{2^n x\}$, $0\le n<N$. With block length $B_N=2^{\lfloor\log_4 N\rfloor}$, the initial and unfinished blocks and those containing an insertion cost $O(B_N(1+\log N)^2)=o(N)$. On every other block the reduced odd denominator is constant. A position passing either depth test supplies a denominator at least $3^{K-h+1}$ or $5^{L-k+1}$, tending uniformly to infinity after the initial block. Its upper bound $N^2\le B_N^6$ eventually holds, and its prime support lies in $\{3,5\}$. The smooth-orbit estimate with the vanishing shifted tail thus bounds the **actual shifts** uniformly over test intervals. Charging the remaining blocks gives $D_N\le\epsilon+F_{h,k}(N)/N$ for all sufficiently large $N$ at fixed $h,k,\epsilon$. [Shifted tail](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFivePrefix.lean) · [Uniform block estimate](../../../NormalityChecker/SupportDiscrepancy.lean) · [First-precision transfer](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveCoverage.lean)

Interchanging the finite sums gives $\sum_{N\le X} F_{h,k}(N)/N^2=d_{h,k}\log X+o(\log X)$. For example, with kernel $1/[N(N+1)]$ the inner sum for $n<N\le X$ telescopes to $1/(n+1)-1/(\lfloor X\rfloor+1)$. Changing kernels costs $O(1)$. Take $X\to\infty$ first, then let $\epsilon$ and $d_{h,k}$ tend to zero. This proves $\sum_{N\le X}D_N/N=o(\log X)$, hence $\liminf D_N=0$. It also proves equidistribution with harmonic weights on the **shifts themselves**. Indeed, for every interval $I$ the centered prefix count has absolute value at most $ND_N$. Partial summation bounds the absolute centered harmonic count through $X$ by $O(1)+\sum_{N\le X}D_N/N$, uniformly in $I$.

At an arbitrary sequence of ordinary cutoffs, $D_N\to0$ exactly when for every fixed $M$ the positions with $\mathrm{den}(R_n)\le M$ occupy $o(N)$ of the prefix. Sufficiency uses the same blocks with the denominator threshold from the smooth-orbit estimate. For necessity, the fractional parts of these rationals lie in a fixed finite set. The shifted tail puts all sufficiently late counted shifts in a union of small arcs about that set. Its observed mass is bounded by its length plus a fixed multiple of $D_N$, including arcs split at zero. Shrinking the arcs proves the claim. A bounded denominator requires *simultaneous* loss within $O_M(1)$ of $K$ and $L$.

One can localize this obstruction to fixed shapes. For $0<\eta<1$, the strip $n-P<\eta n$ is contained in the union of $[P,P/(1-\eta))$. Summing the lengths of these intervals and their quinary counterparts below $N$ costs at most $(3/2+5/4)\eta N/(1-\eta)+O(\log N)$. If bounded denominators ever have positive upper density, discard these strips and $[0,\delta N)$ with sufficiently small fixed $\eta,\delta$. A positive fraction remains. Both deficits there are bounded in terms of $\eta$, and $[\delta N,N)$ meets only boundedly many shell pairs. Within a shell pair, each fixed shape is an interval with constant denominator. Consequently some fixed shape has recurring bounded denominators on intervals of length at least a fixed positive multiple of the cutoff. Rectangles have growing denominators by their explicit valuations, so this recurring shape would be nonrectangular. Conversely, such intervals violate the criterion. [Rectangle valuation formula](../../phase13/independent-axes.md#exact-loss-on-full-rectangles)

The marginal probabilities are $1/(2\cdot3^{h-1})$ and $1/(4\cdot5^{k-1})$. Bounding a diverging loss threshold first by an arbitrary fixed depth and then sending that depth to infinity gives sublinear loss in logarithmic density. For a fixed shape and $T\le m\le2T$, a failure class of period greater than $T$ accounts for at most one index, leaving $O(\log(2T))$ loss elsewhere. A single index can cover a macroscopic interval. Even the *joint* geometry admits abstract isometries with arbitrarily good ordinary approximations. Start with $L_j=1+2z_j$ and $K_j=1+4y_j$ on the one-corner sheet. At each step impose $z_{j+1}\equiv z_j\pmod{3^{K_j+j}}$ and $y_{j+1}\equiv y_j\pmod{5^{L_j+j}}$. The same fixed-modulus rotation gives larger exponents with $5^{L_{j+1}}/3^{K_{j+1}}$ arbitrarily close to $5/6$. The resulting limits in $\mathbb Z_3$ and $\mathbb Z_5$ define translation isometries with simultaneous near-full loss in this model. Deciding ordinary normality for the actual product requires information specific to its zeros or its bounded-denominator frequencies.

## Independent exact checks

I re-elaborated the Lean module from the Git blob at `d323f59` with Lean 4.36.0 and ran all five committed Python tests. Two additional calculations checked the arithmetic independently.

- Up to and including $3^{100}$, use the single denominator $D=3^{100}5^{68}$. At every successive power or insertion $n$, advance the previous residue at $m$ by $W_n=2^{n-m}W_m+\sum_{r+s=n}D/(rs)\bmod D$, where $r,s$ are positive powers of three and five. Compare $D/\gcd(D,W_n)$ with the two clipped shape valuations. They agreed on all 6,899 half-open intervals starting at $n\ge5$, covering 519 oriented shapes. Within each interval only doubling occurs.
- Generate $x_j$ from $x_0=1/2$ by multiplying by $q^{-1}2^{-(q-1)q^{j-1}}$ modulo $p^7$. At depths one through six, scan a complete index period starting at $60u$ for each shape $(0,0),(1,1),(2,7),(8,1),(17,12),(56,25)$. For all 4,368 ternary and 93,744 quinary comparisons there was one failing class per shape and depth, and every failure-sheet increment agreed through its next digit. On the one-corner shape the least nonnegative ternary class representatives were $1,3,9,27,81,405$, and the quinary ones were $1,9,49,349,2349,4849$.
