# What can certify binary normality?

**Theorem.** Even with the promise that a program computes the digits of a transcendental real and that its binary discrepancy converges to either zero or one, there is no algorithm that halts exactly on the normal inputs. There is also no algorithm that halts exactly on the nonnormal inputs. Two explicit reductions prove these statements. Each program reads a finite part of one computation before returning a requested digit. A separate, theory-dependent choice of computation gives a normality assertion independent of PA or ZFC, assuming the respective theory is consistent.

Write $t_n(x)=\{2^n x\}$ and let $D_N(x)$ be the supremum over $0\le u<v\le1$ of the absolute difference between the frequency of $t_0,\ldots,t_{N-1}$ in $[u,v)$ and $v-u$. Write $D^+(x)=\limsup_N D_N(x)$. Digits are numbered from one, so the first digit at shift $n$ is digit $n+1$. Normality in this note means **binary** normality. It is equivalent to $D_N\to0$. Indeed the dyadic cylinders are exactly the fixed-word tests. At each fixed mesh, interval counts can be sandwiched between unions of cells with length error at most twice the mesh. First let $N$ grow, then refine the mesh. We use the terminating expansion at a dyadic rational. [Kernel definitions and equivalence](../../NormalityChecker/BinaryDiscrepancy.lean)

## Eight limitations of existing proof methods

Each entry identifies an estimate that fails and an estimate that would suffice to proceed. Strict discrepancy means $D^+<1$. It is weaker than normality.

1. **Fixed-support rational orbit certificates.** Paper IV proves normality when almost all prescribed blocks of length $T$ admit a rational orbit with vanishing mean shadowing error, growing reduced denominator $q\le T^d$, and all denominator primes in a fixed set coprime to the radix. Paper II supplies such control for localized unsigned sums with an off-base prime. In the full Taylor series for $\ln2$, the shifted prefix at $N$ has $\log q_N=N+o(N)$. Even the optimal fixed finite identity in the real dyadic logarithms has rate $1/2$. On the prescribed scales $\log T=o(N)$ these particular denominators exceed every $T^d$. The fixed-support normality theorem cannot be passed to the infinite support by pointwise convergence. To apply the certificate one needs different accurately shadowing models on enough blocks, or a uniform orbit estimate as the support grows. The denominator calculation alone does not exclude alternative models. [LS-01](../../docs/results.md#ls-01) · [AC-01](../../docs/results.md#ac-01) · [CC-01 through CC-03](../../docs/results.md#cc-01)

2. **Overlapping rational models.** Fix the support and polynomial exponent as in AC-01. On an overlap of actual length $T$, two aligned models with denominators at most $T^d$ that differ modulo one have combined mean error at least a fixed $\delta>0$, against *any* observations. Hence successive models with error tending to zero on the overlaps eventually agree there modulo one. Shifting preserves the denominator by coprimality, so a compatible chain has eventually constant denominator. There is also a counting obstruction on the fixed AC-01 schedule with $B,L\ge2$. For almost every target, only finitely many scheduled blocks have **any** denominator $\le T^d$ model with mean error $\le1/(16b^4)$ in radix $b$. The finite counting bound is kernel-checked and the Borel–Cantelli deduction is on paper. Consequently even almost every normal real lacks these certificates on that schedule. One possible bypass is a target-specific family of disjoint good blocks with a proved covered proportion and controlled uncovered positions. For full normality, coverage tending to one and vanishing within-block discrepancy suffice. [AC-03, overlap rigidity](../../docs/results.md#ac-03) · [AC-10, counting](../../docs/results.md#ac-10) · [AC-02, coverage](../../docs/results.md#ac-02)

3. **Nearby Taylor cutoffs.** Put $T_j=\sum_{k=1}^j2^{-k}/k$. For every integer affine combination $\sum_i a_i T_{j_i}$ with $\sum_i a_i=1$, $K\le j_i\le K+h$ and $h=o(K)$, the odd part $q$ of the reduced denominator after any binary shift satisfies $\log q=K+o(K)$ uniformly. The shared terms in each fixed prime band leave the same nonzero local residue in every row, which survives the affine sum. Arbitrarily many rows and arbitrarily large coefficients are allowed. At width $K$, all the increments together generate the requisite odd-denominator group and some affine choice has odd denominator one. Using that bypass for a real orbit would require simultaneous control of the coefficients, the full denominator including its binary part, and the propagated real error. [CC-40 and its linear-width comparison](../phase15/nearby-taylor.md)

4. **Balanced algebraic trace sources.** For a monic integral polynomial whose roots are all $2^{a/e}\zeta$ at a common rational binary exponent with roots of unity $\zeta$, homogeneous trace cutoffs at integer base parameter $t>a/e$ collapse at **every cutoff** to integer combinations of $A_k=-\log(1-2^{-k})$ and $G_t=-\log|1-(1+i)2^{-t}|^2$. A rational combination with limit $\ln2$ therefore has four collapsed rational parameters. Write $H_N$ for the maximum of their common denominator and the absolute numerators written over it. With $\log H_N=o(N)$ the full shifted log-denominator rate differs by $o(1)$ from the corresponding fixed-formula rate. The lower limit is at least $5/12$, and at least $13/30$ if the unique minimizing vector is eventually avoided. The $5/12$ bound also holds under $\log H_N\le N/528$ for **integer** vectors, or $N/562$ for rational vectors. These are sufficient thresholds. At unrestricted integer height CRT permits rate zero. To exploit this, the resulting numerators must still track the real orbit over usable shifts. Balanced degree alone adds no directions. A different source, or larger height with a simultaneous precision estimate, can get outside the denominator theorem. The collapse and asymptotics have paper proofs. Lean verifies the finite cell certificate. [CC-43, balanced completeness](../../docs/results.md#cc-43) · [CC-42, rates and height](../phase16/adaptive-logarithms.md)

5. **Single-prime and single-band statistics.** Specifying each odd-primary component of a rational phase modulo one leaves its dyadic component free. Pair A below preserves even the entire odd-primary vector at every cutoff while shadowing either of two opposite orbit types. In CC-25, off-diagonal cancellation for selected prime components and their complements leaves the diagonal character $e^{2\pi i hY_n}$ of the full phase untouched. This distinction matters for the upper band $(N/2,N]$. If one *also* fixes all successive exact displacements and the common-denominator bounds of the diagonal logarithm model, CC-39 proves precisely rational-translate ambiguity in the eventual data. Pair A varies the displacements and does not contradict that rigidity. A bypass requires information coupling the components in the actual real phase, for example cancellation on the diagonal for every fixed nonzero frequency, which gives normality by Weyl's criterion. [CC-25](../../docs/results.md#cc-25) · [CC-39, including the fixed-start subgroup](../phase15/conditional-variation.md#4-rigidity-of-eventual-upper-band-data-paper-finite-check)

6. **Higher-rank density and fixed-width projection.** Put $E(t)=1-\cos(2\pi t)$. For every prescribed $\log M_N=o(N)$ a sparse irrational has $D_N\to1$ and $N^{-1}\sum_{n<N}\max_{1\le m\le M_N}E(m2^nx)\to0$, even though its full joint times-two, times-three orbit is dense. Digit gaps are chosen so that the next $\lceil\log_2 M_N\rceil+\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$ digits are zero at almost all starts. Independently, each fixed-width projection of the logarithm with cofactor bounded after removing a fixed finite prime set containing three is normal. Taking width to infinity after this assertion gives no ordinary word-frequency estimate for the full sum. A positive **lower** mean at bounded multiplier cost transfers escape to the binary axis by $E(mt)\le m^2 E(t)$. For normality via projections one needs uniform word or Fourier estimates and an error bound for transferring them as the width grows. [CC-36, including exponential heights](../phase15/multiplier-height.md) · [CC-37, fixed-width projections](../phase15/projection-and-returns.md)

7. **Complex product endpoints.** Let $P_N(a,z)=\prod_{n<N}(1+az^{2^n})$. For *every prescribed* $0<a_N<1$ tending to one there is an irrational $x$ with $D_N(x)\to1$ and both $N^{-1}\log|P_N(a_N,e^{2\pi ix})|$ and its endpoint counterpart at $a=1$ tending to zero. At each fixed interior $a$ the same normalized logarithm tends to $\log(1+a)$. Endpoint telescoping and rare factors near zero explain how these limits coexist. A sufficient and necessary replacement for strict discrepancy is a positive lower mean deficit $\liminf_N[\log(1+a)-N^{-1}\log|P_N(a,e^{2\pi ix})|]>0$ at one fixed $0<a<1$. [CC-32, counterexample and fixed-parameter inequalities](../phase15/fredholm-and-products.md)

8. **Irrationality exponents and digit runs.** If the irrationality exponent is two, a constant run extending from digit $m+1$ through $r$ gives a dyadic approximation of error $\le2^{-r}$ with denominator at most $2^m$. Thus $r\le(2+\varepsilon)m$ eventually, for each $\varepsilon>0$, and iteration guarantees order $\log N$ transitions. This order is sharp. The Kempner number $\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-2^j}$ has bounded partial quotients, only $O(\log N)$ transitions, and $D_N\to1$. A different example covers a much stronger word-occurrence hypothesis. For the Lambert series $L_{\chi_4}=\sum_{m\ge1}\chi_4(m)/(2^m-1)$, CC-46 gives $\Theta_w(N/\sqrt{\log N})$ occurrences of each word containing a one and disjunctivity (every word occurs). Its one density is zero. For fixed $k$, every nonzero $k$-window touches a one, so only $o(N)$ such starts occur. Testing $[0,2^{-k})$ then taking $k\to\infty$ gives **$D_N\to1$**. Positive lower ordinary transition density suffices for strict discrepancy. Normality requires the limiting frequencies $2^{-k}$ of all $k$-words. [Run bounds and the Kempner example](../phase13/logarithm-transitions.md) · [CC-46, word-count theorem and evidence](../../docs/results.md#cc-46) · [Transition criterion](../transition-discrepancy.md)

## Two explicit pairs

**Pair A. Identical odd-primary components and identical odd-denominator rate.** In $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ every rational phase decomposes uniquely into its prime-power-order components by the Chinese remainder theorem. Two phases agree at every odd prime exactly when their difference is dyadic modulo one. The invariant belongs to the *specified sequence of rational models* of each real.

Use $x_1=C_2$, the binary concatenation of the positive integers, and $x_2=L_2=\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k!}$. Their discrepancy limits are zero and one respectively, as proved below. Start both models from the actual shifted Taylor rows $R_n=2^nT_n$. For either target $x$, let $s_n(x)=\lfloor 2^n t_n(x)\rfloor$ and set

~~~math
Y_n(x)=R_n+2^{-n}\big(s_n(x)-\lfloor 2^nR_n\rfloor\big).
~~~

Then $2^nY_n(x)=s_n(x)+\{2^nR_n\}$. Thus $Y_n(x)$ lies in the same half-open mesh cell as $t_n(x)$, and the absolute error is less than $2^{-n}$. Its difference from $R_n$ is dyadic at **each** $n$. Both model sequences therefore have the same odd-primary components, including their values, and the same reduced odd denominators as $R_n$. These have log rate one by CC-03 (the dyadic valuation in the denominator of $R_n$ is at most $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$). Both models are computable from digits $n+1,\ldots,2n$. The cell formula works for any real target with its canonical expansion. Thus even knowing all those residues and an exponentially small per-position shadowing bound does not classify the orbit. [Taylor denominator theorem](../../docs/results.md#cc-03)

**Pair B. Identical logarithmic word laws and disjunctivity.** Let $x_1=C_2$. To form $x_2$, replace by zero its digits in each integer interval $[M_j,jM_j)$, where $M_j=2^{2^j}$ and $j\ge2$. Keep the original digits at their original positions everywhere else. The intervals are disjoint and their membership test terminates. Both reals have logarithmic frequency $2^{-k}$ for *every* word of length $k$, hence logarithmically equidistributed shifts and infinitely many occurrences of every word. Nevertheless $D^+(x_2)=1$.

For a word starting at digit $n$ use weight $1/n$, normalized by $H_N=\sum_{n=1}^N1/n$. Ordinary frequencies for $C_2$ give the same logarithmic frequencies by summation by parts: if $A(m)=pm+o(m)$ then $A(N)/N+\sum_{m<N}A(m)/(m(m+1))=pH_N+o(H_N)$. A word of fixed length $k$ can change only for starts in $[M_j-k+1,jM_j)$. The harmonic mass of such an interval is at most $\log j+O_k(1)$ once $M_j>2k$. Through the last interval whose enlarged left endpoint is at most $N$, say index $J$, the total is $O_k(J\log J)$, whereas $\log N\ge \log(M_J-k+1)\asymp 2^J$. The ratio tends to zero even partway through an interval. Dyadic grid approximation gives the interval version of logarithmic equidistribution. Positive limiting logarithmic frequency implies disjunctivity and ensures a canonical infinite expansion. Now take $N=jM_j$. At least $(j-1)M_j-k+1$ of its first $N$ starts have their next $k$ digits zero. Consequently $\limsup D_N(x_2)\ge 1-2^{-k}$ for every fixed $k$. The upper bound one proves the claim. This witness distinguishes ordinary prefix weights from logarithmic weights; a full ordinary limit of one is neither needed nor asserted here.

## Computable endpoints and their digit oracles

**The normal endpoint.** The bits of $C_2=0.(1)(10)(11)(100)\ldots$ have a primitive-recursive random-access oracle. There are $2^{r-1}$ words of length $r$. Subtract blocks of $r2^{r-1}$ digits from a zero-based offset until it enters one block. Division with remainder by $r$ gives the integer within that block and the digit within its spelling. There are $O(\log(n+1))$ block iterations for digit $n$ (an arithmetic-operation count). Mahler's 1937 theorem gives transcendence in every integer base. Champernowne's original 1933 paper treated decimal concatenation. [Champernowne](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-8.4.254) · [Mahler, original paper reprinted](https://ems.press/books/dms/252/4981)

Here is the binary counting proof, including incomplete blocks. Fix a word of length $k$. In the first $m\le2^{r-1}$ consecutive $r$-bit integers, consider an internal occurrence with $i$ less significant bits to its right, $0\le i\le r-k$. The indicator is periodic in the integer with period $2^{i+k}$ and mean $2^{-k}$ per full period. Its count differs from $m2^{-k}$ by at most $2^{i+k}$, even for the position including the leading digit. Summing these bounds costs at most $2^{r+1}$. Starts crossing a numeral boundary cost at most $k$ per numeral. Thus the count differs from $2^{-k}mr$ by $O_k(2^r)$ throughout a partial block. Sum over shorter lengths and allow an unfinished numeral at cost $O(r+k)$. A prefix reaching length $r$ already has at least $(r-1)2^{r-2}$ digits from the previous block for $r\ge2$. Its relative error is $O_k(1/r)$, proving normality.

**The maximal-discrepancy endpoint.** In $L_2$, bit $n$ is one exactly at $1!,2!,3!,\ldots$. Starting at $1!$ avoids the duplicated $0!$. Bounded factorial membership is primitive recursive. Up through position $N$ there are at most $1+\log_2 N$ ones, since $k!\ge2^{k-1}$. The gaps grow without bound and there are infinitely many ones, so the expansion is irrational. For a fixed $h$, at most $h(1+\log_2(N+h))$ of the first $N$ starts can have a one among their next $h$ digits. Testing $[0,2^{-h})$ gives $\liminf D_N\ge1-2^{-h}$ and hence $D_N\to1$.

The truncation at $k!$ has denominator $q=2^{k!}$ in lowest terms and error at most $2\cdot2^{-(k+1)!}=2q^{-(k+1)}$. To see transcendence directly, an algebraic irrational root $\xi$ of an irreducible integer polynomial $P$ of degree $d$ satisfies $|P(p/q)|\ge q^{-d}$. The derivative is bounded near $\xi$, so $|\xi-p/q|\ge c q^{-d}$ there by the mean value theorem. The displayed approximations violate this bound for every fixed $d$ (Liouville's argument).

**An entirely elementary substitute for Mahler's input.** For a formalization starting without a transcendence theorem for $C_2$, one can also obtain the *normal* endpoint by Liouville's argument. Form $U_j$ by concatenating all $2^j$ binary strings of length $j$ in lexicographic order, with leading zeros. Its length is $\ell_j=j2^j$. Start with $S_0=0$ and at stage $j\ge1$ append the shortest positive number of whole copies of $U_j$ giving total length $S_j\ge(j+1)^2(S_{j-1}+\ell_j)$. This defines a primitive-recursive digit oracle: find the first $j\le n$ with $S_j\ge n$, then use two divisions to locate the word and bit within a copy. For fixed $k\le j$, a full copy contributes $(j-k+1)2^{j-k}$ occurrences of a given $k$-word wholly inside the individual $j$-strings. The remaining starts number $(k-1)2^j$, so the error relative to $\ell_j2^{-k}$ is at most $(k-1)2^j$, independently of the continuation. Complete copies at all sufficiently late stages thus have arbitrarily small relative error. At any cutoff in stage $j$, the unfinished copy contributes at most $\ell_j$ error and $\ell_j/S_{j-1}\to0$, since $S_{j-1}\ge j^2\ell_{j-1}$. The finite early portion has vanishing weight. This proves normality at every cutoff, hence irrationality. The rational continuing the first $S_{j-1}$ bits periodically with $U_j$ has denominator $q_j\le 2^{S_{j-1}}(2^{\ell_j}-1)$. It agrees for $S_j$ digits and has strictly positive error in absolute value, at most $2^{-S_j}\le q_j^{-(j+1)^2}$. The denominators are unbounded since these rationals converge to an irrational. The polynomial lower bound just proved gives transcendence. Substituting this stream for $C_2$ in either reduction makes all endpoint proofs elementary. Normal Liouville numbers are also available under the stronger absolute-normality requirement. [Becher–Heiber–Slaman, a computable absolutely normal Liouville number](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/2964)

**Stoneham comparison.** An alternative normal endpoint is $\alpha_{2,3}=\sum_{j\ge1}(3^j2^{3^j})^{-1}$. The repository proves its binary normality, e.g. by taking $d=1$ in the binary power theorem. Transcendence follows from Coons's published appendix, or from the restricted-denominator argument in DS-07 (Roth already suffices at parameter three). For a direct digit computation, retain the powers $3^j\le n$, let $p$ be the largest retained power (or $1$), and multiply the partial sum by $2^n$. It lies on the grid $p^{-1}\mathbb Z$. The omitted positive terms total at most $(3p)^{-1}\sum_{h\ge1}2^{-h}=1/(3p)$ after scaling. They cannot cross an integer boundary. The numerator modulo $2p$ therefore gives the digit by integer division by $p$. [Lean binary power theorem](../../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomial.lean) · [Coons, transcendence appendix](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788713000682) · [DS-07](../../docs/results.md#ds-07)

## Two reductions from halting

Take a standard enumeration of machines with primitive-recursive initial state and one-step transition, and make the halting state absorbing. Write $B(e,n)$ for the bit that says whether machine $e$ on input $e$ has halted by step $n$, including step $n$ and any initial halt. Iterating the transition $n$ times is primitive recursive in $(e,n)$. Let $K=\{e:\exists n\, B(e,n)=1\}$. Simulating until a halt enumerates $K$. If both $K$ and its complement were computably enumerable (c.e.), dovetailing the two searches would decide $K$. Given such a decider, program a machine to loop on input $i$ if the decider answers yes for $i$ and to halt otherwise. Its behavior on its own index contradicts the decider. Thus the complement of $K$ is not c.e.

**Finite-change lemma.** Suppose two bit streams representing $u,v$ agree after digit $m$. Then

~~~math
u-v=\frac{1}{2^m}\sum_{i=1}^m (u_i-v_i)2^{m-i}.
~~~

In particular $u-v\in\mathbb Z[1/2]$ and $t_n(u)=t_n(v)$ for $n\ge m$. Conversely the latter equality of shifts holds after adding any $z/2^m$, $z\in\mathbb Z$. For every interval the counts through $N>0$ differ by at most $\min(m,N)$. Taking absolute centered frequencies and suprema gives

~~~math
|D_N(u)-D_N(v)|\le \frac{\min(m,N)}{N}.
~~~

Therefore every discrepancy limit is preserved, including both zero and one. Transcendence is preserved under rational translation since a polynomial relation for $u$ gives one for $v$ by substituting $X+u-v$ and clearing denominators.

**Forward switch.** At digit $n\ge1$ of $x_e$, return bit $n$ of $C_2$ while $B(e,n)=0$, and bit $n$ of $L_2$ otherwise. Both endpoints have primitive-recursive bits (all loops above can be bounded by $n+1$). Composition gives a single primitive-recursive digit function in $(e,n)$. Hardwiring $e$ into this algorithm is an effective map from indices to indices. The first $m$ bits give a dyadic approximation with error at most $2^{-m}$, uniformly in $e,m$. Each possible tail has infinitely many zeros and ones, so the real lies strictly between zero and one and its specified bits are canonical. If $e\notin K$, $x_e=C_2$. If $e$ halts at $t$, $x_e$ agrees with $L_2$ at every digit $n\ge\max(1,t)$. The lemma proves transcendence in both cases and gives the exact respective limits zero and one.

**Reverse switch.** Form $y_e$ by exchanging the two endpoints, using the same $B(e,n)$. If $e\notin K$ it equals $L_2$. After a halt it agrees with $C_2$ from the switching digit onward. It too is computable, transcendental and promised, and it is normal exactly for $e\in K$.

**Consequence for recognition.** A promise semidecider for normality must halt on every promised normal name and diverge on every promised nonnormal name. Behavior outside the promise is arbitrary. Applying it to the forward names would semidecide $\mathbb N\setminus K$. A semidecider for *nonnormality* applied to the reverse names would do the same. Hence the promised property is neither c.e. nor co-c.e., in particular undecidable. The same argument applies to programs for fast Cauchy names because our digit names convert uniformly to such names. Every constructed program is total on digit queries. No decision about totality, transcendence, the existence of the limit, or the speed of convergence is supplied to the classifier.

### Relation to prior work

Ki and Linton proved that the set of reals normal to any fixed base is $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^0_3$-complete in the Borel hierarchy (*Fundamenta Mathematicae* **144**, 1994, 163–179). Becher, Heiber and Slaman proved the corresponding boldface and effective completeness of absolute normality in *Fundamenta Mathematicae* **226** (2014), 63–77. Their Theorem 3.2 already maps arithmetic $\Pi^0_3$ sentences computably to indices of reals absolutely normal on truth and normal to no base on falsity. Their corollaries include the fixed-base effective result. [Ki–Linton](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm-144-2-163-179) · [Becher–Heiber–Slaman](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm226-1-4) · [Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.0335)

Becher and Slaman studied which sets of bases can occur and proved $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^0_4$-completeness for normality to *at least one* base (*Journal of the London Mathematical Society* **90**, 2014, 472–494). Lutz and Mayordomo constructed an absolutely normal real whose first $n$ binary digits can be generated in $n\,\mathrm{polylog}(n)$ time. These address different quantifiers: generating one designated normal object and recognizing arbitrary supplied names are distinct algorithmic tasks. Our reduction gives a two-limit, transcendental promise by finite prefix changes. Its purpose is an elementary proof suitable for kernel formalization; historical priority and a completeness classification of the promise problem are outside its claims. [Becher–Slaman](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdu035) · [Lutz–Mayordomo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05911)

### Lean 4 formalization and specifications

The finite-change invariance, uniform computability of the switched digit oracle, forward and reverse halting reductions, and undecidability/non-c.e./non-co-c.e. theorems under the transcendental $\{0,1\}$-discrepancy promise are kernel-verified in [ComputableNormalityUndecidability.lean](../../NormalityChecker/ComputableNormalityUndecidability.lean) (building on [Basic.lean](../../NormalityChecker/Basic.lean) and [BinaryDiscrepancy.lean](../../NormalityChecker/BinaryDiscrepancy.lean)). The Lean module takes any computable transcendental normal endpoint `a` and computable transcendental maximal-discrepancy endpoint `b` as explicit inputs, and proves the reductions both directly over the switched-parameter family and uniformly over arbitrary digit-oracle codes `Nat.Partrec.Code` via s-m-n parameter specialization (`Names`). Below is the blueprint for instantiating the two endpoints unconditionally with `C_2` and `L_2` (zero-based streams $s:\mathbb N\to\mathrm{Bool}$, $V(s)=\sum_{i\ge0}[s(i)]/2^{i+1}$, and $L(s,d)$ for convergence of `binaryDiscrepancy (V s)` to $d$):

~~~lean
abbrev Stream := ℕ → Bool
def sw (s t : Stream) (e i : ℕ) : Bool :=
  if b e (i+1) then t i else s i
def L (s : Stream) (d : ℝ) : Prop :=
  Filter.Tendsto (NormalityChecker.binaryDiscrepancy (V s)) Filter.atTop (nhds d)
~~~

1. **Summation and digits.** For every $s,m$, prove $0\le V(s)-\sum_{i<m}[s(i)]/2^{i+1}\le2^{-m}$. If $s$ has arbitrarily late zeros, prove $\mathrm{binaryBit}(V(s),i)=s(i)$ for all $i$. For the two switches prove arbitrarily late bits of each kind.
2. **Finite tails.** For all $s,t,m$, from $\forall i\ge m,\ s(i)=t(i)$ derive $\exists z:\mathbb Z,\ V(s)-V(t)=(z:\mathbb R)/2^m$. For all reals $u,v$, integers $z$ and naturals $m,N$ with $N>0$, from $u-v=z/2^m$ derive $|D_N(u)-D_N(v)|\le(\min(m,N):\mathbb R)/N$ and $\mathrm{Trans}(u)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Trans}(v)$. Deduce $L(s,d)\leftrightarrow L(t,d)$ for each real $d$ in the finite-tail lemma.
3. **Machine and endpoints.** Exhibit primrec codes on paired naturals for $b$, the two endpoint bits, and both functions $(e,i)\mapsto\mathrm{sw}(s,t,e,i)$ for $(s,t)=(C,F),(F,C)$. Prove monotonicity of $b$ in the bound, $\neg K(e)\Rightarrow\mathrm{sw}(s,t,e)=s$, and $b(e,h)=\mathrm{true}\Rightarrow\forall i\ge h,\ \mathrm{sw}(s,t,e,i)=t(i)$. Prove $L(C,0)$ by the block count and $L(F,1)$ by the sparse-window bound. The elementary switching theorem takes $\mathrm{Trans}(V(C)),\mathrm{Trans}(V(F))$ as explicit inputs. An unconditional endpoint instantiation requires formal proofs of these inputs, including Mahler's theorem or an alternative proved transcendental normal endpoint.
4. **Both reductions.** Set $X(e)=\mathrm{sw}(C,F,e)$ and $Y(e)=\mathrm{sw}(F,C,e)$. For each $S=X(e),Y(e)$ prove $0<V(S)<1$, $\mathrm{Trans}(V(S))$, and $L(S,0)\lor L(S,1)$. Prove for all $e$ that $L(X(e),0)\leftrightarrow\neg K(e)$ and $L(Y(e),0)\leftrightarrow K(e)$, likewise replacing each zero-limit predicate by $\mathrm{NormalIn}(2,V(S))$. Use uniqueness of limits and the existing theorem equating normality with **limsup** discrepancy zero, together with $0\le D_N\le1$.
5. **Names and promise.** For a partial evaluator $\varphi$, define $\mathrm{Names}(a,s):=\forall i,\ \varphi_a(i)\downarrow=[s(i)]$. Define $P(s):=0<V(s)<1\ \land\ \mathrm{Trans}(V(s))\ \land\ (L(s,0)\lor L(s,1))$. Exhibit total computable index maps $f,g$ with $\mathrm{Names}(f(e),X(e))$ and $\mathrm{Names}(g(e),Y(e))$ for all $e$ by parameter specialization. The final two goal statements, one for each $Q(s)=\mathrm{NormalIn}(2,V(s))$ and $Q(s)=\neg\mathrm{NormalIn}(2,V(s))$, are

   ~~~math
   \neg\exists A\subseteq\mathbb N,\quad
   \mathrm{CE}(A)\ \land\ \forall a,s,\ 
     \big(\mathrm{Names}(a,s)\land P(s)\big)\Rightarrow\big(a\in A\iff Q(s)\big).
   ~~~

   Here $\mathrm{CE}(A)$ means $A$ is the domain of a partial computable function. Pull a hypothetical domain back along $f$ or $g$ and apply the diagonal halting theorem. Quantifying over codes enforces uniform recognition for every promised name.

## Normality assertions independent of a theory

**Which sentence is independent?** Fix a recursive enumeration of the axioms of a classical theory $T$ (a computably enumerable presentation suffices), an interpretation of arithmetic, and a digit program. Write $\mathsf{Norm}(s)$ for the arithmetic sentence that for every coded word of length $k\ge1$ and every integer tolerance denominator $m\ge1$, there is $N_0$ such that for all $N\ge N_0$ the number $c$ of occurrences starting in the first $N$ places satisfies $m|2^kc-N|<2^kN$. Take $N_0\ge1$. For the primitive-recursive digit streams used here, these are statements about integers and finite counts. The chosen *description of the real* is part of the sentence.

The **internal implication needed for transfer** is as follows. For a primitive-recursive witness test $W(p)$, let $s_W$ use $C_2$ until there is a witness $p\le n$ at digit $n$, then use $L_2$ forever. Require that the interpreted arithmetic of $T$ proves

~~~math
\mathsf{Norm}(s_W)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \forall p\,\neg W(p).
~~~

PA proves this uniformly for the witness tests below. In the no-witness case the defining bit is always the Champernowne bit. The block and remainder bounds above use integer division, geometric sums and induction, and give for every $k,m$ a cutoff for the displayed frequency test. In the witness case a witness $p$ bounds the number of initial changed starts by $p$ for each word. For the one-bit word, the count through $N$ is at most $p+1+\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$ for $N\ge1$, eventually less than $N/4$. These integer arguments, the primitive recursions, and finite counting are all available in PA and in ZFC's natural numbers. No internal transcendence proof or real-analysis formalism is needed. Either theorem below applies also to weaker theories that prove its indicated internal implications. In particular, the Rosser case needs **only consistency as a soundness assumption**.

**Consistency switch.** Use the ordinary proof predicate $\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,A)$. For c.e. axioms, include finite enumeration-stage certificates for axiom uses in the proof code; this makes checking a presented certificate primitive recursive. Use the standard provability predicate satisfying the Hilbert–Bernays–Löb conditions in $T$. Set $W(p)=\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner)$. Then $T\vdash\mathsf{Norm}(s_W)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Con}(T)$. If $T$ is consistent, Gödel's second incompleteness theorem excludes $T\vdash\mathrm{Con}(T)$, hence excludes a proof of normality. If $T$ is also $\Sigma_1$-sound for the chosen arithmetic, a proof of nonnormality would imply a proof of the false $\Sigma_1$ assertion $\exists p\,W(p)$. This excludes both decisions. Externally, under consistency the switch never fires, so the real is computable, transcendental, and has $D_N\to0$. For either outcome of any such search, the two-limit promise follows from the finite-change lemma.

**Rosser switch under consistency alone.** Use the same certified proof coding and obtain by the diagonal lemma a sentence $R$ with the fixed point

~~~math
T\vdash R\ \longleftrightarrow\
\forall p\big(\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,\ulcorner R\urcorner)\Rightarrow
             \exists q\le p\,\mathrm{Prf}_T(q,\ulcorner\neg R\urcorner)\big).
~~~

Define $W_R(p)$ by “$p$ codes a proof of $R$ and every $q\le p$ fails to code a proof of $\neg R$.” This is a primitive-recursive bounded test. At digit $n$, switch if *some* $p\le n$ passes it. Comparing **proof codes with smaller codes** implements the displayed fixed point even if the underlying enumeration of axioms reports proofs in a different order. One code has one terminal formula, so the convention on ties is immaterial. A switch is permanent since its witness remains available. Classical logic and the fixed point give $T\vdash R\leftrightarrow\forall p\,\neg W_R(p)$.

Here are both unprovability arguments. They use only consistency and numeralwise correctness of the proof tests.

- If $T$ proves $R$, take an actual proof code $p$. Consistency excludes any actual proof of $\neg R$. The finitely many negative checks for $q\le p$ and the positive check at $p$ are provable in the interpreted arithmetic. Hence $T\vdash W_R(\bar p)$, so $T\vdash\neg R$, a contradiction.
- If $T$ proves $\neg R$, take an actual proof code $q$. Consistency excludes any proof of $R$, in particular all codes $p<q$. The theory verifies this finite list. For $p\ge\bar q$ the verified proof at $\bar q$ supplies the bounded witness required by the fixed point. For $p<\bar q$ the antecedent fails by the finite list. The split at a fixed numeral and the bounded enumeration below it are available already in Robinson arithmetic with the usual numeral comparisons. Thus $T\vdash R$, again a contradiction. [Formal analysis of the Rosser argument](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10817-021-09599-8)

Apply the internal frequency implication to $W_R$. It gives $T\vdash\mathsf{Norm}(s_{W_R})\leftrightarrow R$. The two paragraphs exclude $T$-proofs of both normality and its negation, without assuming $\Sigma_1$-soundness. The oracle is explicit uniformly from the effective presentation and the effective diagonal substitution. In the standard natural numbers, consistency implies there is no witness, so the real has the same value as $C_2$. Its switch description has independent normality even though the ordinary concatenation description has provable normality in PA. A theory proving their equality and the latter normality would itself prove the former.

**Scope for very weak arithmetic.** Interpreting Robinson arithmetic $Q$ or representing primitive-recursive functions numeral by numeral suffices for the syntactic Rosser argument. It does not by itself give a proof in $T$ of an unbounded frequency calculation. In particular, merely proving *externally* that normality is equivalent to $R$ gives no implication between $T$-proofs. The formulation for an arbitrary interpreter of $Q$ needs an arithmetization and an internal transfer lemma. PA and ZFC have both.

One can see the obstruction to a coding-independent assertion concretely. Use the common convention that a valid stream name has a coded binary prefix of every length, stored as $2^n$ plus its $n$-bit value, with the power required to exist. Include validity in the predicate asserting that a name represents a normal real. Over standard arithmetic this is exactly the same assertion for our total digit programs. In bounded arithmetic the axiom “exponentiation with base two is not total” refutes validity for **every** name in this encoding. The theory $I\Delta_0+\neg\mathrm{Exp}_2$ is consistent and can even be $\Sigma_1$-sound. To see this, in a nonstandard elementary extension of the natural numbers choose infinite $a$ and take the cut bounded by some $a^k$ with standard $k$. It is closed under addition and multiplication, has absolute bounded formulas and satisfies bounded induction (a bounded counterexample would have a least one). It satisfies every true $\Pi_1$ sentence by downward absoluteness. With the usual bounded graph of exponentiation there is no value of $2^a$ in the cut. This supplies a model and hence $\Sigma_1$-soundness of the displayed theory. It extends $Q$ in the usual arithmetic language. This is why the internal hypothesis matters when stating a theorem across weak theories and choices of coding.

These are explicit computable transcendental reals presented by proof searches depending on $T$. They yield no independence conclusion for the usual descriptions of $\ln2$, $\pi$ or $e$ in PA or ZFC. The normality of those constants remains open. [Repository open problems](../../docs/open-problems.md)
