## B1. From valuations to actual digits

**Local factors and mean.** At $\chi(p)=1$ the local factor in $c(p^e)$ is $e+1$, and at $\chi(p)=0$ it is one. At $\chi(p)=\omega^{\pm1}$ its values for $e\bmod3=0,1,2$ are $1,e^{\pm i\pi/3},0$. Thus $c=A\zeta_6^t$ on the complex support. On a nonzero projected phase the exact valuation is

$$
v_2(a(n))=\sum_{\chi(p)=1}v_2(v_p(n)+1)+v_2\bigl(|\Phi(\zeta_6^t)|\bigr).
$$

Zero phases contribute zero mass. The forbidden geometric tail at a nontrivial prime value has probability $1/(p^2+p+1)$. The weight $w=\mathbf1_{c\ne0}\operatorname{odd}(A)$ has prime values one and $w(p^e)\le e+1$, so the prime powers of exponent at least two have summable corrections. Its mean is $C_{\rm odd}$ of note lines 116–123 and 142.

For fixed $k$, twist $w$ by a character of $(v_2(A)\bmod k,t\bmod6)$. The three prime values away from the modulus have average $(e^{2\pi i u/k}+2\cos(\pi j/3))/3$ by the cubic residue classes. Its real part reaches one only for the trivial twist. The Dirichlet-character factorization and Selberg–Delange estimate of note lines 28–32 give an $o(N)$ twisted sum in every other case, including a vanishing leading reciprocal gamma factor. Fourier inversion gives limiting weight $C_{\rm odd}/(6k)$ per pair. This proves the joint law without an independence assumption. Adding the phase valuation permutes the $k$ residues. Consequently the radix-reduced coefficient $U=a/b^{\lfloor v_2(a)/k\rfloor}$ has limiting mean absolute value $(b-1)C_\Phi/k$, since $\sum_{r<k}2^r=b-1$. The six odd-part projection factors sum to 6 for plus and 4 for minus, giving the multipliers $1$ and $2/3$. See note lines 140–148.

**Displacement and the infinite tail.** Set $\ell=n-\lfloor v_2(a)/k\rfloor$ at a nonzero coefficient. After multiplication by $b^j$, arrivals with $\ell\le j$ are integers. There are finitely many for each $j$, since $|a(n)|\ll_\Phi\tau(n)\ll_\Phi\sqrt n$ implies $\ell\to\infty$. The remaining series converges absolutely. The triangle inequality on the circle bounds the distance to an integer by $\sum_{\ell>j}|U|b^{j-\ell}$, even with coincident arrivals and opposite signs. Summing this kernel over $0\le j<\min(N,\ell)$ costs at most $1/(b-1)$ per unit of $|U|$.

For $H=\lceil3\log_b(2N)\rceil$, the extra $H$ terms beyond the averaging prefix cost $O_{\Phi,b}(\sqrt N\log N)=o(N)$. Beyond $N+H$, the fractional arrivals cost at most the sum of all original absolute terms over that prefix, because $|U|b^{j-\ell}=|a(n)|b^{j-n}$. This costs $O_{\Phi,b}(\sqrt N b^{-H})=o(1)$. Thus note lines 150–156 give the orbit mean-distance limsup $C_\Phi/k$ without a bound on carry length.

**Discrepancy and digits.** Integrating the empirical mass of $[t,1-t)$ gives the mean nearest-integer distance. Endpoint atoms affect only a null set of integration parameters. The canonical digit of $\{b^jz\}$ indexes its actual half-open first-digit cylinder, throughout which the distance is at least $\min(d,b-1-d)/b$. Equal digit frequencies would force lower mean distance at least $1/4-1/(2b)$. Using $C_{\rm odd}<21/25$, the slack at the first plus threshold exceeds $7/800$, and at the first minus threshold exceeds $1/1200$. Both comparisons improve as $k$ increases. The construction-radix restriction matters. Binary simple normality and membership of the dense series in $K_b$ remain open here. See note lines 125–140 and 158–164.

## B2. Conductor primes, classes and widths

**Exact lattice descent.** Divide the form by its positive content before factoring its primitive discriminant $f^2D$. Take an integral invertible representative $I$ prime to $f$. Extension to an overorder $\mathcal O_g$ preserves the ideal norm, since at conductor primes both local ideals equal the full respective orders and elsewhere the orders agree. At $p^a\parallel f$, write $R_a=\mathbb Z_p+p^a\mathcal O_{K,p}$. An element of maximal-order content $t<a$, divided by $p^t$, reduces to a nonzero scalar modulo $p$. Its norm reduces to that scalar squared, also at 2 and in the ramified algebra. Its norm valuation is therefore exactly $2t$. For $s\le a$ the vectors with norm divisible by $p^{2s}$ form $R_a\cap p^s\mathcal O_{K,p}=p^sR_{a-s}$. In the split algebra the content is the minimum of the two component valuations.

Combining these identities gives a bijection by division by $d=\prod_{p^a\parallel f}p^{\min(a,\lfloor v_p(n)/2\rfloor)}$. If $m=n/d^2$ shares a prime with $g=f/d$, its residual valuation there is one and representation is impossible. Otherwise the count is precisely $r_{Q_g}(m)$. Every supported positive integer determines its unique component, including when exponents exceed $2a$. The indices $p^t$ for $t<a$ and $p^{a-1}(p-\chi_D(p))$ upon reaching the maximal local order count unit cosets in the class-summed ideal factor. Individual lattice counts follow the bijection without a unit-index multiplier. See note lines 215–237.

**Prime-to-conductor transfer.** On $(m,g)=1$, the ideal product has the usual split group-ring sum, a singleton ramified class power, and the even-exponent constraint at inert primes. A ramified prime class squares to one. It can shift the genus and has trivial odd component. Deleting a conductor prime from the support Euler product supplies $1-p^{-2}$ if inert and $1-p^{-1}$ if split or ramified. Ring class field Chebotarev assigns an inverse-stable set $A$ of split classes density $|A|/(2|G_g|)$ among rational primes. An ambiguous singleton is counted once. Inverting the chosen form class preserves its coefficient.

The multiplicity twist at note line 254 loses real exponent for every nontrivial tuple. Once all ramified exponents have been fixed, the parity constraint on split multiplicities gives the prescribed coset in $G_g/G_g^2$. Every genus has relative mass $1/e_g$. For a character nontrivial on $G_g^2$, each exactly dividing split prime with $\psi^2(c_p)\ne1$ contracts the normalized eigenvalue by a factor bounded away from one. Such primes have positive density. The resulting $o(1)$ relative error in genus Fourier inversion gives representation by every class of that genus outside a relatively negligible set. Thus $B_g>0$ is a leading constant on the $X/\sqrt{\log X}$ scale. The represented integers have natural density zero.

**Valuations.** Fixing Euler factors first gives the independent geometric laws for any finite list of split exponents within a genus. A killing exponent requires $p^2\mid m$. For $P<p\le X^{1/4}$ the exceptional count is $O(S_K(X)\sum_{p>P}p^{-2})$, and larger primes cost $O(X^{3/4})=o(S_K(X))$. This licenses the infinite survival law. Killing requires the order of the **local value** $\psi(c_p)\ne1$ to divide $E_p+1$. The order of the surviving character itself then enters as its kernel index. The two orders can differ.

The free direction has trivial genus and positive split-prime density in the ring class setting. For odd $q$, the first-power bases are 2 or cyclotomic units.
