## GF-33. Rational approximation, poles, and normality

### The folding inequality and all rational denominators

For reduced $u=a/q$, a competitor $h/l$ gives $L=al-qh$ and $l^2|u-h/l|=l|L|/q$. Every nonzero signed lattice pair $L\equiv al\bmod q$ occurs after a simultaneous change of signs if necessary. Thus $1/q\le B(u)\le1$, and swapping coordinates proves the inverse symmetry. For $q=1$ the minimum is 1. In Lemma 5, write $z=c/d\ne0,\ w=l/q,\ v=u+z/q^2$. The target quantity is $w|L+zw|$. If $L=0$, reducedness implies $w\ge1$ is integral. If $|L|\ge2|z|w$ with $L\ne0$, the triangle inequality gives at least $B(u)/2$. In the remaining case take the integer pair

$$
J=cl+qdL,\qquad F=cL,\qquad aJ-F=q(ch+adL).
$$

Here $F\ne0$, and $J=qc(w+L/z)\ne0$ because $h/l\ne v$. Therefore $B(u)\le |JF|/q=c^2|L|\,|w+L/z|\le2c^2w|L+zw|$. This yields the stated minimum for both signs of $c$, including integer prefixes. The Lean statements quantify over every integer numerator and every positive natural denominator, with only the equal rational excluded. Lean proves this local inequality and its infimum form. The passage to each infinite series is a paper proof. (C lines 170–185. Lean lines 80–91, 152–194, 220–227, 250–295, 307–358, 362–381.)

CRT gives $c_k\le\operatorname{rad}(b)$ and an exact $p^k$ denominator at the distinguished term. Each $\ell_j$ occurs in just one term with negative $\ell_j$-valuation. Multiplication by $b$ preserves these poles, and integer fillers preserve them as well. For the raw shifted prefix throughout $[2^k,2^{k+1})$ this gives exactly $D_k=p^k\prod_{j^2\le k}\ell_j$. At the cluster endpoint $m_k=2^k+w_k$, the last coefficient is a unit at every prime of $b$. All earlier terms acquire a factor of $b$ in the common numerator, giving the exact unshifted denominator $q_k=D_k b^{m_k}$. (C lines 189–200.)

For $W,W'$, if $A$ is the integer numerator of the next cluster as at C line 200, the unreduced correction is

$$
z_k=q_k^2(U_{k+1}-U_k)=
\frac{A D_k^2 b^{2w_k}}{p^{k+1}\mu_{k+1} b^{w_{k+1}}}.
$$

The $2^{k+1}$ radix exponent cancels exactly. Since $w_i/2^i\to0$, the logs of both reduced heights and $\log^+(1/|z_k|)$ are $o(2^k)$. Iterating the folding minimum adds at most $\log 2+2\log|\operatorname{num}z_k|+\log^+(1/|z_k|)$ to $\log(1/B(U_k))$ at each step. A sum of $o(2^i)$ to $k$ is $o(2^k)$.

Now fix **any** large denominator $l$, and choose the first $k$ with $2^k\ge4\log_b l$. Then $2^k<8\log_b l$ and $q_k>l$, so even an unreduced $h/l$ differs from $U_k$. The tail is $O_b(b^{-2^{k+1}})=O_b(l^{-8})$ by bounded coefficients at distinct integer exponents. For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, eventually $B(U_k)\ge l^{-\varepsilon/2}$. Subtracting the tail gives $|W^{(\prime)}-h/l|\ge l^{-2-\varepsilon}$ for every $h$. Multiples of a hypothetical rational denominator rule out rationality, and Dirichlet gives $\mu\ge2$. This exhausts the convergents as well as all other rational approximants. (C lines 198–205.)

### The dense positive series

The raw $V$ in Theorem 7 is **strictly greater than $W$**. Its exact equality is with its own doubling expansion with coefficients $d_k=c_k+\lambda_k-\lambda_{k-1}$. For a finite cutoff $K$, take all distinguished terms through $K$ and all fillers from those shells, including the last filler at $2^{K+1}$. Their sum is exactly

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_k b^{-2^k}-\lambda_K b^{-2^{K+1}}.
$$

This follows because $h_k\mid2^k$ and each block includes its right endpoint and excludes its left. The next distinguished term at that endpoint has its own coefficient. The last remainder vanishes since $\lambda_K\le1/(b-1)$. A raw prefix ending exactly at $2^k$, including $c_k$, can likewise be written as the grouped sum through $k-1$ plus $(c_k-\lambda_{k-1})b^{-2^k}$. Thus the two finite cutoff conventions agree where they should. The preceding full shell gives the lower count $\gg2^k/\sqrt{k}$, summing the shell counts gives the matching upper bound, and the next gap is at most $O(\sqrt{k+1})$. Inverting gives $n_j\asymp j\sqrt{\ln j}$ and consecutive ratios tending to one. This conclusion uses the specified block spacings. C lines 235 and 252 expressly limit the scope. (C lines 223–235, 250–252. SC lines 425–435.)

For these grouped prefixes $E_k=D_k(b^{h_k}-1)$ is a common off-radix denominator. The correction to $c_k$ has $p$-depth at most $a_0=v_p(b^{\operatorname{ord}_p b}-1)$, since $h_k$ is a power of two and $p$ is odd. Thus $A_k=E_kd_k\ne0$ for $k>a_0$. The full numerator over $E_kb^{2^k}$ equals $A_k\bmod b^{2^{k-1}}$. Each prime of $b$ has valuation in $A_k$ bounded by $O_{b,p}(k+\sqrt{k}\log(k+1))$, eventually smaller than its valuation in that modulus. The entire radix cancellation therefore divides $|A_k|$, and the off-radix cancellation divides $E_k$. Both heights of $q_k/b^{2^k}$ have logs of the same order. The identity $z_k=d_{k+1}(q_k/b^{2^k})^2$ gives the same log-height bound and a nonzero signed step. Iterating from any sufficiently late fixed prefix gives $\log(1/B)=o(2^k)$. The grouped tail is $O_b(b^{-2^{k+1}})$ in absolute value and $\log_b q_k=2^k+o(2^k)$ on the logarithmic scale. The preceding all-denominator argument applies to $V$. (C lines 237–250.)

### Polynomial images

For degree $r$, reflecting the distinguished indices $i=K-h_i$ in the convolution produces precisely the nonnegative vectors with $\sum_j2^{-h_j}\le n/2^K$, subject to the original indices being positive. Each infinite upper set contains the fixed diagonal anchor. Dickson's lemma and inclusion–exclusion give its series $F(X)/(1-X)^r$, with $F(1)=1$. Hence $F(p)\equiv1\bmod(p-1)\ne0\bmod(p-1)$ for odd $p$. Its $p$-adic value is nonzero. The set of anchored upper sets is compact, and reduction modulo $p^L$ depends only on the finitely many $|\mathbf h|<L$. The increasing open sets of nonvanishing modulo $p^L$ cover this compact space, so one $L(r,p)$ works for every cutoff. The finite lower-index boundary cannot affect these residues once $K\ge2L$. (C lines 207–213.)

The CRT unit of each distinguished term is $1\bmod p^i$ after multiplying by $p^i b^{-2^i}$ at the coefficient level. For $V$ the precision becomes $i-a_0$, and any fixed early index contributes a depth defect growing at least as $K-O(1)$. A tuple with an integer filler or a lower-degree term has depth at most $(r-1)K+O_P(1)$. The leading coefficient changes depth by a fixed valuation. For large $K$ it follows that the top pole of $P$'s shifted prefix has depth $rK+O_P(1)$ at every shift. All-prime persistence concerns the **specified raw expansion** of each input number. The polynomial argument needs only this one growing pole in the polynomial prefix. For instance $P(X)=\ell_jX$ can erase the simple $\ell_j$-pole. (C lines 7, 198, 211–215, 229, 237–241.)

For the Weyl estimate, a fixed radix shift clears the radix part of the coefficient denominator of $P$, costing $O_P(1/N)$ in discrepancy. At retained shifts $n\ge\sqrt N$, $K_0=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\ge\tfrac12\log_2N-1$. For all $h\le N^\xi$ with sufficiently small fixed $\xi>0$, the remaining depth $e=rK_0+O_P(1)-v_p(h)$ is bounded above and below by positive multiples of $\ln N$. The complementary modulus has log $o(\ln N)$, also for $V$ by the nested factors $b^{h_i}-1$. The stride $\tau=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}_p b,\operatorname{ord}_Q b)$ is $N^{o(1)}$. By LTE its principal-unit depth is $a_0+v_p(\operatorname{ord}_Q b)=o(\ln N)$. On a retained interval of length $T\ge N^{1/3}$ the split length is $M_0\ge N^{1/3-o(1)}$, so $p^{\theta e}\le M_0$ for some fixed $\theta>0$, and the principal-unit depth is below both $e$ and $3\log_p(M_0)/16-1$. These are the actual hypotheses of the [Phase 22 short-orbit estimate](../../phase22/short-orbits-and-irrationality.md) at lines 31–34 and 89–103.

The resulting per-run bound $18T(\tau/T)^\delta$ sums with weights $T/N$. The shifted real tail $O_P(N^{-1})$ costs $O_P(h/N)$ per harmonic, hence $O_P(N^{\xi-1})$ in Erdős–Turán. The logarithmic buffers cover the last incomplete run. The number of discarded shifts for $W,V$ is $O_P(\sqrt N+(\log N)^r(N^{1/3}+\log N))$, giving a power saving after division by $N$. For $W'$ the extra $O_P((\log N)^r w_K)$ is $o(N)$, enough for normality. This argument establishes the claimed scopes for every fixed nonconstant rational $P$. ## GF-34. Orders, bands, and quantitative estimates

**Anchor and exact order.** For $b=2,3,4,7$ the minimal pairs $(s,M)$ are $(2,3),(2,8),(1,3),(2,48)$. In the two odd cases the 2-adic valuations of $M$ are 3 and 4. More generally $4\mid b^s-1$ whenever the anchor is even. This gives $v_p(b^{su}-1)=v_p(M)+v_p(u)$ for every $p\mid M$, including even $u$ at $p=2$. Since $\operatorname{ord}_M b=s$, the simultaneous valuation inequalities for $M^k$ are exactly $sM^{k-1}\mid su$. At $k=2$, $q_2-1$ is a multiple of $sMq_1$. From $k=3$ on, $q_k-1$ is a multiple of $sM\prod_{j<k}q_j$. This uses the product of the indicated factors even if $s$ shares a prime with $M$. (D lines 422–439.)

Each $\widetilde d_k$ is coprime to $M$ by LTE, and two distinct repunits before division have gcd $b^{s\gcd(q_j,q_k)}-1=M$. Thus their quotients are coprime. The order $d_j$ on each quotient satisfies $\operatorname{lcm}(s,d_j)=s q_j$, so $q_j\mid d_j\mid s q_j$. In the overall lcm each pairwise coprime $q_j$ is present and coprime to $sM$, giving exactly $sM^{k-1}\prod_j q_j$. The numerator of $R_k$ is a power of $b$, hence the reduced denominator really is $D_k$. (D lines 440–451.)

**Shifted grids.** With $L_k=s\prod_{j\le k}q_j$, the step $b^{L_k}$ fixes the complementary CRT component. Its order modulo $M^k$ is $M^{k-1}$ by the same valuation test. Its powers therefore exhaust the $M^{k-1}$ residues $1+Mj\bmod M^k$. Multiplying by a unit at $M$ permutes the increments of this grid. Adding the fixed complementary phase gives the exact shifted uniform grid in D lines 450–457. Each interval count differs from length times grid size by at most one. Its Fourier sum is zero off multiples of the grid size. At $k=1$, the grid is a singleton and $T_1=sq_1$. At $k=2$ the grid has $M$ points, although the all-prefix cutoff $M^{K-2}$ for $K=2$ is just 1. The first nontrivial band estimate uses that $k=2$ grid at $K=3$.

**All-prefix Weyl bound.** For $B=b^{m_K}$, divide $B^{i+1}$ by $B-1$. The quotient is $S_i(B)$ and the remainder is 1. In the quotient term $B$ can be replaced modulo $D_{K-1}$ by $b^s$. LTE now gives exactly $M^{K-2}\mid hS_i(b^s)$ iff $d'\mid i+1$, where $d'=M^{K-2}/\gcd(h,M^{K-2})$. There are $\lfloor I/d'\rfloor$ surviving complete bands. For the partial band of relative length $\theta=r_0/m_K$, its extra weight is at most $\theta$ if $d'\mid I+1$. In that case $I=jd'-1$ and $(j-1+\theta)/(jd'-1+\theta)\le1/d'$. Otherwise the complete-band count alone is at most $(I+\theta)/d'$. The $s$ extra steps per complete band and the one incomplete period at the end cost at most $(s+T_{K-1})/m_K$. These estimates also cover $I=1$, $r_0=0$, and $d'=1$. (D lines 460–477.)

For clarity on the tails at transitions, put $c=c_b(h)$ from D line 471 and $R_\infty=y$. On each full or partial band the **unnormalized** phase error is at most $\sum_{t\ge1}\min(2,4\pi |h|R_\infty b^{-t})\le c$, taking $t=m_K-r$. For the real tail, $y-R_K\le4b^{-m_{K+1}}$ follows from $y\le2$ and the geometric bound on the omitted factors. Since $N<m_{K+1}$, the last exponent sampled is at least two places before $m_{K+1}$. The sum of its phase errors over the entire prefix is also at most $c$, using $8\pi |h|b^{-t}\le4\pi |h|R_\infty b^{-(t-1)}$ for $t\ge2$. Thus all tail errors together cost at most $(I+2)c/N\le3c/m_K$, including $N=m_{K+1}-1$. This recovers exactly the displayed Weyl bound. For fixed $h$ both terms tend to zero under the general $\omega_k\to\infty$ assumption. (D lines 469–484.)

**Balanced schedule and Erdős–Turán.** For $k\ge2$ the recurrence is $T_k=M^kT_{k-1}^2+MT_{k-1}$. Its log divided by $2^k$ has positive summable increments, giving $c_*(b,s,q_1)>0$ and $\ln m_k\sim c_*2^{k-1}$. Both sides of $m_K\le N<m_{K+1}$ matter. The upper endpoint yields $H=M^{K-2}\gg_{b,s,q_1}(\ln N)^{\log_2M}$. The lower endpoint yields $K=O_M(\ln\ln N)$ for large $N$, also directly from $T_k\ge T_{k-1}^2$ and $T_1\ge5$. At this cutoff the incomplete-period error is $O(1/H)$ per frequency. The gcd sum is bounded by $\tau(H)(1+\ln H)$ after grouping by the exact gcd, and $\tau(H)=\prod_{p\mid M}(1+(K-2)v_p(M))=O_M((\ln\ln N)^{\omega(M)})$. The frequency-dependent tail contributes $O_b((1+\ln H)^2/m_K)\ll1/H$. Combining with the $1/H$ cutoff cost gives the advertised log and log-log exponents, with the seed dependence in Major 1. For $\log_2M>A$, the strict inequality absorbs the entire log-log factor. (D lines 490–523.)

**Direct rate for $x$.** For $k\ge2$ the recurrence even gives $m_{k+1}=(M^2/s)m_k(m_k-s)+s$. Thus $S_k=\sum_{j\le k}m_j\ll_{b,s}\sqrt{m_{k+1}}$, since $m_k\ge5s$ and the balanced exponents grow at least geometrically. For $m_{k+1}\le N<m_{k+2}$, remove the initial $S_k$ places, at most $S_k$ more before $S_{k+1}$, and the two buffers of length $\Delta=\lceil\log_b N\rceil$. Elsewhere the relevant finite product times $b^n$ is integral and lies strictly above $b^nx$ by at most $2b^{-\Delta}$. The resulting interval count gives $1-D_N(x;b)\le(2S_k+2\Delta+2)/N\ll_{b,s}N^{-1/2}$. This handles $N$ inside either buffer. The analogous geometric-tail sum gives $W_1\le(S_k+4)/N$ for the general schedule. (D lines 396–406, 484–488, 502–506.)

Even the finitely many initial stages of this last **$x$** bound can be made uniform in the seed. For $N<m_3$, expand $X_2=1-b^{-m_1}-b^{-m_2}+b^{-m_1-m_2}$. Remove the $\Delta$ places preceding each of $m_1,m_2,m_1+m_2,m_3$. On all remaining shifts the three finite terms are integral or within $b^{-\Delta}$ of an integer, and $b^n|x-X_2|\le2b^{-\Delta}$. The central interval away from both endpoints by $6b^{-\Delta}$ then contains at most $4\Delta$ samples. This gives $1-D_N\ll_b(1+\log N)/N$ there, with the bounded small $N$ absorbed. The seed obstruction in Major 1 concerns $y$.
