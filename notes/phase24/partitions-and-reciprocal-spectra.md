# Phase 24 Track C: Convergent Reciprocal Discrepancy Spectra, Universal Period-Growth Barriers, Mahler Partition Constants, and Short-Orbit Bridges for $P_b = 1/E_b$

**Notation.** Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$. For $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and $N \ge 1$, let
$$\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(x; b) := \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \delta_{\{b^n x\}}, \qquad D_N(x; b) := \sup_{0 \le u < v \le 1} \left|\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(x; b)\big([u, v)\big) - (v - u)\right|$$
be the empirical orbit measure on $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and extreme interval discrepancy (on $[0, 1)$), with $D^-(x; b) := \liminf_{N\to\infty} D_N(x; b)$ and $D^+(x; b) := \limsup_{N\to\infty} D_N(x; b)$. Following the repository conventions (`docs/research-objectives.md`, `notes/phase23/convergent-reciprocals-and-schedules.md`, `notes/phase23/binary-partition-and-carry-complexity.md`, `GF-23`, `GF-32`, `GF-34`), we write
$$K_b := \left\{x \in \mathbb{R} : \lim_{N\to\infty} D_N(x; b) = 1\right\}, \qquad F_b := \frac{1}{b - 1}\mathbb{Z}, \qquad M_N(w; b) := \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \|b^n w\|_{\mathbb{T}}, \qquad M_b := \left\{w \in \mathbb{R} : M_N(w; b) \to 0\right\} \subset K_b,$$
with $\mu_N(x) := \min_{c \in F_b} M_N(x - c; b)$ and $K_b = F_b + M_b$. Write $e(t) := e^{2\pi i t}$, $\|t\|_{\mathbb{T}} := \min(\{t\}, 1 - \{t\})$, $W_h(N; x, b) := \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} e(h b^n x)$ for the $h$-th Weyl sum, $W_1(\mu, \nu)$ for the $1$-Wasserstein metric on $\mathbb{T}$, $U_D(\theta) := \frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=0}^{D-1} \delta_{\{\theta + j/D\}}$ for the shifted $D$-point uniform grid, and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b) := \{\alpha \in [0, 1] : \exists\, x \in K_b \setminus \mathbb{Q},\ \lim_{N\to\infty} D_N(1/x; b) = \alpha\}$ for the exact convergent reciprocal discrepancy set. For $m \ge 2$, $b_m(n)$ denotes the number of partitions of $n$ into powers of $m$ ($\beta := b_2$), $X_{b, m} := \prod_{j \ge 0}(1 - b^{-m^j})^{-1} = \sum_{n \ge 0} b_m(n) b^{-n}$ ($X_b := X_{b, 2}$), $p(n)$ denotes the ordinary partition function, $E_b := \prod_{j \ge 1}(1 - b^{-j})$, $P_b := 1/E_b = \sum_{n \ge 0} p(n) b^{-n}$, and $R_k^{(E)} := \prod_{j=1}^k (1 - b^{-j})^{-1}$.

## Main Results and Evidence Summary

This note executes **Phase 24 Track C (Sub-tracks C1–C4)**, advancing Objectives 1–4 of [`docs/research-objectives.md`](../../docs/research-objectives.md) across reciprocal spectra, period-growth barriers, Mahler's $m$-ary partition constants, and the short-orbit bridge for $P_b = 1/E_b$.

1. **Sub-track C1 — Convergent Reciprocal Discrepancy Spectrum $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$ and Structural Obstructions to Irrational and Non-Dyadic Attainment:**
   - **Theorem C1.1 (Dense convergent spectrum across all dyadic radices $b = 2^e$ and odd-$e$ $L$-normal spectrum).** For every integer $e \ge 1$ and odd $D \ge 1$, $2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \pmod D \iff \gcd(e, \operatorname{ord}_D(2)) = 1$, in which case $\mu(r; 2^e) = \mu(r; 2)$ identically. Consequently, for **every** power-of-$2$ radix $b = 2^e$ ($e \ge 1$), $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(2^e)$ (attained by irrational $x \in M_{2^e} \subset K_{2^e}$) is **dense in $[0, 1]$** (partially resolving Phase 23 Open Problem #2 across all dyadic radices $b = 2, 4, 8, 16, \dots$). Moreover, for every **odd** $e \ge 1$ ($b = 2, 8, 32, \dots$) and every $L \ge 1$, the set of exact convergent discrepancies attained by irrational $x \in M_{2^e} \subset K_{2^e}$ whose reciprocal $1/x$ is exact $L$-normal in base $2^e$ is **dense in $[0, 2 \cdot (2^e)^{-L}]$** (whereas even $e$ forces $\operatorname{ord}_D(2)$ odd, obstructing even-period $L$-normal cycles under $+1$-inversion).
   - **Lemma C1.2a & Proposition C1.2b (Exact triangular multiplicity law for $P_k(q)$ and leading-multiplier $D_0$-divisibility obstruction).** The band multipliers $S_i^{(k)}$ of Phase 22's cyclotomic polynomial $P_k(q) = \frac{1+q^{2k+1}}{1+q}\cdot\frac{1+q^{2k+3}}{1+q}$ obey the exact triangular multiplicity law $N_k(v) = 2(2k + 1 + v)$ ($-2k \le v \le 0$), $N_k(1) = 4k + 2$, $N_k(v) = 2(2k + 3 - v)$ ($2 \le v \le 2k + 2$), yielding the exact conditional divisibility lower bound $\mathbb{P}(p \mid S_i^{(k)} \mid S_i^{(k)} \neq 0) \ge \frac{1}{4 p}$ for every prime $p \le k$. Consequently, for every base $b \ge 2$, every reduced rational $R_0 = A_0 / D_0 \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ ($D_0 \ge 2$, $\gcd(b, D_0) = 1$), and every admissible unreset sequence of factors $P_{k_j}(b^{-m_j})$ with $k_j \to \infty$, on the all-nonzero band stratum $E_2^{(j)} \subset \{0, \dots, \widetilde{T}_j - 1\}$, the cumulative multiplier $S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)} = \prod_{v=1}^j S_{i_v}^{(k_v)}$ satisfies $\mathbb{P}(D_0 \mid S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)} \mid E_2^{(j)}) \to 1$. Hence the leading grid term $S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)} b^{t_0} R_0$ in the telescoped Phase 22 Lemma 5.2(2) expansion becomes an integer on a $(1 - o(1))$-fraction of $E_2^{(j)}$, obstructing any leading-multiplier-only transfer of the $R_0$-orbit without $-1$ resets. Because intermediate tail terms in the telescoped expansion are multiplied by unbounded products $\prod_{w > v} S_{i_w}^{(k_w)}$, this does **not** determine $\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(1/x; b)$ or prove $D_N(1/x; b) \to 1$; the true asymptotic behavior of unreset infinite $P_{k_j}$-products remains **Open**.
   - **Proposition C1.3 (Idealized prime-simplex invariance, Lucas mod-$p$ obstruction, and exhaustive $L \le 60$ zero-band search).** For any prime $p$, the $2$-dimensional simplex $\{(1 - \gamma) U_p(0) + \gamma \delta_0 : \gamma \in [0, 1]\}$ has exact discrepancy $D((1 - \gamma) U_p(0) + \gamma \delta_0) = \frac{1}{p} + \gamma(1 - \frac{1}{p})$ sweeping $[1/p, 1]$ (including every irrational $\alpha > 1/p$), and is strictly invariant under the *idealized* band-multiplier recursion $\mu \mapsto \frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1} (S_i)_*\mu$ of any cyclotomic factor $Q(b^{-m})^{-1}$ (transferring this recursion to actual infinite-product orbits is subject to the tail-amplification obstruction of §1.2), with mod-$p$ contraction density $\rho^{(p)}(Q) := \frac{1}{L}\#\{i < L : p \mid S_i\}$. By Lucas's theorem, every nontrivial divisor $Q \mid (1 - q^{p^r})/(1 - q)$ satisfies $\rho^{(p)}(Q) \ge 1/p$. Writing $\rho_0(Q) := \frac{1}{L}\#\{i < L : S_i = 0\}$ for the zero-band density, an exhaustive exact search over all $2 \le L \le 60$ with no exclusions (`937` nontrivial cyclotomic divisors with $\deg Q < L/3$) certifies that for $2 \le L \le 59$ every divisor with all $S_i \in \{0\} \cup \{\pm 2^a : a \ge 0\}$ (`487` divisors) satisfies $\rho_0(Q) \ge 1/3$ (attained by $\Phi_6$), whereas at $L = 60$ the $1/3$ bound breaks at the unique witness $Q = \Phi_3 \Phi_4 \Phi_{10}$ (degree $8$, all $S_i \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$, exact $\rho_0(Q) = 18/60 = 3/10 < 1/3$).
   - **Theorem C1.4 (Non-dyadic $+1$-inversion barrier via $\gcd(b^P - 1, 2^P - 1)$).** For every base $b \ge 2$ and reduced $r = A/D$ ($\gcd(b, D) = 1$, $P := \operatorname{ord}_D(b)$), $2 \in \langle b \rangle \pmod D$ forces $D \mid \gcd(b^P - 1, 2^P - 1)$. By Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier (2003, `External input`), for every base $b$ that is **not** a power of $2$, $\log_b D = o(P)$ as $P \to \infty$, so every zero-run of $r$ has length $o(P)$, structurally blocking the `0^z w_{dB, m}` $+1$-inversion route in every non-dyadic base.
   - **Open status:** Whether every irrational $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ belongs to $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$ (i.e. $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b) = [0, 1]$), the true asymptotic behavior of unreset infinite $P_{k_j}$-products, and whether $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$ is dense in $[0, 1]$ for non-dyadic $b \neq 2^e$ remain **Open**.

2. **Sub-track C2 — Universal Doubly-Exponential Period-Growth Barrier (Sharp at $\theta = 1$) for Slower Reciprocal Schedules:**
   - **Lemma C2.1 (Exact Shared-Divisor Order Absorption — zero constant loss).** For every integer $b \ge 2$ and every sequence $(m_k)_{k \ge 1}$ of positive integers, with $D_k := \prod_{j=1}^k (b^{m_j} - 1)$ and $T_k := \operatorname{ord}_{D_k}(b)$, $G_{k+1} := \gcd(D_k, b^{m_{k+1}} - 1) \mid T_{k+1}$ for all $k \ge 1$.
   - **Theorem C2.3 (Universal doubly-exponential barrier, sharp at $\theta = 1$, zero external inputs).** For every integer $b \ge 2$, every real $\theta > 0$, and **every** sequence $(m_k)_{k \ge 1}$ of positive integers with $b^{m_k} - 1 \ge 2$ for infinitely many $k$ (with **no** coprimality, anchoring, or divisibility hypotheses) satisfying $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ for all sufficiently large $k$, an elementary primitive-part bound on $g_k := \gcd(L_{k-1}, m_k)$ ($L_k := \operatorname{lcm}(m_1, \dots, m_k)$) and the Lyapunov functional $\Psi_k(\varepsilon) := \ln L_k + \varepsilon \ln T_k$ prove
     $$\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln \ln T_k}{k} \;\ge\; \ln(1 + \theta), \qquad \liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln \ln m_k}{k} \;\ge\; \ln(1 + \theta).$$
     At $\theta = 1$ ($m_k \ge T_{k-1}$), this yields $\liminf \frac{\ln \ln T_k}{k} \ge \ln 2$, proving that the doubly-exponential **base** $2$ in **Phase 23 `GF-34` Theorem 5's $\ln T_k \sim c_* 2^k$ is universally optimal** across all complete-period reciprocal schedules — dropping pairwise coprimality of $q_k$ permits finite single-step LTE-order recycling (meaning $G_{k+1} = 1$ yet a prime that entered $T_k$ via LTE at an earlier step divides $\gcd(T_k, m_{k+1})$, e.g. $b = 2, m = (2, 4, 15)$ where $G_3 = 1$, $3 \mid \gcd(T_2, m_3)$ from $3^2 \mid D_2$, and $T_3 = 60 = \frac{1}{3} T_2 m_3$), yet cannot reduce the asymptotic doubly-exponential base below $2$. In particular, **no singly exponential ($m_k \asymp C^k$) or sub-doubly-exponential schedule** can satisfy $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ for any $\theta > 0$: every such schedule obeys $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln m_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = 0$ (and the three classical examples $X_{b, m}$, $\prod_p (1 - 2^{-p})^{-1}$, and $P_b = 1/E_b$ satisfy the stronger full limit $\lim_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln m_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = 0$ by direct lower bounds on $\ln T_{k-1}$).

3. **Sub-track C3 — Mahler Partition Constants $X_2$ and $X_{b, m}$:**
   - **Theorem C3.1 (Full $m$-ary Cartier automaticity and 1-line CRA-type divisibility identity).** For every integer $m \ge 2$ and every $L \ge 1$, the $m$-ary Cartier operators $\mathcal{C}_0, \dots, \mathcal{C}_{m-1}$ on $F_k(z) = (1 - z)^{-k} \sum b_m(n) z^n$ satisfy $\mathcal{C}_r F_k = \sum_j u_{k+1, r, j} F_{k + 1 - j}$ with exact leading coefficient $u_{s, r, 0} = U_{s, r}(1) = m^{s - 1}$. Hence $(b_m(n) \bmod m^L)_{n \ge 0}$ is **$m$-automatic** (with $m$-kernel size $\le m^{L(L+1)}$), and for every prime $p \mid m$ ($v_p(m) = v$) and $e \ge 1$, $(b_m(n) \bmod p^e)_{n \ge 0}$ is $m$-automatic (with $m$-kernel size $\le p^{e(\lceil e/v \rceil + 1)}$). At $k = 1$, $\mathcal{C}_r F_1 = m F_2 - (m - 1 - r) F_1$ yields the elementary CRA-type divisibility identity $b_m(m^2 n + m^2 - m) = m \sum_{j=0}^n b_m(m j)$ for all $n \ge 0$.
   - **Theorem C3.2 (Exact functional-equation carry-gauge master congruence and binary carry-parity law).** For the canonical carry $\delta_n$ of $X_{b, m}$ ($b_m(n) = d_n + b \delta_n - \delta_{n+1}$): (i) $\delta_n = \lfloor \sum_{j \ge 0} b_m(n + j) b^{-1-j} \rfloor = \frac{b_m(n)}{b - 1}(1 + o(1))$; (ii) every $L$-digit block $W_L(k) := \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} d_{k+j} b^{L - 1 - j}$ satisfies the exact master congruence $W_L(k) \equiv \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} b_m(k + j) b^{L - 1 - j} + \delta_{k + L} \pmod{b^L}$; (iii) for $X_2$, the $k$-th binary digit equals the parity of the $(k+1)$-st carry identically: $d_k(X_2) = \delta_{k + 1} \bmod 2$ ($\forall k \ge 2$); (iv) the carry increments $\Delta_k := \delta_{k+1} - \delta_k$ satisfy $d_k - d_{k-1} = \Delta_k - b \Delta_{k-1}$ ($m \nmid k$) and $b_m(n) = (d_{mn} - d_{mn-1}) + b \Delta_{mn-1} - \Delta_{mn}$ ($k = mn$).
   - **Theorem C3.3 (Carry-Valuation Barrier, sharp under $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k)$).** For every integer $m \ge 2$, every real $z \in [0, 1)$ (including Champernowne's normal number $C_m$, any non-automatic normal number, $\Lambda_m \in M_m \subset K_m$, or any rational), and every $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/2)$, there exists an explicit integer sequence $(a(k))_{k \ge 0}$ with $a(k) \ge b_m(k) \ge 1$ ($2 \mid a(k)$ for $k \ge 2$ when $m = 2$), $a(k) = b_m(k)(1 + O_\varepsilon(m^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(\log_m k)^2}))$, $F_m(a) \equiv z \pmod 1$, and $v_m(a(k) - b_m(k)) \ge (\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon)(\log_m k)^2 - O_\varepsilon(\log k)$ — so $(a(k) \bmod m^L)$ is **$m$-automatic for every fixed $L \ge 1$**. Conversely, the exponent $\frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2$ is sharp under the growth normalization $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k)$. Thus $m$-automaticity of $(b_m(n) \bmod m^L)$ (even for growing $L(k) \le (\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon)(\log_m k)^2$) carries **zero** implication for automaticity, normality, or $K_m$-membership of $X_{m, m}$.

4. **Sub-track C4 — Short-Orbit Bridge for $P_b = 1/E_b = \prod_{j \ge 1}(1 - b^{-j})^{-1}$:**
   - **Theorem C4.1 (Exact shadowing length and partition-tail identity).** For all $k \ge 1$ and $0 \le m \le k$, $p(k + 1 + m) - p_{\le k}(k + 1 + m) = \sum_{j=0}^m p(j)$, and for all $b \ge 2$ and $k \ge 1$,
     $$\frac{R_k^{(E)}}{(b - 1) b^k} \;<\; P_b - R_k^{(E)} \;<\; \frac{P_b}{(b - 1) b^k},$$
     so $R_k^{(E)}$ shadows $\{b^n P_b\}$ to precision $b^{-\Delta}$ on $0 \le n < k - \Delta - \lceil \log_b(2 P_b/(b - 1)) \rceil$ (while real-line shadowing and digit certification break down at $n = k - \lfloor \log_b(P_b/(b - 1)) \rfloor + 1$, where $b^n(P_b - R_k^{(E)}) > 1$).
   - **Theorem C4.2 (Exact $p$-adic valuations and exact quadratic period asymptotic of $R_k^{(E)}$).** Using the elementary Ramanujan-sum bound $|\ln \Phi_d(b) - \varphi(d) \ln b| \le \frac{1}{b-1}$ and elementary LTE/Möbius primitive-order factorization $\Phi_d(b)/R(d) \mid d$ (so $\Phi_d(b)/d \le R(d) \le \Phi_d(b)$ for every $d \ge 2$ except $(d = 2,\, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4)$, where the excess is $2^{v_2(b+1)}$), we tighten Phase 23 `GF-34`'s $k \log^2 k \ll \ln T_k^{(E)} \ll k^2$ to the **exact universal quadratic asymptotic** (zero external inputs):
     $$\ln T_k^{(E)} \;=\; \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{\pi^2}\right)(\ln b)\, k^2 \;+\; O_b\bigl(k \ln^2 k\bigr) \qquad (\forall\, b \ge 2),$$
     so $\ln T_k^{(E)} / \ln D_k^{(E)} \to 1 - \frac{6}{\pi^2} \approx 0.392073$ universally across all radices, and for any $\Delta = \Delta_N = o(N)$, the shadowed period fraction is $N / T_{k_b(N, \Delta)}^{(E)} = \exp\bigl(-(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{\pi^2} + o(1))(\ln b) N^2\bigr)$.
   - **Theorem C4.3 (Pentagonal orbit coupling and partition-congruence valuation barrier).** From $E_b \cdot P_b = 1$, for every $K \ge 1$ and $n \in [\omega_K^+, \omega_{K+1}^-)$ (gap length $2K + 1$), $\|b^n P_b + \sum_{j=1}^K (-1)^j (b^{n - \omega_j^-} P_b + b^{n - \omega_j^+} P_b)\|_{\mathbb{T}} \le 2 P_b b^{n - \omega_{K+1}^-}$, and the same shifted-perturbation mechanism of Theorem C3.3 proves modular congruences $a(n) \equiv p(n) \pmod{b^{L(n)}}$ up to $L(n) \le (\frac{\pi\sqrt{2/3}}{\ln b} - \varepsilon)\sqrt{n}$ are compatible with every real $z \in [0, 1)$.
   - **Theorem C4.4 (Exact quantitative short-orbit Weyl-sum characterization of $D^+(P_b; b) < 1$).** Setting $k_b(N, \Delta) := N + \Delta + \lceil \log_b(2 P_b / (b - 1)) \rceil$:
     - For every $b \ge 2$, $\limsup_{N\to\infty} \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b) \le 1 - \eta$ ($\Delta_N \to \infty$) implies $D^+(P_b; b) \le 1 - \frac{2\eta}{5\pi^2(b - 1)^2} < 1$.
     - For $b = 2$, $D^+(P_2; 2) < 1 \iff \limsup_{N\to\infty} \operatorname{Re} W_1(N; R_{k_2(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, 2) < 1$ (with explicit two-sided bounds).
     - For **all** $b \ge 2$, $D^+(P_b; b) < 1 \iff \limsup_{N\to\infty} \min\bigl(|W_1(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b)|,\; \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b)\bigr) < 1$ (with explicit two-sided bounds, and exact base-$3$ counterexamples showing neither $|W_{b-1}|$ nor $\operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}$ alone suffices for $b \ge 3$).

## 1. Sub-track C1: Convergent Reciprocal Discrepancy Spectra and Obstructions

### 1.1 All-dyadic dense convergent spectrum and odd-$e$ $L$-normal spectrum (Theorem C1.1)

**Theorem C1.1 (All-dyadic dense $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(2^e)$ and odd-$e$ $L$-normal spectrum).** *Let $e \ge 1$ be an integer and $b := 2^e$.*
1. *(Exact $+1$-inversion criterion and orbit invariance.)* *For every odd integer $D \ge 1$,*
   $$2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \pmod D \iff \gcd\bigl(e,\; \operatorname{ord}_D(2)\bigr) = 1.$$
   *Whenever $\gcd(e, \operatorname{ord}_D(2)) = 1$, for every reduced rational $r = A/D$ with $A$ odd, we have $\operatorname{ord}_D(2^e) = \operatorname{ord}_D(2)$ and the exact measure equality $\mu(r; 2^e) = \mu(r; 2)$.*
2. *(Dense convergent spectrum for every power of $2$.)* *For **every** integer $e \ge 1$, the exact convergent reciprocal discrepancy set $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(2^e)$ (attained by irrational $x \in M_{2^e} \subset K_{2^e}$) is **dense in $[0, 1]$**.*
3. *(Odd-$e$ dense $L$-normal convergent spectrum and even-$e$ obstruction.)* *For every **odd** integer $e \ge 1$ and every $L \ge 1$, the set of exact convergent discrepancies $\lim_{N\to\infty} D_N(1/x; 2^e)$ attained by irrational $x \in M_{2^e} \subset K_{2^e}$ whose reciprocal $1/x$ is exact $L$-normal in base $2^e$ is **dense in $[0, 2 \cdot (2^e)^{-L}]$**. Conversely, when $e$ is **even**, $2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \pmod D$ forces $\operatorname{ord}_D(2)$ to be odd, so no periodic rational $r = A/D$ with even period (in particular no exact $L$-normal periodic rational in base $2^e$, which requires $2^{e L} \mid \operatorname{ord}_D(2^e)$) satisfies the $+1$-inversion hypothesis $2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \pmod D$.*

*Proof.*
(1) Write $t := \operatorname{ord}_D(2)$. In the cyclic group $\langle 2 \rangle \le (\mathbb{Z}/D\mathbb{Z})^\times$ of order $t$, the subgroup $\langle 2^e \rangle$ has order $t / \gcd(e, t)$, so $2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \iff \langle 2^e \rangle = \langle 2 \rangle \iff \gcd(e, t) = 1$. When $\gcd(e, t) = 1$, the map $j \mapsto e j \bmod t$ is a bijection on $\{0, \dots, t - 1\}$, so the orbit $\{2^{e j} r \bmod 1 : 0 \le j < t\}$ is an exact permutation of $\{2^j r \bmod 1 : 0 \le j < t\}$, giving $\mu(r; 2^e) = \mu(r; 2)$ identically.

(2) Fix $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. Take a cyclic binary de Bruijn word $w_{\mathrm{dB}, m}$ of order $m$ (length $2^m$, rotated to end in $1$). For any target zero count $z_0 \ge 0$, choose $z \in \{z_0, z_0 + 1, \dots, z_0 + e - 1\}$ such that $P := z + 2^m \equiv 1 \pmod e$ (so $\gcd(e, P) = 1$). Set $w := 0^z w_{\mathrm{dB}, m}$ and $r := v(w) / (2^P - 1) = A / D$ in lowest terms. Because $w$ ends in $1$, $A$ and $D$ are odd, so $\gcd(2^e, A D) = 1$. Since $D \mid (2^P - 1)$, we have $\operatorname{ord}_D(2) \mid P$, hence $\gcd(e, \operatorname{ord}_D(2)) = 1$. By part (1), $2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \pmod D$ and $\mu(r; 2^e) = \mu(r; 2)$. Applying Phase 23 Theorem 1 (`notes/phase23/convergent-reciprocals-and-schedules.md`, Theorem 1) in base $b = 2^e$ yields an irrational $x = \frac{1}{r}\prod_{j=1}^\infty (1 + (2^e)^{-m_j})^{-1} \in M_{2^e} \subset K_{2^e}$ with $\lim_{N\to\infty} D_N(1/x; 2^e) = D(\mu(r; 2^e)) = D(\mu(r; 2))$. Adjusting $z_0 \mapsto z$ changes $z / (z + 2^m)$ by at most $e / 2^m \to 0$, so choosing $m$ large and $z_0 / (z_0 + 2^m) \to \alpha$ yields values in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(2^e)$ within $\varepsilon$ of $\alpha$.

(3) Suppose $e$ is odd and $L \ge 1$. Put $M := e L$. By Phase 23 Theorem 2 & Theorem 3(a), for any large $R, m \ge M$ (chosen with $R \equiv 1 \pmod e$) and positive integers $c_{\mathrm{ext}}, c_{\mathrm{dB}}$ we can concatenate $c_{\mathrm{ext}}$ copies of $W_R^{\mathrm{exact}}$ (length $R \cdot 2^M$) and $c_{\mathrm{dB}}$ copies of $w_{\mathrm{dB}, m}$ (length $2^m$, both rotated to start with $0^{M-1}$ and end in $1$) to obtain an exact $M$-normal cyclic binary word $w$ of length $P = c_{\mathrm{ext}} R \cdot 2^M + c_{\mathrm{dB}} 2^m$ whose periodic discrepancy $D(\mu(r; 2))$ approximates any target in $[0, 2^{1 - M}] = [0, 2 \cdot (2^e)^{-L}]$. Since $e$ is odd and $R \equiv 1 \pmod e$, we have $\gcd(e, R \cdot 2^M) = 1$; adjusting $c_{\mathrm{ext}}$ by at most $e - 1$ ensures $P \equiv 1 \pmod e$ (hence $\gcd(e, P) = 1$) while changing the mixture ratio $c_{\mathrm{ext}} R \cdot 2^M / P$ by at most $(e - 1) R \cdot 2^M / P$, which tends to $0$ as $c_{\mathrm{ext}}, c_{\mathrm{dB}} \to \infty$ for fixed $R, m$. For $r = v(w)/(2^P - 1) = A/D$ reduced, $\operatorname{ord}_D(2) \mid P$ gives $\gcd(e, \operatorname{ord}_D(2)) = 1$, so by part (1), $2 \in \langle 2^e \rangle \pmod D$ and $\mu(r; 2^e) = \mu(r; 2)$. Because every base-$2^e$ length-$L$ cylinder $C_u^{(2^e)}$ is identically a base-$2$ length-$M$ cylinder $C_{\tilde{u}}^{(2)}$ of measure $2^{-M} = (2^e)^{-L}$, exact $M$-normality of $\mu(r; 2)$ implies exact $L$-normality of $\mu(r; 2^e)$. Phase 23 Theorem 1 in base $2^e$ transfers both the exact $L$-normality and the exact convergent discrepancy $D(\mu(r; 2))$ to $1/x$ ($x \in M_{2^e} \subset K_{2^e}$). Finally, if $e$ is even, then $\gcd(e, \operatorname{ord}_D(2)) = 1$ forces $\operatorname{ord}_D(2)$ odd; yet an exact $L$-normal periodic orbit of length $t = \operatorname{ord}_D(2^e) = \operatorname{ord}_D(2)$ in base $2^e$ must satisfy $(2^e)^L \mid t$, contradicting oddness of $t$. $\square$

### 1.2 Exact triangular multiplicity law for $P_k(q)$ and leading-multiplier divisibility obstruction (Lemma C1.2a & Proposition C1.2b)

To attempt realizing an arbitrary **irrational** $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$, a natural candidate built from Phase 22 `GF-26` Theorem 6 is to replace repeated $-1$ resets (which forced $D^- = 0$) by a single initial rational $R_0 = A_0 / D_0$ (e.g. $R_0 = (1 - b^{-n_1})^{-1}(1 - b^{-n_2})^{-1}$) followed by an infinite unreset sequence of cyclotomic factors $P_{k_j}(b^{-m_j})$ with $k_j \to \infty$ chosen so that $\prod_{j=1}^\infty (1 - \frac{1}{2 k_j + 3}) = 1 - \alpha$. We prove an exact triangular multiplicity law for the band multipliers $S_i^{(k)}$ of $P_k(q)$ (Lemma C1.2a) and deduce that the cumulative multiplier $S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}$ absorbs $D_0$ on a $(1 - o(1))$-fraction of the all-nonzero band stratum $E_2^{(j)}$ (Proposition C1.2b), annihilating the leading $R_0$-grid term in the telescoped Phase 22 expansion. However, as explained in the Remark below, unbounded amplification of intermediate tail terms prevents concluding $\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(1/x; b) \to \delta_0$, so the true asymptotic behavior of unreset infinite $P_{k_j}$-products remains **Open**.

**Lemma C1.2a (Exact triangular multiplicity law for $P_k(q)$).** *For every integer $k \ge 1$, let $P_k(q) := \frac{1 + q^{2k+1}}{1 + q}\cdot\frac{1 + q^{2k+3}}{1 + q}$, $\ell_k := 2(2k+1)(2k+3)$, and $A_k(q) := \frac{1 - q^{\ell_k}}{(1 - q) P_k(q)} = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell_k - 1} S_i^{(k)} q^i$. Put $N_k(v) := \#\{0 \le i < \ell_k : S_i^{(k)} = v\}$. Then $S_i^{(k)} \in [-2k, 2k + 2]$ for all $i$, and for every integer $v \in [-2k, 2k + 2]$,*
$$N_k(v) \;=\; \begin{cases} 2(2k + 1 + v) & \text{if } -2k \le v \le 0, \\ 4k + 2 & \text{if } v = 1, \\ 2(2k + 3 - v) & \text{if } 2 \le v \le 2k + 2. \end{cases}$$
*Consequently, for every prime $p \ge 2$ and every integer $k \ge p$, the nonzero band multipliers satisfy*
$$\mathbb{P}\bigl(p \mid S_i^{(k)} \;\big|\; S_i^{(k)} \neq 0\bigr) \;=\; \frac{\#\{0 \le i < \ell_k : S_i^{(k)} \neq 0,\; p \mid S_i^{(k)}\}}{4(2k + 1)(k + 1)} \;\ge\; \frac{1}{4 p}.$$

*Proof.* Set $p_1 := 2k + 1$, $r_1 := 2k + 3$. By Phase 22 `notes/phase22/normal-reciprocals-and-signs.md`, Proposition 5.1 (equations (5.1) and items 1–4), the multiset $\{S_i^{(k)} : 0 \le i < \ell_k\}$ consists of two copies of $\{t_n : 0 \le n < p_1^2\}$, plus $2 p_1 = 4k + 2$ copies of $+2$ (on $[p_1^2, p_1 r_1)$) and $2 p_1 = 4k + 2$ copies of $0$ (on $[\ell_k - 2 p_1, \ell_k)$), where $t_n$ obeys the palindromic reflection $t_{p_1^2 - 1 - n} = 2 - t_n$ and never equals $0$ or $2$. Put $M_k(v) := \#\{0 \le n < p_1^2 : t_n = v\}$, so $M_k(2 - v) = M_k(v)$, $N_k(0) = 4k + 2$, $N_k(2) = 4k + 2$, and $N_k(v) = 2 M_k(v)$ for $v \notin \{0, 2\}$.

Recall from Proposition 5.1 that $[0, p_1^2)$ splits into left blocks $m = 0, \dots, k - 1$ on $[0, k p_1)$, the symmetric center block $m = k$ on $[k p_1, k r_1]$ (length $p_1$), and mirror right blocks on $(k r_1, p_1^2)$:
- *Odd values $v = 2j + 1$ ($0 \le j \le k$):* Inside each left nonzero block $m \in \{0, \dots, k - 1\}$, $t_n = 2 s_{n-1} + (-1)^m$ visits every odd integer in $[-2m + 1, 2m + 3]$ once (even $m$) or $[-2m - 1, 2m + 1]$ once (odd $m$), and inside the center block $m = k$, $t_n$ visits every odd integer in $[-2k + 1, 2k + 1]$ once. For $j = 0$ ($v = 1$, fixed by $v \mapsto 2 - v$), $v = 1$ occurs once in every block $m = 0, \dots, k - 1$ (and once in its mirror) plus once in the center block, giving $M_k(1) = 2k + 1$ and $N_k(1) = 4k + 2$. For $1 \le j \le k$, by reflection $t_{p_1^2 - 1 - n} = 2 - t_n$, occurrences of $+v = 2j + 1$ in $[0, k p_1) \cup (k r_1, p_1^2)$ equal the combined occurrences of $+v$ and $2 - v = -(2j - 1)$ in $[0, k p_1)$: both occur once in every block $m \in \{j, \dots, k - 1\}$ (contributing $2(k - j)$) and exactly one of them occurs in block $m = j - 1$ (contributing $+1$), while $+v$ occurs once in the center block $m = k$. Hence $M_k(v) = 2(k - j) + 2 = 2k + 3 - v$ and $N_k(v) = 2(2k + 3 - v)$.
- *Even values $v = 2j + 2$ ($1 \le j \le k$):* Even values occur solely in the zero gaps $[m r_1 + 2, (m + 1) p_1 - 1]$ ($0 \le m \le k - 1$), where gap $m$ has length $(m + 1)(2k + 1) - m(2k + 3) - 2 = 2k - 1 - 2m$ and constant value $t_n = 2 S_{\mathrm{after}}(m) \in \{2m + 4 \text{ (even } m),\, -(2m + 2) \text{ (odd } m)\}$. For each $1 \le j \le k$, exactly one of $+v = 2j + 2$ and $2 - v = -2j$ occurs in $[0, k p_1)$, namely on gap $m = j - 1$, of length $2k - 1 - 2(j - 1) = 2k + 3 - v$. Hence $M_k(v) = 2k + 3 - v$ and $N_k(v) = 2(2k + 3 - v)$.
- *Negative values $-2k \le v \le -1$:* By $M_k(v) = M_k(2 - v)$ and $2 - v \in [3, 2k + 2]$, $N_k(v) = 2 M_k(2 - v) = 2(2k + 3 - (2 - v)) = 2(2k + 1 + v)$.

For the mod-$p$ lower bound when $k \ge p \ge 2$, set $M := \lfloor 2k / p \rfloor$. Since $2k / p \ge 2$ and $\lfloor x \rfloor \ge x / 2$ for all $x \ge 2$, we have $M \ge k / p$. Using $p M \le 2k$ and $p \le k$, summing $N_k(m p) + N_k(-m p) = 4(2k + 2 - m p)$ over $1 \le m \le M$ gives
$$\#\bigl\{i < \ell_k : S_i^{(k)} \neq 0,\; p \mid S_i^{(k)}\bigr\} \;\ge\; 4 \sum_{m=1}^{M} (2k + 2 - m p) \;=\; 2 M(4k + 4 - p M - p) \;\ge\; 2 \cdot \frac{k}{p} \cdot (k + 4).$$
Since the number of nonzero bands is $\ell_k - (4k + 2) = 4(2k + 1)(k + 1) = 8k^2 + 12k + 4$ and $8k^2 + 32k > 8k^2 + 12k + 4$ for every $k \ge 1$, dividing yields $\mathbb{P}(p \mid S_i^{(k)} \mid S_i^{(k)} \neq 0) \ge \frac{2k(k + 4)}{p(8k^2 + 12k + 4)} > \frac{1}{4 p}$. $\square$

**Proposition C1.2b (Leading-multiplier $D_0$-divisibility obstruction for unreset $P_{k_j}$-products).** *Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$, any reduced rational $R_0 = A_0 / D_0 \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ with $D_0 \ge 2$ and $\gcd(b, D_0) = 1$, and any sequence $(k_j)_{j \ge 1}$ of positive integers with $k_j \to \infty$. Let $(m_j)_{j \ge 1}$ be any admissible exponent sequence satisfying $\operatorname{ord}_{D_0}(b) \mid m_1$ and $\ell_{k_{j-1}} m_{j-1} \mid m_j$ ($j \ge 2$), and put $R_j := R_0 \prod_{v=1}^j P_{k_v}(b^{-m_v})^{-1}$ and $\widetilde{T}_j := \ell_{k_j} m_j$ (a multiple of $\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{den}(R_j)}(b)$ by Phase 22 Lemma 5.2(1)). For a uniformly random $t \in \{0, \dots, \widetilde{T}_j - 1\}$, let $i_v(t) \in \{0, \dots, \ell_{k_v} - 1\}$ ($1 \le v \le j$) denote its mixed-radix band coordinates, $E_2^{(j)} := \{t : S_{i_v}^{(k_v)} \neq 0\ \forall 1 \le v \le j\}$ the all-nonzero band stratum, and $S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}(t) := \prod_{v=1}^j S_{i_v}^{(k_v)}$. Then*
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(D_0 \mid S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)} \;\Big|\; E_2^{(j)}\right) \;\longrightarrow\; 1 \qquad (j \to \infty).$$
*Consequently, in the telescoped Phase 22 Lemma 5.2(2) expansion on $E_2^{(j)}$,*
$$b^t R_j \;\equiv\; S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}(t)\, b^{t_0} R_0 \;+\; \sum_{v=1}^{j} \left(\prod_{w=v+1}^{j} S_{i_w}^{(k_w)}\right) b^{r_v} R_{v-1} \tau_{i_v}^{(k_v)}(b^{-m_v}) \pmod 1,$$
*the leading grid term $S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}(t)\, b^{t_0} R_0$ is an integer ($\equiv 0 \pmod 1$) on a $(1 - o(1))$-fraction of $E_2^{(j)}$.*

*Proof.* Because $\operatorname{ord}_{D_0}(b) \mid m_1$ and $\widetilde{T}_{v-1} = \ell_{k_{v-1}} m_{v-1} \mid m_v$ ($2 \le v \le j$), the mixed-radix band coordinates $i_v(t) \in \{0, \dots, \ell_{k_v} - 1\}$ of a uniformly random $t \in \{0, \dots, \widetilde{T}_j - 1\}$ are mutually independent and uniform. Conditioning on the product event $E_2^{(j)}$ preserves mutual independence of $i_1(t), \dots, i_j(t)$, with each $i_v(t)$ uniform on the nonzero bands $\{i < \ell_{k_v} : S_i^{(k_v)} \neq 0\}$. For each prime power $p^{e_p} \parallel D_0$, since $k_v \to \infty$ there exists $v_p$ such that $k_v \ge p$ for all $v \ge v_p$. Conditioned on $E_2^{(j)}$, the events $A_v := \{p \mid S_{i_v}^{(k_v)}\}$ ($v_p \le v \le j$) are mutually independent, and Lemma C1.2a gives $\mathbb{P}(A_v \mid E_2^{(j)}) \ge \frac{1}{4 p}$. Because $v_p(S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}) \ge \sum_{v = v_p}^j \mathbf{1}_{A_v}$, the probability that fewer than $e_p$ of the events $A_v$ occur satisfies
$$\mathbb{P}\!\left(p^{e_p} \nmid S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)} \;\Big|\; E_2^{(j)}\right) \;\le\; \sum_{r=0}^{e_p - 1} \binom{j - v_p + 1}{r} \left(1 - \frac{1}{4 p}\right)^{j - v_p + 1 - r} \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (j \to \infty).$$
Summing over the finitely many primes $p \mid D_0$ yields $\mathbb{P}(D_0 \mid S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)} \mid E_2^{(j)}) \to 1$. Whenever $D_0 \mid S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}(t)$, the leading term $S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}(t)\, b^{t_0} R_0 = \frac{S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(j)}(t)}{D_0} A_0 b^{t_0}$ is an integer. $\square$

*Remark (Why Proposition C1.2b does not determine $\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(1/x; b)$; `Open` status of unreset infinite $P_{k_j}$-products).* Proposition C1.2b is a genuine obstruction to bounding $W_1$ or transferring the $R_0$-orbit via the leading-multiplier-only model without $-1$ resets. However, it does **not** establish that $\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(1/x; b) \xrightarrow{W_1} \delta_0$ or $\lim_{N\to\infty} D_N(1/x; b) = 1$: in the telescoped expansion above, each intermediate tail term $b^{r_v} R_{v-1} \tau_{i_v}^{(k_v)}(b^{-m_v})$ (of typical size $\asymp b^{-(m_v - r_v)}$) is multiplied by the unbounded product $\prod_{w=v+1}^j S_{i_w}^{(k_w)}$, whose logarithmic magnitude grows like $\sum_{w=v+1}^j \ln k_w \to \infty$ for every fixed $v$. (Phase 22 Theorem 6 explicitly capped this amplification by using bounded rounds and $-1$ resets, and ensuring $x = R_0^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^\infty P_{k_j}(b^{-m_j}) \in M_b \subset K_b$ likewise requires Phase 22's stronger growth condition on $m_j$.) Indeed, grouping the $v = 1$ tail with the leading term gives $(\prod_{w \ge 2} S_{i_w}^{(k_w)})\{b^{t_1} R_1\}$ modulo $1$, where $\operatorname{den}(R_1) = D_0 P_{k_1}(b^{m_1})$ contains primitive prime divisors $q$ that are not absorbed by $|S_{i_w}^{(k_w)}| \le 2 k_w + 2$ until $k_w \gtrsim q / 2$. Accordingly, the actual asymptotic orbit measure and discrepancy of unreset infinite $P_{k_j}$-products remain **Open**.

### 1.3 Idealized prime-simplex invariance, Lucas mod-$p$ obstruction, and non-dyadic $+1$-inversion barrier

**Proposition C1.3 (Idealized prime-simplex invariance, Lucas mod-$p$ obstruction, and exhaustive $L \le 60$ zero-band search).**
1. *(Exact prime-simplex discrepancy and idealized band-recursion invariance.)* *For every integer $p \ge 2$ and every real $\gamma \in [0, 1]$, the probability measure $\nu_\gamma := (1 - \gamma) U_p(0) + \gamma \delta_0$ on $[0, 1)$ has exact extreme interval discrepancy*
   $$D(\nu_\gamma) \;=\; \frac{1}{p} + \gamma\left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right),$$
   *sweeping continuously over $[1/p, 1]$. Moreover, when $p$ is **prime**, for every integer $S \in \mathbb{Z}$, $S_* U_p(0) = U_p(0)$ if $p \nmid S$ and $S_* U_p(0) = \delta_0$ if $p \mid S$; hence the simplex $\{\nu_\gamma : \gamma \in [0, 1]\}$ is strictly invariant under the **idealized** band-multiplier recursion $\mu \mapsto \frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1} (S_i)_*\mu$ associated with any cyclotomic divisor $Q \mid \frac{1 - q^L}{1 - q}$, with effective contraction $\gamma \mapsto 1 - (1 - \gamma)(1 - \rho^{(p)}(Q))$ where $\rho^{(p)}(Q) := \frac{1}{L}\#\{0 \le i < L : p \mid S_i\}$. (Transferring this idealized recursion to actual orbits of infinite products faces the same unbounded tail-amplification issue noted in §1.2.)*
2. *(Lucas mod-$p$ lower bound at $L = p^r$.)* *For every prime $p$, every integer $r \ge 1$, and every nontrivial divisor $Q(q) \in \mathbb{Z}[q]$ of $\frac{1 - q^{p^r}}{1 - q}$ with $1 \le \deg Q \le p^r - 1$, we have $\rho^{(p)}(Q) \ge \frac{1}{p}$.*
3. *(Exhaustive $2 \le L \le 60$ zero-band density certificate for $\{0, \pm 2^a\}$ band multipliers.)* *Denote the zero-band density of $Q \mid \frac{1 - q^L}{1 - q}$ by $\rho_0(Q) := \frac{1}{L}\#\{0 \le i < L : S_i = 0\}$ (distinct from $\rho^{(p)}(Q)$: for example, $\Phi_6(q) = 1 - q + q^2$ at $L = 6$ has $S = (1, 2, 2, 1, 0, 0)$, giving $\rho_0(\Phi_6) = 1/3$ while $\rho^{(2)}(\Phi_6) = 2/3$). Every divisor with $\deg Q \ge L/3$ trivially satisfies $\rho_0(Q) \ge \deg Q / L \ge 1/3$. Exhaustively across all $2 \le L \le 60$ with no divisor-count exclusions (`937` nontrivial cyclotomic divisors with $\deg Q < L/3$):*
   - *For all $2 \le L \le 59$ (`514` divisors with $\deg Q < L/3$, of which `487` have all band multipliers $S_i \in \{0\} \cup \{\pm 2^a : a \ge 0\}$), every divisor with $S_i \in \{0\} \cup \{\pm 2^a : a \ge 0\}$ satisfies $\rho_0(Q) \ge 1/3$.*
   - *At $L = 60$ (`423` divisors with $\deg Q < 20$, of which `297` have all $S_i \in \{0\} \cup \{\pm 2^a : a \ge 0\}$), the $1/3$ threshold breaks at a **unique** witness: $Q(q) = \Phi_3(q)\Phi_4(q)\Phi_{10}(q)$ (degree $8$), which has all band multipliers $S_i \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\} \subset \{0\} \cup \{\pm 2^a : a \ge 0\}$ and exact zero-band density $\rho_0(Q) = 18/60 = 3/10 < 1/3$ (and hence $\rho^{(p)}(Q) = \rho_0(Q) = 3/10 < 1/3$ for every odd prime $p$).*

*Proof.*
(1) Write $F_{\nu_\gamma}(t) := \nu_\gamma([0, t))$ and $g(t) := F_{\nu_\gamma}(t) - t$ on $[0, 1]$. On each open interval $(\frac{j}{p}, \frac{j+1}{p})$ ($0 \le j < p$), $g(t)$ decreases linearly with slope $-1$, attaining its left limits $g(\frac{j}{p}^-) = \gamma(1 - \frac{j}{p})$ ($1 \le j \le p$) and right limits $g(\frac{j}{p}^+) = \gamma(1 - \frac{j}{p}) + \frac{1 - \gamma}{p}$ ($0 \le j \le p - 1$), with $g(0) = 0$. Therefore $\sup_{t \in [0, 1]} g(t) = g(0^+) = \gamma + \frac{1 - \gamma}{p}$ and $\inf_{t \in [0, 1]} g(t) = g(1^-) = 0$, giving $D(\nu_\gamma) = \sup g - \inf g = \frac{1}{p} + \gamma(1 - \frac{1}{p})$. For prime $p$, multiplication by $S$ permutes $\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}$ if $p \nmid S$ and maps it identically to $0$ if $p \mid S$.

(2) In $\mathbb{F}_p[q]$, $\frac{1 - q^{p^r}}{1 - q} \equiv (1 - q)^{p^r - 1} \pmod p$. By unique factorization in $\mathbb{F}_p[q]$, any divisor $Q \mid \frac{1 - q^{p^r}}{1 - q}$ of degree $d \in [1, p^r - 1]$ satisfies $Q(q) \equiv c(1 - q)^d \pmod p$ ($c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$), so $A(q) = \frac{1 - q^{p^r}}{(1 - q) Q(q)} \equiv c^{-1} (1 - q)^N \pmod p$ where $N := p^r - 1 - d \in [0, p^r - 2]$. Since $N < p^r - 1$, at least one base-$p$ digit $n_s$ of $N$ satisfies $n_s \le p - 2$. By Lucas's theorem, $\binom{N}{i} \equiv 0 \pmod p$ whenever the $s$-th base-$p$ digit $i_s$ of $i \in \{0, \dots, p^r - 1\}$ equals $p - 1$. Exactly $p^{r - 1}$ values of $i \in \{0, \dots, p^r - 1\}$ have $i_s = p - 1$, giving $\rho^{(p)}(Q) \ge p^{r - 1} / p^r = 1/p$.

(3) Verified exhaustively by exact integer polynomial division over all $2 \le L \le 60$ with no exclusions in `scripts/phase24_partitions_reciprocals.py` §C1g (`Finite certificate`). $\square$

**Theorem C1.4 (Non-dyadic $+1$-inversion barrier via $\gcd(b^P - 1, 2^P - 1)$).** *Let $b \ge 2$ be an integer radix and let $r = A/D \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ be reduced with $\gcd(b, D) = 1$ and $P := \operatorname{ord}_D(b)$.*
1. *(Exact divisibility.)* *If $2 \in \langle b \rangle \pmod D$, then $2^P \equiv 1 \pmod D$ and hence*
   $$D \;\Big|\; \gcd\bigl(b^P - 1,\; 2^P - 1\bigr).$$
2. *(Non-dyadic zero-run barrier; uses Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier 2003 as `External input`.)* *If $b$ is **not** a power of $2$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $P_0(b, \varepsilon)$ such that every reduced $r = A/D$ satisfying $2 \in \langle b \rangle \pmod D$ and $P = \operatorname{ord}_D(b) \ge P_0(b, \varepsilon)$ obeys $D \le b^{\varepsilon P}$. Consequently, every run of consecutive base-$b$ zeros (or any constant-digit run) in the periodic expansion of $r$ has length at most $\varepsilon P$; in particular, the `0^z w_{dB, m}` $+1$-inversion route of Theorem C1.1 is structurally blocked in every non-dyadic base $b \neq 2^e$.*

*Proof.*
(1) If $2 \equiv b^L \pmod D$ for some $L \ge 1$, then $2^P \equiv (b^P)^L \equiv 1^L = 1 \pmod D$. Since $b^P \equiv 1 \pmod D$ by definition of $P$, $D$ divides $\gcd(b^P - 1, 2^P - 1)$.

(2) Suppose $b$ is not a power of $2$; then $b$ and $2$ are multiplicatively independent integers $\ge 2$. By Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier (*Math. Z.* 243 (2003), Theorem 1, `External input`), for every $\varepsilon > 0$, $\gcd(b^P - 1, 2^P - 1) \le \exp((\varepsilon \ln b) P) = b^{\varepsilon P}$ for all $P \ge P_0(b, \varepsilon)$. Hence $D \le b^{\varepsilon P}$. If the base-$b$ expansion of $r$ contains a run of $z$ consecutive zeros starting at position $n_0$, then $1 / D \le \{b^{n_0} r\} < b^{-z}$, so $b^z < D \le b^{\varepsilon P}$ and $z < \varepsilon P$. (Similarly, a run of $z$ consecutive digits $d \in \{1, \dots, b - 1\}$ forces $|\{b^{n_0} r\} - d / (b - 1)| < b^{-z}$ while $|\{b^{n_0} r\} - d/(b-1)| \ge 1 / ((b - 1) D)$ unless $r = d / (b - 1)$ has $P = 1$, giving $z < \varepsilon P + 1$.) $\square$

## 2. Sub-track C2: Universal Doubly-Exponential Period-Growth Barrier (Sharp at $\theta = 1$) for Slower Reciprocal Schedules

Phase 23 `GF-34` Theorem 5 broke tetration growth down to doubly exponential growth $\ln T_k \sim c_* 2^k$ via anchored coprime schedules $m_k = s q_k = \omega_{k-1} T_{k-1} + s$ with $\gcd(q_j, q_k) = 1$. We now investigate whether **dropping pairwise coprimality**, anchoring, or divisibility can achieve singly exponential ($m_k \asymp C^k$) or sub-doubly-exponential growth — and prove a **universal doubly-exponential barrier** showing that $\ln T_k \ge c_\varepsilon ((1 + \theta)/(1 + \varepsilon))^k$ for every $\varepsilon \in (0, \theta)$, i.e., $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln \ln T_k}{k} \ge \ln(1 + \theta)$ (sharp at $\theta = 1$, where the doubly-exponential base $2$ matches Phase 23 `GF-34` Theorem 5; sharpness for $\theta \neq 1$ is not established), is forced across **all** nondegenerate schedules ($b^{m_k} - 1 \ge 2$ infinitely often, ruling out the degenerate $b = 2$, $m_k \equiv 1$ case where $D_k = T_k = 1$) obeying $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ ($\theta > 0$) with **zero** other structural hypotheses and **zero** external inputs.

### 2.1 Exact Shared-Divisor Order Absorption and Elementary Primitive-Part Bound

**Lemma C2.1 (Exact Shared-Divisor Order Absorption — zero constant loss).** *Let $b \ge 2$ be any integer radix, and let $(m_k)_{k \ge 1}$ be **any** sequence of positive integers. Put $D_k := \prod_{j=1}^k (b^{m_j} - 1)$ and $T_k := \operatorname{ord}_{D_k}(b)$ (with the convention $\operatorname{ord}_1(b) = 1$ and $T_0 := 1$). Then for **every** $k \ge 1$,*
$$G_{k+1} := \gcd\bigl(D_k,\; b^{m_{k+1}} - 1\bigr) \;\Big|\; T_{k+1}.$$
*In particular, for every $1 \le j \le k$, setting $h_{j, k+1} := \gcd(m_j, m_{k+1})$, we have $(b^{h_{j, k+1}} - 1) \mid G_{k+1} \mid T_{k+1}$.*

*Proof.* Let $p$ be any prime dividing $G_{k+1}$, and put $e_p := v_p(D_k) \ge 1$ and $f_p := v_p(b^{m_{k+1}} - 1) \ge 1$, so $v_p(G_{k+1}) = \min(e_p, f_p)$ and $v_p(D_{k+1}) = e_p + f_p$. Since $p^{e_p + f_p} \mid D_{k+1}$, we have $v_p(T_{k+1}) \ge v_p(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e_p + f_p}}(b))$. We check all prime cases by elementary LTE (Phase 22 Lemma 1.1):
1. *$p$ odd:* Put $a_p := v_p(b^{\operatorname{ord}_p(b)} - 1) \ge 1$. Since $p$ divides some factor $b^{m_j} - 1$ ($1 \le j \le k$), we have $e_p \ge v_p(b^{m_j} - 1) \ge a_p$, and likewise $f_p \ge a_p$. By LTE, $\operatorname{ord}_{p^{e_p + f_p}}(b) = \operatorname{ord}_p(b) \cdot p^{e_p + f_p - a_p}$, so
   $$v_p(T_{k+1}) \;\ge\; e_p + f_p - a_p \;=\; (e_p - a_p) + f_p \;\ge\; f_p \;\ge\; \min(e_p, f_p) \;=\; v_p(G_{k+1}).$$
2. *$p = 2$ and $b \equiv 1 \pmod 4$:* Here $a_2 := v_2(b - 1) \ge 2$, $4 \mid (b - 1)$, $e_2 \ge a_2$, $f_2 \ge a_2$, and $2$-adic LTE gives $v_2(T_{k+1}) \ge e_2 + f_2 - a_2 \ge f_2 \ge v_2(G_{k+1})$.
3. *$p = 2$ and $b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$:* Put $a_2 := v_2(b^2 - 1) \ge 3$. For any $n \ge 1$, $v_2(b^n - 1) = 1$ if $n$ is odd and $v_2(b^n - 1) = a_2 + v_2(n) - 1 \ge a_2$ if $n$ is even; moreover, $\operatorname{ord}_{2^E}(b) = 2$ for $2 \le E \le a_2$ and $v_2(\operatorname{ord}_{2^E}(b)) = 1 + E - a_2$ for $E \ge a_2$.
   - If $v_2(G_{k+1}) = 1$, then $e_2 + f_2 \ge 2$, so $4 \mid D_{k+1}$ and $2 = \operatorname{ord}_4(b) \mid T_{k+1}$, giving $v_2(T_{k+1}) \ge 1 = v_2(G_{k+1})$.
   - If $v_2(G_{k+1}) \ge 2$, then $e_2 \ge 2$ and $f_2 \ge 2$, so $m_{k+1}$ is even and hence $f_2 \ge a_2$. Thus $e_2 + f_2 \ge a_2 + 2$, giving $v_2(T_{k+1}) \ge 1 + e_2 + f_2 - a_2 \ge e_2 + 1 > \min(e_2, f_2) = v_2(G_{k+1})$.

Multiplying over all primes $p \mid G_{k+1}$ yields $G_{k+1} \mid T_{k+1}$. The final assertion follows immediately from $\gcd(b^{m_j} - 1, b^{m_{k+1}} - 1) = b^{h_{j, k+1}} - 1 \mid G_{k+1}$. $\square$

**Lemma C2.2 (Elementary primitive-part bound on $\gcd(L_{k-1}, m_k)$ — zero external inputs).** *Under the notation of Lemma C2.1, put $L_k := \operatorname{lcm}(m_1, \dots, m_k)$ and $g_k := \gcd(L_{k-1}, m_k)$ ($k \ge 2$). Then $L_k \mid T_k$ and*
$$\ln g_k \;\le\; \ln 60 + \sqrt{30 \ln T_k}\,\ln\bigl(15 \ln T_k + 2\bigr).$$

*Proof.* Since $(b^{m_j} - 1) \mid D_k$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{b^{m_j} - 1}(b) = m_j$ for all $1 \le j \le k$, we have $L_k \mid T_k$. Let $p^r \parallel g_k$ be any prime power dividing $g_k$ with $p^r \ge 7$. Because $g_k \mid L_{k-1} = \operatorname{lcm}(m_1, \dots, m_{k-1})$, there exists $1 \le j \le k - 1$ with $p^r \mid m_j$, hence $p^r \mid \gcd(m_j, m_k)$ and therefore $(b^{p^r} - 1) \mid G_k \mid T_k$ by Lemma C2.1.

Consider $\Phi_{p^r}(b) = \frac{b^{p^r} - 1}{b^{p^{r-1}} - 1} \mid (b^{p^r} - 1)$. We verify two completely elementary facts (without Bang–Zsigmondy):
1. *Strict exponential lower bound:* Since $b^{p^r - p^{r-1}} \ge 2 > 1$, we have $b^{p^r} - 1 > b^{p^r - p^{r-1}}(b^{p^{r-1}} - 1)$, whence
   $$\Phi_{p^r}(b) \;>\; b^{p^r - p^{r-1}} \;=\; b^{p^r (1 - 1/p)} \;\ge\; b^{p^r / 2}.$$
2. *Primitive factorization:* Every prime $q \mid \Phi_{p^r}(b)$ satisfies $q \mid (b^{p^r} - 1)$, so $\operatorname{ord}_q(b) \mid p^r$. If $\operatorname{ord}_q(b) < p^r$, then $\operatorname{ord}_q(b) \mid p^{r-1}$, so $q \mid (b^{p^{r-1}} - 1)$ and $q \mid \frac{(b^{p^{r-1}})^p - 1}{b^{p^{r-1}} - 1} \equiv p \pmod q$, forcing $q = p$. Moreover, since $p^r \ge 7$, we always have $v_p(\Phi_{p^r}(b)) \le 1$: if $p \nmid \Phi_{p^r}(b)$ the valuation is $0$, while if $p \mid \Phi_{p^r}(b)$ then $p \mid (b^{p^{r-1}} - 1)$ (by Fermat's little theorem for odd $p$, or because $b$ is odd for $p = 2$), so LTE gives $v_p(\Phi_{p^r}(b)) = v_p(p) = 1$ for odd $p$ and $2$-adic LTE (with $r \ge 3 \implies p^{r-1} \ge 4$ even, hence $4 \mid b^{2^{r-1}} - 1$) gives $v_2(\Phi_{2^r}(b)) = 1$ for $p = 2$. Therefore the primitive part $\Pi_b(p^r) := \Phi_{p^r}(b) / p^{v_p(\Phi_{p^r}(b))}$ consists solely of primes $q$ with $\operatorname{ord}_q(b) = p^r$, and satisfies
   $$\Pi_b(p^r) \;\ge\; \frac{\Phi_{p^r}(b)}{p} \;>\; \frac{b^{p^r / 2}}{p^r} \;\ge\; \frac{2^{p^r / 2}}{p^r}.$$

For $x \ge 7$, the function $f(x) := (\frac{\ln 2}{2} - \frac{1}{15}) x - \ln x$ has derivative $f'(x) = \frac{\ln 2}{2} - \frac{1}{15} - \frac{1}{x} \ge 0.137 > 0$ and $f(7) > 0.013 > 0$, so $\ln \Pi_b(p^r) > p^r / 15$ for all $p^r \ge 7$. Because the condition $\operatorname{ord}_q(b) = p^r$ uniquely recovers $p^r$, the integers $\Pi_b(p^r)$ for distinct prime powers $p^r \parallel g_k$ ($p^r \ge 7$) are **pairwise coprime**, and all divide $G_k \mid T_k$. Thus
$$T_k \;\ge\; G_k \;\ge\; \prod_{\substack{p^r \parallel g_k \\ p^r \ge 7}} \Pi_b(p^r) \implies \sum_{\substack{p^r \parallel g_k \\ p^r \ge 7}} p^r \;\le\; 15 \ln T_k =: S.$$
Let $t$ be the number of prime powers $p^r \parallel g_k$ with $p^r \ge 7$. Since they are distinct positive integers summing to at most $S$, we have $t(t + 1)/2 \le S$, so $t \le \sqrt{2 S}$. If $t \ge 1$, AM–GM yields $\sum_{p^r \ge 7} \ln(p^r) \le t \ln(S / t) \le \sqrt{2 S} \ln(S + 2)$ (also trivially valid for $t = 0$). Finally, the product of all prime powers $p^r \parallel g_k$ with $p^r \le 6$ divides $\operatorname{lcm}(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) = 60$. Combining both parts gives $\ln g_k \le \ln 60 + \sqrt{30 \ln T_k}\,\ln(15 \ln T_k + 2)$. $\square$

### 2.2 Proof of the Universal Doubly-Exponential Barrier (Theorem C2.3)

**Theorem C2.3 (Universal doubly-exponential period-growth barrier, sharp at $\theta = 1$).** *Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$ and a real constant $\theta > 0$. Let $(m_k)_{k \ge 1}$ be **any** sequence of positive integers (with no divisibility, coprimality, or anchoring assumptions) such that $b^{m_k} - 1 \ge 2$ for infinitely many $k$ (automatic whenever $m_k \to \infty$) and $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ for all sufficiently large $k \ge k_0$. Then*
$$\liminf_{k \to \infty} \frac{\ln \ln T_k}{k} \;\ge\; \ln(1 + \theta), \qquad \liminf_{k \to \infty} \frac{\ln \ln m_k}{k} \;\ge\; \ln(1 + \theta).$$
*In particular:*
1. *(Optimality of the doubly-exponential base $2$ at $\theta = 1$.)* *At $\theta = 1$ (every complete-period schedule $m_k \ge T_{k-1}$), $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln \ln T_k}{k} \ge \ln 2$ and $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln \ln m_k}{k} \ge \ln 2$, showing that the doubly-exponential base $2$ in Phase 23 `GF-34` Theorem 5's $\ln T_k \sim c_* 2^k$ is universally optimal. Dropping pairwise coprimality of $q_k$ permits finite single-step LTE-order recycling — where $G_{k+1} = 1$, yet a prime that entered $T_k$ via LTE at an earlier step divides $\gcd(T_k, m_{k+1})$; for example, in base $2$, $m = (2, 4, 15)$ satisfies $m_3 = 15 > T_2 = 12$, $\gcd(D_2, 2^{15} - 1) = 1$, and $T_3 = 60 = \frac{1}{3} T_2 m_3$ — yet cannot lower the asymptotic doubly-exponential base below $2$.*
2. *(Impossibility of singly exponential and sub-doubly-exponential schedules.)* *No singly exponential ($m_k \asymp C^k$) or sub-doubly-exponential ($\ln m_k = e^{o(k)}$) schedule can satisfy $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ for any $\theta > 0$: every such schedule obeys $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln m_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = 0$. Moreover, the three classical sub-doubly-exponential reciprocal products ($X_{b, m}$ with $m_k = m^k$, $\prod_p (1 - 2^{-p})^{-1}$ with $m_k = p_k$, and $P_b = 1/E_b$ with $m_k = k$) satisfy the stronger full limit $\lim_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln m_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = 0$ by direct lower bounds on $\ln T_{k-1}$.*

*Proof.* First verify $T_k \to \infty$: since $b^{m_k} - 1 \ge 2$ for infinitely many $k$, either some prime $p$ divides $b^{m_k} - 1$ for infinitely many $k$ (forcing $v_p(D_k) \to \infty$, hence $v_p(T_k) \to \infty$ by LTE), or infinitely many distinct primes $p_r$ divide $D_k$ (forcing $\max_r \operatorname{ord}_{p_r}(b) \to \infty$, since only finitely many primes divide $b^d - 1$ for bounded $d$). In either case $T_k \to \infty$, and $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta \to \infty$ for $k \ge k_0$.

Set $A_k := \ln L_k$ and $B_k := \ln T_k$. By Lemma C2.2, $0 \le A_k \le B_k$, and from $L_k = L_{k-1} m_k / g_k$ together with $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ ($k \ge k_0$), we obtain
$$A_k \;=\; A_{k-1} + \ln m_k - \ln g_k \;\ge\; A_{k-1} + \theta B_{k-1} - \Bigl(\ln 60 + \sqrt{30 B_k}\,\ln(15 B_k + 2)\Bigr).$$
Fix any $\varepsilon \in (0, \theta)$. Because $B_k \to \infty$ and $\sqrt{30 B}\,\ln(15 B + 2) = o(B)$, there exists $k_1 \ge k_0$ such that $\ln 60 + \sqrt{30 B_k}\,\ln(15 B_k + 2) \le \varepsilon B_k$ and $B_{k_1} > 0$ for all $k \ge k_1$. Rearranging gives
$$A_k + \varepsilon B_k \;\ge\; A_{k-1} + \theta B_{k-1} \qquad (\forall\, k > k_1).$$
Now introduce the Lyapunov functional $\Psi_k(\varepsilon) := A_k + \varepsilon B_k$. Because $A_{k-1} \le B_{k-1}$ and $\theta - \varepsilon > 0$, we have $(\theta - \varepsilon) A_{k-1} \le (\theta - \varepsilon) B_{k-1}$, which rearranges identically to
$$(1 + \theta)(A_{k-1} + \varepsilon B_{k-1}) \;\le\; (1 + \varepsilon)(A_{k-1} + \theta B_{k-1}), \qquad \text{i.e.,} \qquad A_{k-1} + \theta B_{k-1} \;\ge\; \frac{1 + \theta}{1 + \varepsilon}\,\Psi_{k-1}(\varepsilon).$$
Hence $\Psi_k(\varepsilon) \ge \frac{1 + \theta}{1 + \varepsilon}\,\Psi_{k-1}(\varepsilon)$ for every $k > k_1$. Iterating from $k_1$ to $k$ and using $(1 + \varepsilon) B_k \ge \Psi_k(\varepsilon)$ yields
$$(1 + \varepsilon) B_k \;\ge\; \Psi_{k_1}(\varepsilon) \left(\frac{1 + \theta}{1 + \varepsilon}\right)^{k - k_1} \qquad (\forall\, k \ge k_1).$$
Since $\Psi_{k_1}(\varepsilon) \ge \varepsilon B_{k_1} > 0$, taking logarithms, dividing by $k$, and letting $k \to \infty$ gives $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln B_k}{k} \ge \ln(1 + \theta) - \ln(1 + \varepsilon)$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ establishes $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln\ln T_k}{k} \ge \ln(1 + \theta)$. Since $\ln m_k \ge \theta \ln T_{k-1}$ for $k \ge k_0$, we likewise get $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln\ln m_k}{k} \ge \ln(1 + \theta)$.

Finally, for part (1)'s single-step LTE-recycling example in base $2$, $m = (2, 4, 15)$ has $D_1 = 3$ ($T_1 = 2$), $D_2 = 3 \cdot 15 = 45$ ($T_2 = 12$, where $3 \mid T_2$ enters by LTE from $3^2 \mid D_2$), and $2^{15} - 1 = 32767 = 7 \cdot 31 \cdot 151$ is coprime to $D_2 = 45$ (so $G_3 = 1$), yet $\gcd(m_3, T_2) = \gcd(15, 12) = 3$, giving $T_3 = \operatorname{lcm}(12, 15) = 60 = \frac{1}{3} T_2 m_3$. For part (2): if $\ln m_k = e^{o(k)}$, then assuming $m_k \ge T_{k-1}^\theta$ for all sufficiently large $k$ would give $\liminf \frac{\ln\ln m_k}{k} \ge \ln(1 + \theta) > 0$, a contradiction; hence for every $\theta > 0$, $m_k < T_{k-1}^\theta$ holds for infinitely many $k$, i.e., $\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{\ln m_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = 0$. For the three classical examples, the full limit is $0$ directly: (i) for $P_b$ ($m_k = k$), Theorem C4.2 gives $\ln T_{k-1}^{(E)} \asymp_b k^2$, so $\frac{\ln k}{\ln T_{k-1}^{(E)}} \asymp_b \frac{\ln k}{k^2} \to 0$; (ii) for $X_{b, m}$ ($m_k = m^k$), since $m^{k-2} \mid m^{k-1}$, Lemma C2.1 yields $(b^{m^{k-2}} - 1) \mid G_{k-1} \mid T_{k-1}$, so $\ln T_{k-1} \gg_b m^{k-2}$ while $\ln m_k = k \ln m$, giving $\frac{\ln m_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = O_{b, m}(k / m^{k-2}) \to 0$; (iii) for $\prod_p (1 - 2^{-p})^{-1}$ ($m_k = p_k$), $L_{k-1} = \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} p_j \mid T_{k-1}$ with $p_j \ge j + 1$ gives $\ln T_{k-1} \ge \ln(k!)$, while $p_k \le 2^k$ (by Bertrand's postulate) gives $\ln p_k \le k \ln 2$, hence $\frac{\ln p_k}{\ln T_{k-1}} = O(1 / \ln k) \to 0$. $\square$

## 3. Sub-track C3: Mahler Partition Constants $X_2$ and $X_{b, m}$

Phase 23 `GF-32` (`notes/phase23/binary-partition-and-carry-complexity.md`) established the $2$-power tower transfer for $X_b$, elementary bounds $\log_m b_m(n) = \frac{1}{2}(\log_m n)^2 + O(\log n \log\log n)$, the $m = 2$ vs. $m \ge 3$ reciprocal dichotomy ($1/X_b = Y_b \notin K_b$ vs. $1/X_{b, m} \in M_b$ for $m \ge 3$), $2$-automaticity of $(\beta(n) \bmod 2^L)$ for $m = 2$, and the carry-cancellation gauge identity $a' = d + b\delta - S\delta$. Here we extend Cartier automaticity to **all** $m \ge 2$, prove exact functional-equation carry-gauge identities for $X_{b, m}$ (including the exact binary carry-parity law for $X_2$), and establish the **Sharp Carry-Valuation Barrier** up to $\frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2$.

### 3.1 Full $m$-ary Cartier automaticity and congruence extraction (Theorem C3.1)

**Theorem C3.1 (Full $m$-ary Cartier automaticity of $b_m(n)$).** *Fix an integer $m \ge 2$, and let $F(z) := \sum_{n \ge 0} b_m(n) z^n = \prod_{j \ge 0} (1 - z^{m^j})^{-1}$ and $F_k(z) := (1 - z)^{-k} F(z) \in \mathbb{Z}[[z]]$ ($k \ge 0$). Define the $m$-ary Cartier operators $\mathcal{C}_0, \dots, \mathcal{C}_{m-1}$ by $A(z) = \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} z^r (\mathcal{C}_r A)(z^m)$, and let $Q_m(z) := \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} z^j$.*
1. *(Exact $m$-ary Cartier closure.)* *For every $k \ge 0$ and $0 \le r < m$, writing $Q_m(z)^{k+1} = \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} z^r U_{k+1, r}(z^m)$ and $U_{k+1, r}(1 - w) = \sum_{j=0}^{\deg U_{k+1, r}} u_{k+1, r, j} w^j \in \mathbb{Z}[w]$, we have*
   $$\mathcal{C}_r F_k \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{\deg U_{k+1, r}} u_{k+1, r, j}\, F_{k + 1 - j}, \qquad \text{with exact leading coefficient } u_{k+1, r, 0} \;=\; U_{k+1, r}(1) \;=\; m^k.$$
2. *($m$-automaticity modulo $m^L$ and modulo prime powers $p^e$, $p \mid m$; uses Allouche–Shallit Thm. 6.6.2 as `External input`.)* *For every $L \ge 1$, the sequence $(b_m(n) \bmod m^L)_{n \ge 0}$ is **$m$-automatic** with $m$-kernel size at most $m^{L(L + 1)}$. More generally, for every prime $p \mid m$ with $v := v_p(m) \ge 1$ and every $e \ge 1$, $(b_m(n) \bmod p^e)_{n \ge 0}$ is $m$-automatic with $m$-kernel size at most $p^{e(\lceil e / v \rceil + 1)}$.*
3. *(Exact $k = 1$ identity and elementary CRA-type divisibility identity.)* *At $k = 1$, $\mathcal{C}_r F_1 = m F_2 - (m - 1 - r) F_1$ for all $0 \le r < m$. Taking $r = m - 1$ yields the elementary mod-$m$ identity (a base-level relative of the deeper Churchhouse–Rødseth–Andrews congruences for $b_m(m^{r+1} n) - b_m(m^r n)$ modulo growing powers of $m$)*
   $$b_m\bigl(m^2 n + m^2 - m\bigr) \;=\; m \sum_{j=0}^{n} b_m(m j) \;\equiv\; 0 \pmod m \qquad (\forall\, n \ge 0).$$

*Proof.*
(1) From $(1 - z) F(z) = F(z^m)$ and $(1 - z)^{-1} = Q_m(z)/(1 - z^m)$, we obtain
$$F_k(z) \;=\; \frac{F(z^m)}{(1 - z)^{k+1}} \;=\; Q_m(z)^{k+1} \frac{F(z^m)}{(1 - z^m)^{k+1}} \;=\; \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} z^r U_{k+1, r}(z^m) F_{k+1}(z^m).$$
Extracting the $r$-th residue class gives $\mathcal{C}_r F_k(y) = U_{k+1, r}(y) F_{k+1}(y)$. Since $\deg(Q_m^{k+1}) = (k+1)(m-1)$, we have $\deg U_{k+1, r} \le \lfloor ((k+1)(m-1) - r)/m \rfloor \le k$, so substituting $y = 1 - w$ and using $(1 - y)^j F_{k+1}(y) = F_{k + 1 - j}(y)$ ($0 \le j \le k$) yields $\mathcal{C}_r F_k = \sum_j u_{k+1, r, j} F_{k+1-j}$. To evaluate $u_{s, r, 0} = U_{s, r}(1)$ ($s := k + 1 \ge 1$), set $\zeta := e^{2\pi i / m}$. By root-of-unity orthogonality,
$$U_{s, r}(1) \;=\; \frac{1}{m} \sum_{t=0}^{m-1} \zeta^{-r t} Q_m(\zeta^t)^s \;=\; \frac{1}{m} \cdot m^s + 0 \;=\; m^{s - 1},$$
because $Q_m(1) = m$ while $Q_m(\zeta^t) = (1 - \zeta^{m t})/(1 - \zeta^t) = 0$ for every $1 \le t \le m - 1$.

(2) For $q = m^L$, at $k = L$ the only term outside $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}/m^L}(F_0, \dots, F_L)$ is $u_{L+1, r, 0} F_{L+1} = m^L F_{L+1} \equiv 0 \pmod{m^L}$, so $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}/m^L}(F_0, \dots, F_L)$ (cardinality $\le (m^L)^{L+1} = m^{L(L+1)}$) contains $F$ and is closed under all $\mathcal{C}_0, \dots, \mathcal{C}_{m-1}$. Similarly, for $p \mid m$ ($v = v_p(m)$) and $k_0 := \lceil e / v \rceil$, we have $p^e \mid m^{k_0} = u_{k_0 + 1, r, 0}$, so $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^e}(F_0, \dots, F_{k_0})$ (cardinality $\le p^{e(k_0 + 1)}$) is closed under all $\mathcal{C}_r$. By Eilenberg's finite $m$-kernel criterion (Allouche–Shallit, *Automatic Sequences*, Thm. 6.6.2, `External input`), both sequences are $m$-automatic.

(3) Direct expansion gives $Q_m(z)^2 = \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} (r + 1) z^r + \sum_{r=0}^{m-2} (m - 1 - r) z^{m + r}$, so $U_{2, r}(y) = (r + 1) + (m - 1 - r) y = m - (m - 1 - r)(1 - y)$, giving $\mathcal{C}_r F_1 = m F_2 - (m - 1 - r) F_1$. At $r = m - 1$, $\mathcal{C}_{m-1} F_1 = m F_2$. Since $[z^n] F_1 = \sum_{i \le n} b_m(i) = b_m(m n)$, reading the $z^n$ coefficient of $\mathcal{C}_{m-1} F_1 = m F_2$ gives $b_m(m(m n + m - 1)) = m \sum_{j=0}^n b_m(m j)$. $\square$

### 3.2 Exact functional-equation carry-gauge master congruence and binary carry-parity law (Theorem C3.2)

**Theorem C3.2 (Exact carry-gauge identities for $X_{b, m}$ and binary carry-parity law for $X_2$).** *Fix integers $b \ge 2$ and $m \ge 2$, write $X_{b, m} = \lfloor X_{b, m} \rfloor + \sum_{n=1}^\infty d_n b^{-n}$ ($d_n \in \{0, \dots, b - 1\}$), and let $(\delta_n)_{n \ge 1}$ be the unique canonical carry sequence of Phase 23 `GF-32` Theorem 5.2 satisfying $b_m(n) = d_n + b \delta_n - \delta_{n+1}$ with $\delta_n = o(b^n)$ (so $\delta_1 = \lfloor X_{b, m} \rfloor - 1$).*
1. *(Exact floor and asymptotic size.)* *For every $n \ge 1$,*
   $$\delta_n \;=\; \left\lfloor \sum_{j=0}^\infty b_m(n + j)\, b^{-1 - j} \right\rfloor, \qquad \frac{b_m(n)}{b - 1} - 1 \;<\; \delta_n \;=\; \frac{b_m(n)}{b - 1}\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr).$$
2. *(Exact $L$-digit Master Congruence.)* *For every $L \ge 1$ and $k \ge 1$, the $L$-digit base-$b$ block $W_L(k) := \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} d_{k + j} b^{L - 1 - j} \in \{0, \dots, b^L - 1\}$ satisfies*
   $$W_L(k) \;\equiv\; \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} b_m(k + j)\, b^{L - 1 - j} + \delta_{k + L} \pmod{b^L}.$$
3. *(Exact binary carry-parity law for $X_2$.)* *When $(b, m) = (2, 2)$, for every $k \ge 2$,*
   $$d_k(X_2) \;=\; \delta_{k + 1} \bmod 2.$$
4. *(Carry-increment functional equations.)* *Setting $\Delta_k := \delta_{k+1} - \delta_k$ ($k \ge 1$), for every $k \ge 2$:*
   $$d_k - d_{k - 1} \;=\; \Delta_k - b \Delta_{k - 1} \quad (m \nmid k), \qquad b_m(n) \;=\; (d_{m n} - d_{m n - 1}) + b \Delta_{m n - 1} - \Delta_{m n} \quad (k = m n).$$

*Proof.*
(1) From `GF-32` Theorem 5.2(a), $\delta_n = \sum_{j \ge 0} (b_m(n + j) - d_{n + j}) b^{-1 - j} = \sum_{j \ge 0} b_m(n + j) b^{-1 - j} - \{b^{n-1} X_{b, m}\}$. Because $\delta_n \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\{b^{n-1} X_{b, m}\} \in [0, 1)$, this gives the exact floor formula. Monotonicity $b_m(n + j) \ge b_m(n)$ gives $\delta_n > \sum_{j \ge 0} b_m(n) b^{-1-j} - 1 = \frac{b_m(n)}{b - 1} - 1$. For the upper asymptotic, let $K := \lfloor \log_b n \rfloor + 1$; for $0 \le j \le m K$, repeated application of $b_m(r + m) - b_m(r) = b_m(\lfloor r/m \rfloor + 1)$ gives $b_m(n + j) \le b_m(n) + K b_m(\lfloor (n + m K)/m \rfloor + 1) = b_m(n)(1 + O(n^{-1/2}))$ (by `GF-32` Corollary 2.2, since $\log_m b_m(n/m) - \log_m b_m(n) = -\log_m n + O(\log\log n)$), while for $j > m K$ the trivial doubling bound $b_m(r + m) \le 2 b_m(r)$ contributes $\sum_{j > m K} b_m(n + j) b^{-1-j} \le 4 m b_m(n) (2/b^m)^K = O(b_m(n) n^{-\ln(b^m/2)/\ln b}) = o(b_m(n))$. Hence $\delta_n = \frac{b_m(n)}{b - 1}(1 + o(1))$.

(2) Multiply $b_m(k + j) = d_{k + j} + b \delta_{k + j} - \delta_{k + j + 1}$ by $b^{L - 1 - j}$ and sum over $0 \le j \le L - 1$. The carry terms telescope identically to $b^L \delta_k - \delta_{k + L}$, yielding $\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} b_m(k + j) b^{L - 1 - j} = W_L(k) + b^L \delta_k - \delta_{k + L}$. Reducing modulo $b^L$ gives the master congruence.

(3) For $(b, m) = (2, 2)$, $L = 1$, part (2) gives $d_k(X_2) \equiv \beta(k) + \delta_{k + 1} \pmod 2$. Since $\beta(k)$ is even for every $k \ge 2$ (`GF-32`, `Phase23Cores.binPart_even`), $d_k(X_2) = \delta_{k + 1} \bmod 2$ for all $k \ge 2$.

(4) Subtract $b_m(k - 1) = d_{k-1} + b \delta_{k-1} - \delta_k$ from $b_m(k) = d_k + b \delta_k - \delta_{k+1}$ and use the exact recurrence $b_m(k) - b_m(k - 1) = [m \mid k]\, b_m(k / m)$. $\square$

### 3.3 Carry-Valuation Barrier for $X_2$ and $X_{m, m}$ (Theorem C3.3)

By Theorem C3.1 and Theorem C3.2(2), when $b = m$, the first term $\sum_{j < L} b_m(k + j) m^{L - 1 - j} \bmod m^L$ in the Master Congruence IS $m$-automatic, so any obstruction to transferring $m$-automaticity from $(b_m(n) \bmod m^L)$ to the real digits $d_k(X_{m, m})$ lives entirely in $\delta_{k + L} \bmod m^L$. The next theorem proves that this obstruction is **total** up to the valuation threshold $\frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2$ (sharp under the growth normalization $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k)$).

**Theorem C3.3 (Carry-Valuation Barrier for $X_{m, m}$, sharp under $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k)$).** *Fix an integer $m \ge 2$, any real number $z \in [0, 1)$, and any $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/2)$. There exists an explicit integer sequence $(a(k))_{k \ge 0}$ such that:*
1. *(Exact prescribed digits.)* *$F_m(a) := \sum_{k=0}^\infty a(k) m^{-k} \equiv z \pmod 1$, so $F_m(a)$ has the **exact same base-$m$ digits** as $z$.*
2. *(Quadratic $m$-adic congruence and all-$L$ $m$-automaticity.)* *For all sufficiently large $k$,*
   $$v_m\bigl(a(k) - b_m(k)\bigr) \;\ge\; \left(\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon\right)(\log_m k)^2 - O_\varepsilon(\log k).$$
   *In particular, for **every** fixed $L \ge 1$, $a(k) \equiv b_m(k) \pmod{m^L}$ for all sufficiently large $k$, so $(a(k) \bmod m^L)_{k \ge 0}$ is **$m$-automatic for every $L \ge 1$** (and when $m = 2$, $2 \mid a(k)$ holds for all $k \ge 2$).*
3. *(Exact Mahler growth and strict positivity.)* *$a(k) \ge b_m(k) \ge 1$ for all $k \ge 0$, and*
   $$a(k) \;=\; b_m(k)\!\left(1 + O_\varepsilon\!\left(m^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2} (\log_m k)^2}\right)\right),$$
   *so $a(k)$ obeys the exact same Mahler asymptotic $\log_m a(k) = \frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2 + O(\log k \log\log k)$.*
4. *(Consequence and sharpness.)* *Taking $z = C_m$ (Champernowne's base-$m$ normal number, `External input`), $z = \Lambda_m \in M_m \subset K_m$, or $z = 1/3$ shows that $m$-automaticity of $(a(k) \bmod m^L)$ for every fixed $L \ge 1$ — and even congruence modulo $m^{L(k)}$ up to $L(k) = (\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon)(\log_m k)^2$ — together with positivity and exact Mahler asymptotics, implies **nothing** about automaticity, normality, simple normality, or $K_m$-membership of $F_m(a)$. Conversely, the exponent $\frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2$ is **sharp under the growth normalization $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k)$**: if $a(k) \equiv b_m(k) \pmod{m^{L(k)}}$ with $L(k) > \log_m(2 b_m(k)) = \frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2 + O(\log k \log\log k)$ and $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k)$, then $a(k) = b_m(k)$ identically.*

*Proof.* Set $\Delta' := \{z - \{X_{m, m}\}\} \in [0, 1)$ and expand $\Delta' = \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n m^{-n}$ with nonnegative base-$m$ digits $c_n \in \{0, 1, \dots, m - 1\}$. Define the nondecreasing integer shift function
$$L(n) := \max\!\left(1,\; \left\lfloor \left(\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon\right)\bigl(\lfloor \log_m(n + 1) \rfloor\bigr)^2 \right\rfloor\right) \qquad (n \ge 1).$$
Because $n \mapsto n$ is strictly increasing and $n \mapsto L(n)$ is nondecreasing, the shifted indices $k(n) := n + L(n) \ge 2$ are **strictly increasing**. Define $h(k) := c_n m^{L(n)}$ if $k = k(n)$ for some $n \ge 1$, and $h(k) := 0$ otherwise; put $a(k) := b_m(k) + h(k)$ ($k \ge 0$).
1. Since $h(k) \ge 0$ and $\sum_{k=0}^\infty h(k) m^{-k} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n m^{L(n)} m^{-(n + L(n))} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n m^{-n} = \Delta'$, the series $F_m(a) = X_{m, m} + \Delta'$ converges absolutely and satisfies $F_m(a) \equiv z \pmod 1$.
2. Because $L(n) = o(n)$, we have $k(n) = n(1 + o(1))$ and $\log_m n = \log_m k - o(1)$, so $v_m(h(k(n))) \ge L(n) = (\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon)(\log_m k)^2 - O_\varepsilon(\log k)$. For any fixed $L \ge 1$, $L(n) \ge L$ for all large $n$, so $m^L \mid h(k)$ for all $k \ge k_0(L)$; changing finitely many terms of the $m$-automatic sequence $(b_m(k) \bmod m^L)$ (Theorem C3.1) preserves $m$-automaticity. Since $L(n) \ge 1$ and $k(n) \ge 2$, $m \mid h(k)$ for all $k \ge 2$, preserving $2 \mid a(k)$ for $k \ge 2$ when $m = 2$.
3. Since $c_n \ge 0$, $a(k) \ge b_m(k) \ge 1$ for all $k \ge 0$. By `GF-32` Corollary 2.2, $\log_m b_m(k) = \frac{1}{2}(\log_m k)^2 + O(\log k \log\log k)$, while $0 \le h(k) \le (m - 1) m^{L(n)} \le m^{(\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon)(\log_m k)^2 + O(1)}$, giving $h(k) / b_m(k) = O_\varepsilon(m^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(\log_m k)^2})$.
4. Sharpness is immediate: if $m^{L(k)} \mid (a(k) - b_m(k))$ and $|a(k) - b_m(k)| < b_m(k) < m^{L(k)}$, then $a(k) - b_m(k) = 0$. $\square$

## 4. Sub-track C4: Short-Orbit Bridge for $P_b = 1/E_b = \prod_{j \ge 1}(1 - b^{-j})^{-1}$

Phase 23 `GF-34` Theorem 6(c) proved complete-period exponential equidistribution $D(\mu(R_k^{(E)}; b)) \le C_b k p^{-e_0 k / s_b} \to 0$ for the partial products $R_k^{(E)} = \prod_{j=1}^k (1 - b^{-j})^{-1}$, leaving open how $R_k^{(E)}$ behaves on the actual short orbit $[0, O(k))$ where it shadows $P_b = 1/E_b$. We now prove exact shadowing lengths, exact $p$-adic valuations and the exact quadratic asymptotic of $\ln T_k^{(E)}$, unconditional pentagonal orbit coupling, and an exact quantitative short-orbit Weyl-sum characterization of $D^+(P_b; b) < 1$.

### 4.1 Exact shadowing length and partition-tail identity (Theorem C4.1)

**Theorem C4.1 (Exact shadowing length for $R_k^{(E)}$ vs. $P_b$).** *Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$ and $k \ge 1$. Write $R_k^{(E)} = \sum_{n \ge 0} p_{\le k}(n) b^{-n}$ where $p_{\le k}(n)$ counts partitions of $n$ into parts at most $k$.*
1. *(Exact partition-difference identity.)* *For every $0 \le m \le k$,*
   $$p(k + 1 + m) - p_{\le k}(k + 1 + m) \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{m} p(j).$$
2. *(Exact two-sided real shadowing law.)* *For every $b \ge 2$ and every $k \ge 1$,*
   $$\frac{R_k^{(E)}}{(b - 1) b^k} \;<\; P_b - R_k^{(E)} \;<\; \frac{P_b}{(b - 1) b^k}.$$
   *Consequently, $P_b - R_k^{(E)} = \frac{P_b}{(b - 1) b^k}\bigl(1 + O_b(b^{-k})\bigr)$ and $\log_b(P_b - R_k^{(E)}) = -k + \log_b(P_b / (b - 1)) + O_b(b^{-k})$. In particular, for any $\Delta \ge 1$, setting $k_b(N, \Delta) := N + \Delta + \lceil \log_b(2 P_b / (b - 1)) \rceil$ guarantees $0 < b^n(P_b - R_{k_b(N, \Delta)}^{(E)}) < b^{-\Delta}$ for every $0 \le n < N$, whereas real-line shadowing and digit certification break down at $n_1 := k - \lfloor \log_b(P_b / (b - 1)) \rfloor + 1$ (where $b^{n_1}(P_b - R_k^{(E)}) > 1$, though mod-$1$ proximity could still recur at isolated indices).*

*Proof.*
(1) Any partition of $k + 1 + m$ ($0 \le m \le k$) having largest part $\ge k + 1$ contains **exactly one** part $\ge k + 1$ (since two parts $\ge k + 1$ sum to $\ge 2k + 2 > k + 1 + m$). Writing that unique part as $k + 1 + r$ ($0 \le r \le m$) bijectively pairs such partitions with arbitrary partitions of $m - r$, giving $\sum_{r=0}^m p(m - r) = \sum_{j=0}^m p(j)$.

(2) Put $S_k := \sum_{j=k+1}^\infty b^{-j} = \frac{1}{(b - 1) b^k} \in (0, 1)$. For the lower bound, write $P_b - R_k^{(E)} = R_k^{(E)}\bigl(\prod_{j \ge k+1}(1 - b^{-j})^{-1} - 1\bigr)$; since $(1 - x)^{-1} > 1 + x$ on $(0, 1)$, expanding gives $\prod_{j \ge k+1}(1 - b^{-j})^{-1} - 1 > \sum_{j \ge k+1} b^{-j} = S_k$, establishing $P_b - R_k^{(E)} > R_k^{(E)} S_k$. For the upper bound, write $P_b - R_k^{(E)} = P_b\bigl(1 - \prod_{j \ge k+1}(1 - b^{-j})\bigr)$. For any sequence $x_j \in (0, 1)$, put $A_M := 1 - \prod_{j=1}^M (1 - x_j) \in (0, 1)$; then $A_2 = x_1 + x_2 - x_1 x_2$ and $A_{M+1} = A_M + x_{M+1}(1 - A_M) \le A_M + x_{M+1}$, so induction yields $\sum_{j=1}^M x_j - A_M \ge x_1 x_2 > 0$ for every $M \ge 2$. Taking $x_j = b^{-(k+j)}$ and letting $M \to \infty$ gives $1 - \prod_{j \ge k+1}(1 - b^{-j}) \le S_k - b^{-(2k+3)} < S_k$, establishing $P_b - R_k^{(E)} < P_b S_k$ strictly for all $b \ge 2$ and $k \ge 1$. Moreover, $R_k^{(E)} = P_b \prod_{j \ge k+1}(1 - b^{-j}) > P_b(1 - S_k)$ gives $P_b - R_k^{(E)} = \frac{P_b}{(b - 1) b^k}(1 + O_b(b^{-k}))$. For $0 \le n < N$ and $k = k_b(N, \Delta)$, $b^n(P_b - R_k^{(E)}) < \frac{P_b}{b - 1} b^{N - 1 - k} \le \frac{1}{2 b} b^{-\Delta} < b^{-\Delta}$. Conversely, for every $b \ge 2$ and $k \ge 1$, $1 - S_k \ge 1 - \frac{1}{b(b - 1)} \ge \frac{1}{b}$ (as $b^2 - 2b \ge 0$), so $R_k^{(E)} / P_b > 1/b$; thus at $n_1 := k - \lfloor \log_b(P_b/(b - 1)) \rfloor + 1$ we have $b^{n_1}(P_b - R_k^{(E)}) > \frac{R_k^{(E)}}{P_b} b > 1$, breaking real-line shadowing and digit certification. $\square$

### 4.2 Exact $p$-adic valuations and exact quadratic period asymptotic of $R_k^{(E)}$ (Theorem C4.2)

**Theorem C4.2 (Exact $p$-adic valuations and exact quadratic period asymptotic of $R_k^{(E)}$).** *Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$, and write $R_k^{(E)} = b^{k(k+1)/2} / D_k^{(E)}$ in lowest terms, where $D_k^{(E)} := \prod_{j=1}^k (b^j - 1)$ and $T_k^{(E)} := \operatorname{ord}_{D_k^{(E)}}(b)$.*
1. *(Exact $p$-adic valuations.)* *For every prime $p \nmid b$, put $s_p := \operatorname{ord}_p(b)$ and $e_0(p) := v_p(b^{s_p} - 1)$ (when $p = 2$ and $b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, put $e_0(2) := v_2(b^2 - 1) \ge 3$). Then*
   $$v_p\bigl(D_k^{(E)}\bigr) \;=\; \begin{cases} e_0(p)\left\lfloor \frac{k}{s_p} \right\rfloor + v_p\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{k}{s_p} \right\rfloor !\right) & \text{if } p \text{ odd or } (p = 2,\; b \equiv 1 \pmod 4), \\ \left\lceil \frac{k}{2} \right\rceil + e_0(2)\left\lfloor \frac{k}{2} \right\rfloor + v_2\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{k}{2} \right\rfloor !\right) & \text{if } p = 2 \text{ and } b \equiv 3 \pmod 4. \end{cases}$$
   *Moreover, once $k \ge s_p(\lfloor \log_p k \rfloor + 2)$, $v_p(T_k^{(E)}) = v_p(D_k^{(E)}) - e_0(p)$ (odd $p$ or $b \equiv 1 \pmod 4$) and $v_2(T_k^{(E)}) = v_2(D_k^{(E)}) - e_0(2) + 1$ ($p = 2, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$).*
2. *(Elementary Ramanujan-sum bound and primitive-order factorization.)* *For every $d \ge 1$, the $d$-th cyclotomic polynomial satisfies $|\ln \Phi_d(b) - \varphi(d) \ln b| \le \frac{1}{b - 1}$. Defining the primitive-order part $R(d) := \prod_{p:\, \operatorname{ord}_p(b) = d} p^{v_p(\Phi_d(b))}$, for every $d \ge 2$ except $(d = 2,\, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4)$ we have $R(d) \mid \Phi_d(b)$ and $\frac{\Phi_d(b)}{R(d)} \mid d$, hence $\Phi_d(b) / d \le R(d) \le \Phi_d(b)$.*
3. *(Exact quadratic period asymptotic — zero external inputs.)* *For **every** integer radix $b \ge 2$,*
   $$\ln T_k^{(E)} \;=\; \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{\pi^2}\right)(\ln b)\, k^2 \;+\; O_b\bigl(k \ln^2 k\bigr),$$
   *tightening Phase 23 `GF-34`'s $k \log^2 k \ll \ln T_k^{(E)} \ll k^2$ to the exact universal ratio $\ln T_k^{(E)} / \ln D_k^{(E)} \to 1 - \frac{6}{\pi^2} = 0.3920729\dots$ and, for any $\Delta = \Delta_N = o(N)$, shadowed period fraction $N / T_{k_b(N, \Delta)}^{(E)} = \exp\bigl(-(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{\pi^2} + o(1))(\ln b) N^2\bigr)$.*

*Proof.*
(1) For odd $p \nmid b$ (or $p = 2, b \equiv 1 \pmod 4$), $p \mid (b^j - 1) \iff s_p \mid j$, and writing $j = s_p u$ ($1 \le u \le \lfloor k/s_p \rfloor$) gives $v_p(b^j - 1) = e_0(p) + v_p(u)$ by LTE; summing over $u$ yields $e_0(p)\lfloor k/s_p \rfloor + v_p(\lfloor k/s_p \rfloor!)$. For $p = 2, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, odd $j \le k$ ($\lceil k/2 \rceil$ terms) contribute $1$ each, while even $j = 2 u \le k$ ($\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ terms) contribute $e_0(2) + v_2(u)$, giving the stated formula. By `GF-34` §5.4(2), every prime $q \neq p$ dividing $D_k^{(E)}$ contributes at most $\lfloor \log_p k \rfloor$ to $v_p(T_k^{(E)})$, whereas the LTE lift of $p^{v_p(D_k^{(E)})}$ contributes $v_p(D_k^{(E)}) - e_0(p)$ (or $+1$ for $p = 2, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$), which exceeds $\lfloor \log_p k \rfloor$ as soon as $\lfloor k / s_p \rfloor \ge \lfloor \log_p k \rfloor + 2$.

(2) Using $\ln|b - e^{i\theta}| = \ln b - \sum_{r=1}^\infty \frac{\cos(r\theta)}{r b^r}$ and summing over the $\varphi(d)$ primitive $d$-th roots of unity $\zeta_d^a$ ($\gcd(a, d) = 1$) gives $\ln \Phi_d(b) = \varphi(d) \ln b - \sum_{r=1}^\infty \frac{c_d(r)}{r b^r}$, where the Ramanujan sum satisfies $|c_d(r)| \le \gcd(r, d) \le r$. Hence $|\ln \Phi_d(b) - \varphi(d) \ln b| \le \sum_{r=1}^\infty b^{-r} = \frac{1}{b - 1}$. For the primitive-order factorization, let $p \mid \Phi_d(b)$ be any prime with $s_p := \operatorname{ord}_p(b) < d$. Then $s_p$ is a proper divisor of $d$, so write $d = s_p m$ ($m \ge 2$). Excluding $(d = 2,\, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4)$ (and noting that for $p = 2, b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ with even $d \ge 4$, $2^2 \mid b^2 - 1$ makes $v_2(b^{2 u} - 1) = e_0(2) + v_2(u)$, so Möbius inversion over $2 \mid e \mid d$ gives $v_2(\Phi_d(b)) = 1$ if $d = 2^r, r \ge 2$, and $0$ otherwise), LTE + Möbius inversion gives
$$v_p(\Phi_d(b)) \;=\; \sum_{e \mid m} \mu(m/e)\bigl(e_0(p) + v_p(e)\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{e \mid m} \mu(m/e)\, v_p(e) \;=\; \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } m = p^r\ (r \ge 1), \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$
Thus every non-primitive prime $p \mid \Phi_d(b)$ divides $d$ and appears to the first power in $\Phi_d(b)$, so $\Phi_d(b) / R(d) \mid d$.

(3) Write $T_k^{(E)} = \operatorname{lcm}_{p \mid D_k^{(E)}} \bigl(s_p \cdot p^{v_p(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{v_p(D_k^{(E)})}}(b))}\bigr)$. Notice that:
- For every prime $p \mid D_k^{(E)}$, $s_p \le k$, so $L_{\mathrm{ord}} := \operatorname{lcm}_{p \mid D_k^{(E)}} s_p \mid \operatorname{lcm}(1, \dots, k)$, and $\ln \operatorname{lcm}(1, \dots, k) = \sum_{p \le k} \lfloor \log_p k \rfloor \ln p \le k \ln k = O(k \ln k)$.
- Grouping all primes $p \mid D_k^{(E)}$ by $d = s_p \in \{1, \dots, k\}$, the pure LTE-lift product is
  $$E_k := \prod_{p \mid D_k^{(E)}} p^{v_p(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{v_p(D_k^{(E)})}}(b))} \;=\; 2^{O_b(k)} \cdot \left(\prod_{d=1}^k R(d)^{\lfloor k/d \rfloor - 1}\right) \cdot \left(\prod_{p \le k,\, p \nmid b} p^{v_p(\lfloor k/s_p \rfloor !)}\right),$$
  where the $2^{O_b(k)}$ factor absorbs the single prime $p = 2$ (including the exceptional $d = 2$ term when $b \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, which contributes $O_b(k)$ after weighting, while $d = 1$ contributes $(\lfloor k/1 \rfloor - 1) \ln(b - 1) = O_b(k)$), and primes $p > k$ have $\lfloor k / s_p \rfloor \le k < p$ hence $v_p(\lfloor k / s_p \rfloor!) = 0$.
- The factorial product divides $k!$ (since $\lfloor k/s_p \rfloor \le k$), giving $\sum_{p \le k,\, p \nmid b} v_p(\lfloor k/s_p \rfloor!) \ln p \le \ln(k!) = O(k \ln k)$.
- Since $E_k \mid T_k^{(E)} \mid E_k \cdot \operatorname{lcm}(1, \dots, k)$, we obtain $\ln T_k^{(E)} = \ln E_k + O(k \ln k)$. Applying part (2) ($\ln R(d) = \varphi(d) \ln b + O_b(\ln d)$ for $d \ge 3$, while the fixed indices $d = 1, 2$ contribute $O_b(k)$ after weighting) gives
  $$\ln T_k^{(E)} \;=\; \sum_{d=1}^{k} \left(\left\lfloor \frac{k}{d} \right\rfloor - 1\right) \varphi(d) \ln b \;+\; O_b\!\left(k \sum_{d=1}^{k} \frac{\ln d}{d} + k \ln k\right).$$
  By the exact Dirichlet identity $\sum_{d=1}^k \lfloor k/d \rfloor \varphi(d) = \sum_{n=1}^k \sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d) = \sum_{n=1}^k n = \frac{k(k+1)}{2}$ and Mertens' elementary estimate $\sum_{d=1}^k \varphi(d) = \sum_{m=1}^k \mu(m)\frac{\lfloor k/m \rfloor (\lfloor k/m \rfloor + 1)}{2} = \frac{k^2}{2}\sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{\mu(m)}{m^2} + O(k \ln k) = \frac{3}{\pi^2} k^2 + O(k \ln k)$, subtracting gives $\ln T_k^{(E)} = (\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{\pi^2})(\ln b) k^2 + O_b(k \ln^2 k)$. Since $\ln D_k^{(E)} = \sum_{j=1}^k \ln(b^j - 1) = \frac{1}{2}(\ln b) k^2 + O_b(k)$, dividing gives $\ln T_k^{(E)} / \ln D_k^{(E)} \to 1 - \frac{6}{\pi^2}$. $\square$

### 4.3 Pentagonal orbit coupling and partition-congruence valuation barrier (Theorem C4.3)

**Theorem C4.3 (Pentagonal orbit coupling and $\sqrt{n}$ partition-congruence barrier).** *Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$, and let $\omega_j^\pm := j(3j \pm 1)/2$ be the generalized pentagonal numbers.*
1. *(Exact pentagonal orbit coupling.)* *For every integer $K \ge 1$ and every $n \in [\omega_K^+, \omega_{K+1}^-)$ (a gap of exact length $2K + 1$),*
   $$\left\| b^n P_b + \sum_{j=1}^{K} (-1)^j \bigl(b^{n - \omega_j^-} P_b + b^{n - \omega_j^+} P_b\bigr) \right\|_{\mathbb{T}} \;\le\; 2 P_b \, b^{n - \omega_{K+1}^-}.$$
2. *(Exact canonical carry and master congruence.)* *Writing $P_b = \lfloor P_b \rfloor + \sum_{n \ge 1} d_n(P_b) b^{-n}$ with canonical carry $\delta_n(P_b) := \lfloor \sum_{j \ge 0} p(n + j) b^{-1-j} \rfloor$, every $L$-digit block $W_L(n; P_b)$ satisfies $W_L(n; P_b) \equiv \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} p(n + j) b^{L - 1 - j} + \delta_{n + L}(P_b) \pmod{b^L}$.*
3. *(Partition-congruence valuation barrier up to $\sqrt{n}$, sharp under $|a(k) - p(k)| < p(k)$; uses Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic as `External input`.)* *For every real $z \in [0, 1)$ and every $\varepsilon \in (0, \pi\sqrt{2/3}/\ln b)$, pushing the base-$b$ digits of $\{z - \{P_b\}\}$ forward by $L(n) := \max(1, \lfloor (\frac{\pi\sqrt{2/3}}{\ln b} - \varepsilon)\sqrt{n} \rfloor)$ constructs a strictly positive integer sequence $a(k) \ge p(k)$ with $a(k) = p(k)(1 + O_\varepsilon(b^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{k}}))$, $F_b(a) \equiv z \pmod 1$, and $v_b(a(k) - p(k)) \ge (\frac{\pi\sqrt{2/3}}{\ln b} - \varepsilon)\sqrt{k} - O_\varepsilon(1)$. Conversely, $\frac{\pi\sqrt{2/3}}{\ln b}\sqrt{k}$ is sharp under the growth normalization $|a(k) - p(k)| < p(k)$.*

*Proof.*
(1) Put $E_b^{(K)} := 1 + \sum_{j=1}^K (-1)^j (b^{-\omega_j^-} + b^{-\omega_j^+})$. Since $E_b \cdot P_b = 1$ and $b^n E_b^{(K)} \in \mathbb{Z}$ for $n \ge \omega_K^+$, we have $b^n E_b^{(K)} P_b = b^n - b^n (E_b - E_b^{(K)}) P_b \equiv -b^n (E_b - E_b^{(K)}) P_b \pmod 1$. Since $|E_b - E_b^{(K)}| \le \sum_{j \ge K+1}(b^{-\omega_j^-} + b^{-\omega_j^+}) < 2 b^{-\omega_{K+1}^-}$ (the alternating tail is even smaller than its first term $b^{-\omega_{K+1}^-}$, and the factor $2$ covers all $b \ge 2$ without needing sign alternation), we get $\|b^n E_b^{(K)} P_b\|_{\mathbb{T}} \le 2 P_b b^{n - \omega_{K+1}^-}$.

(2)–(3) Identical to the proofs of Theorems C3.2(1)–(2) and C3.3, replacing $\log_m b_m(n) \sim \frac{1}{2}(\log_m n)^2$ by the Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic $\log_b p(n) = \frac{\pi\sqrt{2/3}}{\ln b}\sqrt{n} - \log_b(4\sqrt{3} n) + o(1)$ (`External input`). $\square$

### 4.4 Exact quantitative short-orbit Weyl-sum characterization of $D^+(P_b; b) < 1$ (Theorem C4.4)

**Theorem C4.4 (Exact quantitative short-orbit Weyl-sum bridge for $P_b$).** *Fix an integer radix $b \ge 2$. For $N \ge 1$ and $\Delta \ge 1$, put $k_b(N, \Delta) := N + \Delta + \lceil \log_b(2 P_b / (b - 1)) \rceil$.*
1. *(Short-orbit Weyl shadowing.)* *For every harmonic $h \ge 1$,*
   $$\left|W_h(N; P_b, b) - W_h\bigl(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta)}^{(E)}, b\bigr)\right| \;\le\; 2\pi h\, b^{-\Delta}.$$
2. *(Quantitative sufficient criterion for $D^+(P_b; b) < 1$ in all bases $b \ge 2$.)* *For every real $x$ and $N \ge 1$,*
   $$1 - D_N(x; b) \;\ge\; \frac{2\bigl(1 - \operatorname{Re} W_{b - 1}(N; x, b)\bigr)}{5 \pi^2 (b - 1)^2} - \frac{1}{N}.$$
   *Consequently, if there exist $\eta > 0$ and $\Delta_N \to \infty$ with $\limsup_{N\to\infty} \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b) \le 1 - \eta$, then $D^+(P_b; b) \le 1 - \frac{2\eta}{5 \pi^2 (b - 1)^2} < 1$.*
3. *(Exact two-sided equivalence in base $b = 2$.)* *For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and $N \ge 1$,*
   $$\frac{2}{5 \pi^2}\bigl(1 - \operatorname{Re} W_1(N; x, 2)\bigr) - \frac{1}{N} \;\le\; 1 - D_N(x; 2) \;\le\; 2^{3/4}\bigl(1 - \operatorname{Re} W_1(N; x, 2)\bigr)^{1/4}.$$
   *Hence $D^+(P_2; 2) < 1 \iff \limsup_{N\to\infty} \operatorname{Re} W_1(N; R_{k_2(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, 2) < 1$ (for any $\Delta_N \to \infty$).*
4. *(Exact two-sided characterization in ALL bases $b \ge 2$, and sharpness of the two-harmonic criterion.)* *For every $b \ge 3$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$, and $N \ge 1$, putting $\eta_1 := 1 - \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}(N; x, b)$, $\eta_2 := 1 - |W_1(N; x, b)|$, and $\delta_1 := \frac{\sqrt{\eta_1}}{2\sqrt{2}(b - 1)}$, we have*
   $$1 - D_N(x; b) \;\le\; 2\sqrt{2\left(\delta_1 + \frac{\eta_2 + 2\pi \delta_1}{2 \sin^2(\pi / (b - 1))}\right)},$$
   *while conversely $\max(\eta_1, \eta_2) \le \frac{5\pi^2(b - 1)^2}{2}(1 - D_N(x; b) + \frac{1}{N})$. Therefore, for every $b \ge 2$ and any $\Delta_N \to \infty$,*
   $$D^+(P_b; b) < 1 \iff \limsup_{N\to\infty} \min\!\left(\left|W_1\bigl(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b\bigr)\right|,\; \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}\bigl(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b\bigr)\right) < 1.$$
   *In base $b = 3$, neither single condition suffices alone: $x = 1/4$ satisfies $|W_2(N; 1/4, 3)| = 1$ (with $W_2 = -1$) yet $D_N(1/4; 3) \to 1/2 < 1$; and the two-point fixed-point measure $\sigma = \frac{1}{2}\delta_0 + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{1/2}$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re} \widehat{\sigma}(2) = 1$ yet $D(\sigma) = 1/2 < 1$ (caught by $|\widehat{\sigma}(1)| = 0 < 1$), realized as the empirical limit measure $\mu_N^{\mathrm{emp}}(x; 3) \xrightarrow{W_1} \sigma$ of the explicit base-$3$ number $x = 0.0^1 1^1 0^2 1^2 0^3 1^3 \dots_3$ alternating equal-length blocks of $0$s and $1$s of growing length $r \to \infty$ (since $0.\overline{0}_3 = 0$, $0.\overline{1}_3 = 1/2$, and block boundaries have density $0$, giving $\operatorname{Re} W_2(N; x, 3) \to 1$, $W_1(N; x, 3) \to 0$, and $D_N(x; 3) \to 1/2 < 1$).*
5. *(Short-orbit normality criterion.)* *$P_b$ is base-$b$ normal if and only if for every fixed $h \ge 1$ and $\Delta_N \to \infty$, $W_h(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, b) \to 0$.*

*Proof.*
(1) By Theorem C4.1(2), $0 < b^n(P_b - R_{k_b(N, \Delta)}^{(E)}) \le b^{-\Delta}$ for all $0 \le n < N$. Since $|e(h u) - e(h v)| \le 2\pi h |u - v|$, averaging over $0 \le n < N$ gives $|W_h(N; P_b, b) - W_h(N; R_{k_b(N, \Delta)}^{(E)}, b)| \le 2\pi h b^{-\Delta}$.

(2) Using $1 - \cos(2\pi t) = 2\sin^2(\pi t) \le 2\pi^2 \|t\|_{\mathbb{T}}^2$ and the identity $\|(b - 1) y\|_{\mathbb{T}} = (b - 1) \operatorname{dist}(y, F_b) \le (b - 1)\|y - c\|_{\mathbb{T}}$ for any $c \in F_b$, together with $\operatorname{dist}(y, F_b) \le \frac{1}{2(b-1)} \le \frac{1}{2}$, we get for any $c \in F_b$:
$$1 - \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}(N; x, b) \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bigl(1 - \cos(2\pi(b - 1) b^n x)\bigr) \;\le\; 2\pi^2 (b - 1)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \|b^n(x - c)\|_{\mathbb{T}} \;=\; \pi^2 (b - 1)^2 M_N(x - c; b).$$
Minimizing over $c \in F_b$ gives $\mu_N(x) \ge \frac{1 - \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1}(N; x, b)}{\pi^2(b - 1)^2}$. Applying Phase 23 `GF-32` Lemma 5.3 ($1 - D_N(x; b) \ge \frac{2}{5}\mu_N(x) - \frac{1}{N}$) proves (2), and combining with (1) gives the corollary for $P_b$.

(3) For $b = 2$, $F_2 = \mathbb{Z}$, so $\operatorname{dist}(y, F_2) = \|y\|_{\mathbb{T}}$ and $\mu_N(x) = M_N(x; 2)$. Using $1 - \cos(2\pi t) = 2\sin^2(\pi t) \ge 8\|t\|_{\mathbb{T}}^2$ (since $\sin(\pi u) \ge 2 u$ on $[0, 1/2]$) and Cauchy–Schwarz yields $\mu_N(x) \le \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N}\|2^n x\|_{\mathbb{T}}^2} \le \sqrt{(1 - \operatorname{Re} W_1(N; x, 2))/8}$. Inserting into `GF-32` Lemma 5.3 ($1 - D_N \le 2\sqrt{2\mu_N}$) gives $1 - D_N(x; 2) \le 2\sqrt{2}\cdot 8^{-1/4}(1 - \operatorname{Re} W_1)^{1/4} = 2^{3/4}(1 - \operatorname{Re} W_1)^{1/4}$.

(4) Let $b \ge 3$. For the reverse bound, choose $c \in F_b$ attaining $\mu_N(x)$; then $1 - |W_1(N; x, b)| \le |e(c) - W_1(N; x, b)| \le 2\pi M_N(x - c; b) = 2\pi \mu_N(x) \le \pi^2(b - 1)^2 \mu_N(x)$, and combining with part (2) and `GF-32` Lemma 5.3 ($\mu_N(x) \le \frac{5}{2}(1 - D_N + \frac{1}{N})$) yields $\max(\eta_1, \eta_2) \le \frac{5\pi^2(b - 1)^2}{2}(1 - D_N(x; b) + \frac{1}{N})$.

For the upper bound on $1 - D_N(x; b)$, assign each $0 \le n < N$ a nearest fixed point $c_n \in \{j/(b - 1) : 0 \le j \le b - 2\}$ and let $p_j := \frac{1}{N}\#\{n < N : c_n = j/(b - 1)\}$. Using $1 - \cos(2\pi t) \ge 8\|t\|_{\mathbb{T}}^2$ and Cauchy–Schwarz, $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N} \operatorname{dist}(b^n x, F_b) \le \sqrt{\eta_1 / (8(b - 1)^2)} = \delta_1$. Setting $Z := \sum_{j=0}^{b-2} p_j e(j/(b - 1))$, we have $|W_1(N; x, b) - Z| \le 2\pi \delta_1$, so $|Z| \ge 1 - \eta_2 - 2\pi \delta_1$. Expanding $|Z|^2$ gives the exact identity
$$1 - |Z|^2 \;=\; \sum_{0 \le j < \ell \le b - 2} p_j p_\ell \left|e\!\left(\frac{j}{b - 1}\right) - e\!\left(\frac{\ell}{b - 1}\right)\right|^2 \;\ge\; 4 \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{b - 1}\right) \sum_{j < \ell} p_j p_\ell.$$
Because $2 \sum_{j < \ell} p_j p_\ell = 1 - \sum_{j=0}^{b-2} p_j^2 \ge 1 - \max_j p_j$ and $1 - |Z|^2 \le 2(1 - |Z|)$, we deduce
$$1 - \max_j p_j \;\le\; \frac{1 - |Z|}{\sin^2(\pi / (b - 1))} \;\le\; \frac{\eta_2 + 2\pi \delta_1}{\sin^2(\pi / (b - 1))}.$$
Let $c_* := j_* / (b - 1)$ attain $\max_j p_j$. Bounding $\|b^n x - c_*\|_{\mathbb{T}}$ by $\operatorname{dist}(b^n x, F_b)$ when $c_n = c_*$ and by $1/2$ otherwise yields $\mu_N(x) \le M_N(x - c_*; b) \le \delta_1 + \frac{1}{2}(1 - p_{j_*})$, and `GF-32` Lemma 5.3 ($1 - D_N \le 2\sqrt{2\mu_N}$) completes the bound. Combined with (1) and (2), this establishes the exact equivalence for $D^+(P_b; b) < 1$. The two base-$3$ counterexamples are verified directly.

(5) Follows immediately from (1) and Weyl's criterion. $\square$

Meanwhile, Proposition C1.3 shows that prime-simplex realization under the idealized band recursion requires a cyclotomic family $Q_j \mid \frac{1 - q^{L_j}}{1 - q}$ with $0 < \rho^{(p)}(Q_j) \to 0$ (blocked at $L = p^r$ by Lucas's theorem, though $\rho_0 = \rho^{(p)} = 3/10 < 1/3$ already occurs at $L = 60$ via $Q = \Phi_3 \Phi_4 \Phi_{10}$ for odd primes $p$). Realizing an irrational $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$ remains open.
2. **Dense exact convergent spectrum $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$ in non-dyadic bases $b \neq 2^e$ (`Open`):** Theorem C1.1 establishes density of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(2^e)$ for all powers of $2$, while Theorem C1.4 proves that $+1$-inversion ($2 \in \langle b \rangle \pmod D$) is structurally blocked from long-zero-run constructions in every non-dyadic base $b \neq 2^e$ via Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier. Whether $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{conv}}(b)$ is dense in $[0, 1]$ for $b \in \{3, 5, 6, 7, 10, \dots\}$ remains open.
3. **Distribution of Mahler's constants $X_2, X_{b, m}$ and automaticity of $d_n(X_2)$ (`Open`):** Normality, $D^+ < 1$, $D^- > 0$, $K_b$-membership, and automaticity of the base-$b$ digit sequence of $X_{b, m}$ remain open; Theorem C3.3 proves that modular automaticity of $b_m(n) \bmod m^L$ (even up to $(\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon)(\log_m k)^2$) cannot decide any of them.
4. **Short-orbit Weyl cancellation for $P_b = 1/E_b$ (`Open`):** Theorem C4.2 and Theorem C4.4 sharpen the short-orbit gap (for $\Delta_N = o(N)$) to $N / T_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)} = \exp\bigl(-(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{\pi^2} + o(1))(\ln b) N^2\bigr)$ and reduce $D^+(P_b; b) < 1$ quantitatively to bounding $\min(|W_1|, \operatorname{Re} W_{b-1})$ on the first $N$ steps of $R_{k_b(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}$. Proving $\limsup \operatorname{Re} W_1(N; R_{k_2(N, \Delta_N)}^{(E)}, 2) < 1$ (which would immediately yield $D^+(P_2; 2) < 1$ and $P_2 \notin K_2$) remains open.
