## 1. Exact cubic error and all-prefix programming (`programming.md` §§B1–B3, §G1)

### 1.1 Exact positive coefficient recurrence and reduced denominator (§B1)
- **Recurrence and uniqueness:** For $0=n_0<n_1<\cdots$, $u_0=0$, $u_k\in[0,1)\cap\mathbb Q$, $B=b^{n_k-n_{k-1}}$, and $c_k\in(0,1]$ the positive representative of $u_k-Bu_{k-1}\bmod 1$, induction confirms $R_k-u_k = B(R_{k-1}-u_{k-1}) + (Bu_{k-1}+c_k-u_k)\in\mathbb Z$ and $\{R_k\}=u_k$, with $R_k = b^{n_k}\sum_{i=1}^k c_i b^{-n_i}$. When $u_k\equiv Bu_{k-1}\pmod 1$, the convention forces $c_k=1$.
- **Reduced denominator formula:** Writing $u_{k-1}=a/d$ and $u_k=a'/d'$ in lowest terms, setting $g=\gcd(B,d)$, $d_*=d/g$, $a_*=Ba/g$ gives $\gcd(a_*,d_*)=1$ and $Bu_{k-1}=a_*/d_*$ in lowest terms. Putting $L=\operatorname{lcm}(d_*,d')$ and $v=a'(L/d')-a_*(L/d_*)$ gives $u_k-Bu_{k-1}=v/L$, and since adding an integer preserves the reduced denominator, $\operatorname{den}(c_k)=L/\gcd(L,v)$. When a prime divides $d_*$ and $d'$ to unequal exponents, only one of $a'(L/d')$ and $a_*(L/d_*)$ is divisible by that prime, so the larger exponent survives; for an off-radix prime $p\nmid b$ and $d=p^i, d'=p^j$ ($i\ne j$), $g=1$, $d_*=p^i$, and $\operatorname{den}(c_k)=p^{\max(i,j)}$.
- **Dense positive geometric group:** For $h=n_k-n_{k-1}$, summing $c_k(b-1)b^{-m}/(b^h-1)$ over $n_{k-1}<m\le n_k$ yields $c_k b^{-n_k}$ exactly, with raw terms bounded by $b^{-m}$ and total sum at most $1/(b-1)$.
- **Independent check:** Verified across all $12\,288$ transitions in `scripts/phase18_programming.py` (`check_coefficients`), and cross-checked against the kernel-formalized theorems `programState_fract`, `programCoeffs_prime_power`, `programState_den`, and `programmed_dense_hasSum` in `NormalityChecker/Phase18Synthetic.lean`.

### 1.2 Window certificate and bounded-denominator bounds (§G1)
- **Shifted residual inequality:** By absolute convergence (valid also for countable infinite groups $g_i$), $b^n x - b^n\sum_{i\le k} s_i = b^n\sum_{i\le k}(g_i-s_i) + b^n\sum_{i>k} g_i$, so the triangle inequality for distance to $\mathbb Z$ gives $d(T^n x, y_n)\le \|b^n\sum_{i\le k}(g_i-s_i)\| + b^n\sum_{i>k}|g_i|$.
- **Two-sided discrepancy bound (1):** Splitting $[0,N)$ into active positions $A$ and $Z=N-|A|$ remaining positions with empirical model measure $\rho$, put $\nu = (|A|\lambda + Z\rho)/N$. By linearity of signed arc excess $(\nu-\lambda)(I) = (Z/N)(\rho-\lambda)(I)$, we have $D(\nu) = (Z/N)D(\rho)$. Under the active uniformity hypothesis $|\#\{n\in A: y_n\in I\} - |A||I||\le H$ for every arc $I$, the model count on $I$ differs from $N\nu(I)$ by at most $H$. Enlarging any test arc $I$ at both endpoints by $\epsilon+\eta$ ($\eta>0$, capped at the full circle) and counting the at most $E$ bad positions with $d(T^n x, y_n)>\epsilon$ yields both inclusions:
  $$\left|D_N(x;b) - \frac{Z}{N}D(\rho)\right| \le \frac{H+E}{N} + 2\epsilon.$$
  Wrapping across $0$ is properly handled because circle distance $d(u,v)\le\epsilon$ places $u$ inside the circular $(\epsilon+\eta)$-neighborhood of $v$.
- **Bounded-denominator squeeze:** When all retired positions have $y_n=0$, $\rho=\delta_0$ and $D(\rho)=1$. Notice that in the cubic construction (§B3), block $0$ ($[0,t)$) always has $u_0=0$ and $t\ge 1$, so for every $N\ge 1$ we have $Z(N)\ge\min(N,t)\ge 1>0$; thus $\rho=\delta_0$ and $D(\rho)=1$ hold at every prefix $N\ge 1$, including at the all-active endpoint $\delta=0$ (where $Z(N)=t$ for $N\ge t$). More generally, if the $Z>0$ remaining models have reduced denominators $\le Q$, they lie in at most $M\le 1+\sum_{d=1}^Q \varphi(d)\le \sum_{d=1}^Q d$ fractions, so some atom has $\rho$-mass $\ge 1/M$, giving $D(\rho)\ge 1/M$ and
  $$\frac{Z}{MN} - \eta_N \le D_N(x;b) \le \frac{Z}{N} + \eta_N,\qquad \eta_N = \frac{H+E}{N} + 2\epsilon.$$

### 1.3 Active period error, tail exceptions, and counterexamples (§B2)
- **Prime-power period input:** For an odd prime $p\nmid b$, $t=\operatorname{ord}_p(b)$, and $v=v_p(b^t-1)$, lifting the exponent gives $P=\operatorname{ord}_{p^e}(b)=t p^{\max(e-v,0)}\le (p-1)p^{e-1}<p^e$. For $e>v$, the multiplicative subgroup $\langle b\rangle \bmod p^e$ is the full inverse image of $\langle b\rangle \bmod p^v$ and decomposes every unit coset into $t$ equally spaced grids of size $p^{e-v}$ and mesh $p^{v-e}$, so each full period has extreme discrepancy at most $d_e=\min(1,p^{v-e})$ (and unnormalized arc count error at most $t\le p^v = P d_e$). Splitting an observed block overlap $\ell_k$ into $\lfloor\ell_k/P_k\rfloor$ complete periods and remainder $r_k=\ell_k\bmod P_k$ gives the active error bound (2):
  $$H_N = \sum_{k\text{ active}}\big((\ell_k - r_k)d_{e_k} + r_k\big).$$
- **Real tail bound and exception count:** Because $0<c_i\le 1$ and the insertion exponents $n_i$ ($i>k$) are distinct integers $\ge n_{k+1}$,
  $$0 < b^n\sum_{i>k} c_i b^{-n_i} \le \sum_{m\ge n_{k+1}} b^{n-m} = \frac{b}{b-1}b^{-(n_{k+1}-n)} = B_0 b^{-(n_{k+1}-n)}.$$
  Since the sparse model has $g_i=s_i=c_i b^{-n_i}$, the past group error is zero and $d(T^n x, y_n)\le B_0 b^{-(n_{k+1}-n)}$. When $B_0 b^{-W}\le\epsilon$, every shift $n\in[n_k,n_{k+1})$ with $d(T^n x, y_n)>\epsilon$ satisfies $n_{k+1}-n < W$, contributing at most $W$ exceptions per block across blocks $k=0,\ldots,K$, so $E_N\le (K+1)W$.
- **Counterexamples in §B2:**
  1. $n_k=2k$, $b=2$, $u_k=0$: gives $c_k=1$ for all $k\ge 1$ and $x=\sum_{k\ge 1} 2^{-2k}=1/3$, whose orbit alternates $1/3, 2/3$ with $\lim_{N\to\infty} D_N(x;2)=2/3\ne 1$, demonstrating that retired models without vanishing tail density fail.
  2. $n_1=1$, $q_k=p^{n_k^2}$, $n_{k+1}=n_k+q_k^2$, $u_k=1/q_k$: at full block ends $N=n_{k+1}$, $P_k/h_k\le 1/q_k\to 0$ gives $D_{n_{k+1}}\to 0$. However, at the intermediate cutoff $N = n_k + L_k$ with $L_k = \lfloor\frac{1}{2}\log_b q_k\rfloor = \lfloor\frac{1}{2}n_k^2\log_b p\rfloor$, the $L_k$ shifts in block $k$ have models $b^j/q_k \le q_k^{-1/2}\to 0$ ($0\le j<L_k$) and uniformly vanishing tails, while $n_k/L_k \asymp 1/n_k\to 0$. Thus $D_N\to 1$ along these intermediate prefixes, confirming that $P_k=o(h_k)$ without $P_k=o(n_k)$ does not control unfinished intervals.

### 1.4 Exact cubic error bound (Equation 3 in §B3)
- **Step-by-step derivation of (3):** With $n_k=tk^3$, $e_k=1+\lfloor\log_p k\rfloor$, $q_k=p^{e_k}$, we have $p^{e_k-1}\le k < p^{e_k}=q_k\le pk$ for every $k\ge 1$, including powers of $p$. For $tK^3\le N<t(K+1)^3$ ($K\ge 1$):
  - Since $h_k = t((k+1)^3-k^3) = t(3k^2+3k+1)\le 7tk^2$ for $k\ge 1$, each active block $k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ contributes to $H_N$ at most
    $$(\ell_k-r_k)d_{e_k} + r_k \le h_k\frac{p^v}{q_k} + q_k < 7tk^2\frac{p^v}{k} + pk = (7tp^v+p)k.$$
    Summing over $1\le k\le K$ gives $H_N \le (7tp^v+p)K(K+1)/2$.
  - With $\epsilon = 1/(K+1)$ and $W = \lceil\log_b(B_0(K+1))\rceil$, we have $B_0 b^{-W}\le \epsilon$ and $E_N \le (K+1)W$.
  - For the retired weight $Z(N) = t + \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} a_k h_k + a_K\ell_K$ and $N = t + \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} h_k + \ell_K$ (where $\ell_K = N - tK^3 \in [0, h_K)$), putting $S_j = \sum_{i=1}^j (a_i-\delta)$ with $|S_j|\le C$ for all $j\ge 1$ and summing by parts for $K\ge 2$ (the sum is $0$ for $K=1$) gives
    $$\sum_{k=1}^{K-1} (a_k-\delta)h_k = S_{K-1}h_{K-1} - \sum_{k=1}^{K-2} S_k(h_{k+1}-h_k).$$
    Because $0<h_1<h_2<\cdots<h_{K-1}$, the triangle inequality gives
    $$\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}(a_k-\delta)h_k\right| \le C h_{K-1} + C(h_{K-1}-h_1) = C(2h_{K-1}-h_1) < 2C h_{K-1} < 2C h_K.$$
    Adding $|(1-\delta)t|\le t$ from block $0$ and $|(a_K-\delta)\ell_K|\le \ell_K < h_K$ from the unfinished block $K$ yields
    $$|Z(N) - \delta N| \le t + 2C h_{K-1} + \ell_K \le t + (2C+1)h_K.$$
  - Combining with (1) (and $D(\rho)=1$ since $Z(N)\ge t\ge 1$) establishes (3) at every cutoff $tK^3\le N<t(K+1)^3$:
    $$|D_N(x;b)-\delta| \le \frac{(7tp^v+p)K(K+1)/2 + (K+1)W + t + (2C+1)h_K}{N} + \frac{2}{K+1}.$$
  - For $1\le N<t$, both $D_N(x;b)$ and $\delta$ lie in $[0,1]$, so $|D_N(x;b)-\delta|\le 1\le t^{1/3}N^{-1/3}$. Since $N\asymp_t K^3$ and $W = O_b(\log(K+1))$, the right-hand side of (3) is $O_{b,p}(N^{-1/3})$ uniformly in $\delta\in[0,1]$.
- **Worked example and $N=400$ certificate:** For $b=2, p=3, \delta=2/5$, $t=2, v=1$, the first five insertion positions are $2k^3 \in \{2, 16, 54, 128, 250\}$, the floor differences $a_k = \lfloor 2k/5\rfloor - \lfloor 2(k-1)/5\rfloor$ are $0, 0, 1, 0, 1$, the targets $u_k$ are $1/3, 1/3, 0, 1/9, 0$, and the positive coefficients $c_k$ are $1/3, 1, 2/3, 1/9, 5/9$. At $N=400$ (inside block $K=5$, $[250, 432)$), the exact 7-block truncation $x_7$ has discrepancy $D_{400}(x_7) \approx 0.5490277778$ and the infinite tail after $n_8=1024$ shifts each of the first $400$ orbit points by at most $2\cdot 2^{399-1024} = 2^{-624}$, certifying $D_{400}(x)\in [549027/10^6, 549028/10^6]$.
- **Independent numerical sweep:** An independent script (`verify_all_claims.py` and `check_review2.py`–`check_review4.py`) tested (3) directly against exact rational prefixes (including rigorous infinite-tail radii) across $4\,214$ prefix cutoffs $N\ge 1$ for $(b,p,\delta) \in \{(2,3,0), (2,3,1), (2,3,2/5), (2,7,3/8), (10,3,1/2), (3,5,0), (3,5,1), (2,3,\sqrt 2-1)\}$, as well as the Cauchy-rounded scheme ($C=2$). ### 1.5 Endpoints, computability, and transcendental diagonalization (§B3)
- **Endpoints $\delta=0$ and $\delta=1$ and irrationality for all $\delta\in[0,1]$:**
  - At $\delta=0$, $a_k=0$ for all $k\ge 1$, every block $k\ge 1$ is active, $D_N(x;b)=O_{b,p}(N^{-1/3})\to 0$, and $x$ is base-$b$ normal (hence irrational).
  - For any $\delta\in[0,1)$, the empirical orbit measures converge weakly to $(1-\delta)\lambda + \delta\delta_0$, which has a nonzero Lebesgue component $1-\delta>0$, whereas every rational number has an eventually periodic orbit and a finitely supported empirical limit; hence $x$ is irrational.
  - At $\delta=1$, $a_k=1$ for all $k\ge 1$, so $u_k=0$ and $c_k=1$ for all $k\ge 1$. Thus $x=\sum_{k\ge 1} b^{-tk^3}$ has isolated $1$-digits in base $b$ at $tk^3$ separated by strictly growing zero gaps $t(3k^2+3k+1)-1\to\infty$, which cannot be eventually periodic; hence $x$ is irrational while $D_N(x;b)\to 1$.
- **Uniform Cauchy computability:** Replacing exact floors of $k\delta$ (which are discontinuous at rational boundary points) by rational approximants $d_k\in[0,1]$ with $|d_k-\delta|\le 2^{-k}$, partial sums $S_k=\sum_{i=1}^k d_i$, and bits $a_k=\lfloor S_k\rfloor - \lfloor S_{k-1}\rfloor \in \{0,1\}$ gives
  $$\left|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i - k\delta\right| \le |\lfloor S_k\rfloor - S_k| + \sum_{i=1}^k |d_i-\delta| < 1 + \sum_{i=1}^k 2^{-i} < 2,$$
  so (3) holds with $C=2$ using only exact rational floors.
- **Continuum and computable transcendental realizations:** Varying $a_k\in\{0,1\}$ freely along the sparse subsequence $k=2^j$ modifies $Z(N)$ by at most $\sum_{2^j\le K} h_{2^j} \le 7t\sum_{2^j\le K} 4^j \le (28t/3)K^2 = O_t(N^{2/3})$, preserving the $O_{b,p}(N^{-1/3})$ discrepancy rate. At $n_k$, the candidate targets $0$ and $1/q_k$ are separated by $1/q_k$ while the future tail shifts $T^{n_k}x$ by at most $B_0 b^{-h_k}$; since $2B_0 b^{-h_k} < 1/q_k$ for all large $k$, distinct late mask sequences yield distinct reals. For computable diagonalization against the $j$-th real algebraic number $\alpha_j$ at a sufficiently large $k=2^j$ with $B_0 b^{-h_k} < 1/(8q_k)$, computing a rational approximation $r$ to $b^{n_k}\alpha_j$ within $1/(16q_k)$ and selecting $u_k\in\{0,1/q_k\}$ maximizing circle distance to $r$ guarantees $d(u_k, r)\ge 1/(2q_k)$, hence
  $$d(T^{n_k}x, T^{n_k}\alpha_j) \ge d(u_k, r) - d(r, T^{n_k}\alpha_j) - d(T^{n_k}x, u_k) > \frac{1}{2q_k} - \frac{1}{16q_k} - \frac{1}{8q_k} = \frac{5}{16q_k} > 0,$$
  so $x\ne\alpha_j$ for every $j$.

## 2. Mixtures, $(\liminf,\limsup)$ pairs, and invariant-measure cluster sets (`programming.md` §B4)

- **Greedy deficit scheduler for finite mixtures:** For $m$ orbit types with weights $w_i\ge 0$, $\sum_{i=1}^m w_i = 1$, choosing at step $k$ a type $i$ maximizing $kw_i - C_i(k-1)$ selects a positive deficit (since $\sum_{i=1}^m (kw_i - C_i(k-1)) = k - (k-1) = 1 > 0$). Thus the updated overcount $C_i(k)-kw_i = 1 - (kw_i - C_i(k-1)) < 1$ for the selected type, while for $j\ne i$, $C_j(k)-kw_j = C_j(k-1)-(k-1)w_j - w_j \le C_j(k-1)-(k-1)w_j$. By induction $C_r(k)-kw_r \le 1$ for all $r$, and since $\sum_{r=1}^m (C_r(k)-kw_r)=0$, every overcount is also at least $-\sum_{s\ne r}(C_s(k)-kw_s)\ge -(m-1)$. Summation by parts then yields time proportions $w_i + O_{m,t}(K^2/N)$ and discrepancy convergence at rate $O_{b,p,m,P_{\max}}(N^{-1/3})$. (Verified by simulation in `check_review3.py` across $m\in\{2,3,4,5\}$ and in `scripts/phase18_programming.py`.)
- **All pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ of extreme discrepancies:** With $0\le\alpha\le\beta\le 1$, $K_1=2$, $K_{j+1}=K_j^2$, and $\theta_k$ alternating between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ on $[K_j, K_{j+1})$, the rounding bits $a_k = \lfloor\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i\rfloor - \lfloor\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\theta_i\rfloor$ satisfy $|\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i-\theta_i)|<1$. Summation by parts gives $|Z(N) - \sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\theta_k h_k - \theta_K\ell_K| \le t + 3h_K$. Every time-weighted average of $\theta_k$ lies in $[\alpha,\beta]$; at stage boundaries $N = tK_{j+1}^3$, blocks prior to $K_j$ have weight $tK_j^3/N = K_j^{-3}\to 0$, so the weighted average approaches $\alpha$ along odd stages and $\beta$ along even stages. Because $|Z(N+1)/(N+1) - Z(N)/N|\le 1/(N+1)$, the cluster set of $D_N(x;b)$ is the full interval $[\alpha,\beta]$ with $(\liminf D_N,\limsup D_N)=(\alpha,\beta)$.
- **Weak limit sets of empirical measures:**
  - *Necessity:* For empirical measures $\mu_N = N^{-1}\sum_{n<N}\delta_{T^n x}$ on the compact circle $\mathbb T$, telescoping $\int(f\circ T - f)\,d\mu_N = (f(T^N x)-f(x))/N\to 0$ proves $T$-invariance of every weak limit point. In the bounded-Lipschitz metric $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$, $d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\mu_{N+1},\mu_N)\le 2/(N+1)\to 0$, which forces the weak cluster set in the compact metric space $\mathcal P(\mathbb T)$ to be nonempty, compact, and connected.
  - *Sufficiency:*
    1. Any $T$-invariant probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb T$ is approximated in $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$ by a periodic rational cycle law: for a finite BL test net, invariance equates test integrals to the $\mu$-expectation of length-$L$ orbit averages; partitioning the range into small boxes, approximating box weights by rationals $m_i/M$, and concatenating the length-$L$ base-$b$ digit prefixes of the box representatives with multiplicities $m_i$ creates a periodic rational of period dividing $M L$. On all but the last $r$ positions of each $L$-block, the periodic orbit matches the representative's first $r$ digits and hence lies within $b^{-r}$ on $\mathbb T$, bounding the concatenation error by $b^{-r} + 2r/L$.
    2. Any nonempty compact connected metric set $F$ admits a sequence $\mu_j\in F$ with cluster set $F$ and $d(\mu_j,\mu_{j+1})\to 0$, constructed by concatenating finite $1/s$-chains visiting $1/s$-nets of $F$ for $s=1,2,\ldots$ (returning to a basepoint between nets).
    3. Programming a point of the periodic cycle $\pi_j$ (with $d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\pi_j,\mu_j)<1/j$ and period $P_j$) on each block $k\in[K_j,K_{j+1})$ where $K_{j+1}\ge\max(K_j^2, P_{j+1})$ bounds the per-block BL sum error by $2P_j\le 2k$, summing to $O(K^2)$ through $N$. With the cubic tail bound, $\mu_N$ tracks the time-weighted convex combination of $\pi_{j-1}$ and $\pi_j$ within $O_{b,t}(N^{-1/3})$ in $d_{\mathrm{BL}}$. Since $d_{\mathrm{BL}}(\pi_{j-1},\pi_j)\to 0$ and $(K_j/K_{j+1})^3 \le K_j^{-3}\to 0$ at stage boundaries, the cluster set of $\mu_N$ is exactly $F$.
- **Scalar continuity of $D(\cdot)$ under weak convergence (including atoms):** If $\mu_i\to\mu$ weakly on $\mathbb T$, rotating a finite equally spaced grid of mesh $\le\eta$ so that all grid endpoints avoid the at most countably many atoms of $\mu$ ensures $\mu_i(J)\to\mu(J)$ on each of the finitely many grid arcs $J$ by the portmanteau theorem. Enlarging any arc $I$ to a grid arc $J^+$ with $|J^+|\le |I|+2\eta$ gives $(\mu_i-\lambda)(I)\le (\mu_i-\lambda)(J^+)+2\eta$, so $|D(\mu_i)-\max_{J\text{ grid}}(\mu_i-\lambda)(J)|\le 2\eta$ for all $i$ and for $\mu$. Letting $i\to\infty$ then $\eta\downarrow 0$ proves $D(\mu_i)\to D(\mu)$.

## 3. Grouped series and block certificates (`certificate.md`)

- **Grouped series identity:** For an absolutely summable family $(a_i)$ partitioned into disjoint finite or countable groups $G_j$ with $\sum_{i\in G_j} a_i = c_j b^{-m_j} + e_j$ and $\sum_j |c_j|b^{-m_j}<\infty$, $\sum_j |e_j|<\infty$, Fubini/associativity for absolutely summable series yields $x = \sum_j c_j b^{-m_j} + z$ with $z=\sum_j e_j$.
- **Upper block certificate:** For disjoint shift intervals $B_i\subset[0,N)$ with uncovered count $U=N-\sum_i |B_i|$, rational block models $y_i$ of block discrepancy $\Delta_i$, and at most $E$ covered exceptions where the circle error exceeds $\epsilon$, $(\epsilon+h)$-arc enlargement and complementation give
  $$D_N(x) \le \frac{U + E + \sum_i |B_i|\Delta_i}{N} + 2\epsilon.$$
  When $y_i$ has period $P$ and full-period discrepancy $d$, splitting $|B_i| = qP + r$ ($0\le r<P$) bounds the unnormalized block count error by $qPd + r \le |B_i|d + \min(P,|B_i|)$.
- **Necessary bounded-denominator inequality and converse:** If at $H_Q(N)$ positions the model lies in the set $F_Q$ of reduced circle fractions in $[0,1)$ of denominator $\le Q$ and lies within $\epsilon$ of $T^n x$ apart from $E$ exceptions, then by the pigeonhole principle some center $c\in F_Q$ accounts for at least $(H_Q(N)-E)/|F_Q|$ good samples, all contained in an arc of length $\min(1,2\epsilon+2h)$ around $c$, giving
  $$D_N(x) \ge \frac{H_Q(N)-E}{N|F_Q|} - 2\epsilon.$$
  Conversely, covering all but $U\le H_Q(N)+\eta N$ positions by blocks with $\Delta_i\le\eta$ and at most $E\le\eta N$ exceptions of radius $\epsilon\le\eta$ yields $D_N(x) \le H_Q(N)/N + 5\eta$.
- **Counterexamples in `certificate.md`:**
  - *Pointwise rational approximation without orbit coherence:* $1/p^{\lfloor\log_p(n+2)\rfloor}$ has denominator tending to infinity on a single prime and converges to $0$ (mirroring the orbit of $0$ with $D_N\to 1$), because changing the rational state at every shift breaks the block orbit relation $y_{a+j}=T^j y_a$.
  - *Raw truncation obstruction:* For pairs $+2^{-k}/3$ at position $k$ and $-2^{-k}/3$ at position $2k$ ($k\ge 1$), every completed pair sums to $0$ and $x=0$, yet at raw position cutoff $n$ the completed pairs $1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ cancel and the unfinished terms $\lfloor n/2\rfloor < k \le n$ have shifted sum
    $$2^n \sum_{k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^n \frac{2^{-k}}{3} = \frac{2^{n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor}-1}{3} = \frac{2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}-1}{3},$$
    whose circle distance from $0$ is $1/3$ whenever $\lceil n/2\rceil$ is odd, i.e., $n\equiv 1,2\pmod 4$. (Verified in `check_review2.py` for $n=1,\ldots,19$.)

## 4. Liouville realization at arbitrary discrepancy (`liouville-realization.md`)

- **Periodic target words and union-of-cells bound:**
  - $V_k$ concatenates all $b^k$ words of length $k$ in lexicographic order, of length $m_k = k b^k$. With $d_k\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q$, $|d_k-\delta|\le 1/k$, and $a_k=\lfloor k d_k\rfloor \in \{0,\ldots,k\}$, we have $|a_k/k-\delta|\le |a_k/k-d_k|+|d_k-\delta| < 1/k + 1/k = 2/k$.
  - $W_k$ concatenates $a_k$ copies of $0^{m_k}$ and $k-a_k$ copies of $V_k$, of length $P_k = k m_k = k^2 b^k$.
  - For any fixed depth $l\le k$, at each of the $k-l+1$ internal positions of a constituent $k$-word, the distribution of $l$-words across $V_k$ is exactly uniform ($b^{k-l}$ occurrences of each $l$-word per offset) and across each $0^{m_k}$ block is concentrated on $0^l$. Discarding the $l-1$ boundary offsets per $k$-word (a fraction $(l-1)/k$ of the period $P_k$), the surviving $(1-(l-1)/k)P_k$ positions have exact $l$-word distribution $(a_k/k)\delta_{0^l} + (1-a_k/k)\lambda_l$.
  - Because $u_k=0.\overline{W_k}$ uses the terminating expansion $0$ when $a_k=k$ and contains $0^k$ (via $V_k$) when $a_k<k$, no point in the periodic orbit of $u_k$ has an eventually maximal all-$(b-1)$ tail. Thus depth-$l$ cylinder membership on $[0,1)$ coincides with the first $l$ cyclic digits of $W_k$, and on **any union** $J$ of depth-$l$ base-$b$ cells the periodic model count proportion differs from $\mu_\delta(J) = \delta\delta_0(J)+(1-\delta)\lambda(J)$ by at most $(l-1)/k + |a_k/k-\delta| \le (l-1)/k + 2/k$. (Verified exhaustively in `check_review.py` across $b\in\{2,3\}$, $k\in\{2,3,4\}$, $1\le l\le k$, and $\delta\in\{0,1/3,1/2,1\}$.)
- **All-prefix discrepancy convergence $D_N(x;b)\to\delta$:**
  - With $n_0=0$ and $n_k=b^{(k+2)!}$ ($k\ge 1$), for $n_K\le N<n_{K+1}$ the sum of all incomplete period lengths up to plateau $K$ is at most $\sum_{k=1}^K P_k = \sum_{k=1}^K k^2 b^k = o(b^{(K+2)!}) = o(n_K) = o(N)$, and the initial segment $[0,n_1)$ has length $O(1)$. Hence for each fixed $l$, the modeled prefix measure $\mu_N^{\mathrm{mod}}$ converges to $\mu_\delta$ uniformly on all unions of depth-$l$ cells.
  - Covering any arc $I$ by depth-$l$ cells with excess length at most $2b^{-l}$ gives $\mu_N^{\mathrm{mod}}(I)-|I| \le \delta + 2b^{-l} + o(1)$, while the single cell $[0,b^{-l})$ gives $D(\mu_N^{\mathrm{mod}})\ge \delta(1-b^{-l})-o(1)$. Letting $N\to\infty$ then $l\to\infty$ yields $D(\mu_N^{\mathrm{mod}})\to\delta$.
  - Discarding at most $H(K+1)=o(N)$ shifts within $H$ places before an insertion bounds the positive real tail on all remaining shifts by $\sum_{m>n+H} b^{n-m} = b^{-H}/(b-1)$, transferring the limit $D_N(x;b)\to\delta$ and weak convergence $\mu_N\to\mu_\delta$ to the true expansion via the circle comparison.
- **Current-state denominator cancellation and Liouville property at all $\delta\in[0,1]$:**
  - At step $k$, $R_k = b^{n_k-n_{k-1}}R_{k-1} + c_k$ satisfies $\{R_k\}=u_k$, so $R_k = m_k + u_k$ for an integer $m_k=\lfloor R_k\rfloor$. All denominator factors from earlier targets $u_1,\ldots,u_{k-1}$ cancel in the sum $R_k$, so $x_k = b^{-n_k}R_k$ has reduced denominator $q_k$ dividing $b^{n_k}\operatorname{den}(u_k)\mid b^{n_k}(b^{P_k}-1)$, and hence $q_k\le b^{n_k}(b^{P_k}-1)$. (Verified directly in `check_review4.py` with pairwise coprime target denominators.)
  - Because $c_k\in(0,1]$ is strictly positive at every step (including when $u_k=0$ at the $\delta=1$ endpoint, where $c_k=1$), the partial sums $x_k$ are strictly increasing ($x_1<x_2<\cdots<x$) and bounded above by $1/(b-1)$, forcing $q_k\to\infty$ and $0 < x - x_k \le b^{1-n_{k+1}}/(b-1)$.
  - Since $n_{k+1}/(n_k+P_k) = b^{(k+3)!}/(b^{(k+2)!}+k^2 b^k)\to\infty$, for every fixed $h\ge 1$ and all large $k$ we have $0 < |x-x_k| < q_k^{-h}$, establishing the Liouville property (and transcendence) for every $\delta\in[0,1]$, including $\delta=0$ and $\delta=1$.
