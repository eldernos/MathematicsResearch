# Review of the Phase 18 translation and summation notes

Independent review of `notes/phase18/translations.md` and `notes/phase18/summation.md`. The files were read from the parent checkout `MathematicsResearch-phase18`, where they were untracked on top of `dd8545b`, on 25 November 2026. The source files were not edited. This review checks the written proofs by hand, checks them against the repository theorems they cite, and runs small exact computations looking for counterexamples. It is not a kernel check of any new claim.

**Verdict.** No mathematical error was found in either note. Every main claim survives: the finite circle comparison, the sparse signed translation bounds, the determinism tests, the finite compatibility theorem, the BBP and logarithm ray decompositions, the tail obstruction, and the infinite-prime grid theorem with rate $O((\log N)^{-1/2})$. Five qualifications should be applied before integration. Q1 and Q2 fix statements that are too broad as written. Q3 to Q5 concern links and wording.

## Qualifications to apply

**Q1. Polylogarithm rays need primes coprime to the radix (summation.md, residue section).** The logarithm partition picks "the least odd prime" above earlier data. That is safe for $b=2$. For $\operatorname{Li}_s(1/b)$ with general $b$, the same rule can pick a prime dividing $b$. With $b=10$, the second ray is $r=2$, $p=5$, and $5\mid10$. The finite compatibility theorem then does not apply to that ray, because its denominators are no longer coprime to the radix. Fix: in the $\operatorname{Li}_s$ sentence, choose each $p_i$ to be the least prime with $p_i\nmid b$ that exceeds the earlier data. The disjointness and exhaustion proof is unchanged.

**Q2. MW-02 does not certify normality in this setting (summation.md, positive controls).** The sentence "For masks use the hypotheses of the masked theorem, e.g. retaining all sufficiently late pure powers of a specified off-radix prime under the conditions in MW-02" suggests that MW-02 supplies the missing normality certificate. MW-02 proves only
$$
\limsup_N D_N \le 1-\frac{1}{p^h-1},
$$
which is $1-1/(p-1)$ when all late powers are kept. It gives normality only when $p=2$ and $b$ is odd. For the binary logarithm every off-radix prime is odd, so MW-02 never yields normality there. It gives a bound below one, for example $1/2$ at $p=3$. Recommended wording: say that MW-02 bounds the discrepancy below one, and that normality of a masked row selection is open except where the unsigned full-support theorem or the $p=2$, odd-$b$ case applies.

**Q3. Some links had no target at review time.** `notes/phase18/programming.md`, `classical.md` and `README.md` are not in the parent checkout. `programming.md` exists only in worktree `t3-40fbe287…` at commit `61d8991`. I read that version. Its §B3 proves weak convergence to $(1-\delta)\lambda+\delta\,\delta_0$, which supports the cubic-family entropy claim in translations.md (see V7). The links must be checked again after integration. `classical.md` and the README were not reviewed.

**Q4. Ambiguous sentence about bounded overlaps (translations.md, sparse signed integers).** "A sufficient qualitative condition for bounded overlaps is upper density zero of the occupied positions" can be read as saying that density zero implies bounded overlaps $a_h\le A$. That reading is false: density zero says nothing about how large the $a_h$ are. The intended statement is true: **under** bounded overlaps, upper density zero of the occupied positions implies that the discrepancy difference is $o(1)$. Take $H_N(L)\le L\cdot\#\{\text{occupied }h\le N+L\}=o(N)$ with $L$ fixed and $\epsilon=Ab^{-L}/(b-1)$, then let $L\to\infty$. Rephrase accordingly.

**Q5. Undefined term and unclear predictor direction (translations.md, determinism section).**
- "Radix-primary rationals" is not defined. The intended class is the rationals whose reduced denominator divides a power of $b$. A rational has $M_N\to0$ exactly when its eventually periodic orbit ends at $0$, that is, when $b^Kz\in\mathbb Z$ for some $K$. That class should be named explicitly.
- The note describes Rauzy's predictor as using "finite lookahead". I could not confirm the direction of Rauzy's prediction window from the 1976 paper. The zero-noise class does not depend on the direction, because the entropy of a stationary process is the same under time reversal. The wording should still be checked against the source.

## Optional strengthening found during review

Binary determinism and $M_N\to0$ also follow from **zero density of digit changes**, without requiring zero density of ones. If digit changes have density zero, then for each fixed $l$ the proportion of shifts whose next $l$ digits are constant tends to one. At such a shift, $T^nz$ lies within $2^{-l}$ of $0$ or of $1$, which is the same point on the circle. Hence $M_N(z)\le2^{-l}+o(1)$. With this lemma the bullet would also cover the Phase 16 benchmark $L_{(-1)^{\omega}}$, whose paper bound is bit-transition density $O(\sqrt N\log N/N)$ rather than density zero of ones. In radix $b>2$ the lemma only places limit measures on the fixed points $d/(b-1)$. Entropy zero still follows, but the mean-displacement conclusion holds only when the constant runs use the digits $0$ and $b-1$. This is a reviewer deduction and was not checked further.

## Scope verified

The following were checked line by line on paper. Repository theorem hypotheses were compared where they are cited.

- **V1. Finite circle comparison.** The bound $|D_N(u)-D_N(v)|\le\min(1,E/N+2\epsilon)$ is proved by arc enlargement, with complementation handling deficits. The orientation remark for arcs that wrap through zero is correct. The Markov bound is correct: the optimum is $\epsilon=\sqrt{M/2}$, giving $2\sqrt{2M}$. So is the equivalence "$M_N\to0$ if and only if $E_N(\epsilon)/N\to0$ for every $\epsilon>0$". 3000 random trials, including atoms at $0$ and $1/2$, found no violation (largest excess $6\times10^{-17}$, which is rounding).
- **V2. Sparse signed translations.** Each source charges only shifts in $[m_j-w_j+1-L,\,m_j)$. Finitely many nonpositive positions occur, since $\sum_{h\le0}a_hb^{-h}<\infty$ and each $a_h$ is an integer. The uncharged error bound $U_{n,L}$ and the bounded-overlap choice of $\epsilon$ are correct. The block-coding counterexample with $m_j=2^j$ is valid. The $k(k+1)$ example is valid: about $O(\sqrt{N+L})$ positions matter, and with $L=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$ the rate is $O(\log N/\sqrt N)$. The proofs of irrationality, of $D_N(z)\to1$ and of the factorial Liouville bound $b^{1-(k+1)!}/(b-1)$ are correct. For a random binary $x$, the measured $|D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)|$ at $N=256,1024,4096$ was $0.016,0.005,0.0015$, inside the bound. $D_N(z)$ was $0.76,0.85,0.91$, increasing toward one.
- **V3. Lifting entropy from the circle.** The proof is correct. The expansion map has fibres of size at most two. Symbolic weak limits project onto circle weak limits, and every circle weak limit is such a projection, by compactness. A factor map with finite fibres preserves entropy.
- **V4. The orbit of $1/3$.** Its limiting extreme discrepancy is $2/3$, confirmed exactly ($D_{1000}=2/3$). The conclusion that universal preservation need not preserve discrepancy is correct.
- **V5. Tightness criterion.** The cutoff argument gives each limit mass at least $1-\eta$ on $F_\eta$. A countable union of cycles carries only measures of entropy zero, by ergodic decomposition. Correct.
- **V6. Character Lambert scope.** Phase 16 Theorem 3.2 states that the proportion of 1-bits on $[1,N]$ is $O_\chi(1/\sqrt{\log N})$ for every nonprincipal real character. This refers to the actual binary digits, with carries included, so the density-zero bullet applies. It gives determinism and $M_N\to0$. Together with Theorem 3.3 (disjunctivity), $L_\chi$ is a disjunctive deterministic number. That is consistent with the bullet's phrase "even for a disjunctive expansion". The input remains a Phase 16 **paper** theorem (Markov's inequality plus the Lean arrival-sum identity). I did not re-derive it.
- **V7. Signed-shell entropy.** The hypotheses match the Lean theorems: $p$ prime, $p\nmid b$, bounded integer phases. The Lean results are `nonzero_shells_uniform` (SignedShellDiscrepancy.lean:24), which gives uniformity relative to $N$; `zero_shells_miss_interior` (line 115); and the irrationality criterion. From these, every subsequential circle limit is $\theta\delta_0+(1-\theta)\lambda$. At the ends of nonzero shells the nonzero share is at least $(p-1)/p$. So infinitely many nonzero phases force a limit of entropy at least $\frac{p-1}{p}\log b>0$, and only the rational members are deterministic. The cubic family claim matches programming.md §B3. Its $\delta=1$ member has ones only at $tk^3$, so the density-zero bullet gives $M_N\to0$.
- **V8. Arithmetic of the universal test.** The difference of two jointly normal ray components is normal, and translating by its negative gives $0$. Correct.
- **V9. Logarithm partition.** The inner-sum identity $(2^{-r}+\alpha_{2^r,p})/r$ is correct. The normality inputs are Bailey–Crandall for coprime bases, which is external, or the single-ray proof. The least-unused construction is disjoint and exhaustive. An exact simulation confirmed coverage of $1..3000$, with rays $(1,3),(2,5),(4,7),(5,11),(6,13),\ldots$.
- **V10. Finite compatibility theorem.** The valuation argument gives the $p_i$-exponent of the reduced denominator as exactly $s_ij_i-v_{p_i}(d_i)$. Denominators grow and are $O(n^{\sum s_i})$. The per-block step is **kernel-checked**: `smooth_interval_block` (SupportDiscrepancy.lean:35), with the estimate `fixed_support_orbit_estimate` (SmoothOrbit.lean:207). Its hypotheses are reduced denominator $\ge M$, denominator dividing $c^t$, denominator $\le B^d$, the relation $P(n+i)=b^iP(n)$ on the block, and a tail tending to zero. These hold with $c=\prod p_i$ over the nonzero axes (coprime to $b$ by hypothesis), $B=\lfloor\sqrt M\rfloor$ and $d=2\sum s_i+1$. The following steps remain paper arguments: insertion counting, $O(\log M)$ bad blocks, summing over dyadic shells, rational dilation (`normal_mul_nat_iff`), and the Weyl step through `fourier_of_normal`. All are correct.
- **V11. Residue-preserving rays and BBP.** Primes $p\equiv1\bmod H$ keep each ray in its residue class. The $u^4+1$ alternative for $H=8$ is valid. For BBP ($b=16$, $H=8$, classes $1,4,5,6$, numerators $4,-2,-1,-1$), $n_{ij}=(rp^j-a)/8$ is a nonnegative integer because $r\ge a$. The coefficient is $c_a/(rp^j)$ and $p\nmid16$. The theorem applies with $s=1$. An exact simulation confirmed exhaustion of the four classes up to 3000, with rays $(1,17),(4,41),(5,73),(6,89),\ldots$.
- **V12. Positive controls.** The $\{3,5\}$ rows are positive multiples of affine Stoneham components. Normality of the full sum follows from the Lean unsigned finite-support theorem. Correct, subject to Q2 for masks.
- **V13. Rational sum of jointly uniform terms.** The recursion gives $e_{i-1}/3<e_i<2e_{i-1}/3$, so the series sums exactly to $R$. Its finite subfamilies are jointly uniform by V10 with $j\ge1$. A numerical run with $R=1$ and primes $3,\ldots,23$, taking midpoint choices, gave residual $2^{-8}$ after 8 steps, as expected. The computability recipe terminates because each target interval has nonempty interior.
- **V14. Tails.** The bound $\overline M(E_k)+\overline M(E_{k+1})\ge1/4$ holds because $\int\|t\|\,dt=1/4$. Universal preservers form a group: closure under negation uses $x-z=-((-x)+z)$. Hence at most one tail is a universal preserver when all consecutive finite sums are normal. Correct.
- **V15. Finite and dynamic transfer.** Both inequalities, $2b^{N-1}|x-X|$ and $(N-G)/N+(d+2\epsilon)G/N+E/N$, are correct, including for an unfinished observation window.
- **V16. Infinite-prime grid theorem and rate.** Each part was checked:
  - $\mathcal P$ is infinite: $6\prod p-1$ has a new prime factor $\equiv2\bmod3$.
  - The insertion cutoff is exactly $p^j\le L$, and the reduced denominator is $q=\prod p^{\lfloor\log_pL\rfloor}$ with $q\le L^L$.
  - $3\nmid\operatorname{ord}_Q(2)$, because $3\nmid\ell(\ell-1)$ for every $\ell\equiv2\bmod3$.
  - $v_3(2^t-1)=1$, by lifting the exponent from $4-1$.
  - The subgroup $1+3\mathbb Z$ modulo $3^e$ has order $3^{e-1}$, so each coset is one residue class modulo $3Q$ and forms an equally spaced grid. The full-period discrepancy is at most $3^{1-e}<9/L$.
  - Discarding $n<\sqrt N$ keeps $L\asymp\sqrt{\log N}$. There are $O(\sqrt{\log N})$ insertions up to $2N$.
  - After removing $\lceil\log_2N\rceil$ positions before each insertion, the tail is at most $2^{-\lceil\log_2N\rceil}$. The note states $2^{1-\lceil\log_2N\rceil}$, which is weaker and still valid.
  - Unfinished periods cost $N^{o(1)}$ in total. Together these give $D_N(Y)=O((\log N)^{-1/2})$ for every $N$.

  Exact full-period discrepancies of $2^n a/q$ for the actual moduli $q$ at $L=9,11,17,26,27,29$ were $0.18,0.083,0.069,0.019,0.0065,0.0019$. The corresponding bounds are $1/3,1/3,1/3,1/3,1/9,1/9$. For finite combinations of the $Y_p$, the $p$-part of $\operatorname{ord}_Q(2)$ is bounded by $\max_\ell v_p(\ell-1)$ over the fixed remaining axes, and lifting the exponent bounds $v_p(2^t-1)$. Exact checks at $p=5$ with the axes $7$ and $11$ gave $w=v_5(2^t-1)=2$ for every exponent tested, with discrepancies below $5^{w-E}$. The dynamic truncation bound $2^N|Y-X_{k(N)}|\le2^{-N}$ is correct.
- **V17. Approaching the full logarithm.** The least omitted integer is the least omitted prime $l$. The two-sided bound $2^{-l}/l\le\log2-X_P\le2^{1-l}/l$ holds, and so does the stated criterion. Numerically, $(\log2-X_P)\,l\,2^l$ was $1.25,1.20,1.05,1.06$ for $P=\{2\},\{2,3\},\{2,3,5\},\{2,\ldots,11\}$, inside $[1,2]$.

## Scope of the grid theorem (accurate, worth stating)

The grid proof for $Y$ needs the prime $3$ and the fact that every other prime in $\mathcal P$ is $\equiv2\bmod3$. Neither note claims normality of $Y-Y_3$ or joint uniformity of $Y$ with any $Y_p$, and neither follows from the method. The method gives no conclusion at other primes, and stating this limit explicitly would help readers.

> **Correction (publication wave 2, [issue #1](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/issues/1)).** An earlier version of this paragraph inferred from primes $\ell\equiv1\bmod5^k$ in $\mathcal P$ that $v_5(\operatorname{ord}_Q2)$ is unbounded, and concluded that no grid survives at $p=5$. That inference is unsupported. The order of $2$ modulo $\ell$ divides $\ell-1$, so a congruence $\ell\equiv1\bmod5^k$ does not make $5^k$ divide the order. For example, $431$ is prime, $431\equiv2\bmod3$ and $431\equiv1\bmod5$, while the order of $2$ modulo $431$ is $43$. Even an unbounded order valuation would need comparison with the growing pole depth before it could rule out increasingly fine grids. The inference and the conclusion are withdrawn. The normality theorem for $Y$ uses only the protected ternary grid, and its proof is unaffected. [Phase 19 handoff](../../phase19/publication-handoff.md) · [Grid analysis](../../phase19/grids.md)
