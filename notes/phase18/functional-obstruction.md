# Testing functional identities before specializing the variable

A finite identity among functions would avoid some of the completion costs of rearranging a fixed Taylor series. There is an obstruction even when products, derivatives and rational-function weights are allowed.

**Support criterion.** Let $f_i(X)$ be finitely many formal power series in characteristic zero. Suppose the number of occupied exponents through $N$ in each is $O((\log(2+N))^A)$ for some fixed $A$. Every member $F$ of the differential algebra they generate over the rational-function field has a nonzero polynomial multiplier $Q$ such that $QF$ has only $O((\log(2+N))^B)$ occupied nonnegative exponents through $N$, for some finite $B$ depending on the expression. Here the coefficient field may be taken as $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb C$, and derivatives are ordinary formal derivatives. This permits poles at zero before clearing the rational functions.

Indeed the product and quotient rules for rational coefficients write each expression as a finite sum of monomials in finitely many derivatives of the $f_i$, with rational-function coefficients. Clear their fixed denominators. A derivative of fixed order shifts the support by a bounded amount. A product of $r$ such derivatives has at most the product of the $r$ support counts through $N+O(1)$ possible contributing tuples. Finite polynomial shifts and finite sums preserve a polylogarithmic bound. The same proof applies to any specified expression for which these products of counts are $o(N)$.

**Applications.** For each of the following target functions and every nonzero polynomial $Q$, the coefficients of $QF$ are nonzero at **all** sufficiently large indices. Consequently none belongs to this differential algebra of lacunary rays.

* For $F=\operatorname{Li}_s(X)$ at integer order $s\ge1$, write $Q=\sum_t q_tX^t$. At $n>\deg Q$ the coefficient is the value at $n$ of $\sum_t q_t/(n-t)^s$. A nonzero $q_t$ gives a pole at $t$ shared by no other summand. This rational function has finitely many zeros. This includes $-\log(1-X)$.
* For the BBP generating function $F=\sum_{n\ge0}X^n\sum_{a} c_a/(8n+a)$ with $(a,c_a)=(1,4),(4,-2),(5,-1),(6,-1)$, the poles of the shifted coefficient functions occur at $t-a/8$. These are pairwise distinct as $(t,a)$ varies, so a pole with nonzero coefficient survives. The value at $X=1/16$ is $\pi$. [Original BBP identity](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-97-00856-9)
* For $e^X$, multiply the coefficient by $n!$ to obtain $\sum_t q_t n(n-1)\cdots(n-t+1)$, a nonzero polynomial by its highest degree.
* For $F=(1-X)^\gamma$ with rational noninteger $\gamma$, each coefficient $u_n$ is nonzero and $u_{n-t}/u_n$ is a rational function of $n$ for fixed $t$. The resulting sum after multiplying by $Q$ could vanish identically only if $QF$ were polynomial. This is impossible: a rational function has integral order of zero or pole at $X=1$, while $(1-X)^\gamma$ has order $\gamma$. Equivalently raise to the denominator of $\gamma$ and compare integer valuations. This includes binomial generating functions for algebraic irrationals after rational substitution in the argument, subject to checking the actual transformed function.
* For $F=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n-1}X^n/(n^3\binom{2n}{n})$, the value at one is $2\zeta(3)/5$. Again the quotients $u_{n-t}/u_n$ are rational functions of $n$. The function is irrational over the rational-function field in the sense $F\notin\mathbb C(X)$: as $X$ approaches $-4$ from inside the interval of convergence the series remains bounded, since $|u_n|4^n\asymp n^{-5/2}$ by Stirling. But the terms of its second derivative there have constant sign and their magnitudes at $-4$ are of order $n^{-1/2}$, making that derivative unbounded. A rational function bounded near a finite point is analytic there after removal of any removable singularity. Thus every nonzero multiplier again leaves a cofinite coefficient support. [Apéry series and its generalization](https://www.numdam.org/article/BSMF_1981__109__269_0.pdf)

In particular finitely many geometric Stoneham generating functions, even with independent axes, polynomial combinations and arbitrary rational-function prefactors, cannot furnish these functional identities. A pole or order calculation for the specified *function* cannot exclude an identity only at a particular number such as $X=1/2$, a different analytic substitution, infinite summation, a nonsparse remainder or division by a lacunary generator. The infinite ray representations are consistent with the obstruction. [Logarithm and BBP rays](summation.md)

A functional equation that actually transforms an infinite group is
$$
 \operatorname{Li}_s(X)+\operatorname{Li}_s(-X)=2^{1-s}\operatorname{Li}_s(X^2).
$$
Comparison of coefficients proves it for $|X|<1$. Iterating splits the original series according to the power of 2 dividing its indices. On a fixed odd part it still leaves the off-radix denominator of that odd part. Likewise the ray generator $H_p(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-sj}X^{p^j}$ satisfies $H_p(X)=X+p^{-s}H_p(X^p)$. These identities explain the separate components but give no global bound for their remainder.
