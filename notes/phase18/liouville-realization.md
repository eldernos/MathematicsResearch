# Prescribing a discrepancy limit and Liouville approximation together

For every integer $b\ge2$ and real $\delta\in[0,1]$ there is a Liouville number in an explicitly programmed positive rational expansion whose full extreme discrepancy tends to $\delta$. The construction is computable uniformly from a Cauchy name for $\delta$. This is a different schedule from the cubic construction. It supplies transcendental realizations even where the arithmetic nature of the particular cubic example is undecided.

## Periodic target states

For $k\ge1$ concatenate all $b^k$ words of length $k$ over the base-$b$ alphabet in lexicographic order, obtaining a word $V_k$ of length $m_k=k b^k$. Choose a rational $d_k\in[0,1]$ within $1/k$ of $\delta$ and set $a_k=\lfloor k d_k\rfloor$. Form a word $W_k$ by concatenating $a_k$ copies of the all-zero word of length $m_k$ followed by $k-a_k$ copies of $V_k$. Its length is $P_k=k^2b^k$, and $|a_k/k-\delta|\le2/k$. The rational $u_k=0.\overline{W_k}$ in base $b$ has denominator dividing $b^{P_k}-1$. Take its representative on the circle in $[0,1)$.

For a fixed length $l\le k$, at each of the $k-l+1$ positions internal to a $k$-word the multiset of $l$-words across the enumeration is exactly uniform. On the zero copies the internal positions have word $0^l$. In a full cyclic period of $W_k$, discard the last $l-1$ positions of each constituent $k$-word, a proportion at most $(l-1)/k$. The surviving word distribution is exactly the mixture with zero weight $a_k/k$ and uniform weight $1-a_k/k$. Thus on **any union** of base-$b$ cells of depth $l$ the periodic model has count proportion differing from $\mu_\delta=\delta\delta_0+(1-\delta)\lambda$ by at most $(l-1)/k+2/k$. This includes $\delta=0,1$. All-zero periods use the terminating expansion of zero. Other periods contain a zero digit, so their orbit avoids ambiguity from an eventually maximal tail.

## Inserting the states

Set $n_0=0$, $n_k=b^{(k+2)!}$ for $k\ge1$. From $R_0=0$ use the rational recurrence $R_k=b^{n_k-n_{k-1}}R_{k-1}+c_k$ with $c_k$ the positive representative in $(0,1]$ of $u_k-b^{n_k-n_{k-1}}R_{k-1}$ modulo one. Then $x=\sum c_k b^{-n_k}$ has the prescribed rational state at every $n_k$ in its shifted prefix. For $n_k\le n<n_{k+1}$ this model follows the cyclic word $W_k$ exactly. In a prefix ending at $N$ with $n_K\le N<n_{K+1}$, all incomplete period costs together are at most $\sum_{k\le K}P_k=o(n_K)$. The fixed initial interval before $n_1$ costs $O(1)$. Consequently for any fixed $l$ the union-of-cells estimate for the whole modeled prefix tends to zero, uniformly over the unions: discard finitely many early plateaus, then use the displayed period bound on every later one, including the unfinished plateau.

Here is a direct interval-discrepancy deduction. An interval can be covered by whole depth-$l$ cells with excess total length at most $2b^{-l}$ (use the full circle if necessary). On any such union $J$, $\mu_\delta(J)-|J|\le\delta$. Complements handle deficits, and the single cell $[0,b^{-l})$ gives a lower bound $\delta(1-b^{-l})-o(1)$. The modeled discrepancy therefore tends to $\delta$ on letting $l$ grow. To pass to the true expansion, for fixed $H$ discard shifts within $H$ places before an insertion. Through $N$ these cost at most $H(K+1)=o(N)$ for fixed $H$ and large $N$ (late gaps exceed $H$). At the other shifts the positive real-tail error is at most $b^{-H}/(b-1)$ by summing over distinct future positions greater than $n+H$. Apply the finite comparison and then let $H$ grow. This also gives weak convergence to $\mu_\delta$ on the circle. [Circle comparison](translations.md)

## Rational approximations and computability

The $k$th partial sum $x_k=b^{-n_k} R_k$ has reduced denominator $q_k\le b^{n_k}(b^{P_k}-1)$. This bound involves only the **current** state denominator. The positive tail is at most $b^{1-n_{k+1}}/(b-1)$. If $x=a/q$ were rational, the distinct value $x_k$ would be at distance at least $1/(q q_k)$. The tail violates this for all large $k$. The $q_k$ tend to infinity since the increasing distinct $x_k$ stay bounded. For every fixed $h$ the tail is eventually less than $q_k^{-h}$ because $n_{k+1}/(n_k+P_k)\to\infty$. This proves Liouville approximation and hence transcendence, including at both endpoints. A computable approximation $d_k$ with the given tolerance suffices. The recurrence uses exact rational arithmetic and the future tail has an explicit bound, so no test of equality of an unknown real with a rational is required. [Positive programming recurrence](programming.md)

The same proof permits other finite words with a prescribed limiting distribution and lengths $P_k=o(n_k)$ for which $\sum_{i\le k}P_i=o(n_k)$. The long gaps are compatible with intermediate discrepancy because each long rational plateau already approximates the desired mixture. A single zero or uniform plateau occupying almost the entire prefix would instead force a corresponding subsequential endpoint.
