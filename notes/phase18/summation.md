# Geometric rays, tails and infinite sums

Write $T(x)=bx\bmod 1$, with $b\ge2$ an integer. Joint uniformity means convergence of the empirical measures of $(T^nx_1,\ldots,T^nx_h)$ to Haar measure on the full torus as the unrestricted prefix length tends to infinity. Lean verifies the fixed-prime Taylor partition, the existence of positive rational weights giving a prescribed total, and the fixed-support orbit input. The joint-ray and growing-support conclusions have written proofs. ## Partitioning the logarithm

The integrated geometric series gives $\log 2=\sum_{m\ge1}2^{-m}/m$. For an odd prime $p$ write $m=rp^j$, $p\nmid r$. Each inner sum is $(2^{-r}+\alpha_{2^r,p})/r$, with $\alpha_{B,p}=\sum_{j\ge1}1/(p^j B^{p^j})$. Stoneham normality in base $2^r$, equivalence of normality in power-related bases, and rational affine invariance give binary normality of each component. Equivalently use the single-ray argument below. Positivity justifies the partition.

For a partition with stronger finite compatibility, let $r_i$ be the least unused positive integer and take the least odd prime $p_i$ greater than all bases $r_j$ up to $i$ and all previous primes. The new ray $r_ip_i^j$ is disjoint from previous rays. Its initial point is unused, and every later point has the prime factor $p_i$ which divides neither an older initial point nor an older ray prime. Least-unused initial points are strictly increasing. Any omitted integer would bound infinitely many of them. Thus every integer occurs exactly once.

**Finite compatibility theorem.** More generally take any fixed finite collection of rays of distinct primes $p_i\nmid b$, with insertion positions $n_{ij}=(r_i p_i^j-a_i)/H$, all nonnegative integers, $H,r_i>0$, and real series with coefficients $c_i/p_i^{sj}$ for fixed nonzero rationals $c_i$ and positive integer $s$. Different orders $s_i\ge1$ also work. Every nonzero rational combination (some coefficient nonzero) is normal, and the ray components are jointly uniform in base $b$.

To see this, clear all fixed rational factors in the proposed combination to give integer coefficients $d_i$ and omit zero axes. For $n$ large, the largest index $j_i$ inserted on an axis satisfies $p_i^{j_i}\asymp_i n$. In its shifted prefix the last numerator $d_i b^{n-n_{ij_i}}$ has valuation $v_{p_i}(d_i)$ and all older terms have strictly lower pole depth. The other axes are $p_i$-integral. Hence its reduced denominator has $p_i$-exponent exactly $s_i j_i-v_{p_i}(d_i)$ once positive. All denominators have support in the fixed set of axes, grow to infinity and are at most $O(n^{\sum_i s_i})$. This reasoning also permits coincident insertions on different axes.

For clarity, partition $[M,2M)$ into blocks of length $\lfloor\sqrt M\rfloor$. There are $O(\log M)$ insertions through $3M$. Blocks containing one and an unfinished block have aggregate length $O(\sqrt M\log M)$. Every other full block follows one rational orbit exactly at the prefix level. The true shifted tail tends to zero uniformly for $n\ge M$: dominate its terms by $\sum_{i,j}|d_i|p_i^{-s_i j}$ and use that every fixed term eventually enters the prefix. The fixed-support short-orbit estimate gives $o(\sqrt M)$ interval-count error per good block, uniformly in the reduced starting numerator, since the denominator grows and is polynomial in the block length. The [circle comparison](translations.md) transfers this to the true orbit. The bound $o(M)$ for the count error holds for any initial segment of the shell, charging its last incomplete block. Geometric addition over dyadic shells proves the all-prefix statement. Integer dilation equivalence removes the clearing factor. For any nonzero integer vector the resulting normal linear form has vanishing first Fourier average. Multidimensional Weyl gives joint uniformity. ## Residue-preserving rays for pi and polylogarithms

The same argument works on any union of positive residue classes modulo $H$, using primes $p_i\equiv1\bmod H$, $p_i\nmid b$, larger than all previously chosen data. Their existence follows from Dirichlet's theorem (for $H=8$ one can also use odd prime divisors of $u^4+1$ with $u$ a multiple of all forbidden primes and 2). Every ray stays in its original class. The least-unused argument takes place within that union. It still exhausts it.

For the Bailey–Borwein–Plouffe identity take $b=16,\ H=8$, residues $a=1,4,5,6$, with the respective signed numerators $4,-2,-1,-1$. On the ray starting at $r\equiv a\bmod8$ the denominators are $rp^j$ and the exponents are $(rp^j-a)/8$ for $j\ge0$. Multiplying by the constant signed factor for that residue preserves normality of the ray. The entire signed sum is absolutely convergent (bound the four geometric tails separately), and equals $\pi$. Each finite set of these signed components is jointly uniformly distributed in base 16. For $\operatorname{Li}_s(1/b)$, $s$ a positive integer, choose every fresh ray prime coprime to the selected radix and use the least-unused partition on all positive indices with coefficients $r^{-s}p^{-sj}$. These are identities for classical constants with finite-dimensional distribution results for their components. The actual discrepancy of each full classical sum requires additional estimates. [BBP original paper, formula (1.2)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-97-00856-9)

## Positive controls and counterexamples

For fixed distinct primes 3 and 5, split the localized binary logarithm into its $i$-rows, $r=3^i$, $p=5$. Each row is a positive rational multiple of an affine Stoneham component. The full sum is normal by the finite-support unsigned theorem. In any fixed finite support containing an off-radix prime, split by the exponents of the remaining primes and retain the chosen prime as ray variable. The same proof applies to all positive integer orders (for an individual row use the finite compatibility proof with one axis). The global theorem depends on the null measure of persistent modular cancellations on that support. For masks on finite support, retaining all sufficiently late pure powers of an off-radix prime $p$ gives the upper discrepancy bound $1-1/(p-1)$ under MW-02. It proves normality for $p=2$ in odd radix. An infinite mask on a single axis is normal under the pole hypothesis of MW-03. An arbitrary selection of individually normal rows supplies no such certificate. [Unsigned classification](../unsigned-finite-support.md) · [Masked supports](../../docs/results.md#mw-02)

Conversely, fix a positive rational $R$ and choose independently normal generators $\alpha_i=\alpha_{b,p_i}$ on distinct primes coprime to $b$. Start with $e_0=R$. At step $i$ take a positive rational $c_i$ strictly between $e_{i-1}/(3\alpha_i)$ and $2e_{i-1}/(3\alpha_i)$ and set $e_i=e_{i-1}-c_i\alpha_i$. This gives $e_{i-1}/3<e_i<2e_{i-1}/3$ and a positive infinite sum exactly equal to $R$. It is computably explicit: enumerate positive rationals by increasing sum of reduced numerator and denominator, breaking ties by numerator. In parallel refine rigorous intervals for the two endpoints; at stage $l$ test the first $l$ candidates with precision $2^{-l}$, and take the first strictly certified candidate at the first successful stage. The generator's geometric tail gives effective intervals. A candidate in the interior ensures termination. The finite compatibility proof applies with $j\ge1$ and is unaffected by $c_i$. Thus every finite subcollection is jointly uniform, while the sum has an eventually periodic orbit.

For a convergent series of normal terms $S_i$, put $E_k=\sum_{i>k}S_i$ and $\overline M(z)=\limsup_N N^{-1}\sum_{n<N}\|b^nz\|$. The triangle inequality gives $\overline M(E_k)+\overline M(E_{k+1})\ge1/4$, by uniformity of $S_{k+1}$ and integration of circle distance. At least one in every pair is at least $1/8$. Moreover, if all nonempty consecutive finite sums are normal, at most one fixed tail preserves normality under every translation of a normal input. Universal preservers form a group: composition gives addition, and normality is invariant under negation, so $-z$ preserves whenever $z$ does. A normal $z$ fails the universal test on $-z$. The difference between any two distinct indexed tails is a consecutive normal sum, which proves the stronger obstruction.

## Finite and dynamic transfer

For any partial sum $X$ and $N>0$, pair the first $N$ shifts of $X$ and $x$. The circle bound gives
$$
 |D_N(x)-D_N(X)|\le 2b^{N-1}|x-X|.
$$
One can instead use the exceptional count at any tolerance or the square-root bound from mean circular distance. These are finite inequalities; $X$ may depend on $N$. Consequently $D_N(X_{k(N)})\to0$ and $b^N|x-X_{k(N)}|\to0$ suffice. For disjoint modeled pieces covering $G$ positions of a prefix, suppose each piece's model has extreme discrepancy at most $d$ and at most $E$ covered pairs in total have circle error exceeding $\epsilon$. Arc expansion on each piece and trivial counts on its complement give
$$
 D_N(x)\le (N-G)/N + (d+2\epsilon)G/N + E/N.
$$
Both statements allow an unfinished observation window. The following example supplies arithmetic data for applying them on unbounded prime support.

## A normal positive sum on infinitely many denominator primes

Take $b=2$, and let $\mathcal P$ consist of 3 and all odd primes congruent to 2 modulo 3. There are infinitely many of the latter: if a finite list exhausted them, a positive number $3(2\prod p)-1$ would have a new prime factor congruent to 2 modulo 3. Define
$$
 Y_p=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{1}{p^j\,2^{\,2^{(p^j)^2}}},\qquad Y=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}Y_p.
$$
Then $Y$ is binary normal, with $D_N(Y)=O((\log N)^{-1/2})$. All nonzero finite rational combinations of the $Y_p$ are normal. The proof of the full sum uses a grid surviving in the presence of every other prime.

At shift $n$ put $L=\lfloor\sqrt{\log_2 n}\rfloor$ ($n\ge2$). Up to this cutoff exactly the terms with $p^j\le L$ have inserted. A given axis contributes its unique maximal pole, so the reduced denominator is exactly
$$
 q=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P,\ p\le L}p^{\lfloor\log_p L\rfloor}.
$$
In particular $q\le L^L=\exp(o(\log n))$. Write $q=3^e Q$. Since every prime divisor $p$ of $Q$ satisfies $3\nmid p(p-1)$, the order of 2 modulo $Q$ is prime to 3 (order 1 when $Q=1$). Let $t$ be its least common multiple with 2. Then $v_3(2^t-1)=1$ by lifting the exponent from $4-1$, and the iterates of $2^t$ modulo $3^e$ constitute the entire subgroup congruent to 1 modulo 3. Indeed its order is $3^{e-1}$ by the same valuation identity. Modulo $Q$ the power $2^t$ is one. For $e\ge1$, the Chinese remainder theorem therefore partitions any full reduced orbit modulo $q$ into cosets which, divided by $q$, are equally spaced grids of size $3^{e-1}$. Each grid is a single residue class modulo $3Q$ lifted to modulus $q$. Counts on a grid of size $m$ in any arc differ from $m|I|$ by at most one. Full periods thus have discrepancy at most $3^{1-e}=O(1/L)$, uniformly in the number of other primes. One unfinished period costs at most $q$ samples.

For $N$ large discard $n<\sqrt N$. The remaining $L$ are comparable to $\sqrt{\log N}$. Split at insertions, at most $O(\sqrt{\log N})$ through $2N$ because the integers $p^j$ are distinct. Remove the final $\lceil\log_2 N\rceil$ positions before every such insertion (intersect with the observation window). Elsewhere the real tail after shift has size at most $2^{-\lceil\log_2 N\rceil+1}$, since insertions occupy distinct integer positions and coefficients are at most one. Insertions beyond $2N$ satisfy the same remote-tail bound. Each remaining interval follows one rational model up to this error. The count cost of unfinished periods is at most $O(\sqrt{\log N})\,\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N}\log\log N))=N^{o(1)}$. The discarded positions have proportion $O(N^{-1/2}+(\log N)^{3/2}/N)$. Apply arc enlargement to these estimates for every $N$. This proves the rate.

For any fixed nonzero rational combination clear coefficients as in the ray lemma. Its denominator is still $N^{o(1)}$, and the pole at any chosen participating prime $p$ grows. To obtain the grid, let $Q$ be the part coprime to $p$. The $p$-valuation of $\operatorname{ord}_Q(2)$ is at most $\max_{\ell\ne p}v_p(\ell-1)$ over the fixed remaining axes (zero for an empty maximum). Take $t=\mathrm{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}_Q(2),\operatorname{ord}_p(2))$. Then $v_p(2^t-1)$ is bounded independently of the cutoff, by lifting from $\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$. The same grid proof at $p$ applies, with growing size. Finite constant factors in the real tail after clearing just change its bound. Multidimensional Weyl again gives finite joint uniformity.

One explicit dynamic choice keeps all components with $p\le\sqrt{\log_2(2N)}$. The first omitted insertion then exceeds $2N$, giving $2^N|Y-X_{k(N)}|\le 2^{-N}$ by the geometric tail. The same grid estimates hold for the modeled shifts. This example uses a uniform period bound, a uniform surviving grid, sparse insertions and an independently bounded tail. None requires a distribution hypothesis on the infinite sum.

## What happens on approaching the full logarithm

Let $X_P$ be the binary logarithm localized on all integers supported on a finite set $P$ of primes, and let $l$ be the least prime omitted. The least omitted integer is exactly $l$. Positivity and the geometric tail give the sharp factor-two comparison
$$
 \frac{2^{-l}}{l}\ \le\ \log 2-X_P\ \le\ \frac{2^{1-l}}{l}.
$$
The factor-two estimate already has a [kernel proof](../../NormalityChecker/LocalizedLogGap.lean). Thus $2^N|\log 2-X_{P(N)}|\to0$ **if and only if** $l+\log_2 l-N\to+\infty$. In particular all primes through $N-O(\log N)$ must already be admitted (or the least omitted prime is even larger). The modeled insertions include every integer below $l$. The fixed-support constants and exponents are allowed to depend on $P$, and the sparse schedule disappears on almost the entire sampled prefix. The localized theorem therefore gives no uniform estimate of the required kind. This obstruction concerns the absolute-tail version of the transfer and leaves open the mean-circle and positive-coverage methods. [Classical synthesis](classical.md)
