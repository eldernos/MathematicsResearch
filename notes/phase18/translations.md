# Translation of radix orbits

Fix an integer radix $b\ge2$. Use the circle $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, $T(x)=bx\bmod 1$, circle distance $\|u-v\|$, and the first $N>0$ iterates numbered from zero. Extreme discrepancy $D_N$ is the supremum of the absolute centered count over half-open intervals. An arc crossing zero has the negative centered count of its complementary interval. Thus taking all oriented circle arcs gives exactly the same discrepancy, including at point masses.

## A finite comparison

For any paired samples $u_n,v_n$ and $\epsilon\ge0$, set $E=\#\{n<N:\|u_n-v_n\|>\epsilon\}$. Then
$$
 |D_N(u)-D_N(v)|\le \min(1,E/N+2\epsilon).
$$
To bound excess mass on an arc $I$, its good preimages are in an enlarged arc of length at most $|I|+2\epsilon+2h$, capped at one. Take $h>0$ to include both displaced endpoints even for atoms. The enlarged count for $u$ is at most its length plus $D_N(u)$. The $E$ bad samples add at most $E/N$. Let $h$ decrease to zero. Deficits are excesses on the complementary arc, and interchanging samples proves the assertion.

For $v_n=T^n(x+z)$ and $u_n=T^nx$ the bad set depends only on $z$. Take the infimum over $\epsilon$. For an integer $z$ all samples coincide. A radix-primary rational has reduced denominator dividing some power of $b$. For $z=A/b^K$ all samples from $K$ on coincide, so the difference is at most $\min(1,K/N)$. Finite sums use the largest clearing exponent. Infinite sums require control of the contributions still outstanding. Write $M_N(z)=N^{-1}\sum_{n<N}\|b^nz\|$. Markov's inequality gives the additional bound $\min(1,2\sqrt{2M_N(z)})$ by choosing $\epsilon=\sqrt{M_N(z)/2}$ and taking a limit at zero. Vanishing mean is equivalent to $E_N(\epsilon)/N\to0$ for every fixed positive $\epsilon$, by boundedness. Consequently the numerical discrepancies differ by $o(1)$ uniformly in $x$, preserving their entire cluster set and both extrema.

## The exact worst-case change

For each real $z$ and every $N>0$,
$$
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}\left|D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)\right|=1-D_N(z).
$$
Indeed, take any circle arc $J$ of length $l$, center $c$, containing a fraction $1-e$ of the samples $T^nz$. On these positions $T^n(x+z)$ differs in circle distance by at most $l/2$ from $T^nx+c$. The latter sample has exactly the discrepancy of $x$, by rotation invariance of the arc tests. The finite comparison gives $e+l=1-(\mu_N^z(J)-l)$ as an upper bound. The supremum of the **positive** centered arc counts equals the extreme discrepancy by complementation. Taking the infimum of the bounds therefore gives $1-D_N(z)$. At $x=0$ the discrepancy is one for every $N$, proving equality. [Kernel-checked maximum and attainment](../../NormalityChecker/SharpTranslation.lean) The upper bound holds for arbitrary paired finite circle samples as well. The orbit property enters only when specializing the samples.

Consequently the following are equivalent: $D_N(z)\to1$; the discrepancy difference tends to zero for every $x$; it tends to zero uniformly in $x$; translation by $z$ preserves both lower and upper asymptotic discrepancies for every $x$. For the last converse, even equality of the lower discrepancies at $x=0$ suffices.

There is an intrinsic description of these translations. They are precisely $c+w$ modulo one, where $(b-1)c$ is an integer and $M_N(w)\to0$. To see necessity, nearly maximizing positive arcs for $z$ have length tending to zero and mass tending to one. Every weak subsequential limit of the empirical measures is therefore a point mass (pass to a convergent subsequence of arc centers). It is invariant under $T$ by telescoping continuous tests, so its center is one of the $b-1$ fixed points. The cluster set is connected: consecutive empirical measures have distance tending to zero in the bounded-Lipschitz metric, and in a compact metric space a sequence with this property cannot have a disconnected cluster set. Otherwise disjoint positively separated neighborhoods of the two compact parts would be visited infinitely often, forcing a cluster point outside them. Thus there is a single limit $\delta_c$. Integrating distance to $c$ gives $M_N(z-c)\to0$ since $T^nc=c$. Conversely $M_N(w)\to0$ gives this concentration and hence $D_N(z)\to1$. In binary the only fixed circle point is zero.

For example, the supremum over $x$ of the discrepancy change after adding $L_\chi$ equals $1-D_N(L_\chi)$ exactly. For the Phase 17 real character theorem its order is $\asymp_\chi (\log\log N)/\sqrt{\log N}$, with character-dependent constants. The same equality transfers any all-prefix bound for the discrepancy deficit of another arithmetically constructed remainder. [Phase 17 hypotheses and rates](../phase17/multiplicative-lambert.md)

## Sparse signed integers

Let $z=\sum_j A_j b^{-m_j}$ with integer $A_j$, integer $m_j\ge1$, and absolute convergence. Ignore zero coefficients. Expand $|A_j|$ in base $b$ with width $w_j=1+\lfloor\log_b|A_j|\rfloor$. A digit at exponent $h$ contributes a signed multiple of $b^{-h}$. Digits at $h\le n$ give integers after shift $n$. Let $a_h$ be the sum of the **absolute** digit magnitudes at position $h$ over all sources, coalescing overlaps only for this bound. Absolute convergence justifies the regrouping. In particular $\sum_{h>0} a_h b^{-h}<\infty$. A bounded set of nonpositive positions contributes only integers.

For any $L\ge0$, charge each $n<N$ with an occupied digit in $(n,n+L]$ once. Call their number $H_N(L)$. On the remaining shifts the circle error is at most $U_{n,L}=\sum_{h>n+L}a_h b^{n-h}$. Thus an explicit bound for the discrepancy difference is
$$
 2\epsilon+N^{-1}\big(H_N(L)+\#\{n<N:U_{n,L}>\epsilon\}\big).
$$
The second count can be restricted to uncharged shifts. This formulation also applies to unbounded overlaps. If $a_h\le A$ uniformly for $h>0$, take $\epsilon=A b^{-L}/(b-1)$. A source can charge only shifts in $[m_j-w_j+1-L,m_j)$, intersected with $[0,N)$; union lengths or the smaller exact occupied set suffice. These bounds allow either sign and do not require a rule for carries or borrows. Under the bounded-overlap assumption, upper density zero of the occupied positions suffices, since a fixed backward neighborhood then has upper density zero. Just counting the indices $m_j$ need not work for unbounded widths. For example, code consecutive blocks of the digits of any normal number as the integers $A_j$ at endpoints $m_j=2^j$ (include the initial block). Even this sparse endpoint schedule then represents a normal number.

For $b=2,\ m_k=k(k+1),\ A_k=1,\ k\ge1$, at most $O(\sqrt{N+L})$ digit positions matter, and $H_N(L)\le L\,O(\sqrt{N+L})$. Taking $L=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$ gives uniformly in $x$
$$
 |D_N(x+z)-D_N(x)|=O(\log(2N)/\sqrt N).
$$
The expansion has infinitely many ones and arbitrarily long zero runs, impossible for an eventually periodic sequence unless its tail is all zeros. Thus $z$ is irrational. Taking $x=0$ gives $D_N(z)\to1$, since a point mass has extreme discrepancy one. The same argument applies to $m_k=k!$, starting at $k=2$. Its partial sums have denominators at most $b^{k!}$ and positive errors at most $b^{1-(k+1)!}/(b-1)$. The sparse digit proof gives irrationality separately, and these bounds prove the Liouville property and transcendence. Both examples may be added to any previously proved normal component in its radix.

## Determinism and arithmetic examples

Use ordinary Cesàro averages on $[0,N)$ in a single fixed integer base. A digit sequence is completely deterministic when **every** weak limit of its empirical shift measures has measure-theoretic entropy zero. Rauzy's original test uses the upper limiting minimum digit-prediction error with finite lookahead, followed by increasing the lookahead length. His theorem identifies this zero-noise class with the translations preserving every normal number. Bergelson and Downarowicz give the dynamical formulation (Definitions 3.5 and 3.10, Theorem 3.12). The one-sided base expansion maps continuously to the circle, intertwines the shifts and has fibers of size at most two. Invariant lifts and their projections have the same entropy: conditional uncertainty of an entire symbolic name given its image is bounded by $\log 2$, hence zero per digit. This permits entropy tests on the circle even when mass accumulates on both sides of zero. [Rauzy, Acta Arithmetica](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-29-3-211-225) · [Bergelson and Downarowicz, full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12929v1)

* If $M_N(z)\to0$, its only circle limit is the point mass at zero. Every rational number has an ultimately periodic orbit and likewise has entropy zero. The classes intersect at radix-primary rationals. General rationals can have positive mean displacement. For example in base two, the orbit of $1/3$ alternates between $1/3$ and $2/3$. Its extreme discrepancy tends to $2/3$ (the largest oscillation of its centered counting function), whereas $D_N(0)=1$. Universal normality preservation therefore need not preserve numerical discrepancy.
* More generally, suppose that for every $\eta>0$ there is a finite union $F_\eta$ of periodic cycles such that for **each** radius $\rho>0$ the upper density of visits outside the $\rho$-neighborhood of $F_\eta$ is at most $\eta$. Each empirical limit assigns at least $1-\eta$ to $F_\eta$, by continuous cutoffs and then shrinking the radius. Taking $\eta=1/j$ shows concentration on a countable union of cycles, whose invariant probabilities have entropy zero. Long plateaus without this tightness condition can approximate Lebesgue measure instead.
* If the ones in a binary expansion have density zero, then the frequency of length-$l$ windows containing a one tends to zero, for every fixed $l$. This proves determinism **and** the mean-displacement conclusion, even for a disjunctive expansion. The same proof works for nonzero digits in any radix. It applies to the binary character Lambert benchmarks wherever the established digit-frequency bound gives density zero of ones, including the nonprincipal real Dirichlet character case under the hypotheses of the [character estimate](../phase16/weighted-lambert.md). The same binary conclusion follows from density zero of digit changes: away from a fixed-length neighborhood of each change the next digits are either all zeros or all ones, both near zero on the circle. In larger bases a long constant block of an intermediate digit $c$ lies near $c/(b-1)$, which need not be zero on the circle. The binary result also covers the signed powerful-support example with weight $(-1)^{\omega(m)}$ in the cited note. An estimate about sparse coefficient support needs a carry or width bound before it becomes such a digit estimate.
* For the distinct power-exponent Lambert family $\sum_{m\ge1}(b^{m^r}-1)^{-1}$, $b,r\ge2$, Captain Sude's pinned external theorem supplies a unique limiting circle law of measure entropy zero, with full support, no atoms, and positive word frequencies. Thus these numbers are deterministic translations by Rauzy's criterion, and adding one to a base-$b$ normal Stoneham number preserves normality. Their limiting law is not a point mass; the sharp translation classification therefore excludes uniform numerical discrepancy preservation. The entropy and orbit-measure statements here are external inputs. [Pinned external Lean statement](https://github.com/CaptainSude/generalized-Lambert-disjunctivity-and-nonnormality/blob/fe33be210446c6c0b127ca7757fc8f1182c5b541/lean/PowerLambert/Results.lean)
* Paper III's signed phases admit a converse test. Its uniformity theorem for nonzero shells and concentration theorem for zero shells give circle limits $\theta\delta_0+(1-\theta)\lambda$ along each subsequence on which the share of zero shells tends to $\theta$. For continuous functions this follows by interval step approximation on active positions and continuity at zero on retired positions. If infinitely many phases are nonzero, the ends of those shells have a nonzero-shell share at least $(p-1)/p$. A subsequential limit then has entropy $(1-\theta)\log b>0$ by affinity of entropy on invariant measures. If the phases eventually vanish, the expansion is rational. Thus exactly the rational members of this particular bounded-phase family are deterministic. The same mixture test applies to the cubic programmable family: its member with discrepancy $\delta<1$ has entropy $(1-\delta)\log b$, while the fully retired member has mean displacement zero. [Signed shell theorems](../signed-shell-discrepancy.md) · [Programmable mixtures](programming.md)

Compatibility with **one** fixed normal component is weaker than universal preservation. For example, take two jointly normal ray components $u,v$ from the [ray construction](summation.md). Their difference is normal and hence fails the universal test (translate its negative). Nevertheless adding $v-u$ to $u$ gives the normal number $v$. Normality is also preserved by nonzero rational dilations and rational translations. Beyond these operations the arithmetic restrictions matter: Bergelson and Downarowicz, Theorem 8.1 and Corollary 8.14, construct in base two a normal times a nonzero deterministic number that is deterministic, and two deterministic numbers with normal product. [Arithmetic operations](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12929v1)
