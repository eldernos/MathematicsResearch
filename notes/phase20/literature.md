## 1. Euler's pentagonal number theorem and Jacobi's identities; what is known about digits

**The identities [R] (as quoted by Serre 1985, Introduction, who calls them "les identités d'Euler et Jacobi" and cites Hardy–Wright, Chap. XIX):**

```math
\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^m)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^n q^{n(3n+1)/2},\qquad
\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^m)^3=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n(2n+1)\,q^{n(n+1)/2}.
```

The first is Euler's pentagonal number theorem; the second is Jacobi's cube identity, a special case of his triple product. The dates 1750/1783 (Euler) and 1829 (Jacobi, *Fundamenta nova*) in the task brief are **[U]**: I did not read the originals. J. Bell, *Euler and the pentagonal number theorem*, arXiv:math/0510054 [A: abstract only], is an exhaustive survey of Euler's correspondence and papers on the theorem and is the place to confirm Euler's dates. Jacobi's *Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum* (1829) is **[U]**: no record was located in Crossref (the search returned unrelated items), and I did not attempt another source.

**Digits of E_b, E_b², E_b³ (b ≥ 2 an integer): what the literature gives.**

1. *No source located that states the base-b digit distribution of E_b, of E_b² or of E_b³.* Searches: a web search for "normality binary expansion of Euler's function ∏(1 − 2⁻ⁿ) pentagonal number theorem digits lacunary q-series base b normal number" returned only expository pages on the pentagonal number theorem and unrelated normal-number papers (Pollack–Vandehey, normal numbers from arithmetic functions). I did not search MathSciNet, zbMATH or Google Scholar for this topic. The absence of a hit is not evidence of novelty.
2. *Transcendence is known (see §4) and says nothing about digits.* No implication in either direction between transcendence of an algebraic-q value and its digit distribution is known; BBCP 2004 [R] state, for the theta values z = Σ_{n≥0}2^{−n²}, z′ = Σ_{n∈ℤ}2^{−n²}, that they are transcendental, and then prove that almost all binary bits of z′² are 0. Both facts hold for the same number.
3. *Structure that follows from the identities and Serre's theorem (not a digit theorem):* E_b and E_b³ are signed series supported on the quadratic-growth exponent sets {n(3n+1)/2} and {n(n+1)/2}, so their coefficient supports are far sparser than the supports of the Lambert series of Phase 17 (these have counting function x/√log x). For E_b², Serre (§5 below) shows the support of the coefficients of ∏(1 − qᵐ)² has density zero but is not that sparse in general (Serre 1981, Thm 18 gives a count of order x/(log x)^λ with λ ≤ 3/4 for weight-1 eigenforms; I did not compute λ for η²). **[M]** An elementary consequence for the Phase 20 digit questions, not a literature result: for E_b the carries from a signed sparse series with exponents gₖ ≈ 3k²/2 are local (gaps between consecutive exponents grow like k), so digits 0 and b − 1 occupy a set of positions of density one, and E_b is nonnormal with D_N → 1. Phase 20 should state its own proof of this; I did not find it in a source.
4. *Coefficients:* the coefficient of b⁻ⁿ in E_b³ is ±(2k + 1), growing like √n, so for b = 2 carries are not local for the cube; this is the "interfering carries" difficulty BBCP remark on for higher powers (§3).

**Classification.** The identities are classical. Statements about the digits of E_b, E_b², E_b³ in base b are "not located in the sources examined".

**Hypotheses to match against Phase 20.** (a) Bernays needs the form primitive. (b) For an **imprimitive** form Q = mQ′ (m ≥ 2, Q′ primitive) the represented set is exactly m times the set represented by Q′, so **[M]** B_Q(x) = B_{Q′}(x/m) ∼ (C(D′)/m)·x/√(log x), where D′ is the discriminant of Q′ and D = m²D′. This is an elementary reduction, not a quotation. (c) The constant depends on the discriminant only, not on the class of the form, so every primitive form of discriminant D has the same asymptotic constant even when the class group is not trivial. (d) The support {n : r_Q(n) > 0} of the theta series Σ r_Q(n) qⁿ is exactly the represented set. For a **character** Lambert series c = χ_D ∗ 1 (Phase 17's setting) the support is the set of norms of integral ideals of the quadratic field, which is a union over the classes of the represented sets of the forms of discriminant D (and for D = −4 it is the set of sums of two squares); this identification is **[M]** and should be checked before Phase 20 transfers the single-form constant K = C(D) to the Lambert case.

**Classification.** Landau–Bernays asymptotic for represented integers: classical (1908, 1912), and Phase 20's input K is that constant. The Phase 20 contribution is not the counting asymptotic but its use as the exact constant in a discrepancy asymptotic (§3).

## 3. Bailey–Borwein–Crandall–Pomerance (2004), Section 9, versus Phase 17 and Phase 20

**Source [R].** D. H. Bailey, J. M. Borwein, R. E. Crandall and C. Pomerance, *On the binary expansions of algebraic numbers*, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux **16** (2004), no. 3, 487–518, DOI 10.5802/jtnb.457, §9 "Study of a 'borderline' number" (pp. 505–512), read in the Numdam scan of the item PDF. Notation there: z = Σ_{n≥0} 2^{−n²}, z′ = 2z − 1 = Σ_{n∈ℤ} 2^{−n²} = θ₃(1/2), z′² = Σ r₂(n)2⁻ⁿ (r₂ counts representations by two squares with signs and order), and L is the Landau constant.

- **Theorem 9.4 (p. 509), as printed:** "Almost all bits of z² are 0's; in fact the 1's-count has asymptotic behavior #(z², N) ∼ C₀ N/√(log N) for an absolute constant C₀ ≈ 0.7996… (we give a formula for C₀ in the proof)." (The displayed asymptotic is typeset as an image in the scan; my text extraction lost it and the sentence above agrees with the transcription in `papers/06-multiplicative-lambert/LEDGER.md`.) The next paragraph: "Incidentally the asymptotic density also holds for z′² as follows from a slight modification (actually simplification) of the proof." **Scope of the counting:** the number of ones among the first N binary digits, a frequency count, not a discrepancy.
- **Lemma 9.5 (p. 509):** "There is an absolute constant c such that for any integers N, B > 2, the number of integers n ≤ N with r₂(z, n) > 0 and r₂(z, m) > 0 for some integer m with 0 < |n − m| ≤ B is at most cBN/log N." **Lemma 9.6:** the number of n ≤ N with r₂(z, n) > B is at most (√N + 1)²/B.
- **The proof (pp. 509–511):** keeps the n whose coefficient is isolated, so that its binary digits appear unchanged, counts these using Landau-type asymptotics (the asymptotics for the auxiliary counts T_g(N) are "not given" and said to follow from the Landau asymptotic formula), and gets C₀ as a sum over squarefull integers g divisible only by primes ≡ 1 (mod 4); it is not K.
- **Context on p. 506:** "the Landau density of representable numbers does not on the face of it imply a similar density of 1-bits in the expansion of z′²"; and "it is already known that theta functions at algebraic arguments, hence z, z′, are transcendental [5, 12]". **p. 509:** BBCP expect higher powers z³, z⁴ to be 2-normal ("likely"; no proof), noting that r₃(n) > 0 for a fraction 5/6 of n.

**Sharp distinctions.**

| | BBCP 2004, Thm 9.4 | Phase 17 (CC-48) | Phase 20 (P20) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quantity | number of 1-bits in the first N digits (prefix count) | extreme discrepancy D_N (sup over intervals) and the **order** of 1 − D_N, plus window counts for every word | the **asymptotic** of 1 − D_N, with an explicit constant |
| Result | #(z², N) ∼ C₀N/√(log N), C₀ ≈ 0.7996 [R] | 1 − D_N ≍ (log log N)/√(log N) (δ = ½), within constants [LEDGER] | 1 − D_N ∼ [K/(2 log 2)](log log N)/√(log N) (relayed) |
| Constant | C₀ involves binary weights of the divisor structure, not K | none (order only) | K, the density constant of the support |
| Class | θ₃(1/2)², essentially one number (a "slight modification" gives z′²) | general multiplicative class (H1)–(H4) | to be matched to the Phase 20 statement |
| Words | the digit one only | every fixed word, short windows N^a ≤ H ≤ N | not applicable to a pure D_N asymptotic |

BBCP do not discuss discrepancy; as the ledger records, their one-bit result gives D_N → 1 via Lemma 7.1 and an upper bound on 1 − D_N of the order of Phase 17's, but not the lower bound with the log log N factor. **[M]** Why the constant of P20 involves K and not C₀: heuristically, the extreme discrepancy is governed by runs of zeros. If the support has density ρ_N = K/√(log N), then 1 − D_N ≈ min_R [R ρ_N + c·2^{−R}], the minimum is at 2^{−R} ≈ ρ_N/(c ln 2), and R ≈ ½ log₂ log N, giving 1 − D_N ≈ (K/(2 log 2)) (log log N)/√(log N). This reproduces the relayed constant and shows that only the support density K, not the binary weights in C₀, enters. This is a consistency check on P20, not a proof, and it does not replace the Phase 20 argument.

**Classification.** Prefix one-bit asymptotic for θ₃(1/2)²: already proved (BBCP 2004). Phase 17's short windows, all words, order of 1 − D_N and the general class: apparently new in the sources examined (LEDGER). Phase 20's exact constant: apparently new in the sources examined **if** the proof is complete; I found no source stating a discrepancy asymptotic with an explicit constant for any arithmetic series. This is a statement about this search only.

**What follows, and what does not.** The following deductions are **[M]**: I did not find them stated in a source, and the chain of standard facts (the identity Δ = (E₄³ − E₆²)/1728 with Δ(q) = q ∏(1 − qⁿ)²⁴) was not re-derived here.

- For algebraic q with 0 < |q| < 1, E₂(q), E₄(q), E₆(q) are algebraically independent (Nesterenko), hence each is transcendental.
- Δ(q) = (E₄(q)³ − E₆(q)²)/1728 is then transcendental, so q ∏(1 − qⁿ)²⁴ and hence E_q := ∏(1 − qⁿ) are transcendental; for q = 1/b this gives E_b, E_b², E_b³ and P_b = 1/E_b transcendental. The same deduction is made informally in a Kyoto lecture survey I found (that survey says transcendence of related q-series at q^k follows from Nesterenko for algebraic q); I did not find a reference that states the case ∏(1 − qⁿ) explicitly, so the brief's "exact transcendence results for E_b, P_b" must be cited as "a consequence of Nesterenko's theorem", not as a quoted theorem.
- Θ₃(1/b) = Σ_{n∈ℤ} b^{−n²} is transcendental by Duverney–Nishioka–Nishioka–Shiokawa and Bertrand (as cited by BBCP).
- E₂(1/b), E₄(1/b), E₆(1/b): algebraically independent, from Nesterenko as quoted above.

**Transcendence versus digit distribution.** Transcendence says only that the number is not algebraic. It implies neither normality nor nonnormality. Examples in the record: Liouville numbers are transcendental and nonnormal in some cases and normal in others (Bugeaud 2002, Becher–Heiber–Slaman 2015, in the Phase 18 sources); BBCP's z′ is transcendental while z′² has almost all bits zero **[R]**. BBCP's criterion in the opposite direction: an algebraic irrational of degree D has at least C N^{1/D} ones among the first N binary digits **[R, from the abstract]**, so digit sparsity of order below N^{1/D} is a *transcendence* criterion, not a normality criterion. Adamczewski–Bugeaud, Ann. of Math. **165** (2007), no. 2, 547–565 (Crossref checked; theorem not read) concerns the combinatorial complexity of digit sequences of algebraic numbers. It is cited only to note that complexity results are about digit *patterns* (a different property from frequency), and Rivoal, J. Aust. Math. Soc. **85** (2008), 95–111 (Crossref checked), on the bits counting function, claims by its abstract an answer to a BBCP question about a Fibonacci-related series; I did not read it.

## 5. Serre (1985) on lacunary powers of η, and Serre (1981)

**Serre 1985 [R].** J.-P. Serre, *Sur la lacunarité des puissances de η*, Glasgow Math. J. **27** (1985), 203–221 (dedicated to R. A. Rankin; DOI 10.1017/S0017089500006194; the 19-page PDF from Cambridge Core was read: Introduction, §1.1 and the statement of Theorem 1′).

- Definitions in the Introduction: ∏(1 − qᵐ)^r = Σ p_r(n) qⁿ. The series is **lacunary** when {n : p_r(n) ≠ 0} has density zero.
- r = 1 and r = 3 are lacunary by the Euler and Jacobi identities. "On ne connaît pas d'autres valeurs impaires de r pour lesquelles η^r soit lacunaire" (no other odd value of r is known to give a lacunary η^r); computer calculations make it probable that there is none (cf. his §3.2). So the odd case beyond r = 1, 3 is open as of 1985. A 2019 paper (arXiv:1901.07168, summarizing) reports it as still unknown **[S]**; I did not search for a later resolution.
- For r even: r = 2, 4, 6, 8 are lacunary by Ramanujan, and r = 10, 14, 26 by other cited papers (his references [5], [10], [14], not identified here).
- **Théorème 1** (verbatim): "Supposons r pair > 0. La fonction η^r est lacunaire si et seulement si r est égal à 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14 ou 26." **[R]**
- The proof rests on Théorème 17 of Serre 1981 (§1.1), which in turn depends on Deligne's ℓ-adic representations attached to cusp forms of integral weight. §2 expresses η^r for r = 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14, 26 as combinations of CM forms attached to Hecke characters of ℚ(√−1) and ℚ(√−3) and says in which cases p_r(n) = 0. The criterion (his Theorem 1′ and Lemmas 1–2) is that f_k(z) = η(12z)^{2k} ∈ S(N, k, ε_k), N = 144, belongs to the span of CM forms exactly for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 13}, i.e. r = 2k.

**Serre 1981 [R].** J.-P. Serre, *Quelques applications du théorème de densité de Chebotarev*, Publ. Math. IHÉS **54** (1981), 123–201 (Numdam scan; §6, §7.7 and the Introduction read).

- **Théorème 17 (weight k ≥ 2, f ∈ M(N, k, ω)):** for the part of f outside the span of CM forms, M_f(x) ≫ x; for every nonzero cusp form, M_f(x) ≫ x/(log x)^{1/2}, where M_f(x) counts n ≤ x with a_n(f) ≠ 0 (his (209), (210)). Corollary: a cusp form of weight k ≥ 2 outside the CM space has positive lower density of nonzero coefficients, i.e. is not lacunary. The Introduction states that such a form is not lacunary "sauf si elle est combinaison linéaire de formes de type CM".
- **§7.7 and Théorème 18 (weight 1, f ≠ 0 an eigenfunction of the Hecke operators T_p and U_p):** by Deligne–Serre (Ann. Sci. ÉNS (4) **7** (1974), 507–530, DOI 10.24033/asens.1277), f corresponds to an irreducible Artin representation ρ of finite image with a_p = tr ρ(σ_p); the projective image PG is dihedral D_n, or 𝔄₄, 𝔖₄ or 𝔄₅ ("exotic"). With λ = |PG₂|/|PG| (the proportion of elements of order 2, his (219)–(220)), Théorème 18 states that M_f(x) ∼ a x/(log x)^λ with a > 0 as x → ∞, proved in his reference [42], Thm 4.2, which I did not read. He remarks that the exotic types have λ < 1/2 and the most lacunary are those of type D with λ = 3/4. The explicit values (220) were garbled in the scan, so I do not quote them.
- **Consequence for Phase 20 [M]:** η² has weight 1, so its coefficient support is governed by Théorème 18, and by Serre 1985 the support has density zero; η, η³ are the sparse cases (counting function ≍ √x). I did not determine the value of λ for η², and I did not determine whether η(12z)² is dihedral of the type with λ = 1/2 or 1/2 + 1/(2n); do not state λ for E_b² without computing it. For E_b⁴, E_b⁶, … the lacunarity classification is that of Serre's Théorème 1 (r = 4, 6, 8, 10, 14, 26 lacunary; all other even r non-lacunary; odd r ≥ 5 unknown); the Phase 20 hypotheses should list which r they use.

**The distinction between p(n) mod b and the digits of P_b.** The results above concern the residue of p(n) modulo m as n varies over integers, equivalently the coefficients of the generating function reduced mod m. The Phase 20 object is the real number P_b = Σ_{n≥0} p(n) b⁻ⁿ = 1/E_b. Two differences:

1. **[M]** The digits of P_b in base b are not the numbers p(n) mod b. For any real x = Σ a_m b^{−m} with integer a_m ≥ 0 write d_n = ⌊bⁿ x⌋ mod b for the n-th base-b digit. Splitting bⁿx = Σ_{m≤n} a_m b^{n−m} + T_n with T_n = Σ_{k≥1} a_{n+k} b^{−k}, the first term is an integer congruent to a_n modulo b, so d_n = (a_n + ⌊T_n⌋) mod b. For a_n = p(n) the tail T_n is large, because p(n + k)/p(n) → 1 as n → ∞ for fixed k (Hardy–Ramanujan), so T_n ≈ p(n)/(b − 1), and d_n depends on the leading digits of p(n) and its neighbours, not on p(n) mod b alone. This is an elementary identity; it is stated here so that no result of Ono, Radu or Parkin–Shanks about p(n) mod m is mistaken for a digit statement.
2. The exact reciprocal route is through E_b, which is sparse (§1): P_b·E_b = 1 gives a recursive relation for the digits of P_b from those of the sparse series. I found no literature on the base-b digits of Σ p(n) b⁻ⁿ.

**Classification.** Partition congruences and parity: classical or published (Ramanujan, Ono, Radu and the cited parity bounds). Digits of P_b: not located in the sources examined.
