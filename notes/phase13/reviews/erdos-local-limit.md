# Independent review of Mira’s Erdős–Borwein word-density proof

**Verdict.** Let $E=\sum_{m\ge1}(2^m-1)^{-1}$. For every fixed nonempty binary word $w$ and $7/12<a\le1$, there is $c_{a,w}>0$ such that every sufficiently late window of $N^a\le H\le N$ digits after position $N$ contains at least $c_{a,w}H$ wholly contained occurrences. I obtain this as a **paper theorem** from the short-interval Bombieri–Vinogradov estimate. The weighted local estimate follows by bounding the indicators of exact prime counts between explicit polynomials. Their degrees give the required dependence on the strip width, including the additional prime-power conditions arising from the divisor terms. Taking the second half of a prefix proves positive ordinary lower density. The limiting frequencies required for binary normality remain unknown. [Mira’s pinned theorem and proof][proof]

The earlier audit is available locally with `git show 97324a19238d422731c0bb5497b0d9d98e34af71:notes/phase13/erdos-borwein.md`. Write $\tau(m)$ for the divisor count and $\omega(m)$ for the number of distinct prime factors. Expanding the Lambert series gives $E=\sum_m\tau(m)2^{-m}$ over positive integers. At the one-based starting digit $n$, the fractional part of $2^{n-1}E$ equals the fractional part of $\sum_{j\ge0}\tau(n+j)2^{-j-1}$. The proof places one term at the target word’s midpoint and controls the sum of all the other terms modulo one.

## Prime intervals and the principal character

Write $T=\log\log N$. In Mira’s construction $b\le N^\theta$, $j_0\ll T$, and $n=bp-j_0$. The eligible starts give a single real interval for $p$, of length $Y=(H-k+1)/b$ using half-open endpoints, at scale $X\asymp N/b$, where $k=|w|$. Put $t=\log\log X=T+O(1)$. Fix $7/12<a'<a$ and then $\theta>0$ small enough that $(a'-\theta)/(1-\theta)>7/12$ and $\theta<a'/4$. The interval length exceeds $X^\vartheta$ by a fixed power for some $7/12<\vartheta<29/48$. All estimates below are uniform in the eligible $b,H,j_0$.

On printed pp. 529–530, equation (2) of Perelli–Pintz–Salerno has the sum over moduli **outside** the maximum over reduced residues, starting points and lengths up to $x^\vartheta$. The quoted Huxley–Iwaniec exponent allows any sufficiently small positive power below $3\vartheta-7/4$. Split the prime interval into $O(Y/X^\vartheta)$ pieces using comparable dyadic scales if necessary. The final shorter piece has the same error allowance. Fix $0<\rho<\min(3\vartheta-7/4,\vartheta-1/2)$ with a strict margin. Removing higher prime powers costs $O(X^{1/2+\rho}\log^2 X)$ over these moduli. If $P$ denotes normalized log-prime measure on the actual interval, this gives, for every fixed $B$,

```math
\sum_{d\le X^\rho}\max_{(h,d)=1}
\left|P(p\equiv h\pmod d)-\frac1{\varphi(d)}\right|
\ll_B (\log X)^{-B}.
```

Let $S$ be the sum of $\log p$ over the whole interval. In the character expansion of the prime sum the principal character contributes exactly $S/\varphi(d)$, since every sampled prime exceeds $d$. Equation (2) subtracts the main term $Y/\varphi(d)$ and its modulus-one case gives $S/Y=1+O_B((\log X)^{-B})$ after prime-power removal. Normalizing by $S$ leaves the principal term $1/\varphi(d)$. Replacing $Y$ by $S$ in the subtracted term costs at most their relative discrepancy times $\sum_{d\le X^\rho}1/\varphi(d)\ll\log X$, absorbed by taking one extra logarithmic saving. Nonunit classes are empty. On this interval the density of ordinary prime counting relative to $P$ is $1+O(1/\log X)$ pointwise, so positive marked expectations transfer as well. [Printed equation and exponent range][pps] · [Mira’s application][marked]

For a word of length $k$ and integer value $u$ let $A=2^{2u}$, $b=Am\le N^\theta$ with $m$ odd squarefree, and $j_0=\omega(m)+k+1\le CT$ for a sufficiently large absolute $C$ at fixed $w$. Each eligible $n=bp-j_0$ gives the **actual** midpoint term $\tau(bp)2^{-j_0-1}=(2u+1)2^{-k-1}$. Write $F(n)$ for the number of these incidences at a uniformly sampled eligible start. The prime interval count and the odd squarefree reciprocal sum give $\mathbb EF\sim 2\log(1/(1-\theta))/(3A\zeta(2))$. The trim at $CT$ costs $o(1)$ by a fixed exponential moment of $\omega(m)$ in the harmonic cofactor sum. Each $m$ has unnormalized mass $O(1/(Am\log N))$, so the normalized mass on multiples of $d$ is $O_{a,w}(1/d)$ by summing $1/m$ over those multiples.

## A polynomial bound for one exact count

This gives a direct check on the several-stage local-limit argument. For an integer count $u\ge0$, truncation degree $r\ge5$ and target $v\ge0$, write $I_v(u)=1_{u=v}$. For $v<r$ set

```math
P_v(u)=\sum_{k=v}^{r-1}(-1)^{k-v}\binom{k}{v}\binom{u}{k},
\qquad M_v(u)=\binom rv\binom ur .
```

Bonferroni gives $|I_v-P_v|\le M_v$. Indeed, after choosing $v$ successes, the alternating sum tests that none of the remaining successes occur. The first omitted absolute term is $M_v$. Put $K_v=P_v+M_v$ and $D_v=2M_v$. For $v\ge r$ put $K_v=D_v=\binom ur/\binom vr$. Then $0\le K_v-I_v\le D_v$ on every integer input. The bounds below control the probability and arithmetic errors.

* Summing over all $v$ gives $\sum D_v\le (2^{r+1}+2)\binom ur$ and $\sum K_v\le1+\sum D_v$, since $\sum_{v\ge r}1/\binom vr=r/(r-1)$.
* In the basis $\binom uk$, the sum over $v$ of the absolute coefficients of either polynomial at a fixed $k\le r$ is at most $2^{r+1}+2$. This bound also applies when summing over just the nonnegative targets consistent with a specified total.

For a product of exact indicators, an upper polynomial is the product of the corresponding upper bounds $K$. Subtracting the sum of each $D$ times the upper bounds for the other coordinates gives a lower polynomial by telescoping. We may sum both inequalities over all allocations of an exact total among disjoint prime ranges. This proves the comparison below in one application of the progression estimate and avoids having to assume a local limit for an earlier count under future weights. [Original weighted induction][weighted]

## The weighted joint atom

For $q$ distinct shifts $j_i\ne j_0$ in $0\le j_i\le J=O(T)$, strip $g_i=(b,j_i-j_0)$ and count the distinct primes up to $X^c$ dividing
$L_i(p)=(b/g_i)p+(j_i-j_0)/g_i$. Denote the resulting coordinate by $U_i$. At a test prime use one uniform reduced residue for $p$, shared by all coordinates, independently across primes. A force specifies a reduced residue on a modulus with at most two distinct primes and size at most $X^{\rho/4}$. Conditional model probabilities at its primes use the prescribed residue. A positive sum of such tests $Z$ has comparator mass $z=\sum_f w_f/\varphi(f)$, with incompatible choices removed. The proof uses the explicit digit-problem marks described below. [Definition of the comparison][weighted]

Fix a large strip width $W$, take $Q$ the least even integer at least $100W$, and allow $q\le D=Q+1$. We can use a **single cutoff** for all these orders. Set $R=\lceil W^4\rceil$, $c=\rho/[100D(R+1)]$ and $m=\lceil8\log_2 t\rceil$. The first range ends at $X^{c2^{-m}}$, with degree $r_*=\lceil C_D t\rceil$ per coordinate. In the remaining ranges the endpoints are $X^{c2^{-h-1}}$ and $X^{c2^{-h}}$, with degree $r_h=R+h$, for $0\le h<m$. Each later range has reciprocal prime mass at most 2 for large $N$ by Mertens’ theorem. Its primes exceed all shift differences. Outside the divisors of $b$, the roots modulo such a prime are the distinct residues $-(j_i-j_0)/b$. Primes dividing $b$ there hit no coordinate. Every smaller prime uses the compatible classes of the stripped forms. This also covers coincident roots at primes dividing a shift difference. In particular a divisor of a stripped slope cannot divide its primitive form.

**Arithmetic error.** A product of the binomial test functions expands into choices of unordered prime subsets. Each compatible choice, together with its force, imposes one reduced class modulo the least common multiple $d$. Its model mass is exactly $1/\varphi(d)$ by the Chinese remainder theorem, including overlaps with a prescribed prime power. Across all coordinates the range degrees spend at most

```math
D c 2^{-m}r_*+Dc\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}2^{-h}(R+h)<\rho/4
```

for large $N$. At each recovered prime of the modulus a nonempty subset of the $q$ labels specifies the range tests in which it participates, with the range already fixed by its size. A force prime can also participate in none. There are $O_D(t)$ prime factors because $r_*+\sum_h r_h=O_D(t)$ at fixed $W$. Recovery of one or two ordered prime or power tests adds at most a fixed power of $\log X$ choices, including powers of the same prime recovered from their maximum exponent. The coefficient bound above, multiplied across the ranges and labels, is $\exp(O_D(t))$, **already summed over all allocations of the target**. The number of possible defect terms is $O(D(m+1))$. Thus the absolute coefficient charged to each modulus costs at most $\exp(O_D(t))$ for these marks. Arbitrary fixed log savings absorb this in aggregate over moduli, regardless of which classes are selected. For a deterministic gcd weight one can multiply the unmarked bound separately. This argument evaluates the upper and lower polynomials with an additive error $O(e^{-B t})$ for any desired fixed $B$, by first choosing the saving in the prime theorem sufficiently large.

**Probability error.** Fix each force class in the model first. In a later range, a prime can hit at most one label. For distinct coordinates the mixed binomial moment of orders $k_i$ is bounded by $\prod_i \mu_i^{k_i}/k_i!$, where $\mu_i\le 5$ is an upper bound on the expected count including forced successes. Expand into prime choices and drop the distinctness restriction between labels in the positive sum. This also works for a prescribed hit of probability one. It follows that the total mass of the upper polynomial in this range, summed over its vector targets, is at most $(1+\xi_h)^q$, with $\xi_h=(2^{r_h+1}+2)5^{r_h}/r_h!$. The sum of its defect terms has mass at most $q\xi_h(1+\xi_h)^{q-1}$. For sufficiently large $W$, $\sum_h q\xi_h\le e^{-4R}$.

In the first range let $S_i$ be the $i$-th count. For fixed $s>2$, $\mathbb E(1+s)^{\sum S_i}\le\exp(O_{D,s}(t))$. Each unforced prime hits at most $D$ labels, with union probability at most $D/(\ell-1)$ at prime $\ell$, and the force fixes at most two primes. Use $\binom{u}{r}\le s^{-r}(1+s)^u$ on every nonempty subset of defect factors in $\prod_i(1+(2^{r_*+1}+2)\binom{S_i}{r_*})$. Taking $C_D$ large makes their combined mass $O(e^{-2t})$. The first-range exact vector has maximum atom at most $(C/\sqrt t)^q$ with absolute $C$ for all sufficiently large $N$ at fixed $D$. Indeed the primes $\ell>T^{D+3}$ in the range, excluding divisors of $b$ and the force, have reciprocal mass $t+O_D(\log T)$. The primes dividing $b$ cost $O(\log T)$ by splitting at $(\log N)^2$. Each of those eligible large primes has an empty label with probability at least $1/2$. Pairing the empty outcome with each hit in the squared characteristic function bounds its product by $\exp(-c_1 t\sum_i\sin^2(y_i/2))$ for an absolute $c_1>0$. Fourier inversion proves the bound.

The first upper polynomial therefore also has maximum atom at most $(C/\sqrt t)^q+O(e^{-2t})$. Convolve the range measures at the required total. When the defect belongs to the first range, use its total mass. When it belongs to a later range, use the first range’s maximum atom and the total masses of the other factors. Independence between ranges holds after fixing the force. The model conditions on each force. The arithmetic estimate above was taken collectively over all the force terms, so summing their positive model masses incurs only one aggregate progression error. Uniformly in the target vector $v$ this gives

```math
\left|\mathbb E_P[1_{U=v}Z]-
\sum_f\frac{w_f}{\varphi(f)}\Pr_f(U=v)\right|
\le z\bigl(e^{-2R}t^{-q/2}+O_D(e^{-2t})\bigr)+O(e^{-B t}).
```

Here $\Pr_f$ is the comparator with that force prescribed. This proves the terminal error needed in the weighted induction, including its dependence on $W$. The polynomial brackets can equally be multiplied by any specified nonnegative product of future-range binomial tests within the degree budget. On each fixed force those ranges are independent of the earlier model counts. This accounts for the future weights that appear in Mira’s recursive formulation.

For central targets, the comparator has product Gaussian atoms with absolute error $o_D(t^{-q/2})$ uniformly on any fixed box $|v_i-t|\le C_0\sqrt t$. For detail, above $T^{D+3}$ couple each mutually exclusive prime trial to independent Bernoulli trials with the same marginals. The total variation cost is $O_D(\sum_{\ell>T^{D+3}}\ell^{-2})=o(t^{-q/2})$. The lower primes have a fixed exponential moment bounded by a power of $\log T$, so their displacement exceeds $t^{1/4}$ with probability $o(t^{-q/2})$. Forced increments are bounded. Each independent high-prime coordinate has mean and variance $t+O_D(\log T)$. On the Fourier scale $y/\sqrt t$, its centered log characteristic function is $-y^2/2+o(1)$ for bounded $y$, by the third-moment bound $O(t)$. The integrable bound from $\exp(-c_1 t\sin^2(y/2))$ in the original frequency controls the rest. Fourier inversion and averaging back the small displacements give the stated uniform shape. In particular the moving shifts $j\asymp\log\log N$ cost no extra factor in the atom comparison.

## Actual divisor terms and the outer offsets

Write $V_j=v_2(\tau(n+j))$, $g=(b,j-j_0)$, $G=\Omega(g)$, and $s=j+1-U_j$. Here $\Omega$ counts prime factors with multiplicity. The same bank deficit bounds the angle at both central and outer offsets. Fix $\eta=\rho/16$. The total multiplicity of factors larger than $X^{\eta/4}$ in the stripped form is $O_a(1)$ by size. At a smaller prime $\ell$, count repeated increments by the tests $\ell^e\mid L_j(p)$ with $e\ge2$ and $\ell^e\le X^\eta$. An omitted higher power forces the largest power below $X^\eta$, which is at least $X^{3\eta/4}$. Summing its reciprocal totient over $\ell\le X^{\eta/4}$ costs $O(X^{-\eta/2})$. Each such modulus occurs once, so the progression estimate pays an additional $O(e^{-Bt})$. The union of these exceptions over $O(T)$ sites has negligible probability, and their summed angular cost is negligible as each chord is at most 2.

Let $M_j$ count the distinct factors in $(X^c,X^{\eta/4}]$, and $P_j$ the clipped repeated increments just described. If a prime has exponents $e$ in the stripped form and $d$ in $g$, then

```math
|v_2(e+d+1)-1_{e\ge1}|\le d+(e-1)_+,
\qquad \log_2\bigl(\mathrm{odd}(e+d+1)\bigr)\le2[d+(e-1)_+].
```

Both follow by checking $e+d\le1$ first and using $\log_2(e+d+1)\le e+d$ otherwise. Thus $|V_j-U_j|$ and the base-two logarithm of the odd part of $\tau(n+j)$ are bounded by $C_a(1+G+M_j+P_j)$ outside the exceptions. With $e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$, take $Z_j=C_a[(1+G)^2+M_j^2+P_j^2]$. Away from the exceptional powers we have, for every integer $s$,

```math
1_{j+1-U_j=s}\,|e(\tau(n+j)2^{-j-1})-1|
\le 1_{j+1-U_j=s}\frac{C Z_j}{1+s^2}.
```

Indeed, if the valuation displacement plus the logarithm of the odd part is less than $|s|/2$, then negative $s$ gives an integer and positive $s$ gives a chord of order $2^{-s/2}$. In the other case $Z_j/(1+s^2)$ is bounded below by a positive constant, including at $s=0$. This estimate keeps the actual carries in the complementary product.

The expanded squares force at most two primes or powers. For powers of the same prime take their maximum exponent in the modulus. The series $\sum_{\ell,e\ge2}(2e-3)/\varphi(\ell^e)$, with $\ell$ prime, converges. For the count of distinct supplementary primes Mertens gives mean $O_a(1+\log(1/c))$. These facts give comparator mass $z_j\ll_a(1+G)^2+\log^2(W+2)$. All force products have size at most $X^{2\eta}$. The deterministic factor $(1+G)^2$ grows at most polynomially in $T$ and may multiply an exponential additive error.

**One-coordinate envelope.** For the sites outside $|j-T|\le L\sqrt T$ use a separate fixed cutoff above with one label and a fixed large $R$. The mark then has $z_j\ll_a(1+G)^2$. Uniformly in the target $v$ we have

```math
\mathbb E_P[1_{U_j=v}Z_j]\ll_a
\frac{z_j}{\sqrt t\,(1+(v-t)^2/t)}+(1+G)^2 e^{-B t}.
```

Split the first range into two sets with comparable model variance, for instance by accumulating half its unforced variance. Conditioning on two primes changes either variance by $O(1)$. Give each set its own first-range polynomial. Doubling the first degree costs $o(1)$ in modulus exponent. In one coordinate the masses $\mathbb E K_v$ define a positive measure on the targets, with total mass between 1 and $1+\mathbb E\sum D_v$. Its excess over the exact distribution has second raw moment bounded by $C(1+r^2)(2^{r+1}+2)\mathbb E\binom{u}{r}$. Indeed $v<r$ costs at most $r^2$, and $\sum_{v\ge r}(1+v^2)/\binom vr\ll 1+r^2$ for $r\ge5$, by inserting $1/\binom{r+n}r=r\int_0^1 x^n(1-x)^{r-1}dx$ and summing. In either first set this cost is exponentially small in $t$ by increasing $C_D$. Each first set therefore retains variance $O(t)$ and maximum atom $O(t^{-1/2})$ after normalization, the latter by one-dimensional Fourier inversion. Each normalized later measure has bounded second moment. Their number is $O(\log t)$ and their masses have bounded product by the factorial estimates above.

Convolve the normalized measures, attaching the later sets to one first set. The two independent summands each have maximum atom $O(t^{-1/2})$ and variance $O(t)$. Their means add to $t+O(\log T)$, since the first sets have this total mean already and later means are bounded individually. At a total $v$ far from $t$ one of the summands must deviate from its own mean by at least half the distance to the total mean. Chebyshev on that summand and the maximum atom on its partner give the displayed envelope. Within $O(\sqrt t)$ of $t$ the maximum atom alone suffices. Undo the bounded normalization, sum over force masses, and apply the arithmetic comparison only to the upper polynomial. This proves the envelope with arbitrary fixed saving $B$ in the additive term. It suffices here to have the envelope for the stripped bank count with the positive mark. [Original true-valuation route][integrated] · [Incidence averaging][marked]

Let $f(x)=(1+(x-t)^2/t)^{-1}$. Uniformly in $x$, $\sum_s f(x-s)/(1+s^2)\ll f(x)$ for integer $s$. At distance $d=|x-t|>2\sqrt t$, split at $|s|=d/2$ and use $\sum_s f(x-s)\ll\sqrt t$ on the far part. This and the angular bound control one outer site by $C_a z_j f(j+1)/\sqrt t$ plus a negligible error. To sum the gcd factor, write $b=A m$ with fixed $A$ and $m$ odd squarefree. Pull out $1+\Omega(A)$ and expand $(1+\omega((m,j-j_0)))^2$ with nonnegative divisor coefficients. They are 1 at 1, 3 at a prime, 2 at a product of two distinct primes and zero elsewhere. For a divisor $d$ the shifts lie in $j\equiv j_0(b)\pmod d$. Sum **first at fixed $b$**. Outside the central interval the normalized envelope on this progression has sum $O(1/(Ld)+1/\sqrt T)$ by its tail integral and maximum. The subsequent normalized incidence mass of cofactors with $d\mid m$ is $O_{a,w}(1/d)$ by the harmonic cofactor sum. All these $d$ are $O(T)$ because $j\ne j_0$. The squared-denominator series converges, and the secondary sum is $O((1+\log\log(3T))^2/\sqrt T)$. The outer offsets therefore have total expected angular cost $O_{a,w}(1/L)+o(1)$, with constant independent of $W$.

## Avoidance at the marked term and ordinary density

For the central sites take $L=e^{20W}$ and $B_*=\lceil e^{50W}\rceil$. Omit only $j_0$. The avoidance event requires $|j+1-U_j|>W$ at every remaining central site. Let $\gamma_t(v)=(2\pi t)^{-1/2}\exp(-(v-t)^2/(2t))$ and $p_j=\sum_{|s|\le W}\gamma_t(j+1-s)$. We take $W$ integral. The $p_j$ have maximum $o(1)$ and sum tending uniformly to $(2W+1)\Pr(|G_0|\le L)$ with $G_0$ standard normal. Thus $P_0(b)=\prod_j(1-p_j)$ has a common limit in $b$, bounded below by $e^{-4W}$ for large $W$.

Bonferroni at orders $Q-1,Q$ compares the true avoidance probability to $P_0(b)$. The model bracket width is bounded by $(3eW/Q)^Q$. Each order $q$ has $O((L\sqrt T)^q)$ tuples, at most $(2W+1)^q$ choices of integer targets, and absolute atom error on the lattice scale $t^{-q/2}$. The total error from the terminal comparison is at most $\exp[-2R+O(W\log(WL))]+o_W(1)$, where $o_W(1)$ tends to zero in $N$ at fixed $W$. Thus the avoidance probability is at least $P_0(b)/2$.

For $W<|s|\le B_*$ mark the atom $U_j=j+1-s$ with $Z_j$ and omit $j$ from that Bonferroni sum. Every marked target is in a fixed central box for sufficiently large $N$ at fixed $W$. The main upper bound is $2P_0(b)\gamma_t(j+1-s)z_j$, since omitting $j$ divides the model product by $1-p_j=1+o(1)$ and the Bonferroni remainder also multiplies the marked atom. For each $s$, summing $\gamma_t(j+1-s)z_j$ in normalized incidence mean costs $O_{a,w}(1+\log^2(W+2))$ by the same divisor expansion and progression argument, with bound $O(1/d+1/\sqrt T)$ for the full Gaussian. For the absolute local errors, the additional marked coordinate contributes $t^{-1/2}$ and its site sum costs $O_{a,w}(L(1+\log^2(W+2)))$ after this normalization. The weighted sum over $s$ uses $\sum_{|s|>W}(1+s^2)^{-1}\ll1/W$. The extra $L$ and the tuple factors are absorbed by $\exp[-2R+O(W\log(WL))]$. The Gaussian approximation error tends to zero at fixed $W$, even with the fixed box $L$ and fixed $B_*$. The terms with $|s|>B_*$ need only the one-coordinate maximum atom with the central cutoff. Their total incidence cost is $O_{a,w}(L(1+\log^2(W+2))/B_*)+o_W(1)$, by the progression bound for the count of central sites. Both this and the outer $O(1/L)$ bound are exponentially smaller than $P_0(b)$ as $W$ increases. [Mira’s conditional isolation, §§2–5][isolation]

There is also a direct check on the **second moment** that avoids a prime-pair estimate. At each shift the prime $p>\sqrt{n+j}$ is unique. A different certified incidence at $j$ requires $\tau(n+j)=(2u+1)2^{j-k}$, hence $V_j=j-k$. On that event $|s|\le k+1+|V_j-U_j|$ and $1\ll_{a,w} Z_j/(1+s^2)$ away from the power exceptions. Apply the one-coordinate envelope with the fixed cutoff at **all** $0\le j\le CT$, omitting the selected $j_0$. The convolution and full progression bound just proved give a total $O_{a,w}(1)$ in normalized incidence mean, including the negligible summed power exceptions. The resulting bound controls the mean number of other certificates seen from one certificate, which is $\mathbb E[F(F-1)]/\mathbb EF$. Thus $\mathbb EF^2\ll_{a,w}\mathbb EF$. This suffices at fixed $\theta$. [Mira’s alternative collision count][incidences]

Finally choose $J=\lceil C'T\rceil$ with $C'\ge\max(C,10)$. Since $F\le CT+1$, the mean real tail after $J$ on incidences is $o(1)$. Indeed for $j\le4\log_2 N+1$ divisor pairing on the ordinary interval bounds the mean $\tau(n+j)$ by $O(\log N+\sqrt N/H)$, so the weighted tail is $O_{a,w}(T\log N\,2^{-J})$. For larger $j$ use $\tau(n+j)\le n+j$. On avoidance the conditional mean distance of the complete complementary product from 1 is therefore $O_{a,w}((1+\log^2(W+2))/W)+o_W(1)$, by telescoping the unit factors. All positive estimates transfer to ordinary prime counts as above. Choose $W$ after $k,a$ so that the conditional mean is smaller than half the chord distance $2\sin(\pi/2^{k+1})$ from the midpoint to either cylinder boundary. Markov leaves a positive fraction of genuine incidences inside the cylinder, including the end words. Cauchy–Schwarz now gives $\Pr(F_{\rm good}>0)\ge(\mathbb EF_{\rm good})^2/\mathbb EF^2\gg_{a,w}1$ on the ordinary interval of $H-k+1$ eligible starts. All choices precede $N$, and the use of $a'<a$ pays the end trim even at $H=\lceil N^a\rceil$. Taking $N=H=\lfloor M/2\rfloor$ puts these copies wholly within the first $M$ digits and gives the claimed ordinary lower density.
