# Review of the Stoneham discrepancy bounds

For $b\ge2$, write $\alpha_{b,c}=\sum_{j\ge1}c^{-j}b^{-c^j}$. Let $D_N(x;b)$ be the supremum over half-open intervals in $[0,1)$ of the absolute error in their relative frequency among $\{b^n x\}$, $0\le n<N$. The proof in `648fb5a:notes/phase13/quantitative-normality.md` establishes the effective bound

```math
D_N(P(\alpha_{b,p^e});b)\ll N^{-1/[2(2^{d+1}-2)]}\qquad(N\ge2)
```

for every fixed odd prime $p\nmid b$, $e\ge1$ and $P\in\mathbb Q[X]$ of degree $d\ge1$. The three sharper rates below also follow. The asymptotic estimates are paper deductions. At `90bc475` the distinct assertion $\limsup_N D_N(\alpha_{2,3}\alpha_{2,5};2)\le27/40$ has a complete Lean proof. Both proofs cover arbitrary final cutoffs.

## The analytic input and its range

I checked the formulas against pp. 13–14 of arXiv:1606.07911v1 (25 June 2016). Theorem 5.1 has the two terms $A_k q^\alpha L^\gamma+B_k q^{-\alpha}L^\nu$, **both** multiplied by $(1+\log q)^{2^{-k}}$, with $\alpha=1/(2^{k+2}-2)$. The support is a fixed finite set of primes coprime to $b$, the numerator is a unit modulo $q$, $k$ may be zero, and the inequality holds for every integer $L\ge1$. The single-period restriction in Lemma 2.2 is removed in Lemma 2.3 before the induction. The ranges in §6 describe where this upper bound saves a power. For $q>1$, replace the numerator by $ab^{-1}\bmod q$ to start at exponent zero. At $q=1$ the sum equals $L$ and is covered by $B_kL^\nu$, since $B_k\ge1$ and $\nu\ge1$. [Preprint, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07911v1#page=13)

The local Crossref record confirms the 2019 publication in *Journal d’Analyse Mathématique* **138**, 405–439. The cached full texts of this paper are copies of the same preprint. The theorem numbers, endpoints and recurrence checked here refer to that version. Bailey–Crandall’s Theorem 2.2(9) uses extreme interval discrepancy and $1/H$ in its first term. This gives the stated $1/(H+1)$ formulation on increasing the absolute constant by a factor of two. Their supremum with right endpoint $<1$ also covers right endpoint $1$ by a limit for the finite sample. [Publication record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11854-019-0038-2) · [Bailey–Crandall, Theorem 2.2](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bcnormal-em.pdf#page=4)

## From the real value to every shell prefix

1. **Reduced denominators.** Put $k_0=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor$. The unique highest-degree monomial retains a pole of order at least $dk_0-O(1)$ at $p$ at every late cutoff. Replacing a positive index by an index starting at zero changes its dilation from $m_i$ to $m_i p^{e_i}$ and its coefficient by $p^{-e_i}$, a fixed factor. For integral coefficients the leading multiplier costs at most its own valuation. All lower degrees together have pole order at most $(d-1)k_0+O(1)$. Every tuple in a degree-$a$ prefix has denominator dividing $p^{ak_0}$. This gives both denominator bounds throughout $[X,2X)$. For rational coefficients split their common denominator as $D_bD_0$ with $D_b\mid b^t$ and $(D_0,b)=1$. The orbit of $b^t x$ has interval counts differing by at most $t$ through $N$. Dividing an integral combination's prefix of reduced denominator $q_n$ by $D_0$ gives $q_n\mid Q\mid D_0q_n$. Thus $Q\asymp X^d$ with fixed prime support $S$ coprime to $b$, including when $p\mid D_0$ or the leading coefficient is negative. This proves the same conclusion for the stated finite sums of products on a common prime with one highest-degree monomial. [Denominator survival](../../../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomialPrefix.lean) · [Lower-degree perturbations](../../../NormalityChecker/StonehamPolynomialNormal.lean)
2. **Tail and insertions.** For a degree-$a$ term set $q_i=p^{e_i}$ and $W=\sum_i m_i$. An omitted tuple satisfies $\sum_i m_iq_i^{j_i}>n$, so some $q_i^{j_i}\ge(n+1)/W$. On that coordinate the sum of $q_i^{-j_i}$ is at most $W/((n+1)(1-q_i^{-1}))$. The other weight sums are bounded and $b^{n-\sum_i m_i q_i^{j_i}}\le1$. This proves the $O(1/(n+1))$ error for the true shifted product even one position before an insertion. Summing against the absolute coefficient values also covers signed polynomials, and the constant term has zero tail. In $[X,2X)$ one coordinate of an insertion lies in $[X/a,2X)$ after dilation. It has $O(1)$ choices and each other coordinate has $O(1+\log X)$ choices. Starting a new piece at each distinct interior insertion gives $J\ll(1+\log X)^{d-1}$ pieces of exact rational orbit. All terms at a collision enter that sample. An insertion at the right endpoint belongs to the next interval.
3. **Growing frequencies.** On a piece starting with denominator $Q$, frequency $h$ gives $q=Q/\gcd(h,Q)$. In the positive power one may enlarge $q$ to $Q$. For the inverse power, grouping by $\gcd(h,Q)=r$ and writing $h=rj$ bounds the harmonic loss by

   ```math
   (1+\log H)\sum_{r\mid Q}r^{\alpha-1}\le (1+\log H)\prod_{p'\in S}\frac{1}{1-(p')^{\alpha-1}}.
   ```

   The product is finite even at the largest $\alpha=1/2$. This is uniform in $Q$, its exponents and $H$. For an interval of $T\le X$ positions the real-tail contribution at frequency $h$ is $O(hT/X)$, hence $O(HT/X)$ after harmonic weighting.
4. **Final intervals.** Write $L_j$ for the positive piece lengths. Concavity gives $\sum L_j^\gamma\le J^{1-\gamma}X^\gamma$, including the value $J$ at $\gamma=0$. Also $\sum L_j^\nu\le X^\nu$ since $\nu\ge1$. Up to the common factor $(1+\log X)^{1+2^{-k}}$, the two rational terms in the unnormalized count error are bounded by $J^{1-\gamma}X^{d\alpha+\gamma}$ and $X^{\nu-d\alpha}$. The remaining costs are $O(X/H+H)$. These estimates include $T=1$. A cutoff exactly at a dyadic boundary needs only the complete shells. For every other cutoff the last partial shell has the same error bound. The count errors at dyadic scale $2^j$ grow at most as $2^{j(1-\eta)}(1+\log N)^\ell$, with $1-\eta\ge1/2$ as below. Summing this geometric majorant for each test interval before taking the supremum preserves the logarithmic exponent.

## Exponents and effectivity

Take $k=d-1$, $s_k=(1-\gamma_k)/\alpha_k$ and $t_k=(\nu_k-1)/\alpha_k$. Substitution in Theorem 5.1 gives $s_k-t_k=4-2^{1-k}$ and $s_{k+1}=s_k+1-\alpha_k t_k$. Since $t_k\ge0$, $s_k\le k+2$. In particular $t_0=t_1=0$ and $t_j\le j-1$ for $j\ge2$. Since $s_0=2$, the difference $(k+2)-s_k$ is bounded by a finite initial sum of $(j-1)/2^{j+1}$ for $j\ge2$. Its infinite sum equals $1/2$. Hence $s_k-d>1/2$ and $d-t_k\ge1$. Both rational terms save strictly more than $\alpha_k/2$.

In the table the two margins are $1-\gamma-d\alpha$ and $1-\nu+d\alpha$. With $\eta$ the smaller of these and $1/2$, take $H=\lfloor X^\eta\rfloor$. The last column gives the normalized bound with $L_N=1+\log N$ and $\ell=1+2^{-k}+(d-1)(1-\gamma)$.

| Degree | $(\alpha,\gamma,\nu)$ | Two margins | $N^{-\eta}L_N^\ell$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $(1/2,0,1)$ | $1/2,\ 1/2$ | $N^{-1/2}L_N^2$ |
| 2 | $(1/6,1/2,1)$ | $1/6,\ 1/3$ | $N^{-1/6}L_N^2$ |
| 3 | $(1/14,5/7,29/28)$ | $1/14,\ 5/28$ | $N^{-1/14}L_N^{51/28}$ |

The compactness step yields a terminating algorithm for the constant. For $L$ exceeding every coordinate of the fixed anchor, enumerate the upper subsets of $\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^d$ containing it and test their weighted sums modulo $p^L$. Each pattern extends by upward closure, and every outside point has stretched weight at least $L$. Thus this finite test is equivalent to the global one at precision $L$. The uniform-precision theorem guarantees success, and reduction preserves success at larger precisions. The denominator proof then gives an explicit shell threshold. Vandehey specifies $M$ by a finite order and valuations in (3) and $A_k,B_k$ recursively. Erdős–Turán has an effective absolute constant from interval trigonometric majorants. For $\sigma=\eta-\alpha_k/2>0$, maximizing $(1+u)^\ell e^{-\sigma u}$ over $u\ge0$ absorbs the logarithms effectively. Before the computable shell threshold one can use $D_N\le1$. ## The mixed binary product

At `90bc475`, `independent_product_discrepancy` specializes directly to the product of the two defining positive-index series. Its prefix includes every pair $i,j\ge1$ with $3^i+5^j\le n$, with weight $2^{n-3^i-5^j}/(3^i5^j)$. The omitted terms tend to zero in the shifted value by domination by the summable weights $3^{-i}5^{-j}$. The local interval theorem therefore applies to $\{2^n\alpha_{2,3}\alpha_{2,5}\}$ itself. [Defining statement](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveStatements.lean) · [Prefix and tail](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFivePrefix.lean) · [Uniform interval theorem](../../../NormalityChecker/SupportDiscrepancy.lean)

On $5^l\le n<5^{l+1}$ with $l\ge1$, put $y=n-5^l$. The number of active entries in the top column is $m=\lfloor\log_3 y\rfloor$, with $m=0$ for $y=0$. An entry at $y=3^i$ is included. After removing a unit, the individual residues mod 5 repeat $4,2,1,3$, so the partial sums indexed by $m\bmod4$ are $0,4,1,2$. The full $5^l$ divides the reduced denominator exactly when $4\nmid m$. The charge for a failed test is therefore one at $y=0$ and on $[81^j,3\cdot81^j)$, $j\ge0$. If $C(t)$ counts charged $0\le y<t$, then $40C(81^j)=81^j+39$. Across the next charged segment $40C(t)-27t$ increases to $39$ at $t=3\cdot81^j$ and then decreases. Summing $40C(t)\le27t+39$ over the full quinary shells and one arbitrary truncation gives the formal global upper bound $27N+39\lfloor\log_5 N\rfloor+52$ for forty times the charged count when $N\ge1$. [Exact denominator tests](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveResidues.lean) · [Finite count](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveCount.lean)

Set $B=2^{\lfloor\log_4 N\rfloor}$, so $B^2\le N<4B^2$. The first block, blocks meeting an insertion and the unfinished last block cost $O(B(1+\log N)^2)=o(N)$. On any remaining block the reduced denominator is constant because the rational prefix doubles and its denominator divides a power of 15. One uncharged position gives a full $5^l$ there and hence at the start. This tends uniformly to infinity since that start is at least $B$. The upper bound $Q\le n^2\le B^6$ holds at the start for $B\ge4$. Together with the true tail, these bounds give an $o(B)$ centered count uniformly over intervals on each such block. A clean block with no uncharged position pays its whole length. The resulting bound is on the limsup of extreme discrepancy. For instance $D_1=1$. Sharpness and binary normality of this product remain open here. The separate simultaneous-loss theorem detects the loss of the two **full** powers and allows smaller powers to survive. [Coverage proof](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveCoverage.lean) · [Simultaneous top loss](../../../NormalityChecker/ProductThreeFiveFlow.lean)

I re-elaborated the mixed proof from the pinned sources, ran `leanchecker` on `ProductThreeFiveCoverage` and `ProductThreeFiveFlow`, and checked the final statement against the defining series. Its axiom list is `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. An independent finite check used $D=3^{\lfloor\log_3 100000\rfloor}5^{\lfloor\log_5 100000\rfloor}$. Starting with zero, compute $A_n$ by doubling the previous numerator, adding $D/(3^i5^j)$ for every pair inserted at $n$, and reducing modulo $D$. Then $D/\gcd(D,A_n)$ is the exact denominator. Both local survival tests agreed at all applicable $0\le n<100000$, and the quinary count charged 33,037 positions.

## Scope at other parameters

* **One generator in degree one.** For every $c\ge2$ coprime to $b$, clearing the prefix by $c^k$ on $[c^k,c^{k+1})$ for $k\ge1$ leaves $b^{n-c^k}$ modulo $c$, a unit. This supplies $Q\asymp X$ for every nonconstant rational affine image after the coefficient shift, so the first table entry holds. Bailey–Crandall already report this rate for their standard perturbed orbit after Theorem 4.8. Discarding the first $\lceil\sqrt N\rceil$ samples controls the remaining circle displacement by $O(N^{-1/2})$ and transfers the rate to the real generator. Historical priority for the nonlinear rates remains open in this review. [Bailey–Crandall, p. 535](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bcnormal-em.pdf#page=9)
* **Composite $c>2$.** A bounded valuation loss at one $p\mid c$ gives the lower slope $\lambda=d\log_c(p^{v_p(c)})$ and upper slope $d$. If the real tail is $O(X^{-\theta})$ with $\theta>0$, set $\beta=\min(1,\lambda,\theta)/2$. Every $h\le X^\beta$ leaves $q\gg X^{\lambda-\beta}$. On $L\ge X^{1/2}$, the upper bound $q\ll X^d$ gives $q^{1/(3d)}\le L$ eventually, as required by Corollary 1.2. Corollary 1.2 then saves a fixed positive power uniformly on the long pieces. Short pieces cost at most $JX^{1/2}$ before harmonic weighting. For a single generator the separate cycling-phase paper argument bounds the loss at **every** $p\mid c$. Taken together these give $Q\asymp X^d$ and hence the same rates as Track F, as a further paper deduction. [Composite denominator review](composite-polynomials.md) · [Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07911v1#page=2)
* **Parameter-two square.** For odd $b$ and $k\ge2$, the exact denominator is $2^{2k-2}$ on $[2^k,3\cdot2^{k-1})$ and $2^{k-1}$ on $[3\cdot2^{k-1},2^{k+1})$. Thus $X\ll Q\ll X^2$ for the pure square. At differencing depth one, the positive-power term costs $X^{2/6+1/2}J^{1/2}$ and the inverse-power term costs $X^{1-1/6}$. This independently yields $O(N^{-1/6}L_N^2)$ on paper for $\alpha_{b,2}^2$. In the second half the linear prefix also has denominator of order $X$, so the same profile alone gives no pole separation for an arbitrary quadratic perturbation. [Exact dyadic square profile](../parameter-two-square.md)
