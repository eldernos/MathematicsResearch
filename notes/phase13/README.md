# Phase 13 research checkpoint

Development began at `944e0c466978dc636f348552058a4ef65c9ed8be`, the commit released as **manuscripts-v1.1.0**. Throughout this checkpoint α(*b*,*c*) denotes the Stoneham series starting at index one. Discrepancy is the supremum of the absolute interval-frequency error over half-open intervals. The two classical targets are the *actual* real radix orbit and the reduced arithmetic of its specified rational prefixes. They require different estimates. [Published baseline](https://github.com/eldernos/MathematicsResearch/releases/tag/manuscripts-v1.1.0) · [Result registry](../../docs/results.md) · [Proof verification](../../docs/verification.md)

## Stoneham and localized expansions

| Track | Conclusion and scope | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Parameter two | For every odd radix *b* ≥ 3 the square is normal. Before digit 4 its shifted denominator is 1. On the shell from 2ᵏ to 2ᵏ⁺¹, *k* ≥ 2, it is 2²ᵏ⁻² up to but excluding 3·2ᵏ⁻¹ and is 2ᵏ⁻¹ thereafter. The proposed congruence and the valuation *k* + 1 hold. For every odd prime *q*, every rational polynomial of degree *qʳ* is normal. The independent analysis also finds exact cubic losses and arbitrarily deep extra loss for *X*² − 2*X*. A uniform bounded-loss proof for all quadratics would fail. | [Square, endpoints and 15,360 checks](parameter-two-square.md) · [Higher-degree Lean theorem](../../NormalityChecker/StonehamOddDegree.lean) · [Cyclic-orbit proof, cubic checks and quadratic exception](reviews/symmetric-degree.md) |
| B. Composite parameters | Every nonconstant rational polynomial in α(*b*,*c*) is normal in radix *b* when *c* > 2 and gcd(*b*,*c*) = 1. This includes odd composite parameters for which **every** local unit sequence cycles. The general result and the joint frequencies of distinct positive powers are kernel-verified. Products with distinct strides and dilations have their own hypotheses. | [Proof](composite-polynomials.md) · [Independent cycle and truncation checks](reviews/composite-polynomials.md) · [Lean conclusion](../../NormalityChecker/PeriodicPolynomialNormal.lean) |
| C. Change of radix | The binary-normal square α(2,3)² fails simple normality in base 48. The lower limiting zero frequency is at least 3/log₂48 − ½, greater than 1/48. All rational quadratics in the same generator are nonnormal there. For each degree bound *d*, an explicit threshold on *a* gives nonnormality in base *b*ᵃ*c*. The rational-coefficient discrepancy bounds account for the common denominator. | [Proof, coverage and bounds](alternate-bases.md) · [Lean theorems](../../NormalityChecker/AlternateBaseStoneham.lean) |
| D. Independent axes | The binary product α(2,3)α(2,5) has upper discrepancy at most **27/40**, improving the earlier **43/45**. The loss of any fixed number of top layers at both primes recurs on intervals of positive relative length. The upper bound, local residue tests and a family with tied leading monomials are kernel-verified. Normality of this product is open. | [Residues, flow and coverage](independent-axes.md) · [Independent review](reviews/quantitative-and-mixed.md) |
| E. Localized orders | Lean now proves simultaneous half-open rectangle frequencies for distinct positive orders on a fixed full support with an off-radix prime. The common depth for any positive tolerance and finitely many orders is computable. No general rate in the digit cutoff, or finite termination at tolerance zero for arbitrary supports, follows. Additional results cover shared masks, signed periodic weights on an axis and a precise obstruction for rational-function denominator primes. | [Statements, proofs and examples](localized-joint.md) · [Independent definitions](../../NormalityChecker/LocalizedJointStatements.lean) |
| F. Quantitative normality | For a fixed odd prime-power generator and rational polynomial of degree *d*, interval discrepancy has a power saving with exponent 1/[2(2ᵈ⁺¹ − 2)]. For a quadratic the sharper bound is *O*(*N*⁻¹ᐟ⁶(1 + log *N*)²). Lean checks the arithmetic and the finite transfer with explicit hypotheses. The all-prefix asymptotic bound invokes Vandehey and Erdős–Turán on paper. Constants are computable for fixed inputs; numerical threshold values are not supplied. | [Rate and effectivity](quantitative-normality.md) · [Published input and support correction](reviews/quantitative-coverage.md) |

## Classical constants and external work

| Track | Conclusion and remaining obstacle | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G. The real orbit of ln 2 | For all sufficiently large *N*, Lean proves 1 − *D*<sub>*N*</sub> ≥ ⌊(log₆₈ *N*)/2⌋²/*N*. Transition, age, spike and first-frequency estimates give finite improvements. A badly approximable sparse-digit counterexample shows why such bounds can coexist with discrepancy tending to one. The proof distinguishes an auxiliary rational recurrence from the real orbit. | [Proofs, external inputs and counterexamples](logarithm-transitions.md) · [Lean bound](../../NormalityChecker/LogarithmTransitionBounds.lean) |
| G. Actual numerator residues | On a short prime block, an actual residue of the prime-free two-stride prefix occurs at most *O*(*T*²ᐟ³) times. Across primes, three adjacent diagonal prefixes can have an affine fit at at most one exceptional prime in the shared upper band. Exact overlapping CRT tests force 1,253 changes in 3,840 bits across four fixed shells. None of these estimates gives positive lower density of changes on arbitrarily late real prefixes. | [Raw prefixes and kernel proofs](raw-logarithm-residues.md) · [Corrected numerators, proof status and finite counts](corrected-numerators.md) |
| H. Complementary logarithms | A Gaussian representation of ln 2 has reduced-denominator exponent **5/12**, compared with the **1/2** optimum in the finite real dyadic class. The identity, actual missing prime band and upper exponent are kernel-verified. The matching lower exponent and modular character bounds have paper proofs. A deficit of a local character has yet to transfer to the first Fourier character of the real logarithm. | [Construction and component estimates](complementary-characters.md) · [Independent band and residue audit](reviews/classical-bands.md) |
| I. Erdős–Borwein constant | The earlier September conditional Lean statement and Captain Sude’s subsequent unconditional disjunctivity release have distinct hypotheses. Mira Cult’s stronger result gives positive word density throughout windows of length at least *N*ᵃ for fixed *a* > 7/12 up to 1. The follow-up paper audit checks the weighted local-limit argument, including the principal-character contribution. Together with the binary transition criterion this yields upper discrepancy strictly below one for **this constant** on paper. A kernel-checked implication converts single-word counts to Fourier bounds; the external analytic proof is separate. Shiu’s estimate localizes the divisor Fourier sum. Binary normality remains open. | [Pinned sources and tools](erdos-borwein.md) · [Weighted local-limit review](reviews/erdos-local-limit.md) · [Lean transfer](../../NormalityChecker/ErdosBorweinFourier.lean) |

## Attribution and reproducibility

The parameter-two square, the general coprime polynomial proof, the improved mixed-product coverage, and the Gaussian denominator computation extend the previous checkpoint. The localized joint theorem formalizes a corollary already printed in Paper II. Bailey–Crandall’s single-generator normality, Vandehey’s short-orbit estimates, the arithmetic and digital results cited from Mira, Captain Sude, and the published work listed in the individual notes are antecedents. Release of these proofs establishes an exact verifiable repository state. Historical priority for the extensions remains open to specialist assessment.

The six approaches to ln 2 in Track G leave the same quantitative question open: no fixed positive lower Fourier deficit or transition density on all sufficiently long prefixes has been proved here. In particular the target *D*∞ ≤ 0.9999 remains open.

From the checkpoint checkout, run `bash scripts/verify.sh` for the full Lean build, axiom audit, independent statement checks, Python tests, registry and theorem directory checks, and kernel replay. The exact classical and independent-axis experiments can be repeated with the following commands.

```sh

python3 -B scripts/check_independent_axes.py
python3 -B scripts/phase13_corrected_numerators.py
python3 -B notes/phase13/complementary_characters.py
```
