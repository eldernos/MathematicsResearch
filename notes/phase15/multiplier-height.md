# How far extra multipliers can see

Put $E(t)=1-\cos(2\pi t)$ and let $M_N\ge1$ be a prescribed integer height. Consider the strongest statistic obtainable by choosing a multiplier separately at every position,
$$
J_N(x)=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\max_{1\le m\le M_N} E(m2^nx).
$$
Restricting the maximum to a box of products of powers of three and five, or averaging over such a box with probability weights, can only decrease this statistic. The height also controls precision: an unwrapped error $\epsilon$ at a given start becomes at most $m2^j\epsilon$ after multiplication by $m$ and $j$ binary shifts.

**Subexponential heights (paper theorem).** For every sequence with $\log M_N=o(N)$ there is an irrational $x$ with $J_N(x)\to0$ and $D_N(x)\to1$. In particular, even arbitrary position-dependent choices among *all* these multipliers can simultaneously fail to see any average escape. Furstenberg's topological theorem still makes the full joint orbit under two and three dense for this $x$. [Literature and projection identities](projection-and-returns.md)

Put $L_N=\lceil\log_2 M_N\rceil+\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$. Choose increasing digit positions $p_j$ recursively, with $p_{j+1}\ge2p_j$, so late that $L_N/N\le j^{-2}$ for every integer $N\ge p_j/2$. Take ones exactly at the positions $p_j$. Growing gaps imply irrationality. For large $N$ let $j$ count the ones through digit $2N$. Since $N\ge p_j/2$ we have $j L_N/N\le1/j$. As $L_N\le N$ eventually, at most $jL_N$ starts in the prefix can see a one within their next $L_N$ digits. Elsewhere $0\le\{2^nx\}\le2^{-L_N}$, so even after multiplying by any $m\le M_N$ the escape is at most $2\pi^2/(N+1)^2$. The exceptional starts contribute at most $2/j$ to the mean. Hence $J_N(x)\to0$. For each fixed $b$, the frequency of starts whose next $b$ digits are zero tends to one by the same count. Testing $[0,2^{-b})$ and then letting $b$ grow gives the discrepancy limit.

**Linear logarithmic height (paper theorem).** Conversely, if $\log_2 M_N\ge cN$ eventually for $c>0$, then for *every* irrational $x$ even the maximum restricted to powers of three has upper limiting mean at least $\min(c,1)/2$. This lower bound concerns the adaptive statistic and its **upper** limit. It holds in particular at the sparse examples, whose binary discrepancy tends to one.

There are infinitely many cutoffs $N$ for which $\{2^{N-1}x\}\in[1/4,3/4)$, since an irrational binary word changes infinitely often. For $n<N$, the distance $d=\|2^nx\|$ to the nearest integer satisfies $d\ge2^{-(N-1-n)}/4$ at such cutoffs, by the Lipschitz bound for doubling. Fix $0<h<\min(c,1)$. At each of the last $\lfloor hN\rfloor$ positions, the largest power of three at most $M_N$ is greater than $M_N/3$ and its product with $d$ exceeds $1/6$ eventually. If $d\ge1/6$, use multiplier one. Otherwise the first power of three taking the ordinary product with $d$ to at least $1/6$ takes it to less than $1/2$, before any wrap can reduce the distance. The escape there is at least $1/2$. Let $h$ increase to $\min(c,1)$.

Even for an arbitrarily fast prescribed height sequence there is no general positive *lower* limit. Having chosen the first $j$ one-positions, choose $N_j>j p_j$ and place the next one beyond $N_j+L_{N_j}$, using the same definition of $L$ without a growth restriction. All starts from $p_j$ through $N_j-1$ are then good in the preceding argument. This gives $J_{N_j}\le 2/j+2\pi^2/(N_j+1)^2$ for that one irrational. The construction leaves open the logarithm-specific estimate.

A single fixed multiplier admits a converse with a cost: $E(mt)\le m^2E(t)$. Hence strict positive *lower* mean for a bounded-height statistic suffices on the binary axis after dividing by the square of the height. The two theorems show why this argument loses information when the permitted height grows. The subexponential construction is a sparse-word counting argument, and the exponential assertion is an elementary quantitative consequence of expansion. Neither uses a measure-rigidity theorem.
