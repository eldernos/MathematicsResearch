# Open problems and limitations

This page lists what the repository has **not** proved, with the strongest partial results and where they stop. A result that bounds discrepancy, certifies part of an orbit or checks a finite range is listed with its exact scope.

## Classical constants

**Normality of ln 2, π, *e*, √2 and φ.** Open in every base. Nothing here settles normality or simple normality for any of these constants.

**A discrepancy bound below one.** For none of these constants is any asymptotic bound *D*⁺ < 1 known from this work. In particular **the binary bound *D*⁺ ≤ 0.9999 for ln 2 remains open.**

What is proved points sharply at the structural obstacles rather than past them:

- Binary discrepancy is below one exactly when digit transitions have positive lower density. For ln 2, π and *e* the proved transition counts grow only logarithmically; for quadratic irrationals (√2, φ) known bounds give ≫ √*N* ones (BBCP 2004), still density zero. [Sharp transition theorem](../notes/transition-discrepancy.md) · [Unified comparison](../notes/phase17/classical-approximants.md)
- A mean first-frequency deficit of 0.0128 in each four sufficiently late dyadic shells would give *D*⁺ ≤ 0.9999 for ln 2. No such deficit is known. [Moment and dyadic criteria](../notes/classical-discrepancy.md)
- **Denominator and window–denominator barriers.** The shifted denominators of every balanced algebraic trace source for ln 2 have rate at least 5/12 (with a (5/12, 13/30) gap preserved adaptively up to exponential height, [CC-42](results.md#cc-42), [CC-43](results.md#cc-43)). Outside balanced traces, Legendre–Bezout correlated rounding yields *w* = 0 and linear windows *T*<sub>*N*</sub> = log₂ *L*<sub>*D*</sub> − *O*(1), but its numerator is exactly round(*L*<sub>*D*</sub> 2ⁿ ln 2), and the unconditional window–denominator barrier *T*<sub>*N*</sub> ≤ (μ − 1)*N* + μ log₂ *q*<sub>*N*</sub> + *O*(1) rules out the incomplete-orbit hypothesis *T*<sub>*N*</sub> ≥ *q*<sub>*N*</sub><sup>δ</sup> at every positive denominator rate ([CC-50](results.md#cc-50)). [Classical-constant figure](figures.md#classical-denominator-growth)
- **Erdős–Borwein constant *E*.** Disjunctivity (Captain Sude) and positive lower word density (Mira Cult) are known, while simple non-normality (*f*(1) < 1/2, predicted near *p*\* ∈ [0.492450290, 0.492460864]) remains open. Track C establishes that 16 columns (*Ψ*₁₆) is the sharp minimal column truncation given Phase 14's tail mean, that moments of order ≤ 4 fail globally (and ≤ 8 at *D* = 16; order 92 suffices), and that trigonometric majorants require degree > 110 (degree 116 suffices), pinpointing lattice-scale *r*-point divisor correlations (*r* ≥ 9 at *D* = 16) as the exact open arithmetic input ([CC-49](results.md#cc-49)).
- Exact finite computations cover initial segments (e.g., 888 of the first 1,024 positions of ln 2 in twelve-position blocks; certified shell frequencies of *E* through 2²⁴). **Finite digit tests are not asymptotic progress.** [Coverage proofs and finite scope](../notes/intermittent-coverage.md) · [Sieve](../notes/sublinear-residue-windows.md)

**Restricted versus unrestricted logarithms.** The finite-support theorem ([LS-01](results.md#ls-01)) proves normality of Σ 1/(*m*ˢ*b*ᵐ) when *m* ranges over integers built from a *finite* set of primes. The ordinary series for ln 2 uses every positive integer. Normality on every finite support does not pass to the limit, and no uniform estimate of the required kind is known. Likewise, the logarithmic Euler-product representation ln 2 = Σ μ(*m*) *F*(2<sup>−*m*</sup>) collapses chronologically to the ordinary Taylor prefixes and inherits the superpolynomial period barrier ([GF-09](results.md#gf-09), [OP-11](results.md#op-11)).

**Dense *q*-series, partition values and higher modular weights.** While *E*<sub>*b*</sub>, *E*<sub>*b*</sub><sup>2</sup>, *E*<sub>*b*</sub><sup>3</sup>, every binary quadratic-form theta value Θ<sub>*Q*</sub>(1/*b*), and every integer-coefficient holomorphic weight-1 modular form *f*(1/*b*) belong to the numerical translation kernel *K*<sub>*b*</sub> ([GF-03](results.md#gf-03), [GF-04](results.md#gf-04), [GF-06](results.md#gf-06)), distributional properties (normality, simple normality, *D*<sup>+</sup> < 1, or non-membership in *K*<sub>*b*</sub>) remain completely open for the partition constant *P*<sub>*b*</sub> = 1/*E*<sub>*b*</sub> = Σ *p*(*n*)*b*<sup>−*n*</sup>, higher theta powers Θ<sub>3</sub>(1/*b*)<sup>3</sup> and Θ<sub>3</sub>(1/*b*)<sup>4</sup>, the Eisenstein values *E*<sub>2</sub>(1/*b*), *E*<sub>4</sub>(1/*b*), *E*<sub>6</sub>(1/*b*), the discriminant Δ(1/*b*), and Serre's lacunary even η-powers *E*<sub>*b*</sub><sup>*r*</sup> for *r* ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10, 14, 26} ([GF-08](results.md#gf-08)). Coefficient transfer fails at weight ≥ 2 because polynomial coefficient growth causes radix-digit overlap despite lacunarity, and *K*<sub>*b*</sub> is closed under neither squaring nor inversion.

## Stoneham-type numbers

**Independent-prime products.** For α(2, 3) α(2, 5), the binary shifts are logarithmically equidistributed with *D*⁻ = 0, *D*⁺ ≤ 4/11 + 2.85 × 10⁻¹⁰ ([ST-32](results.md#st-32)), and *O*<sub>ε</sub>(log\* *T*) scale sparsity; Phase 17 reduces the zero-slack bound *D*⁺ ≤ 4/11 to the exact equivalence limsup #{*n* < *N* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> ∈ ℤ}/*N* ≤ 4/11 and extends logarithmic equidistribution, *D*⁻ = 0, power-tower spacing, and strict sub-one upper discrepancy bounds to an infinite family of radices *B* for every distinct odd prime pair (*p*, *q*) ([ST-35](results.md#st-35)). Ordinary normality remains open for every mixed-prime product. [Exceptional orbits and infinite family](../notes/phase17/mixed-product-orbits.md)

**Parameter two.** Every rational quadratic has logarithmic equidistribution, *D*⁻ = 0, and *D*⁺ ≤ 1/4, with exact plateau-cycle formulas and unconditional bounds strictly below 1/4 (e.g., (1 − 1/(3 · 2<sup>*v*₂(*b*+1)</sup>))/4 for *D* = 3, *b* ≡ 2 (mod 3), and 5/72 for *b* = 5, *D* = 9, [ST-34](results.md#st-34)). Normality of *X*² − 2*X* at any specified odd integer radix remains open. For higher degrees, the exact least orbit order *F*(*d*) = Σ<sub>*k*≤*d*</sub> ⌊log₂ *k*⌋ and nonvanishing on *J*<sub>*d*</sub> are proved for all *d* ≥ 2, and full polynomial normality is established for degrees 3–120, degree 128, every odd prime power, and sixteen infinite cyclic-descent families *e p*ʳ ([ST-33](results.md#st-33)). Proving *M*<sub>*d*</sub> ≠ 0 on [1, 2) for all *d* ≥ 3 (including general powers of two 2ʳ, *r* ≥ 8, and even multiples 2*p*ʳ > 120) remains open. [Phase 17 dyadic Stoneham classification](../notes/phase17/dyadic-stoneham-classification.md)

**Ternary normality of the binary Stoneham number.** Normality of α with *b* = 2 and *p* = 3 in base 3 is open. A paper proof gives superlinear growth of the number of distinct ternary words. [Ternary word complexity](../notes/ternary-stoneham-complexity.md)

**Rational functions and several independent generators.** [ST-01](results.md#st-01) covers polynomials in one Stoneham number, and [ST-04](results.md#st-04) covers combinations of products along one prime axis with a unique monomial of top degree. Rational functions such as 1/α, and general polynomials in Stoneham numbers on different prime axes, are not covered.

## Certificates and finite-support theory

**Finite termination.** On every unsigned full support with an off-base prime, the worst-prefix certificate tends to zero ([LS-03](results.md#ls-03)). Whether it reaches zero at a finite depth is proved for two-prime supports on paper and is open in general. A fixed mask has failures at all depths. [Finite-depth analysis](../notes/fixed-depth-question.md)

**Positive persistent mass on generalized supports.** Intermediate discrepancy occurs for signed, masked and weighted families. For unsigned full supports it is impossible. The classification of masked supports with nonmonomial polynomial denominators is incomplete. [Polynomial masks](../notes/polynomial-masks.md)

## Novelty and review

**Historical priority.** Each result has been compared with the literature known to the project (Stoneham; Korobov; Bailey and Crandall; Wagner; Kano and Shiokawa; Coons; Vandehey; Dibag; recent Stoneham-sum and localized-logarithm manuscripts), but a search that finds nothing does not establish novelty. [Literature comparisons](../notes/reviewers.md#relation-to-prior-work)

**Independent review.** The Lean kernel has checked the formal statements. Whether each formal statement captures the intended mathematics, and whether the written proofs are complete, needs review by mathematicians outside the project. None of the manuscripts has been peer reviewed. [Guide for mathematicians](guide-mathematicians.md) · [Guide for Lean reviewers](guide-formal-review.md) · [Contributing](../CONTRIBUTING.md)
